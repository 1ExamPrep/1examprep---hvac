Exam Details

The Maryland HVACR Journeyman examination is administered by PSI Services LLC through the licensing examination program overseen by the Maryland Board of Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractors.

The examination evaluates technical knowledge involving electrical systems, piping, refrigeration, air conditioning, heating equipment, air distribution, and workplace safety.

  • Exam Name: Maryland HVACR Journeyman
  • Testing Provider: PSI Services LLC
  • Licensing Authority: Maryland Board of Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractors
  • Exam Format: Multiple-choice examination
  • Number of Questions: 100
  • Time Limit: 240 minutes (4 hours)
  • Passing Score: 70%
  • Exam Type: Open book
  • Online Practice Access: 3 months

The official PSI examination outline identifies six major subject areas:

  • Electrical Knowledge, Motors, and Controls – 20 Questions: AC and DC circuits, electrical motors, control devices, and electrical operating principles.
  • Piping – 15 Questions: Blueprints, piping materials, joints, fittings, installation requirements, pressure testing, and insulation.
  • Refrigeration and Air Conditioning – 25 Questions: Refrigeration theory, refrigerants, equipment sizing, system installation, operation, troubleshooting, and maintenance.
  • Heating Systems – 20 Questions: Heating equipment, combustion air, system sizing, installation, hydronic systems, venting, and troubleshooting.
  • Air Distribution Systems – 15 Questions: Duct sizing, installation, materials, insulation, ventilation, and testing and balancing.
  • Safety – 5 Questions: Workplace safety, personal protective equipment, ladders, fall protection, tools, and construction safety requirements.

Refrigeration and Air Conditioning represents the largest examination category, with 25 questions. Electrical Knowledge and Heating Systems each account for 20 questions.

These three categories represent 65% of the examination. Understanding their relative importance can help you allocate your study time while maintaining familiarity with all six examination subjects.

With four hours available for 100 questions, candidates have an average of 2.4 minutes per question. Efficient technical reasoning and reference navigation are important elements of examination preparation.

Open Book Test

The Maryland HVACR Journeyman (PSI) examination is an open book test. Candidates may bring approved reference publications into the examination room according to PSI's testing rules.

Approved references include the 2018 International Mechanical Code, Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning in the 21st or 22nd Edition, Manual J Residential Load Calculation in the specified eighth edition, OSHA construction safety regulations, and the ACCA Ductulator.

PSI permits reference books to contain highlighting, underlining, and approved indexing tabs. Books must not contain handwritten notes or additional loose or attached papers. Study guides are not permitted in the examination room.

The examination provider does not supply the approved references at the testing center.

Effective open-book preparation includes:

  • Learning the organization of the International Mechanical Code.
  • Reviewing refrigeration, heating, and ventilation terminology.
  • Understanding residential heating and cooling load calculation principles.
  • Practicing navigation through approved technical references.
  • Becoming familiar with duct sizing and airflow relationships.
  • Reviewing OSHA construction safety requirements.
  • Practicing technical questions within a realistic time allowance.

Open-book examinations reward candidates who understand the material and can locate relevant information efficiently. Knowing which reference addresses a particular subject can help reduce unnecessary searching.

Use online practice sessions to develop familiarity with the topics and technical publications before examination day.

Licensing Steps

Maryland HVACR licensing is administered through the state's Board of Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractors.

The licensing process establishes qualifications for individuals performing HVACR work under the direction and control of appropriately licensed contractors.

  1. Register as an HVACR Apprentice: Begin the applicable Maryland apprenticeship licensing process.
  2. Complete Required Training: Accumulate qualifying HVACR training and practical experience under a licensed contractor.
  3. Maintain Training Documentation: Organize apprenticeship records and evidence of qualifying work experience.
  4. Apply for Examination Eligibility: Submit the required application and supporting documentation through PSI's examination registration process.
  5. Prepare for the Journeyman Examination: Review technical references, study the six examination categories, and use online practice questions.
  6. Schedule the PSI Examination: Arrange your testing appointment after your eligibility documentation has been accepted.
  7. Pass the Examination: Achieve the required passing score of 70%.
  8. Apply for Journeyman Licensure: Complete the Maryland licensing application process after satisfying examination and experience requirements.

Completing the apprenticeship requirements and organizing documentation before applying for examination authorization helps create a more manageable path toward journeyman licensing.

State Requirements

The Maryland Board of Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractors regulates HVACR licensing throughout the state.

Maryland establishes specific qualifications for the Journeyman HVACR license, including apprenticeship experience and successful completion of the licensing examination.

The established requirements include:

  • Apprenticeship Period: Holding an HVACR apprentice license for at least four years.
  • Practical Training: Completing at least 6,000 hours of HVACR training during the apprenticeship period.
  • Licensed Supervision: Completing qualifying training under the direction and control of a licensed HVACR contractor.
  • Examination: Passing the Maryland HVACR Journeyman examination with a minimum score of 70%.
  • Current Apprentice Status: Holding an apprentice license when applying through the established journeyman licensing pathway.

Maryland law also provides for recognition of certain comparable professional training, formal education, teaching experience, and qualifying work experience when accepted by the Board.

A Journeyman HVACR license allows the license holder to provide HVACR services within the scope established by state law and under the applicable direction and control of a licensed contractor.

Understanding these requirements helps candidates organize their professional development and prepare for the responsibilities associated with journeyman-level HVACR work.

Reference Books

The following references support preparation for the Maryland PSI HVACR Journeyman examination.

  • International Mechanical Code, 2018
    Published by the International Code Council, the 2018 International Mechanical Code addresses mechanical installations, ventilation, refrigeration systems, ductwork, equipment requirements, exhaust systems, and safety provisions. It is an important reference for interpreting mechanical installation requirements and reviewing HVACR code questions.
  • Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning, 22nd Edition
    This comprehensive HVACR textbook provides explanations of refrigeration theory, refrigerants, compressors, electrical controls, heating systems, air conditioning equipment, maintenance, and troubleshooting. It supports understanding of the technical principles used in residential and commercial HVACR applications.
  • Residential Load Calculation - Manual J, 8th Edition
    Published by the Air Conditioning Contractors of America, Manual J provides a recognized methodology for residential heating and cooling load calculations. It addresses building characteristics, heat gain, heat loss, and equipment sizing considerations, helping technicians understand the relationship between building loads and HVAC system selection.
  • Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA)
    These federal construction safety regulations address workplace hazards, personal protective equipment, fall protection, ladders, scaffolds, tools, electrical safety, and other construction-related safety requirements. They support preparation for the safety portion of the Maryland HVACR examination.
  • ACCA Ductulator
    The ACCA Ductulator is a duct sizing slide-rule tool used to evaluate duct dimensions, airflow, and friction relationships. It supports preparation involving air distribution system design and practical duct sizing concepts.

These references complement one another by addressing mechanical codes, HVACR technical theory, residential load calculations, workplace safety, and air distribution design.

Using the references alongside online practice questions encourages a broader understanding of the subjects included in the Maryland HVACR Journeyman examination.

Piping Systems

Piping knowledge is important for HVACR equipment installation and service.

Heating Systems

Heating Systems accounts for 20 examination questions.

Study heating principles, equipment types, combustion air, fuel oil systems, hydronic systems, vents, chimneys, and equipment operation.

Air Distribution Systems

Air Distribution Systems represents 15 examination questions and includes both technical installation and system design concepts.

Study duct dimensions, airflow, duct materials, insulation, fittings, ventilation requirements, and testing and balancing principles.

Residential Heating and Cooling Load Calculations

Residential load calculations help determine heating and cooling requirements based on building characteristics and design conditions.

Review the principles presented in Manual J, including heat gain, heat loss, insulation, windows, infiltration, and building orientation.

Understanding load calculations helps connect equipment selection with the actual heating and cooling needs of a building.

Workplace Safety

Safety accounts for five examination questions and addresses important principles associated with construction and HVACR work.

Review personal protective equipment, ladder safety, scaffolding, fall protection, tools, material handling, and other subjects covered by OSHA's construction safety regulations.

Understanding these principles supports responsible work practices in the HVACR trade.
