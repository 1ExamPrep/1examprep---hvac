Exam Details

The current Utah HVAC written examination is identified in the Prov examination program as the Utah HVAC Master Technician written exam.

The examination contains 110 total items. Of those, 100 items are weighted and contribute to the final score, while 10 are pilot items. The examination requires a 70% passing score and provides 120 minutes for completion.

The current examination outline includes the following major areas:

  • General Knowledge – 17 weighted questions
  • Systems and Sizing – 16 weighted questions
  • Maintenance – 7 weighted questions
  • Plan Reading – 8 weighted questions
  • Refrigeration Systems – 7 weighted questions
  • Piping – 7 weighted questions
  • Electrical – 6 weighted questions
  • Math and Calculations – 7 weighted questions
  • Rules and Law – 25 weighted questions
  • Pilot Items – 10 questions

Rules and Law is the largest weighted category with 25 questions. Candidates should therefore give Utah licensing and regulatory material significant attention rather than treating it as a minor supplement to the technical references.

General Knowledge contains 17 weighted questions and includes topics such as applying mechanical-code requirements, understanding electrical requirements that affect HVAC installation, recognizing factors that affect heat gain and heat loss, and applying heat-pump installation and service principles.

Systems and Sizing contains 16 weighted questions. Preparation should include hot-water and chilled-water systems, exhaust systems, indoor air quality, energy-saving accessories, humidification, compressor systems, load calculation, duct design, and equipment selection concepts.

Open Book Test

The Utah HVAC written examination is an open book test. Candidates are permitted to use reference books identified for the examination and are responsible for bringing their own approved materials to the testing session.

Prov encourages candidates to prepare reference materials by highlighting important text and placing permanent tabs on important pages. This can help candidates develop a faster navigation system before exam day.

The International Mechanical Code 2024 should be familiar for questions involving mechanical systems, ventilation, exhaust, equipment, ducts, combustion air, and related HVAC requirements. Fuel-gas questions may require the International Fuel Gas Code 2024 together with applicable Utah amendments.

The International Residential Code 2024 supports residential HVAC code preparation, while Manual J and Manual D support residential load calculations and duct-system design. A ductulator can assist with air-distribution and duct-sizing calculations during preparation.

Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning provides broader technical coverage involving refrigeration cycles, refrigerants, components, heat pumps, heating systems, controls, troubleshooting, piping, and practical HVAC principles.

Open-book candidates should practice selecting the correct reference before beginning a detailed lookup. A load-calculation question should immediately suggest Manual J, a duct-sizing problem should suggest Manual D or the ductulator, and a mechanical-code question should direct attention toward the IMC.

Licensing Steps

Utah contractor licensing is administered through the Utah Division of Professional Licensing. HVAC contracting is identified within Utah's contractor classification system as the S350 HVAC Contractor specialty classification.

  1. Determine the applicable Utah HVAC licensing path. Review the current requirements associated with HVAC work and the contractor classification being pursued.
  2. Meet applicable experience and qualification requirements. Candidates and contractor qualifiers must satisfy the requirements associated with the applicable Utah HVAC licensing process.
  3. Prepare for the written HVAC examination. Study mechanical codes, fuel-gas codes, HVAC design, refrigeration, piping, electrical controls, mathematics, system sizing, and Utah rules and law.
  4. Work directly with the examination references. Use the codes, manuals, technical textbooks, calculation tools, and state regulatory material throughout preparation.
  5. Practice open-book navigation. Learn which reference applies to each major technical category and practice locating information under timed conditions.
  6. Register for the examination through Prov. Follow the current Prov registration process for the Utah HVAC examination.
  7. Complete the written examination. The current exam contains 110 total items, including 100 weighted questions and 10 pilot questions.
  8. Meet the required passing score. The written examination is graded against a 70% cut score.
  9. Complete any additional examination requirements that apply. Utah's HVAC examination program includes practical testing requirements for applicable candidates.
  10. Complete the remaining DOPL licensing requirements. Examination success is one component of the overall Utah licensing process.

State Requirements

Utah regulates HVAC contracting through its construction-trades licensing system. The current contractor classification list identifies S350 HVAC Contractor as the applicable specialty classification for HVAC contracting work.

Utah law recognizes HVAC work as a regulated construction activity. Installation, repair, or replacement of heating, ventilation, or air-conditioning systems where licensing is required must be performed within the scope of the applicable contractor licensing requirements.

The current HVAC examination program is administered through Prov. The written examination may be taken at approved Prov testing locations, and the current candidate bulletin also provides a remote-proctoring option for the written exam.

The written examination is only one part of the applicable Utah HVAC qualification process. Candidates should complete all experience, examination, application, business, and contractor-classification requirements that apply to the license or classification they are pursuing.

Utah licensing law and administrative rules are important enough to account for 25 weighted questions on the current HVAC written examination. Candidates should become comfortable navigating both technical construction references and regulatory material.

Reference Books

  • International Mechanical Code 2024
    Provides requirements for mechanical systems, equipment, ventilation, exhaust, ducts, combustion air, appliances, and other HVAC installations. It is a primary code reference for the current Utah HVAC written examination.
  • Utah Amendments to International Mechanical Code, 2015
    Provides Utah-specific modifications associated with mechanical-code requirements and supports preparation for state-specific code application.
  • International Fuel Gas Code 2024
    Covers fuel-gas piping, equipment, appliances, combustion air, venting, and related installation requirements important to gas-fired HVAC systems.
  • Utah Amendments to International Fuel Gas Code, 2015
    Provides Utah-specific modifications to fuel-gas requirements and should be reviewed alongside the applicable International Fuel Gas Code provisions.
  • International Residential Code (IRC), 2024
    Provides code requirements for residential construction, including provisions applicable to residential mechanical systems, fuel gas, equipment, ventilation, and related installations.
  • Residential Load Calculation - Manual J, 8th Edition
    Provides recognized procedures for calculating residential heating and cooling loads. Candidates can use it to study heat gain, heat loss, building characteristics, and HVAC system sizing.
  • Manual D, 2016, 3rd Edition
    Provides residential duct-system design procedures involving airflow, friction, duct sizing, fittings, static pressure, and distribution-system design.
  • Ductulator
    A practical duct-sizing tool used to relate airflow, duct dimensions, velocity, and friction rate during air-distribution calculations.
  • Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning, 22nd Edition
    Provides broad technical coverage of refrigeration and HVAC equipment, refrigerants, components, controls, heat pumps, heating systems, troubleshooting, electrical principles, piping, and service procedures.
  • General Rules of the Division of Occupational and Professional Licensing, Utah Administrative Code, R156-1
    Provides general Utah professional licensing rules relevant to regulated occupations and supports preparation for the Rules and Law portion of the exam.
  • Utah Construction Trades Licensing Act Rule, R156-55
    Addresses administrative requirements associated with Utah construction-trades licensing and contractor regulation.
  • Division of Occupational and Professional Licensing Act, Utah Code Title 58, Chapter 1
    Provides Utah statutory provisions governing professional licensing authority, administration, regulated conduct, and other licensing matters.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Utah HVAC study plan should follow the current examination categories. Begin by balancing technical HVAC study with Rules and Law preparation, because state regulatory material represents a substantial portion of the scored examination.

For General Knowledge, review mechanical-code requirements, heat gain and loss concepts, heat-pump installation and service, basic HVAC design principles, and electrical requirements affecting HVAC equipment.

For Systems and Sizing, work with Manual J and Manual D. Candidates should understand how building characteristics influence heating and cooling loads and how calculated airflow requirements connect to duct design.

Practice with the ductulator until airflow, friction rate, velocity, and duct dimensions can be interpreted efficiently. Duct-design questions are easier when candidates understand the relationship among these values instead of treating the tool as a simple answer finder.

For Maintenance, review pressure testing, heat-pump troubleshooting, gas-heating systems, soldering and brazing practices, HVAC design principles, and common service procedures.

Plan Reading requires candidates to interpret blueprints and architectural plans, recognize HVAC information shown on drawings, and understand how code and equipment requirements apply to the project information presented.

For Refrigeration Systems, study refrigerants, refrigeration-cycle components, compressor systems, refrigerant classifications, piping support, and practical refrigeration principles using Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning.

For Piping, review vent systems, refrigerant piping, condensate piping, friction-loss concepts, pipe joining, flared and compression fittings, brazing practices, and installation considerations.

The Electrical section includes HVAC power, load, and control circuits. Candidates should understand basic HVAC electrical components and troubleshooting concepts and know how electrical requirements affect HVAC equipment installations.

For Math and Calculations, practice measurements used in air-distribution systems and the calculations associated with loads, airflow, ducts, equipment, and piping. Repeated calculation practice helps candidates recognize which information is necessary before reaching for a formula or table.

Finally, devote significant preparation time to Rules and Law. With 25 weighted questions, candidates should be comfortable navigating Utah licensing statutes and administrative rules rather than relying only on technical HVAC references.
