Washington identifies HVAC/Refrigeration as electrical specialty classification **06A**. This specialty covers defined electrical work associated with heating, ventilation, air-conditioning, and refrigeration systems. Candidates preparing for the 06A Electrician examination should be ready to work with motors, controllers, HVAC equipment, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, wiring methods, electrical equipment, electrical theory, and Washington-specific licensing and specialty rules.

Although the 06A specialty is not generally limited by voltage, phase, or amperage, the work must remain within the HVAC/Refrigeration specialty scope. Holding the 06A certificate does not provide unrestricted authority to perform general electrical work outside that specialty.

### Licensing Steps

Washington HVAC/Refrigeration Electrician certification is administered through the **Washington State Department of Labor & Industries**. Candidates must complete the applicable experience and education requirements before qualifying for the 06A specialty examination.

1. **Begin through an electrical trainee or other qualifying pathway.** Candidates generally gain supervised electrical work experience before applying for specialty certification.
2. **Accumulate qualifying 06A HVAC/Refrigeration experience.** Washington requires 4,000 hours of qualifying work experience for this specialty.
3. **Work under appropriate supervision.** Trainees must work under a specialty electrician certified for the work being performed or under a general journey level electrician.
4. **Meet the minimum supervision requirement.** Washington requires trainees in 4,000-hour specialties to be supervised at least 75 percent of the time.
5. **Complete required classroom instruction.** Candidates must complete 48 hours of qualifying basic classroom instruction for the 4,000-hour specialty pathway.
6. **Maintain accurate experience records.** Qualifying electrical work hours must be properly documented according to Labor & Industries requirements.
7. **Apply for examination eligibility.** Submit the required electrician examination application and supporting documentation to Washington Labor & Industries.
8. **Receive examination approval before scheduling.** Candidates should not schedule with PSI until Labor & Industries has issued its determination.
9. **Prepare using the approved references.** Study the 2020 NEC, RCW Chapter 19.28, and WAC Chapter 296-46B.
10. **Take and pass the required examination.** Complete the applicable PSI testing process and follow Washington requirements for issuance of the 06A specialty electrician certificate.

### State Requirements

Washington identifies HVAC/Refrigeration as specialty electrical classification **06A**. Candidates pursuing this specialty must complete **4,000 hours of qualifying work experience**.

Trainees performing 06A work must be supervised by an appropriately certified specialty electrician or by a general journey level electrician. Washington requires trainees in this 4,000-hour specialty to receive supervision at least **75 percent of the time**.

Candidates must also complete **48 hours of basic classroom instruction** as part of the qualification requirements for examination or applicable trainee renewal requirements.

The 06A HVAC/Refrigeration specialty is not generally limited by voltage, phase, or amperage. However, all electrical work must remain within the HVAC/Refrigeration scope established by Washington rules.

The specialty includes specified telecommunications and Class 2 low-voltage control wiring associated with HVAC and refrigeration systems. Washington's scope rules also authorize specified line-voltage component work within HVAC/R equipment and limited work involving controllers, disconnecting means, raceways used for physical protection, and qualifying supply connections.

Washington places additional restrictions on trainees working on HVAC/Refrigeration systems that contain circuits rated over 600 volts. Candidates should understand these supervision requirements as part of the state-code portion of their preparation.

The 06A classification does not create unrestricted authority to install general line-voltage services, feeders, branch circuits, external disconnect switches, or controllers outside the specialty scope. Candidates should study WAC 296-46B-920 carefully to understand the exact limits.

Washington also allows qualifying experience from the 06B HVAC/Refrigeration Restricted specialty to be credited toward the work-experience requirement for 06A certification under the applicable rules.
