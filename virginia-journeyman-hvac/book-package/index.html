Open Book Test

The Virginia Journeyman HVAC examination is an open book test. Candidates must bring their own qualifying reference materials because the references are not provided at the testing location.

PSI permits reference books to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are permitted for organizing references. Removable tabs are not permitted, and references containing handwritten notes are not allowed. Candidates are also prohibited from writing in their books during the examination.

The open-book format makes preparation with the actual references especially valuable. With 210 minutes to answer 85 questions, candidates have an average of slightly less than two and a half minutes per item. Searching through unfamiliar books can quickly consume that time.

During preparation, learn which reference corresponds to each major subject. Mechanical-code questions involving ventilation, duct systems, boilers, mechanical refrigeration, combustion air, exhaust, and related installations may lead to the International Mechanical Code. Questions involving oil-burning equipment and fuel-oil installations can point toward NFPA 31. Technical HVAC theory, refrigeration principles, components, controls, service practices, and equipment operation may involve Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning.

Virginia-specific questions involving licensure, conduct, renewal, qualifications, and regulatory responsibilities should be studied using the Individual License and Certification Regulations. The more familiar candidates become with the organization of each reference, the more efficiently they can decide where to look when a question requires verification.

Licensing Steps

Virginia Journeyman HVAC licensure is an individual tradesman licensing process. Candidates must meet one of the Board’s qualification pathways before receiving authorization to sit for the examination.

  1. Select the Journeyman HVAC designation. Virginia identifies this individual tradesman credential as Journeyman Heating, Ventilation & Air Conditioning.
  2. Review the qualifying pathways. The Board recognizes combinations of practical experience, formal vocational training, related education, apprenticeship completion, and certain out-of-state credentials.
  3. Document practical HVAC experience. Candidates qualifying through an experience-based pathway must submit the required experience verification.
  4. Provide training or education records when applicable. Applicants using formal vocational training, an associate-level program, or an engineering-related bachelor’s program must provide the required supporting documentation.
  5. Submit the Tradesman Exam & License Application. The Board reviews the application and determines examination eligibility.
  6. Receive Board approval. Pre-approval is required before sitting for the Journeyman HVAC examination.
  7. Prepare with the examination references. Study both HVAC technical subjects and Virginia regulatory requirements using the books associated with the exam.
  8. Schedule with PSI. After approval, candidates follow PSI procedures to schedule the examination.
  9. Take the Journeyman HVAC examination. Candidates must meet the applicable passing requirement.
  10. Complete the licensing process. The Virginia Board for Contractors handles issuance of the individual tradesman license after applicable requirements are satisfied.

State Requirements

Virginia offers several qualification pathways for Journeyman tradesman applicants. One pathway requires four years of practical experience and 240 hours of formal vocational training in the trade. Candidates can also qualify with five years of practical experience and 160 hours of formal vocational training.

Additional pathways include six years of practical experience and 80 hours of formal vocational training, or seven or more years of practical experience and 40 hours of formal vocational training.

An applicant may also qualify with an associate degree or certificate of completion from at least a two-year program in a tradesman-related field from an accredited community college or technical school together with two years of practical experience in the trade.

A bachelor’s degree or certificate of completion from an accredited college or university in an engineering curriculum related to the trade can be combined with one year of practical experience. Virginia also provides a pathway based on eight years of practical experience verified by individuals who have observed the applicant’s work in the trade.

Journeyman applicants who successfully completed an approved apprenticeship program may also use the applicable apprenticeship pathway. Candidates who currently hold certain journeyman or master credentials issued by a governing body outside Virginia may have another qualification route, subject to the documentation required by the Board.

Virginia Journeyman HVAC licensees are also subject to continuing education requirements for renewal. HVAC tradesmen must complete three hours of Board-approved continuing education during the applicable license term. The education must address a subject directly related to the current mechanical code applicable to HVAC work.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong study plan for the Virginia Journeyman HVAC examination should combine technical knowledge with deliberate reference-navigation practice. Because the exam covers many different mechanical subjects, organize study sessions around the published content categories rather than attempting to read every book from beginning to end without a plan.

Begin with warm-air heating, ventilation, and air conditioning. Review equipment, airflow, system operation, installation requirements, distribution systems, ventilation principles, mechanical components, and relevant code provisions. These three categories account for a significant part of the exam.

Duct systems should receive dedicated review. Study materials, supports, installation requirements, insulation, clearances, construction, and air-distribution concepts. Learn where these requirements are organized in the International Mechanical Code so you can locate them efficiently.

Chimneys, flues, vents, and combustion air are closely connected with safe operation of fuel-burning appliances. Review ventilation and venting principles, combustion-air requirements, clearances, terminations, and associated mechanical-code requirements.

Boilers and hot-water piping require familiarity with hydronic systems, equipment, components, installation practices, and mechanical requirements. Candidates should also study controls and low-voltage topics involving thermostats, relays, transformers, control circuits, switches, and related HVAC components.

Fuel-oil piping and storage should be reviewed using NFPA 31. Practice locating provisions involving piping, tanks, storage, equipment, installation, and protection. Knowing the structure of the standard can reduce the time needed to research a fuel-oil question during the exam.

Refrigeration preparation should include refrigerants, refrigerant piping, mechanical refrigeration, system components, operating principles, and chilled and condenser water systems. Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning can help candidates strengthen the practical trade knowledge that complements code-based study.

Commercial kitchen ventilation is another dedicated exam category. Review exhaust hoods, ducts, ventilation, makeup air, clearances, and related mechanical requirements. Candidates should also review insulation and firestopping requirements because these smaller categories can still contribute important points to the final score.

Virginia regulatory material should remain part of the study schedule throughout preparation. Reviewing a few regulatory topics at a time can make standards of practice, standards of conduct, qualifications, renewal requirements, definitions, and disciplinary provisions easier to retain.

Timed reference practice can bring all of these skills together. Work through sample-style questions while using the books. Identify the subject, decide which reference applies, locate the supporting provision, and confirm the answer. Over time, this process can strengthen technical understanding and reference familiarity at the same time.
