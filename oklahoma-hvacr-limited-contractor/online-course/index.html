Exam Details

The Oklahoma HVACR Contractor Limited examination evaluates technical knowledge involving heating, cooling, refrigeration, ventilation, air distribution, electrical controls, natural gas systems, and mechanical safety.

The Oklahoma Construction Industries Board oversees mechanical contractor licensing, while PSI administers the applicable examinations.

  • Examination: Oklahoma HVACR Contractor - Limited
  • Licensing Authority: Oklahoma Construction Industries Board
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Number of Scored Questions: 80
  • Time Allowed: 210 minutes
  • Passing Score: 70%
  • Correct Answers Required: 56
  • Examination Format: Open book
  • Mechanical Code Reference: International Mechanical Code, 2024
  • Fuel Gas Code Reference: International Fuel Gas Code, 2024

The examination addresses six major technical subject areas:

  • Electrical Knowledge and Controls: 15 questions covering electrical theory, motors, AC and DC circuits, and controls.
  • Natural Gas: 12 questions involving piping materials, valves, connectors, supports, sizing, and testing.
  • Air Distribution Systems: 15 questions addressing duct design, installation, insulation, ventilation, and testing.
  • Refrigeration and Air Conditioning: 20 questions involving refrigerants, equipment, piping, system operation, and troubleshooting.
  • Heating Systems: 15 questions covering heating equipment, combustion air, venting, installation, and hydronic systems.
  • Safety: 3 questions involving workplace protection and construction hazards.

With 210 minutes available for 80 questions, candidates have approximately two minutes and 38 seconds per question. Effective preparation involves developing technical understanding while practicing efficient reference navigation.

Oklahoma mechanical contractor licensing also includes a separate Mechanical Business and Law examination. This online course focuses on the HVACR Limited Contractor trade examination and the seven listed technical references.

Open Book Test

The Oklahoma HVACR Limited Contractor examination is an open-book test. Candidates may consult approved examination references according to PSI testing requirements.

Open-book testing requires more than locating individual terms in reference books. Candidates must identify the mechanical subject, determine the appropriate source, interpret technical information, and apply installation requirements correctly.

The International Mechanical Code addresses mechanical equipment, ventilation, duct systems, refrigeration, and installation requirements. The International Fuel Gas Code covers gas piping, regulators, appliance connections, combustion air, and venting.

The Ductulator supports duct-sizing calculations, while Manual J addresses residential heating and cooling load calculations. Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning provides technical information about refrigeration systems and equipment operation.

Oklahoma adopted the 2024 International Mechanical Code and International Fuel Gas Code, with applicable state amendments, effective September 14, 2026.

The examination transition period runs from September 14 through December 14, 2026. During this period, affected candidates may test using either the previously adopted or newly adopted building codes.

Beginning December 15, 2026, affected examinations will use the newly adopted codes exclusively.

PSI permits approved references to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed. Permanent tabs are permitted, while unauthorized handwritten notes and temporary removable tabs are prohibited.

Regular practice using code indexes, equipment tables, duct-sizing tools, load calculation references, and technical formulas helps candidates develop more efficient examination preparation habits.

Licensing Steps

Oklahoma HVACR Limited Contractor licensing is administered by the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board under the state's mechanical licensing framework.

  1. Review the Contractor Classification: Understand the authorized work and equipment capacity limits associated with HVACR Limited contracting.
  2. Meet Mechanical Experience Requirements: Complete the applicable qualifying mechanical trade experience.
  3. Satisfy Journeyman Qualifications: Meet the requirements for mechanical journeyman eligibility and obtain the additional qualifying experience required for contractor licensing.
  4. Prepare for the Trade Examination: Study mechanical codes, refrigeration, heating systems, natural gas, ductwork, calculations, and safety.
  5. Prepare for Business and Law: Review the separate examination addressing contractor business and legal responsibilities.
  6. Submit the Licensing Application: Complete the required application through the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board.
  7. Receive Examination Authorization: Complete the Board's eligibility review.
  8. Complete Required Examinations: Schedule and pass the applicable examinations through PSI.
  9. Finalize Contractor Licensing: Satisfy remaining licensing, firm, insurance, and bonding requirements.

Oklahoma mechanical contractor applicants must meet the applicable journeyman qualifications plus one additional year of qualifying mechanical trade experience.

Mechanical contractors must also be permanent employees, owners, partners, or officers of the mechanical firms associated with their contractor licenses.

State Requirements

HVACR contracting in Oklahoma is regulated through state mechanical licensing laws, administrative regulations, and adopted mechanical construction codes.

The HVACR Limited Contractor classification authorizes qualified license holders to install, alter, modify, service, maintain, or repair cooling systems with capacities of 25 tons or less and heating equipment with heat input ratings of 500,000 Btu/h or less per appliance.

The classification also includes authorized sheet metal, natural gas piping, refrigeration, and related process piping work.

These capacity limitations distinguish the limited classification from the HVACR Unlimited Contractor classification.

Contractors must understand mechanical installation requirements involving equipment selection, airflow, ductwork, refrigeration, ventilation, fuel gas systems, and safety.

The International Mechanical Code and International Fuel Gas Code establish technical provisions adopted by Oklahoma with applicable amendments.

Contractor responsibilities also include maintaining applicable business licensing, insurance, bonding, and professional compliance requirements.

Reference Books

    Contains federal construction safety regulations involving personal protective equipment, electrical hazards, ladders, scaffolding, excavation, fall protection, tools, and workplace conditions.
