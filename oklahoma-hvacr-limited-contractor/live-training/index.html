Exam Details

The Oklahoma HVACR Contractor Limited technical examination tests knowledge of the installation, maintenance, repair, fabrication, alteration, and extension of air conditioning, refrigeration, heating, and ventilation systems, including ductwork within a complete system subject to the Limited classification limits.

The current technical examination contains 80 scored questions with 210 minutes allowed. The passing requirement is 70%, equal to 56 correct answers.

Electrical Knowledge and Controls contains 15 questions. Topics include general electrical theory, motors, DC circuits, AC circuits, and controls. Candidates should understand how electrical systems and controls interact with HVAC/R equipment and how electrical problems can affect operation and troubleshooting.

Natural Gas contains 12 questions. Topics include piping valves, connectors, accessories, hangers and supports, sizing, materials, and testing. Candidates should become comfortable navigating the International Fuel Gas Code for gas-system requirements.

Air Distribution Systems contains 15 questions. Subjects include duct definitions, sizing and design, duct assembly and installation, hangers and supports, materials and shapes, insulation, fire and smoke control, hoods and exhaust systems, testing and balancing, ventilation requirements, plans, and symbols.

Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Systems is the largest category with 20 questions. Preparation should include refrigeration theory, refrigerants, equipment sizing and design, equipment and components, piping, equipment installation, system operation, troubleshooting, and maintenance.

Heating Systems contains 15 questions. Topics include heating theory, system types, combustion air, vents and chimneys, equipment sizing, heating equipment, installation, troubleshooting, maintenance, hydronics, and hydronic piping.

Safety contains three questions. Candidates should review personal protective equipment, safety training, ventilation, first-aid requirements, hazardous materials, fall protection, lockout/tagout, ladders, scaffolds, and related construction safety subjects.

The separate Mechanical Business and Law examination contains 50 scored questions and allows 120 minutes. Its current content areas include 10 Bidding and Estimating questions, seven Project Management and Supervision questions, five Contracts questions, eight Financial questions, five Labor and Personnel questions, four Risk Management questions, five Payroll and Payroll Taxes questions, and six Licensing Requirements questions.

Open Book Test

The Oklahoma HVACR Contractor Limited technical examination is an open-book test. The Mechanical Business and Law examination is also open book.

The technical examination reference list includes the ACCA Ductulator, International Mechanical Code, 2018 with Oklahoma revisions, International Fuel Gas Code, 2018 with Oklahoma revisions, Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning, Manual J, Mathematics for Plumbers and Pipefitters, and OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926.

Code questions are based on the editions specifically identified for the examination. Candidates preparing for code-based questions should therefore become comfortable with the 2018 International Mechanical Code and 2018 International Fuel Gas Code with applicable Oklahoma revisions.

Current PSI rules permit later editions of approved non-code references. The 22nd Edition of Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning listed for this training is a later edition of the applicable non-code HVAC/R reference.

Approved references may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are permitted. Temporary tabs such as Post-it notes are not allowed.

References may not contain written notes before or during the examination. Candidates may not bring additional loose papers into the testing room with approved references.

A silent, non-printing, non-programmable calculator meeting the testing requirements may be used. Candidates benefit from practicing reference searches before the exam rather than relying on unfamiliar books for the first time at the testing center.

Licensing Steps

  1. Gain qualifying HVAC/R experience. Build verifiable mechanical trade experience while working within Oklahoma’s mechanical licensing structure.
  2. Meet the underlying journeyman qualifications. Mechanical Contractor applicants must satisfy the applicable journeyman-level qualification requirements.
  3. Complete additional contractor experience. Oklahoma contractor applicants must meet the journeyman requirements plus the additional qualifying mechanical experience required for contractor licensing.
  4. Maintain supporting documentation. Keep employment records, apprentice information, licensing history, qualifying educational records, and other documentation used to establish eligibility.
  5. Submit the Mechanical Contractor application. Apply to the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board for the HVAC/R Limited Contractor classification.
  6. Receive examination authorization. The Construction Industries Board determines eligibility before examination scheduling.
  7. Schedule the required examinations through PSI. Approved candidates arrange their testing appointments after receiving authorization.
  8. Prepare for the HVACR Contractor Limited technical exam. Review refrigeration, electrical controls, air distribution, natural gas, heating systems, load calculations, duct sizing, trade mathematics, and safety.
  9. Prepare for Mechanical Business and Law. Study bidding, estimating, project management, contracts, financial management, personnel, risk management, payroll, Oklahoma licensing requirements, liens, and contractor regulations.
  10. Complete the required examinations. Meet the passing requirements for the technical and Mechanical Business and Law examinations.
  11. Complete active contractor requirements. Provide the bond and commercial general liability insurance documentation required by the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board.
  12. Maintain contractor compliance. Keep licensing, insurance, bond, continuing education, and other applicable contractor requirements current.

State Requirements

The Oklahoma Construction Industries Board regulates mechanical licensing, including the HVAC/R Limited Contractor classification.

An HVAC/R Limited Contractor may perform qualifying installation, alteration, modification, service, maintenance, and repair of cooling products, systems, and equipment, including process piping, with a cooling capacity of no more than 25 tons.

The classification also covers heating equipment and associated process piping with a heat input of no more than 500,000 Btu/h per appliance. Sheet metal, natural gas piping, and refrigeration are also included within the authorized Limited scope.

These limits distinguish HVAC/R Limited licensing from the HVAC/R Unlimited classification. Work beyond the Limited capacity thresholds requires the appropriate Unlimited licensing.

Oklahoma Mechanical Contractor applicants must meet the applicable journeyman-level qualifications and the additional contractor experience requirement established by the state.

A Mechanical Contractor must be a permanent employee, owner, partner, or officer of the mechanical firm for which the individual serves as licensed contractor.

After successfully completing the required examinations, a contractor license remains inactive until applicable bond and insurance requirements are completed. An active Oklahoma Mechanical Contractor must maintain a $5,000 corporate surety bond payable to the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board.

An active Mechanical Contractor must also maintain at least $50,000 in commercial general liability insurance. The individual license holder must be identified on the insurance certificate, and the Construction Industries Board must be listed as certificate holder.

Oklahoma mechanical journeymen and contractors currently complete six hours of approved continuing education every 36 months. Contractors must also keep required license information, insurance, and active status current while engaging in mechanical contracting.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Oklahoma HVACR Limited Contractor study plan should reflect the weight of each technical examination category. Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Systems contains 20 questions, while Electrical Knowledge and Controls, Air Distribution Systems, and Heating Systems each contain 15 questions.

Prioritize Refrigeration and Air Conditioning. Review refrigeration theory, refrigerants, equipment sizing, components, piping, installation, system operation, troubleshooting, and maintenance.

Strengthen Electrical Knowledge and Controls. Study electrical theory, motors, AC and DC circuits, controls, and the relationship between electrical operation and HVAC/R equipment troubleshooting.

Practice Air Distribution and duct sizing. Use the Ductulator while reviewing airflow, duct sizing, system design, installation, supports, insulation, ventilation, testing, balancing, plans, and symbols.

Review Manual J load concepts. Practice heating and cooling load calculations, heat gain, heat loss, and equipment-sizing principles.

Study Natural Gas. Become comfortable locating sizing provisions, valves, connectors, materials, supports, and testing information in the International Fuel Gas Code.

Prepare for Mechanical Business and Law. Review Oklahoma licensing requirements, mechanical industry regulations, fines, liens, estimating, project management, contracts, financial management, personnel, risk management, payroll, insurance, bonds, and renewal requirements.

The official Mechanical Business and Law examination reference list also includes contractor business-management and workers’ compensation materials. Candidates preparing for the complete business examination should study all references approved for that examination in addition to the Oklahoma-specific business references listed with this training.

Combine live training with self-study. Use live virtual sessions to organize technical concepts and reference strategies, then reinforce the material through self-study, practice questions, calculations, and repeated reference navigation between classes.

Practice at the actual testing pace. For the technical examination, work toward completing 80 questions within 210 minutes. For Mechanical Business and Law, prepare for 50 questions within 120 minutes.
