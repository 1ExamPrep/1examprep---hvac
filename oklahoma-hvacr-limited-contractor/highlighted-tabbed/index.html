Exam Details

The Oklahoma HVACR Contractor, Limited examination is administered by PSI for candidates approved through the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board. The exam evaluates technical knowledge associated with installing, maintaining, repairing, fabricating, altering, and extending air conditioning, refrigeration, heating, and ventilation systems within the limited contractor classification.

  • Exam: Oklahoma HVACR Contractor, Limited
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Number of Questions: 80
  • Time Allowed: 210 minutes
  • Passing Score: 70%
  • Passing Raw Score: 56 correct answers
  • Exam Format: Open book

The examination is divided into six major technical areas. Electrical Knowledge and Controls contains 15 questions. Natural Gas contains 12 questions. Air Distribution Systems contains 15 questions. Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Systems contains 20 questions. Heating Systems contains 15 questions, and Safety contains 3 questions.

Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Systems is the largest individual section. Topics include refrigeration theory, refrigerants, equipment sizing and design, components, piping, equipment installation, system operation, troubleshooting, and maintenance.

Heating Systems includes heating theory and types, combustion air, vents and chimneys, equipment sizing, heating equipment, installation, system operation, troubleshooting, maintenance, hydronics, and hydronic piping.

Air Distribution Systems includes duct definitions, sizing and design, duct assembly and installation, hangers and supports, duct materials and shapes, insulation, fire and smoke control, hoods and exhaust systems, testing and balancing, ventilation requirements, plans, and symbols.

Natural Gas questions address piping valves, connectors, accessories, hangers and supports, sizing, materials, and testing. Electrical Knowledge and Controls covers general electrical theory, motors, DC circuits, AC circuits, and controls. Safety topics include personal protective equipment, employee safety training, ventilation, first aid, fall protection, lockout/tagout, ladders, scaffolds, and related construction safety subjects.

Open Book Test

The Oklahoma HVACR Contractor, Limited examination is an open book test. Candidates are responsible for bringing approved references to the examination center and must follow PSI rules governing the condition and preparation of those materials.

Reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are permitted. Temporary tabs such as Post-it notes are not allowed. Candidates may not write in their references before or during the examination session, and additional loose papers cannot be brought into the test room with the approved books.

This makes preparation with highlighted and tabbed references particularly useful. An open-book exam does not mean every answer can be found quickly without preparation. Candidates still need to identify which reference applies to a question, locate the correct section, and interpret the information within the available testing time.

Mechanical-system questions may direct you toward the International Mechanical Code. Fuel gas questions may require the International Fuel Gas Code. Refrigeration, heating, controls, and equipment questions may involve Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning. Residential load-calculation study can involve Manual J, while air-distribution work can involve the Ductulator. OSHA safety questions may require 29 CFR Part 1926.

PSI’s current examination bulletin identifies the 2024 editions of the International Mechanical Code and International Fuel Gas Code for code questions. Code examination questions are based on the specific code editions listed for the exam.

The current PSI bulletin also states that Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning, 22nd Edition becomes permitted beginning August 13, 2026. Candidates using the 22nd Edition should prepare according to the reference rules applying on their examination date.

Licensing Steps

The Oklahoma Construction Industries Board regulates mechanical contractor licensing. The contractor licensing process involves qualifying experience, application review, examination eligibility, contractor testing, and completion of active contractor requirements.

  1. Gain qualifying mechanical trade experience. Build verifiable experience in the mechanical trade appropriate to the HVACR classification.
  2. Meet contractor-level qualifications. Oklahoma requires mechanical contractor applicants to satisfy the applicable journeyman requirements and obtain an additional year of mechanical trade experience.
  3. Submit the mechanical contractor application. Apply for the appropriate mechanical contractor classification through the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board.
  4. Receive examination eligibility. The Construction Industries Board reviews applicant qualifications and determines eligibility to take the contractor examination.
  5. Schedule testing through PSI. After approval, candidates arrange the applicable examination through PSI.
  6. Complete the required contractor testing. Take the examinations required for the Oklahoma mechanical contractor license.
  7. Complete active contractor requirements. Satisfy the applicable Oklahoma contractor requirements before operating under an active mechanical contractor license.

Preparing with the examination books before testing gives candidates time to learn where important information is located. Rather than treating each book as a reference to discover on exam day, use the materials regularly throughout your preparation.

State Requirements

The Oklahoma HVACR Limited Contractor classification establishes specific limits for the size of HVACR equipment that may be covered by the license.

An HVACR Limited Contractor may perform qualifying work involving cooling products, systems, or equipment, including process piping, with a cooling capacity of no more than 25 tons.

The classification also includes qualifying heating equipment and related process piping with a heat input of no more than 500,000 Btu/h per appliance.

The HVACR Limited classification also incorporates refrigeration, sheet metal, natural gas, and related process piping within the authorized scope of the license.

Oklahoma mechanical contractor applicants must satisfy the requirements applicable to a journeyman and have an additional year of experience in the mechanical trade. A mechanical contractor must also be a permanent employee, owner, partner, or officer of the mechanical firm.

Licensing involves more than passing the examination. Applicants should complete the applicable Construction Industries Board licensing process and meet the requirements associated with maintaining an active Oklahoma mechanical contractor license.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Oklahoma HVACR Limited Contractor study plan should combine technical review with repeated book navigation. Because the exam contains questions from several different HVACR areas, candidates should practice moving between references instead of studying each book in isolation.

Start with Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Systems, the largest examination category. Review refrigeration theory, refrigerants, system components, piping, installation, equipment sizing and design, operation, troubleshooting, and maintenance.

Give significant attention to Electrical Knowledge and Controls, Air Distribution Systems, and Heating Systems, which each contain 15 questions. Electrical preparation should include motors, AC and DC circuits, basic electrical theory, and HVACR controls. Air-distribution preparation should include duct sizing, duct design, installation, insulation, ventilation, exhaust systems, fire and smoke control, and system balancing.

Heating preparation should include combustion air, vents and chimneys, heating equipment, equipment sizing, hydronic systems, installation, operation, maintenance, and troubleshooting.

Natural Gas accounts for 12 questions. Review piping valves, connectors, accessories, supports, materials, testing, and sizing. Use the International Fuel Gas Code while studying these subjects so that code navigation becomes part of the learning process.

Safety contains fewer questions but should still be included in your preparation. Use 29 CFR Part 1926 to practice locating construction safety requirements involving PPE, fall protection, ladders, scaffolds, employee training, hazardous materials, first aid, ventilation, and lockout/tagout.

Use the highlighted and tabbed books actively. Pick a subject, identify the correct reference, and locate the relevant chapter or section. For example, practice finding mechanical ventilation requirements, fuel gas sizing provisions, refrigeration-system information, residential load-calculation material, or OSHA safety requirements.

Reference-navigation drills can be particularly useful for open-book preparation. Set a timer and give yourself a series of mixed questions. Before opening a book, determine which reference should contain the information. Then locate the answer efficiently. This helps develop both subject recognition and navigation habits.

The exam allows 210 minutes for 80 questions, so candidates must manage time carefully. Some questions may be answered directly from trade knowledge, while others may require calculations or reference research. Effective preparation helps candidates recognize when a book lookup is useful and which reference should be used first.
