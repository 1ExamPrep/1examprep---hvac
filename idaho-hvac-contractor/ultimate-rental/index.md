### Open Book Test

DOPL states Idaho HVAC exams are **open book** and provides a list of approved references in the exam bulletin. Open book is not unlimited time. A timed open-book exam rewards candidates who can answer many questions from understanding and use the references primarily to confirm details—thresholds, exceptions, definitions, and required steps.

DOPL’s HVAC exam bulletin states that candidates may:

- **Highlight** reference materials
- **Tab** different sections
- **Leave notes** in the reference material

DOPL also states that **loose paper in reference materials will not be allowed**. This matters for how you prepare. Your exam-day system must be built into the approved books themselves, not into extra sheets you won’t be allowed to use. For open-book success, practice the exact behavior you’ll use during testing:

- **Read the question carefully** and identify what it is asking.
- **Decide whether a lookup is necessary** or if you can answer from understanding.
- **If you look up, navigate quickly** to the most likely section.
- **Confirm the key detail** and answer confidently.
- **Move on** to protect your pacing.

Because DOPL emphasizes that answers should be based on approved references, a reference-driven strategy is not optional—it’s how the exam is designed to be taken.

### Test Information and Study Materials

This Ultimate prep structure is designed to help you prepare the way you’ll test: open book, timed, and reference-based. Here are practical methods that match DOPL’s exam rules and the realities of contractor testing:

- **Start with the Business & Law portion early.** DOPL’s contractor blueprint places heavy weight on the Business & Law manual. Treat it as a primary focus, not a last-minute topic.
- **Train question recognition.** Many contractor questions fall into patterns—definitions, responsibilities, required steps, compliance expectations, and scenario judgment. If you can label the pattern quickly, you’ll navigate faster.
- **Practice “find-and-confirm.”** Use the manual to confirm details and exceptions. Avoid relying on the book to “teach you” during practice; time limits require you to already understand most concepts.
- **Build a clean tab and highlight system.** DOPL allows tabs and highlighting but prohibits loose paper. Keep your system simple enough to remain useful under pressure.
- **Use timed practice sets.** A 4-hour exam requires pacing. Train in timed blocks so you learn when to confirm in the book and when to make the best choice and move on.
- **Review mistakes by learning location.** Open-book speed improves when you remember where the rule lives, not just what the rule says.
- **Practice exam-day behavior.** Answer every question you can, avoid long searches, and keep momentum. Your score improves when you protect time for the full exam.
