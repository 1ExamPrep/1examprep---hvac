Open Book Test

The Arkansas Class D HVACR Contractor exam is open book. Candidates may use approved references during the examination, subject to current rules concerning permitted editions, highlighting, annotations, permanent tabs, bindings, and loose materials.

An open-book examination still requires serious preparation. Five references contain a substantial amount of information, and candidates may not have enough time to search every book from the beginning for each question. Efficient performance depends on identifying the subject, selecting the correct reference, locating the applicable section, and confirming the answer without unnecessary searching.

A question involving Arkansas HVACR licensing requirements may direct the candidate to Arkansas HVAC/R Law or Arkansas HVAC/R Rules and Regulations.

Questions involving metal or flexible duct construction, reinforcement, joints, seams, fittings, connectors, supports, fabrication, or installation details may require HVAC Duct Construction Standards.

Fuel-gas piping, appliance, combustion-air, venting, testing, or installation questions may require the International Fuel Gas Code.

Questions involving mechanical equipment, ventilation, exhaust systems, ductwork, combustion air, refrigeration systems, or related mechanical requirements may require the International Mechanical Code.

Students should review the table of contents and index in every reference before beginning timed practice. Learn what each book covers, how it is organized, and which formal terms are used for common HVACR, ductwork, and fuel-gas subjects.

During practice, decide which reference should contain the answer before opening a book. This habit helps reduce unnecessary movement between references and strengthens the candidate’s understanding of the purpose of each title.

After locating a possible answer, review the surrounding information carefully. A definition, note, table, diagram, exception, footnote, or connected paragraph may affect the correct response. The first sentence that appears relevant may not provide the complete information needed.

When working with construction standards or code tables, candidates should pay close attention to headings, dimensions, conditions, notes, and exceptions. A value taken out of context may not apply to the specific situation described in the question.
