Exam Details

The Mississippi Residential HVAC contractor examination evaluates knowledge associated with residential heating, ventilation, air conditioning, refrigeration, mechanical systems, fuel-gas installations, duct systems, load calculations, oil-burning equipment, and construction safety.

Candidates should combine practical HVAC experience with repeated use of the listed references. Examination questions may require direct code lookup, interpretation of a table, use of a formula, review of equipment requirements, or application of technical knowledge to a residential heating or cooling condition.

Manual J—Residential Load Calculations, Reprinted 2016, Eighth Full Edition, supports preparation involving residential heating and cooling load calculations. Proper load calculation helps determine the amount of heating or cooling required for the building rather than relying only on square footage or the size of existing equipment.

Load-calculation preparation may involve indoor and outdoor design conditions, building orientation, insulation, windows, doors, infiltration, ventilation, walls, ceilings, floors, duct gains, duct losses, sensible heat, latent heat, and room-by-room loads.

Candidates should understand how climate conditions, glazing, insulation levels, air leakage, occupancy, duct location, and building orientation can affect the calculated load. Accurate inputs are essential because incorrect assumptions can change the result.

The International Mechanical Code, 2021, is a primary code reference in this package. It supports preparation involving mechanical-system installation, ventilation, exhaust, duct systems, combustion air, appliances, equipment clearances, access, piping, chimneys, vents, refrigeration systems, and related safeguards.

Mechanical-code questions may involve equipment location, access, clearances, condensate disposal, ventilation, exhaust systems, duct construction, air distribution, appliance installation, combustion air, venting, refrigeration machinery, or system protection.

Candidates should become familiar with the organization of the International Mechanical Code and practice moving between chapters, definitions, tables, exceptions, referenced standards, and the index. Some questions may require several connected provisions rather than one isolated section.

The Ductulator supports practical airflow and duct-sizing work. Candidates can use it to review relationships between airflow, duct dimensions, velocity, friction loss, and equivalent duct sizes.

Duct-sizing questions may require candidates to identify a required airflow, select a friction rate, evaluate a duct size, or compare round and rectangular duct dimensions. Efficient use of the Ductulator requires repeated hands-on practice before examination day.

HVAC Duct Construction Standards—Metal and Flexible, 2005, 3rd Edition, supports preparation involving duct materials, construction classifications, reinforcement, joints, seams, supports, hangers, fittings, flexible duct, and installation practices.

Residential duct systems must be constructed and supported to maintain airflow, limit leakage, resist damage, and remain serviceable. Candidates should understand how duct size, pressure, material, reinforcement, connection methods, and installation conditions affect construction requirements.

Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning, 22nd Edition, supports broad technical review of refrigeration cycles, air-conditioning systems, heating systems, electrical components, controls, refrigerants, compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, service procedures, troubleshooting, and equipment operation.

Refrigeration questions may involve heat transfer, pressure-temperature relationships, superheat, subcooling, system components, refrigerant flow, charging, recovery, evacuation, leak detection, or system performance.

Air-conditioning preparation may involve comfort conditions, sensible heat, latent heat, airflow, humidity, filters, blowers, coils, condensate, controls, and equipment operation.

Heating topics may involve electric heat, heat pumps, furnaces, combustion, burners, heat exchangers, controls, venting, airflow, and safety devices. Candidates should understand how the major components operate together as a complete system.

The International Fuel Gas Code, 2021, supports preparation involving fuel-gas piping, appliance installation, combustion air, venting, chimneys, regulators, pressure, shutoff valves, sizing, testing, and safety requirements.

Fuel-gas questions may require candidates to size piping, identify acceptable materials, review appliance connections, determine combustion-air requirements, evaluate venting arrangements, or locate required clearances and safeguards.

Candidates should practice using the fuel-gas code tables and understand the information needed before selecting a pipe size. Gas type, developed length, connected load, pressure, allowable pressure drop, and table conditions can affect the result.

Residential Oil Burners, 3rd Edition, supports preparation involving oil-fired heating equipment, fuel delivery, burners, nozzles, combustion, ignition, controls, draft, venting, maintenance, adjustment, and troubleshooting.

Oil-burner questions may involve fuel supply, pump pressure, nozzle selection, combustion air, ignition, flame characteristics, draft, safety controls, cleaning, testing, or service procedures.

The Code of Federal Regulations, 29 CFR Part 1926, supports preparation involving federal construction-safety requirements. Residential HVAC contractors may need to review electrical hazards, ladders, scaffolds, personal protective equipment, tools, material handling, housekeeping, and general jobsite responsibilities.

HVAC work may involve rooftops, attics, crawl spaces, ladders, sharp sheet metal, electrical systems, pressurized equipment, combustion appliances, refrigerants, and heavy components. Candidates should connect written safety requirements with practical field conditions.

The eight references function as a coordinated technical library. A single residential HVAC question may involve a mechanical-code provision, a load calculation, duct sizing, fuel-gas requirements, refrigeration knowledge, and a safety responsibility.

Mississippi contractor applicants may also need separate preparation for applicable Business and Law requirements. A Mississippi Business and Law reference is not included in this trade-only package.

Open Book Test

The Mississippi Residential HVAC contractor examination is open book. Candidates may use permitted references during the examination, subject to current testing rules for approved titles, editions, highlighting, tabs, notes, markings, and supplemental materials.

An open-book format still requires detailed preparation. Having the correct books available does not help when a candidate cannot identify the proper reference, locate an effective search term, interpret a technical table, or complete a calculation within the available testing time.

Candidates should learn the purpose of each reference before examination day. Questions involving equipment installation, ventilation, exhaust, duct systems, appliance access, clearances, condensate, mechanical rooms, combustion air, or refrigeration-system requirements may lead to the International Mechanical Code.

Questions involving residential heating and cooling loads may require Manual J. Candidates should practice identifying the building information needed for a load calculation and locating the correct procedures, tables, and worksheets.

Questions involving airflow, duct dimensions, velocity, or friction loss may require the Ductulator. Duct-construction questions may lead to HVAC Duct Construction Standards—Metal and Flexible.

Technical questions involving refrigeration, air conditioning, heat pumps, furnaces, electrical components, controls, system operation, charging, service, or troubleshooting may lead to Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning.

Questions involving gas piping, appliance connections, combustion air, venting, shutoff valves, regulators, testing, or fuel-gas sizing may require the International Fuel Gas Code.

Questions involving oil-fired equipment, burner components, nozzles, combustion, ignition, draft, controls, or service procedures may require Residential Oil Burners.

Questions involving ladders, scaffolds, electrical hazards, personal protective equipment, tools, housekeeping, or construction-safety responsibilities may require 29 CFR Part 1926.

Search terminology is important. A gas-piping question may involve developed length, connected load, pressure, allowable pressure drop, appliance demand, or pipe-sizing tables.

A duct question may involve airflow, velocity, friction rate, equivalent diameter, static pressure, fittings, reinforcement, supports, or flexible duct.

A refrigeration question may involve superheat, subcooling, evaporator, condenser, compressor, metering device, saturation temperature, pressure, or refrigerant flow.

Candidates should read the surrounding material rather than stopping at the first matching phrase. Definitions, exceptions, table notes, installation conditions, and referenced sections can change how a requirement applies.

Practice timed reference exercises with the complete package. Identify the central subject, select the correct reference, locate the applicable provision or formula, and confirm the answer by reviewing related information.

Licensing Steps

  1. Confirm the appropriate classification. Determine that the Mississippi Residential HVAC classification matches the heating, ventilation, air-conditioning, or mechanical services the business intends to provide.
  2. Select the qualifying party. Identify the person who will qualify the business and complete the required examinations.
  3. Document HVAC experience. Organize project information involving installation, service, refrigeration, duct systems, heating equipment, fuel-gas work, supervision, or project management.
  4. Choose the applicant structure. Determine whether the application will be submitted by an individual, corporation, limited liability company, partnership, or another accepted business entity.
  5. Prepare the contractor application. Gather the requested business, ownership, qualifying-party, experience, financial, insurance, reference, and supporting information.
  6. Submit the application. Send the completed filing to the Mississippi State Board of Contractors for review.
  7. Respond to application requests. Provide missing documents, corrections, explanations, or additional information requested during the review process.
  8. Receive examination eligibility. Follow the registration and scheduling instructions after authorization to test is issued.
  9. Schedule the required examinations. Select an available examination date and approved testing location.
  10. Obtain the reference books. Secure the correct titles and editions needed for examination preparation.
  11. Prepare the references. Add only highlighting, tabs, notes, or markings permitted under current testing rules.
  12. Study the trade material. Review mechanical code, load calculations, duct systems, refrigeration, fuel gas, oil burners, and construction safety.
  13. Prepare for Business and Law. Obtain separate preparation for applicable Mississippi contractor business and law requirements.
  14. Complete timed practice. Work through code questions, calculations, tables, and reference-navigation exercises under structured conditions.
  15. Take the required examinations. Bring acceptable identification and only the references and materials permitted under current testing rules.
  16. Complete outstanding requirements. Submit any remaining records, corrections, or supporting documents requested during final review.
  17. Receive licensing approval. Passing the examinations does not automatically issue a contractor license. The complete application and supporting documentation must also be approved.
  18. Maintain the license. After issuance, the contractor is responsible for renewals, insurance, permits, business records, taxes, safety obligations, and ongoing compliance.

State Requirements

The Mississippi State Board of Contractors administers the applicable contractor licensing process. Applicants should select the classification that accurately reflects the residential HVAC or mechanical work their business intends to perform.

The qualifying party must have a relationship with the applicant that satisfies the applicable requirements. Depending on the business structure, the qualifying party may be an owner, officer, executive staff member, or responsible managing employee.

The contractor application may require business-registration information, ownership records, qualifying-party documentation, detailed HVAC experience, references, financial information, insurance records, and other supporting materials.

Application information should be complete and consistent across company filings, ownership records, experience documents, and supporting materials. Missing or conflicting information can affect the review process.

Passing the required examinations is one part of contractor licensing. Applicants must also satisfy the application, documentation, experience, financial, insurance, and final approval requirements.

Residential HVAC contractors may also be responsible for local permits, inspections, adopted mechanical and fuel-gas codes, utility requirements, employee safety, insurance, tax registrations, and project-specific approvals.

Local requirements can vary by project location. Contractors should confirm applicable permitting, inspection, code, fuel-gas, electrical, and utility requirements before beginning regulated work.
