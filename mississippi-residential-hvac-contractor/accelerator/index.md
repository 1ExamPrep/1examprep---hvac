### Exam Details

The Mississippi Residential HVAC examination is administered by PSI as part of the licensing process overseen by the Mississippi State Board of Contractors. The Residential HVAC classification is a residential license classification involving the **installation, maintenance, and repair of residential HVAC systems**. Contractors holding this classification are limited to residential HVAC work.

### Open Book Test

The Mississippi Residential HVAC Contractor examination is an **open-book test**. PSI permits candidates to bring approved reference materials into the examination center under current testing rules.

Candidates are responsible for bringing their own approved references. Reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs may also be used. Temporary tabs, including removable Post-it-style tabs, are not permitted.

Candidates may not write in reference books during the examination. Additional loose or attached papers are not permitted with approved references. A silent, nonprinting, non-programmable calculator may be used under PSI examination rules.

Code-book editions are especially important. PSI bases code questions on the code editions identified for the examination and permits only the listed code editions into the testing center for those questions. Candidates should use the current PSI examination reference list when determining which code editions to bring on exam day.

Open-book testing rewards preparation and navigation skills. A four-hour examination may sound generous, but 80 questions covering numerous HVAC disciplines can require careful time management. Candidates should practice identifying the subject of a question, selecting the appropriate reference, locating the relevant section, and interpreting the information efficiently.

### Licensing Steps

The Mississippi State Board of Contractors administers the Residential HVAC contractor licensing process. The examination is one component of that process.

1. **Complete the residential contractor application.** Applicants begin by submitting the appropriate licensing application to the Mississippi State Board of Contractors.
2. **Select the Residential HVAC classification.** This classification is for installation, maintenance, and repair of residential HVAC systems.
3. **Identify the qualifying party.** The qualifying party is the individual connected to the application who is responsible for satisfying the applicable examination requirements.
4. **Document relevant HVAC experience.** Applicants must provide the experience information required for the classification requested.
5. **Submit required supporting documents.** Complete the application with the qualifying-party, business, reference, employment, insurance, and other documentation required by the Board.
6. **Complete the examination eligibility process.** The qualifying-party portion of the application must be completed before the candidate becomes eligible for required testing.
7. **Receive PSI registration information.** MSBOC provides examination registration instructions after the application reaches the appropriate stage for testing.
8. **Prepare for the Residential HVAC examination.** Study the technical subjects represented in the examination outline and become familiar with the approved reference materials.
9. **Complete the required contractor examinations.** Applicants must complete the required trade examination and Mississippi Law & Business Management examination.
10. **Complete remaining licensing requirements.** Passing examinations does not by itself issue a license. The complete application must satisfy MSBOC requirements before approval.

### State Requirements

The Mississippi Residential HVAC classification is regulated by the Mississippi State Board of Contractors. The classification involves installation, maintenance, and repair of residential HVAC systems and is limited to residential HVAC work.

Mississippi requires residential subcontractors performing life-safety trades, including residential HVAC work, on covered residential construction and residential improvement projects to hold the appropriate state contractor license.

Applicants must complete a residential license application and provide evidence of experience in the classification requested. The residential application asks applicants to identify completed jobs demonstrating experience in their requested field.

Applicants must also provide proof of employment for the qualifying party when applicable. Mississippi accepts qualifying documentation such as employment records or business ownership information under the Board's application requirements.

Residential licensing applications require supporting references and other documentation identified by MSBOC. Applicants should complete all applicable sections accurately so the Board can determine examination eligibility and continue processing the license application.

All applicants are required to take a **Law & Business Management examination**. Applicants must also take the applicable trade examination. Residential HVAC is identified by the Board as a classification requiring a test.

Residential licenses issued by MSBOC are renewed annually. Qualifying parties associated with residential licenses are also subject to Mississippi's continuing education requirements for residential license renewal.

### Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Mississippi Residential HVAC study plan should follow the examination content outline. Because ducts account for 14 of the 80 questions, candidates should devote substantial preparation time to air-distribution systems. Review duct sizing, construction, fittings, supports, insulation, airflow, static pressure, flexible duct, metal duct, and practical installation requirements.

Load calculations are another important area. Candidates should understand the purpose of residential heating and cooling load calculations and become comfortable using **Manual J**. Practice identifying the information required for a calculation and understanding how building characteristics influence heating and cooling loads.

Use the **Ductulator** during practice rather than simply carrying it as an exam tool. Learn how airflow, velocity, friction rate, and duct size relate to one another. Repeated use can make the device more familiar when solving air-distribution problems.

Heating and cooling principles should include the refrigeration cycle, heat transfer, pressure-temperature relationships, sensible and latent heat, airflow, system capacity, and equipment operation. **Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning** provides broad technical coverage for these subjects.

Study air-conditioning and heat-pump equipment with attention to major components and operating sequences. Candidates should understand compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, reversing valves, indoor and outdoor equipment, controls, and common system behavior.

Furnaces and heaters should also receive dedicated review. Study combustion, burners, heat exchangers, controls, venting, safety devices, airflow, and equipment installation. Fuel-burning appliances connect directly with combustion-air, chimney, vent, fuel-gas, LP-gas, and fuel-oil topics represented on the examination.

The **International Mechanical Code** should be used regularly during study sessions. Practice using the index and table of contents to locate mechanical-system requirements. Become familiar with chapters addressing ventilation, exhaust systems, duct construction, combustion air, chimneys, vents, equipment, refrigeration, and related mechanical work.

Controls are another practical study area. Residential HVAC equipment relies on thermostats, relays, contactors, pressure switches, limit controls, transformers, motors, and other control components. Candidates should understand basic control functions and how components interact during normal operation.

Safety preparation should include familiarity with OSHA construction standards. HVAC contractors may work around ladders, electrical equipment, tools, elevated surfaces, construction materials, and other hazards. Knowing how the OSHA reference is organized can improve lookup efficiency during preparation.

Practice under timed conditions. With 80 questions and four hours available, candidates have an average of three minutes per question. Some questions may be answered quickly from trade knowledge, while load calculations, code research, or technical lookup questions may require additional time.

Highlighted books can support this preparation when used actively. Instead of simply reading highlighted passages, locate each highlighted section, understand its context, and identify related tables or sections. This builds familiarity with the reference rather than relying only on visual markers.
