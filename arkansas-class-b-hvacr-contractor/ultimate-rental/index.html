Exam Details

The Arkansas Class B HVACR Contractor exam requires preparation across technical, electrical, mechanical, fuel-gas, legal, and regulatory subjects. Candidates should be ready to work with both practical HVACR knowledge and detailed information contained in multiple references.

Arkansas HVAC/R Law and Arkansas HVAC/R Rules and Regulations support preparation involving state-specific licensing subjects, regulated work, contractor responsibilities, classifications, professional obligations, and compliance requirements.

The International Fuel Gas Code, 2021 supports study involving fuel-gas piping, appliance connections, combustion air, venting, testing, installation, inspection, and related safety provisions. Candidates should practice locating requirements by system type, appliance, installation condition, or indexed term.

The International Mechanical Code, 2021 supports preparation involving mechanical equipment, ventilation, exhaust systems, ductwork, combustion air, refrigeration systems, installation procedures, and related code requirements. Questions may require candidates to compare definitions, tables, exceptions, notes, and connected sections.

Residential Load Calculation - Manual J, 8th Edition supports preparation involving residential heating and cooling load calculations. Students should understand how building construction, insulation, windows, climate conditions, orientation, infiltration, and internal gains affect calculated loads and equipment-sizing decisions.

Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning, 22nd Edition provides broad technical coverage of refrigeration principles, heating and cooling systems, equipment components, airflow, controls, installation, maintenance, service, and troubleshooting.

Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Technology, 10th Edition supports additional study involving refrigeration cycles, compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, refrigerants, electrical controls, diagnostics, system operation, installation practices, and service procedures.

The National Electrical Code, 2020 supports preparation involving electrical requirements associated with HVACR equipment. Candidates should practice locating information concerning motors, conductors, branch circuits, disconnecting means, overcurrent protection, grounding, controls, wiring methods, and equipment installation.

Because the exam uses several references, candidates should not depend only on memorized answers. Strong preparation combines technical understanding with repeated practice using indexes, code structures, tables, diagrams, definitions, and chapter organization.

Open Book Test

The Arkansas Class B HVACR Contractor exam is open book. Candidates may use approved references during the examination, subject to the current rules concerning permitted books, highlighting, annotations, permanent tabs, bindings, and loose materials.

Every book included in this package is highlighted and permanently tabbed. The highlighting helps direct attention to useful laws, rules, code provisions, formulas, tables, diagrams, definitions, and technical procedures. The tabs provide visible access points for major chapters and frequently reviewed subjects.

An open-book examination still requires serious preparation. Eight references contain a substantial amount of information, and candidates may not have enough time to search every book from the beginning for each question. Effective performance depends on identifying the subject, choosing the correct reference, locating the relevant section, and confirming the answer efficiently.

A question involving Arkansas HVACR licensing requirements may direct the candidate to Arkansas HVAC/R Law or Arkansas HVAC/R Rules and Regulations. Fuel-gas piping, venting, appliance, or combustion-air questions may require the International Fuel Gas Code.

Questions involving ventilation, ducts, exhaust, mechanical equipment, or refrigeration-code requirements may require the International Mechanical Code. Residential heating and cooling load questions may direct the candidate to Manual J.

Questions involving refrigeration cycles, system components, controls, airflow, installation, maintenance, service, or troubleshooting may require one of the two refrigeration and air-conditioning textbooks. Electrical questions involving motors, conductors, disconnects, overcurrent protection, grounding, or HVACR equipment may require the National Electrical Code.

Students should use the included books throughout the one-year course and rental period. Learn the purpose of every reference, review the chapters behind each tab, and become familiar with the terminology used in each table of contents and index.

A useful practice routine is to read the question carefully, identify the technical subject, select the most relevant book, locate the applicable section, review the surrounding material, and choose the best-supported answer.
