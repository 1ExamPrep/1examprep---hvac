Exam Details

The Arkansas Class B HVACR Contractor exam requires preparation across several technical and regulatory subjects. Candidates should be ready to work with Arkansas HVACR law, state rules, mechanical and fuel-gas codes, residential load calculations, refrigeration principles, air-conditioning systems, electrical requirements, and contractor business practices.

Arkansas HVAC/R Law and Arkansas HVAC/R Rules and Regulations support preparation involving licensing, regulated work, contractor responsibilities, professional standards, classifications, and compliance requirements. Candidates should become familiar with the organization and terminology of both references.

The International Fuel Gas Code, 2021 supports preparation involving fuel-gas piping, appliance connections, venting, combustion air, installation requirements, inspection, testing, and related safety provisions.

The International Mechanical Code, 2021 supports review involving mechanical equipment, ventilation, exhaust, duct systems, combustion air, refrigeration systems, equipment installation, and other regulated mechanical subjects.

Residential Load Calculation - Manual J, 8th Edition supports preparation involving residential heating and cooling load calculations. Students should understand how climate conditions, insulation, windows, orientation, infiltration, building construction, and internal gains affect calculated loads and equipment-sizing decisions.

Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning, 22nd Edition and Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Technology, 10th Edition provide broad technical coverage. These references support study involving refrigeration cycles, compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, refrigerants, heating systems, cooling systems, airflow, controls, installation, service, maintenance, diagnostics, and troubleshooting.

The National Electrical Code, 2020 supports preparation involving electrical requirements related to HVACR equipment. Candidates should practice locating information concerning motors, conductors, branch circuits, disconnecting means, overcurrent protection, grounding, controls, and equipment wiring.

The NASCLA Arkansas business book supports contractor business preparation involving contracts, estimating, project management, financial responsibilities, insurance, employment matters, taxes, liens, safety administration, and professional business practices.

Open Book Test

The Arkansas Class B HVACR Contractor exam is open book. Candidates may use approved references during the examination, subject to the current rules concerning permitted books, highlighting, annotations, permanent tabs, bindings, and loose materials.

Every reference included with The 1 Package is highlighted and permanently tabbed. Highlighted passages draw attention to useful laws, regulations, formulas, code provisions, diagrams, tables, definitions, and technical procedures. Permanent tabs create visible access points for major chapters and commonly reviewed subjects.

An open-book examination still requires serious preparation. Nine references contain a large amount of information, and candidates may not have enough time to search every book from the beginning for each question. Effective performance depends on identifying the subject, selecting the correct reference, locating the applicable section, and confirming the answer efficiently.

A question involving Arkansas HVACR licensing may direct the candidate to Arkansas HVAC/R Law or Arkansas HVAC/R Rules and Regulations. Fuel-gas piping, appliance, venting, or combustion-air questions may require the International Fuel Gas Code.

Questions involving ventilation, duct systems, exhaust, mechanical equipment, or refrigeration-code requirements may require the International Mechanical Code. Residential heating and cooling load questions may direct the candidate to Manual J.

Questions involving refrigeration cycles, system components, controls, installation, service, maintenance, airflow, or troubleshooting may require one of the refrigeration and air-conditioning textbooks. Electrical questions involving conductors, motors, disconnects, grounding, or overcurrent protection may require the National Electrical Code.

Questions involving contracts, financial management, estimating, insurance, project administration, employment matters, or contractor business practices may require the NASCLA Arkansas reference.

For code preparation, practice moving from an indexed term to the applicable section. Read definitions, tables, exceptions, notes, and related provisions before selecting an answer.

For electrical preparation, review motors, branch circuits, conductors, disconnecting means, overcurrent protection, grounding, controls, and HVACR equipment requirements. Practice using the National Electrical Code index and article structure.

Complete practice questions with all nine references available. Before opening a book, decide which reference is most likely to contain the answer. When a question is missed, locate the correct information and record the book, chapter, and subject for future review.
