Exam Details

The Arkansas Class A HVACR Contractor exam requires preparation across a wide range of heating, ventilation, air conditioning, refrigeration, electrical, code, legal, and business subjects. Candidates should be ready to work with both technical trade knowledge and detailed information contained in the approved reference materials.

The Arkansas HVAC/R Law and Arkansas HVAC/R Rules and Regulations support preparation involving the legal and regulatory framework that applies to HVACR work in Arkansas. Candidates should become familiar with the organization of these materials and the terminology used for licensing, compliance, responsibilities, and regulated HVACR activity.

The International Fuel Gas Code, 2021 supports preparation involving fuel-gas piping, appliance connections, combustion air, venting, installation requirements, testing, inspection, and related safety provisions. Candidates should practice identifying which chapter or section applies to common fuel-gas questions.

The International Mechanical Code, 2021 supports review of mechanical systems, ventilation, exhaust, ductwork, equipment installation, combustion air, refrigeration systems, and related code requirements. Because mechanical-code questions may involve definitions, tables, exceptions, and connected provisions, students should practice reading beyond a single highlighted sentence.

Residential Load Calculation - Manual J, 8th Edition supports preparation involving residential heating and cooling load calculations. Students should understand how building characteristics, outdoor conditions, insulation, windows, orientation, infiltration, internal gains, and other factors affect equipment-sizing calculations.

Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning, 22nd Edition and Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Technology, 10th Edition support broad HVACR trade preparation. These references address refrigeration principles, system components, heating and cooling equipment, controls, airflow, installation, service, maintenance, diagnostics, and troubleshooting.

The National Electrical Code, 2020 supports preparation for electrical subjects associated with HVACR systems. Students should be comfortable navigating requirements involving conductors, branch circuits, motors, equipment, disconnecting means, overcurrent protection, grounding, and related electrical installations.

The NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, Arkansas, 8th Edition supports preparation involving contractor business responsibilities. Study areas include contracts, estimating, project management, financial practices, insurance, bonding, employment matters, taxes, liens, safety administration, and Arkansas contractor business topics.

Open Book Test

The Arkansas Class A HVACR Contractor exam is open book. Candidates may use approved references during the examination, subject to the testing provider’s rules for permitted books, highlighting, annotations, permanent tabs, bindings, and loose materials.

Every included book in this rental package is highlighted and permanently tabbed before shipment. Highlighted passages draw attention to important laws, rules, code provisions, formulas, tables, diagrams, and technical procedures. Permanent tabs provide visible access points for major chapters and commonly reviewed topics.

An open-book exam still requires serious preparation. Nine references contain a substantial amount of information, and candidates may not have enough time to search every book from the beginning for each question. Efficient performance depends on recognizing the topic, selecting the appropriate reference, locating the relevant section, and confirming the answer without unnecessary searching.

A question involving Arkansas HVACR licensing requirements may direct the candidate to the Arkansas HVAC/R Law or Arkansas HVAC/R Rules and Regulations. Fuel-gas questions may require the International Fuel Gas Code, while ventilation, duct, exhaust, or mechanical-equipment questions may require the International Mechanical Code.

Residential heating and cooling load questions may direct the candidate to Manual J. Refrigeration-cycle, component, control, service, installation, or troubleshooting questions may require one of the two refrigeration and air-conditioning textbooks.

Electrical questions involving motors, wiring, disconnects, conductor protection, grounding, or HVACR equipment may require the National Electrical Code. Questions involving business operations, contracts, estimating, insurance, financial responsibilities, project management, or Arkansas contractor law may require the NASCLA Arkansas reference.
