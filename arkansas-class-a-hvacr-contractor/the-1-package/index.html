Exam Details

The Arkansas Department of Labor and Licensing oversees HVACR contractor licensing through the State HVAC/R Licensing Board. The department administers licensing requirements and provides testing for HVACR contractor classifications. Arkansas HVACR examinations are proctored through Prov.

The Class A license is the broad Arkansas HVACR classification. It authorizes the license holder to perform HVACR work without a restriction based on BTUH or horsepower capacity. Candidates should be prepared to demonstrate knowledge across heating, cooling, refrigeration, ventilation, fuel-gas systems, electrical principles, load calculations, state law, and licensing rules.

The included Arkansas HVAC/R Law and Arkansas HVAC/R Rules and Regulations support preparation involving licensing authority, contractor responsibilities, permitted work, compliance requirements, enforcement, and professional standards.

The International Fuel Gas Code, 2021 supports review of fuel-gas systems, piping, combustion air, appliance installation, venting, testing, inspection, and related safety requirements. The International Mechanical Code, 2021 addresses mechanical systems, ventilation, exhaust, ductwork, equipment installation, combustion air, refrigeration, and other code-regulated HVACR work.

Residential Load Calculation - Manual J, 8th Edition supports the calculation principles used to determine residential heating and cooling loads. Candidates should understand how building characteristics, climate conditions, insulation, windows, infiltration, orientation, and internal gains affect equipment sizing.

Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning, 22nd Edition and Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Technology, 10th Edition support broad technical preparation. Subjects include refrigeration cycles, compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, refrigerants, troubleshooting, controls, air distribution, heating systems, cooling systems, installation, maintenance, and service procedures.

The National Electrical Code, 2020 supports review of electrical requirements associated with HVACR equipment. Candidates should be comfortable researching conductors, overcurrent protection, disconnecting means, grounding, motors, equipment, wiring methods, and other electrical topics related to HVACR installations.

The NASCLA Arkansas business book supports preparation involving contracts, estimating, project management, financial responsibilities, insurance, bonding, employment, taxes, lien laws, safety administration, and Arkansas contractor requirements.

Licensing Steps

The Arkansas Class A HVACR licensing process begins with reviewing the current requirements established by the State HVAC/R Licensing Board. Applicants should determine whether they meet the experience and documentation requirements associated with the Class A classification before submitting the application.

The applicant must complete the required HVACR contractor license application and provide accurate personal, employment, experience, and business information. Supporting documentation should be organized according to the current application instructions.

The candidate should prepare for the Class A examination using the approved technical references. A structured study plan should combine code review, HVACR theory, calculations, system knowledge, Arkansas law, reference navigation, and practice-oriented preparation.

When ready, the candidate completes the examination registration and scheduling steps through the designated testing provider. Identification, scheduling, testing-center, and reference-material requirements should be reviewed before the examination date.

After satisfying the examination requirement, the applicant must complete any remaining licensing steps and submit the required documents and fees. Passing the examination does not independently create an HVACR license. The application must be reviewed and approved by the Arkansas HVAC/R Licensing Board.

State Requirements

Arkansas HVACR contractor licensing is administered by the Arkansas Department of Labor and Licensing through the State HVAC/R Licensing Board. The Board classifies licenses, establishes licensing rules, oversees examinations, and regulates HVACR work performed under Arkansas law.

A Class A license authorizes HVACR work without limitation to BTUH or horsepower capacity. Because this classification has a broad scope, applicants should be prepared to demonstrate experience and knowledge appropriate for heating, ventilation, air-conditioning, and refrigeration systems.

Applicants must use the current HVACR license application and provide the information required for processing. Incomplete applications or missing supporting documents can delay the licensing process.

Arkansas does not currently maintain reciprocal HVACR licensing agreements with other state HVACR licensing boards. An applicant licensed in another jurisdiction may still need to complete the Arkansas application and examination requirements applicable to the requested classification.

Licensed HVACR contractors must comply with Arkansas HVACR law, Board rules, adopted mechanical and fuel-gas codes, permit requirements, inspection requirements, and applicable professional standards. License holders are also responsible for maintaining their credentials and completing required renewal obligations.

Arkansas HVACR license renewal includes continuing-education responsibilities. Licensed professionals should maintain records and follow the current Board requirements for approved instruction, including applicable mechanical-code education.

The contractor compliance guidance included with The 1 Package helps customers understand how licensing, business formation, tax identification, and continuing operational responsibilities fit together. Final decisions concerning examination eligibility and licensing approval remain with the Arkansas HVAC/R Licensing Board.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive study plan should divide preparation into Arkansas law and rules, fuel-gas code, mechanical code, load calculations, refrigeration theory, air-conditioning technology, electrical requirements, and contractor business management.

Begin by reviewing the table of contents and index in every reference. Learn the primary purpose of each book and connect the permanent tabs with the sections behind them. This creates a mental map of the complete reference set.

For technical preparation, review refrigeration cycles, heat transfer, compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, refrigerants, airflow, duct systems, heating equipment, cooling equipment, controls, installation practices, maintenance, and troubleshooting.

For code preparation, practice moving from an indexed term to the applicable section. Read definitions, tables, exceptions, notes, and related provisions before selecting an answer. Code questions may depend on more than one sentence.

For load calculations, practice identifying the building information needed for the calculation and understanding how construction characteristics influence heating and cooling requirements. Formula familiarity and accurate use of tables are important.

For electrical preparation, review motors, branch circuits, disconnecting means, conductors, overcurrent protection, grounding, controls, and equipment requirements. Practice locating electrical information using the National Electrical Code index and article structure.

Complete practice questions with all references available. Before opening a book, decide which reference is most likely to contain the answer. When a question is missed, locate the correct information and record the book, chapter, and subject for future review.
