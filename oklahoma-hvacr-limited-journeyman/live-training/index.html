Exam Details

The Oklahoma HVACR Journeyman Limited examination tests knowledge of the installation, maintenance, repair, fabrication, alteration, and extension of air conditioning, refrigeration, heating, and ventilation systems, including ductwork within a complete system subject to the Limited classification’s capacity restrictions.

The current examination includes 60 scored questions with a testing time of 180 minutes. The minimum passing score is 70%, equal to 42 correct answers.

Electrical Knowledge and Controls contains 15 questions, making it the largest individual category. Candidates should study general electrical theory, motors, DC circuits, AC circuits, and HVAC/R controls. Understanding how controls interact with mechanical equipment is particularly important for installation, service, and troubleshooting questions.

Natural Gas contains nine questions. Subjects include piping valves, connectors, accessories, hangers and supports, sizing, materials, testing, installation, and assembly. Candidates should develop familiarity with the International Fuel Gas Code and learn how to locate gas-system requirements efficiently.

Air Distribution Systems contains 10 questions. Topics include duct definitions, sizing and design, duct assembly and installation, hangers and supports, duct materials and shapes, insulation, fire and smoke control, hoods and exhaust systems, testing and balancing, ventilation requirements, plans, and symbols.

Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Systems contains 13 questions. Preparation should include refrigeration theory, refrigerants, equipment sizing and design, components, piping, equipment installation, system operation, troubleshooting, and maintenance.

Heating Systems contains 10 questions. Candidates should review heating theory and system types, combustion air, vents and chimneys, equipment sizing, heating equipment, installation, system operation, troubleshooting, maintenance, hydronics, and hydronic piping.

Safety contains three questions. Topics include personal protective equipment, safety training, adequate ventilation, first-aid requirements, hazardous materials, fall protection, lockout/tagout, ladders, and scaffolds.

The examination can also include questions based on trade knowledge and general industry practices. Live instruction and self-study preparation should therefore address both reference-book navigation and practical HVAC/R concepts.

Open Book Test

The Oklahoma HVACR Limited Journeyman examination is an open-book test. Candidates are responsible for bringing the approved references they plan to use at the examination center.

The current reference list includes the 2024 International Mechanical Code with Oklahoma revisions and the 2024 International Fuel Gas Code with Oklahoma revisions. Code questions are based on the specific code editions identified for the examination.

The current examination also permits the ACCA Ductulator, Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning, Manual J - Residential Load Calcs, Mathematics for Plumbers and Pipefitters, and OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926.

The current reference list identifies earlier editions of Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning, and later editions of approved non-code references are allowed. The 22nd Edition listed with this training is a later edition of the approved non-code reference.

Approved references may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are permitted and can be helpful for organizing frequently used sections covering duct systems, refrigeration, heating, ventilation, gas piping, and safety.

Temporary removable tabs such as Post-it notes are not permitted. References may not contain written notes before the examination, and candidates may not write in approved references during the test.

Additional loose papers are not allowed with the approved references. Downloaded references must be properly secured according to current testing requirements.

Candidates may use a silent, non-printing, non-programmable calculator. With 60 questions and 180 minutes available, candidates average approximately three minutes per question. Effective preparation should include timed practice and repeated use of the references before test day.

Licensing Steps

  1. Begin working in the mechanical trade. Gain qualifying HVAC/R experience within Oklahoma’s mechanical licensing structure.
  2. Maintain apprentice registration when required. Oklahoma experience used toward mechanical licensing generally must be supported by appropriate apprentice registration unless a lawful exception applies.
  3. Gain experience in the HVAC/R Limited category. Develop verifiable experience involving the type of heating, cooling, refrigeration, natural gas, and air-distribution work associated with this license classification.
  4. Meet journeyman qualification requirements. The standard pathway requires three years of verifiable mechanical experience in the category being pursued.
  5. Use qualifying mechanical education when applicable. Oklahoma recognizes approved combinations of formal mechanical education and field experience.
  6. Maintain supporting documentation. Keep records of apprentice registration, employment, qualifying education, and other information used to establish licensing eligibility.
  7. Submit the Mechanical License Application. Apply through the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board for HVAC/R Limited Journeyman examination eligibility.
  8. Receive approval to test. The Construction Industries Board evaluates the application and determines examination eligibility.
  9. Schedule through PSI. Approved candidates arrange the examination date, time, and testing location with PSI.
  10. Prepare with live virtual and self-study training. Review technical topics, calculations, approved references, and open-book navigation before testing.
  11. Take the HVACR Journeyman Limited examination. Complete 60 scored questions within the 180-minute testing period.
  12. Earn the required passing score. The current passing requirement is 70%, or 42 correct answers.
  13. Complete license issuance requirements. Follow the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board process for obtaining the HVAC/R Limited Journeyman license after meeting applicable requirements.

State Requirements

Applicants for an Oklahoma mechanical journeyman license must be at least 18 years old.

The standard qualification pathway requires three years of verifiable mechanical experience in the category for which the applicant is applying. Oklahoma defines one year of verifiable mechanical experience as 2,000 hours.

Oklahoma also recognizes qualifying mechanical education. An approved associate degree or vocational diploma consisting of at least 1,000 hours may be combined with one year of verifiable mechanical experience. Other approved education-and-experience combinations are available for qualifying vocational programs with fewer instructional hours.

Experience used toward licensing generally must involve mechanical work obtained while employed by a licensed Mechanical Contractor or through another method approved by the Committee of Mechanical Examiners. Equivalent qualifying military experience may also be considered.

The HVAC/R Limited Journeyman license allows qualifying work involving cooling products, systems, and equipment, including process piping, with a cooling capacity of no more than 25 tons.

The classification also covers heating equipment and associated process piping with a heat input of no more than 500,000 Btu/h per appliance. Sheet metal, natural gas piping, and refrigeration are included within the Limited classification.

A licensed journeyman works for an active, licensed Mechanical Contractor. Journeyman licensing does not independently authorize the license holder to operate as a Mechanical Contractor.
