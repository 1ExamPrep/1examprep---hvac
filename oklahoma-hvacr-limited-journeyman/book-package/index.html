Licensing Steps

Oklahoma mechanical journeyman licensing is administered by the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board. Candidates pursuing an HVACR Limited Journeyman license must complete the applicable state licensing process in addition to preparing for the examination.

  1. Gain qualifying mechanical experience. Build verifiable HVACR experience appropriate to the license category.
  2. Meet journeyman qualification requirements. Satisfy one of the approved experience or education-and-experience pathways for mechanical journeyman licensing.
  3. Submit the mechanical journeyman application. Apply through the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board for the appropriate HVACR Limited category.
  4. Receive examination eligibility. The application must be reviewed and approved before proceeding with testing.
  5. Schedule the examination. Approved candidates complete the applicable examination scheduling process.
  6. Prepare with the exam references. Review technical material while practicing open-book navigation.
  7. Complete the licensing process. Follow the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board requirements associated with obtaining and maintaining the journeyman license.

The licensing process is separate from simply purchasing exam books. Candidates should plan for both the administrative licensing requirements and the technical preparation needed for the examination.

State Requirements

The Oklahoma HVACR Limited Journeyman classification applies to qualifying HVACR work within limits established by the state.

The classification allows qualifying work involving cooling products, systems, and equipment, including related process piping, with a cooling capacity of no more than 25 tons.

It also covers qualifying heating equipment, including process piping, with a heat input of no more than 500,000 Btu/h per appliance.

The HVACR Limited Journeyman classification also includes qualifying sheet metal, natural gas piping, and refrigeration work within the authorized scope of the license.

Applicants for an Oklahoma mechanical journeyman license must be at least 18 years old and meet an approved experience or education-and-experience pathway. One qualifying route is three years of verifiable mechanical trade experience in the category being pursued. Oklahoma also recognizes certain approved education programs combined with qualifying mechanical experience.

A licensed mechanical journeyman performs work under an active licensed mechanical contractor and must remain within the scope of the applicable journeyman category.

Understanding the limits of the HVACR Limited classification is important during preparation because the licensing category determines the type and capacity of work authorized after licensure.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive study plan for the Oklahoma HVACR Limited Journeyman examination should combine technical review, code navigation, trade mathematics, safety preparation, and repeated practice using the books.

Begin with refrigeration and air conditioning fundamentals. Review refrigeration theory, refrigerants, system components, equipment sizing, piping, installation, operation, troubleshooting, and maintenance. Use Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning during these study sessions so the book becomes familiar.

Heating preparation should include heating principles, combustion air, equipment, vents and chimneys, hydronic systems, installation, operation, maintenance, and troubleshooting. Code and technical references can both play a role in these subjects.

Natural gas preparation should include piping, valves, connectors, supports, materials, sizing, testing, appliances, combustion air, and venting. Practice using the International Fuel Gas Code while studying these topics.

Air distribution preparation should address duct systems, sizing, installation, materials, insulation, ventilation, exhaust requirements, supports, testing and balancing, and system layouts. The International Mechanical Code can support code-based study in many of these areas.

Electrical Knowledge and Controls should include basic electrical theory, motors, circuits, controls, and the relationship between electrical systems and HVACR equipment. Candidates with strong mechanical experience but less exposure to controls may benefit from spending additional study time in this area.

Trade mathematics should be practiced through actual calculations. Review measurements, formulas, conversions, area, volume, and other practical mathematical concepts. Work through problems instead of simply reading examples.

Safety preparation should include direct navigation of 29 CFR Part 1926. Practice locating information on personal protective equipment, fall protection, ladders, scaffolds, employee safety, workplace hazards, and other construction safety subjects.

Mixed-reference practice can be especially useful. Work through questions from several different areas during the same study session. Begin with a mechanical code topic, move to fuel gas, then refrigeration, mathematics, controls, and safety.

Before opening a book, decide which reference should contain the information. This habit reinforces both subject recognition and reference navigation.

Repeated practice can help you learn which information should be answered directly from trade knowledge and which questions may benefit from a code or technical reference lookup.
