Exam Details

The Oklahoma HVACR Journeyman Limited examination evaluates technical knowledge associated with heating, ventilation, air conditioning, refrigeration, equipment installation, system maintenance, and mechanical safety.

The Oklahoma Construction Industries Board oversees mechanical licensing, while PSI administers the applicable examination.

  • Examination: Oklahoma HVACR Journeyman Limited
  • Licensing Authority: Oklahoma Construction Industries Board
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Number of Scored Questions: 60
  • Time Allowed: 180 minutes
  • Passing Score: 70%
  • Correct Answers Required: 42
  • Examination Format: Open book
  • Mechanical Code Reference: International Mechanical Code, 2024, with Oklahoma amendments
  • Fuel Gas Code Reference: International Fuel Gas Code, 2024, with Oklahoma amendments

Important examination knowledge areas include:

  • Heating Systems: Heating equipment, combustion, venting, installation, and operational principles.
  • Ventilation and Air Distribution: Duct systems, airflow, mechanical ventilation, and equipment requirements.
  • Refrigeration and Air Conditioning: Refrigeration cycles, compressors, refrigerants, condensers, evaporators, and system performance.
  • Fuel Gas Systems: Gas piping, sizing, regulators, combustion air, and appliance connections.
  • Electrical Controls: Electrical fundamentals, motors, switches, relays, and HVAC control circuits.
  • Trade Mathematics and Safety: Measurements, calculations, pressure, heat, and construction workplace safety.

With 180 minutes available for 60 questions, candidates have approximately three minutes per question. Efficient reference navigation and strong technical understanding are valuable examination preparation skills.

Open Book Test

The Oklahoma HVACR Journeyman Limited examination is an open-book test. Candidates may consult approved examination reference materials according to PSI testing requirements.

Open-book testing requires more than finding information in a technical book. Candidates must identify the mechanical subject, locate the applicable provision or formula, interpret the information, and apply it correctly.

The International Mechanical Code addresses equipment installations, ventilation, ductwork, refrigeration systems, and mechanical safety. The International Fuel Gas Code covers fuel gas piping, system sizing, combustion air, and gas appliance requirements.

Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning supports refrigeration theory, electrical controls, component operation, and troubleshooting. Mathematics for Plumbers and Pipefitters strengthens calculation knowledge, while OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 addresses construction safety.

Oklahoma adopted the 2024 International Mechanical Code and International Fuel Gas Code with applicable amendments effective September 14, 2026.

The examination transition period runs from September 14 through December 14, 2026. During this period, affected candidates may test using either the previously adopted or newly adopted building codes.

Beginning December 15, 2026, affected examinations will use the newly adopted codes exclusively.

Regular reference practice should include locating code articles, mechanical installation requirements, gas piping tables, refrigeration diagrams, and mathematical formulas. Familiarity with these materials can support more efficient research during testing.

State Requirements

HVACR work in Oklahoma is regulated through the state's mechanical licensing laws, administrative regulations, and adopted mechanical construction codes.

The Oklahoma Construction Industries Board establishes professional qualifications and authorized work classifications for mechanical journeymen and contractors.

The HVACR Limited Journeyman classification covers authorized installation, alteration, modification, service, maintenance, and repair of cooling equipment rated at no more than 25 tons and heating equipment with a heat input of no more than 500,000 Btu/h per appliance.

The classification also encompasses applicable sheet metal, natural gas piping, and refrigeration activities within its authorized scope.

HVACR professionals must understand how mechanical systems operate together and apply relevant requirements for equipment installation, piping, ventilation, duct construction, and safety.

The International Mechanical Code and International Fuel Gas Code establish technical installation provisions adopted by Oklahoma with applicable amendments.

These standards support consistent mechanical installation practices involving heating equipment, cooling equipment, refrigerant systems, gas piping, ventilation, and related components.
