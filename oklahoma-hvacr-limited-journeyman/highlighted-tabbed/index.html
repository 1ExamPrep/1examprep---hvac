Exam Details

The Oklahoma HVACR Journeyman Limited examination evaluates knowledge related to the installation, maintenance, repair, fabrication, alteration, and extension of air conditioning, refrigeration, heating, and ventilation systems, including ductwork, within the scope of the limited HVACR classification.

  • Exam: Oklahoma HVACR Journeyman Limited
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Number of Questions: 60
  • Time Allowed: 180 minutes
  • Passing Score: 70%
  • Passing Raw Score: 42 correct answers
  • Exam Format: Open book

The examination covers several major HVACR knowledge areas, including Electrical Knowledge and Controls, Natural Gas, Air Distribution Systems, Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Systems, Heating Systems, and Safety.

Electrical Knowledge and Controls can include general electrical theory, motors, AC and DC circuits, and controls. HVACR equipment depends heavily on electrical components and control systems, so candidates should understand how electrical principles relate to heating, cooling, refrigeration, and ventilation equipment.

Natural Gas subjects include piping, valves, connectors, accessories, hangers and supports, materials, sizing, testing, installation, and related requirements. Candidates should be comfortable navigating the International Fuel Gas Code when preparing for these topics.

Air Distribution Systems may involve duct definitions, sizing and design, installation, duct materials, insulation, hangers and supports, ventilation requirements, exhaust systems, fire and smoke control, testing and balancing, and interpretation of plans and symbols.

Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Systems can include refrigeration theory, refrigerants, equipment sizing and design, system components, piping, installation, system operation, troubleshooting, and maintenance. Heating Systems can include heating theory, combustion air, vents and chimneys, equipment sizing, installation, hydronics, system operation, troubleshooting, and maintenance.

Safety preparation can involve workplace requirements such as personal protective equipment, fall protection, ladders, scaffolds, employee safety, hazardous conditions, and other construction-industry requirements found in OSHA regulations.

Open Book Test

The Oklahoma HVACR Journeyman Limited examination is an open book test. Candidates may bring approved reference materials into the examination center according to the testing provider's published rules.

Approved reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are permitted. Temporary tabs such as Post-it notes are not allowed, and references may not contain written notes.

Licensing Steps

The Oklahoma Construction Industries Board administers mechanical journeyman licensing. Candidates pursuing an HVACR Limited Journeyman license must complete the applicable experience, application, examination, and licensing requirements.

  1. Work as a mechanical apprentice. Gain qualifying HVACR experience while meeting Oklahoma apprentice registration requirements applicable to the work being performed.
  2. Build qualifying mechanical experience. Accumulate the experience needed for the HVACR Limited Journeyman category.
  3. Meet the journeyman qualification requirements. Oklahoma allows qualifying pathways involving mechanical trade experience and certain approved education combined with experience.
  4. Submit the mechanical license application. Apply through the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board for the appropriate journeyman category.
  5. Receive examination approval. Once the application is approved, the candidate can proceed with scheduling the required examination.
  6. Take the HVACR Journeyman Limited examination. Prepare for the technical subjects and approved open-book references before testing.
  7. Complete the licensing process. Follow the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board requirements for obtaining and maintaining the HVACR Limited Journeyman license.

Oklahoma describes the mechanical licensing path as progressing from apprentice to journeyman and potentially to contractor. Journeymen work for an active licensed mechanical contractor and perform work within the scope of the journeyman license classification.

State Requirements

Applicants for an Oklahoma mechanical journeyman license must be at least 18 years old and meet an approved experience or education-and-experience pathway.

One pathway requires three years of verifiable experience in the mechanical trade in the category for which the applicant is applying. Oklahoma also recognizes certain approved mechanical education programs combined with specified amounts of qualifying mechanical experience.

For Oklahoma experience to count toward licensing, mechanical apprentices generally need to maintain the required apprentice registration while gaining qualifying experience. Oklahoma defines one year of verifiable mechanical experience as 2,000 hours.

The HVACR Limited Journeyman license allows qualifying work involving cooling products, systems, or equipment, including process piping, with a cooling capacity of no more than 25 tons.

The classification also covers qualifying heating equipment, including process piping, with a heat input of no more than 500,000 Btu/h per appliance.

The scope also includes sheet metal, natural gas piping, and refrigeration work within the HVACR Limited Journeyman classification.

A licensed journeyman works for an active licensed mechanical contractor. Candidates should understand the limits of the HVACR Limited classification so they know how the credential fits into Oklahoma's mechanical licensing structure.

Test Information and Study Materials

For Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Systems, review refrigeration principles, refrigerants, equipment and components, piping, equipment sizing, installation, operation, troubleshooting, and maintenance. Use Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning during study sessions so you become familiar with where major technical subjects are located.

For Heating Systems, study heating principles, combustion air, vents and chimneys, equipment sizing, installation, hydronic systems, system operation, maintenance, and troubleshooting. Heating questions can require both technical knowledge and the ability to locate code requirements.

Natural Gas preparation should include piping, valves, connectors, supports, materials, sizing, testing, combustion air, venting, and appliances. Practice using the International Fuel Gas Code so you know where these subjects are organized.

Air Distribution Systems preparation should include duct sizing and design, assembly, materials, supports, insulation, ventilation, exhaust systems, fire and smoke requirements, testing and balancing, and plans and symbols.

Electrical Knowledge and Controls should include general electrical theory, AC and DC circuits, motors, and HVACR controls. Candidates who spend most of their field time on mechanical installation may benefit from dedicating additional study time to electrical concepts and control systems.

Trade mathematics should be practiced through actual problems. Work on measurements, conversions, formulas, area, volume, and other mechanical calculations. Instead of simply memorizing formulas, practice determining which calculation is needed and how the information provided in a question should be used.

Safety preparation should include direct navigation of 29 CFR Part 1926. Practice finding requirements related to personal protective equipment, ladders, scaffolds, fall protection, workplace hazards, and employee safety.

Mixed-reference practice can help create a more realistic open-book study environment. Work through questions from different subjects during the same study session. A sequence might include a mechanical code question, a natural gas question, a refrigeration concept, an electrical control topic, a mathematical calculation, and an OSHA safety requirement.

Before opening a reference, determine which book is most likely to contain the answer. This simple habit can help prevent unnecessary searching and reinforce your understanding of how the books relate to the different examination subjects.
