Exam Details

The Maryland Master HVACR Contractor examination is administered by PSI Services LLC through the licensing examination program overseen by the Maryland Board of Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractors.

The examination evaluates technical knowledge involving electrical systems, piping, refrigeration, air conditioning, heating, air distribution, and construction safety.

  • Exam Name: Maryland Master HVACR Contractor
  • Testing Provider: PSI Services LLC
  • Licensing Authority: Maryland Board of Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractors
  • Exam Format: Computer-administered multiple-choice examination
  • Number of Questions: 100
  • Time Limit: 240 minutes (4 hours)
  • Passing Score: 70%
  • Exam Type: Open book
  • Online Practice Access: 3 months

The PSI Master HVACR Contractor content outline identifies six examination categories:

  • Electrical Knowledge, Motors, and Controls – 20 Questions: Electrical principles, AC and DC circuits, motors, and control equipment.
  • Piping – 15 Questions: Piping materials, installation requirements, joints, valves, fittings, supports, insulation, and troubleshooting.
  • Refrigeration and Air Conditioning – 25 Questions: Refrigeration theory, refrigerants, system design, equipment installation, operation, and maintenance.
  • Heating Systems – 20 Questions: Heating equipment, combustion air, hydronic systems, system sizing, venting, and troubleshooting.
  • Air Distribution Systems – 15 Questions: Duct design, installation, materials, insulation, ventilation, and testing and balancing.
  • Safety – 5 Questions: Construction hazards, personal protective equipment, ladders, scaffolds, fall protection, and workplace safety.

Refrigeration and Air Conditioning is the largest examination category, accounting for 25% of the test. Electrical Knowledge and Heating Systems each represent 20%.

Together, these three areas account for 65 of the 100 questions. Understanding the examination distribution helps you prioritize preparation while maintaining familiarity with every technical category.

With four hours available for 100 questions, candidates have an average of 2.4 minutes per question. Efficient reference navigation and strong technical understanding are important parts of examination preparation.

Open Book Test

The Maryland Master HVACR Contractor (PSI) examination is an open book test. Candidates may bring reference materials specifically authorized by PSI into the examination room.

Approved references include the 2018 International Mechanical Code, designated editions of Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning, Manual J Residential Load Calculation, OSHA construction safety regulations, and the ACCA Ductulator.

The 22nd Edition of Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning is included in this product's study reference list. Examination-room eligibility for textbook editions is determined by PSI's applicable candidate bulletin.

Under PSI's published reference rules, approved books may contain highlighting, underlining, and acceptable indexing tabs. Handwritten notes, loose or attached papers, and study guides are not permitted.

Reference materials are not supplied at the testing center.

Effective open-book preparation includes:

  • Learning the organization of the International Mechanical Code.
  • Practicing navigation through HVACR technical textbooks.
  • Understanding refrigeration and heating equipment terminology.
  • Reviewing mechanical installation requirements.
  • Practicing residential load calculation concepts.
  • Developing familiarity with duct sizing tools.
  • Reviewing OSHA construction safety requirements.
  • Practicing technical questions within a realistic time allowance.

Open-book examinations require more than finding information in a reference. Candidates benefit from understanding the subject, identifying the appropriate publication, and recognizing how technical requirements apply.

Licensing Steps

Maryland Master HVACR Contractor licensing is administered through the state's Board of Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractors.

The licensing process establishes professional qualifications for individuals seeking authority to provide HVACR contracting services.

  1. Review Master Contractor Qualifications: Understand Maryland's required professional experience and licensing conditions.

  3. Maintain Professional Documentation: Organize employment verification, license records, and supporting information.
  4. Apply for Examination Eligibility: Submit the examination application and required documentation to PSI.

  7. Pass the Examination: Achieve the required minimum passing score of 70%.

Preparing experience records before beginning the examination application can make the licensing process more organized and manageable.

State Requirements

The Maryland Board of Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractors establishes the qualifications for Master HVACR Contractor licensing.

The standard qualification pathway includes:

  • Three Years of Qualifying Experience: At least three years of active HVACR experience in qualifying licensed roles under the direction and control of a Master HVACR Contractor.
  • Recent Employment: At least 1,875 hours of qualifying HVACR work during the year preceding the application.
  • Current Professional License: Holding a qualifying Maryland journeyman or higher-level HVACR license.
  • Passing Examination: Achieving a score of at least 70% on the Master HVACR Contractor examination.
  • Licensing Application: Completing the applicable Maryland Board requirements.
  • Insurance Requirements: Meeting applicable liability and property damage insurance obligations when contracting HVACR work.

Maryland's Master HVACR Contractor license authorizes installation and maintenance services across the heating, ventilation, air conditioning, and refrigeration trades.

Reference Books

The following five references support preparation for the Maryland Master HVACR Contractor examination.

  • International Mechanical Code, 2018
    Published by the International Code Council, the 2018 International Mechanical Code establishes requirements for mechanical equipment, ventilation, exhaust systems, ductwork, refrigeration equipment, and mechanical installations. It supports preparation involving mechanical code interpretation and installation requirements.
  • Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning, 22nd Edition
    This comprehensive HVACR textbook provides explanations of refrigeration theory, heating systems, air conditioning, electrical controls, motors, piping, equipment installation, maintenance, and troubleshooting. It supports technical review across several major Master HVACR Contractor examination subjects.
  • Residential Load Calculation - Manual J, 8th Edition
    Published by the Air Conditioning Contractors of America, Manual J provides a recognized methodology for residential heating and cooling load calculations. It addresses heat gain, heat loss, insulation, windows, infiltration, and building characteristics that influence equipment sizing.
  • Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA)
    These federal construction safety regulations address workplace hazards, protective equipment, scaffolding, ladders, fall protection, tools, excavation safety, and other construction-related safety requirements. They support preparation for the examination's Safety category.
  • Ductulator
    The ACCA Ductulator is a duct sizing slide-rule tool used to evaluate relationships involving airflow, duct dimensions, velocity, and friction loss. It provides practical support for studying air distribution system calculations and duct design principles.

These references complement one another by covering mechanical codes, HVACR theory, residential load calculations, duct sizing, and construction safety.

Studying the references alongside online practice questions helps connect technical knowledge with practical installation and troubleshooting scenarios.

Residential Load Calculations

Residential heating and cooling load calculations help determine a building's mechanical equipment requirements.

Understanding how building characteristics influence heating and cooling loads supports broader preparation involving equipment sizing.

Ventilation Requirements

Ventilation systems move outdoor air, conditioned air, and exhaust air through buildings.

Construction Safety
