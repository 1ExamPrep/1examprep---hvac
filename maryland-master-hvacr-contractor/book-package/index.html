Open Book Test

The Maryland Master HVACR Contractor exam is an open book examination. Candidates may bring in reference books, and study guides are not allowed. Reference materials may be indexed and may contain highlighted or underlined text, but they must be unmarked (not written in) and may not contain additional papers (loose or attached).

Open-book testing does not mean “easy.” It means your performance depends on two skills working together:

  • Understanding: You must recognize what the question is testing and narrow down the correct concept quickly.
  • Navigation: You must be able to locate the supporting rule, definition, table, or process efficiently inside your references.

Tabbing guidance matters: Open-book exams often allow tabbing, but only within specific rules. If you plan to tab your references, use durable, exam-compliant tabs and avoid tab styles that are not accepted. A smart approach is to use tabs as section markers—not as a substitute for understanding. Tabs help you arrive quickly. Your knowledge helps you choose correctly once you’re there.

State Requirements

Maryland HVACR licensing is regulated by the Maryland State Board of HVACR Contractors. For the Master license level, Maryland’s published requirements include experience as a licensed journeyman and qualifying employment under the direction and control of a licensed HVACR Master contractor. Requirements also include meeting minimum recent work hours, passing the Master examination, and holding the appropriate current license level.

Master eligibility (high-level):

  • Experience: Must have been licensed as a journeyman and regularly and principally employed to provide all areas of HVACR services for at least three years of active experience under the direction and control of an HVACR master.
  • Recent work hours: Must meet a minimum work-hour requirement in the year prior to applying.
  • Exam passage: Must pass the required examination for the Master license category.
  • Current license: Must currently hold a journeyman or higher level license.

Insurance requirements (when applicable): Certain individuals who hold a Master contractor license must carry personal liability and property damage insurance coverage. Minimum coverage amounts and combined per-occurrence minimums apply, and insurance is typically required when any of the following situations apply:

  • You are the owner-operator of an HVACR business.
  • You have authority to approve HVACR contracts on behalf of a company.
  • You plan to pull permits from local government agencies to perform HVACR services.

Because requirements and documentation standards can be updated, confirm current requirements before you apply. The goal of this package is to help you prepare more effectively—strengthening knowledge, improving recall, and building confidence from consistent study habits.

Test Information and Study Materials

To get the most out of this book package—especially for an open-book contractor exam—use a study approach that emphasizes understanding, repetition, and fast navigation. These references are strong sources, but results come from how you use them. Below is a practical way to turn these materials into consistent preparation.

1) Build a weekly topic rhythm. Instead of trying to study everything at once, rotate through major categories. For example, dedicate specific sessions to mechanical code concepts, refrigeration fundamentals, load calculation workflow, air distribution/duct sizing concepts, and OSHA safety. This keeps you from overstudying one area while neglecting others.

2) Make code study practical. Mechanical code can feel abstract if you study it like a dictionary. Make it jobsite-focused. When you learn a concept, ask:

  • What decision does this guide in the field?
  • What mistake would violate this expectation?
  • What safety or performance reason sits behind the rule?

Code study becomes easier when it is tied to real-world reasoning.

3) Strengthen refrigeration fundamentals through systems thinking. Rather than memorizing isolated facts, focus on how components interact. Ask yourself:

  • If one variable changes, what else changes in the system?
  • What symptoms show up when performance is off?
  • What troubleshooting step logically comes next?

This approach supports scenario-based questions and improves confidence under exam pressure.

4) Learn load calculation workflow, not just terminology. Load calculations are easiest to remember when you understand the purpose of the process. Focus on the sequence of how a load is approached, why certain inputs matter, and how results influence equipment sizing and comfort outcomes. Even when exact calculation details aren’t the focus of a question, workflow familiarity helps you reason through conceptual items.

5) Treat air distribution study as comfort and performance study. Air distribution is not simply “ductwork.” It affects airflow delivery, leakage considerations, noise, balancing, and overall comfort. When studying duct sizing concepts and using the duct sizing tool, tie each decision to outcomes. Ask: What happens if ducts are undersized? What performance issues show up? What installation approach reduces risk?

6) Make OSHA study scenario-based. OSHA regulations can be dense, so study them through scenarios: ladder use, fall protection, tool hazards, housekeeping, PPE decisions, and general jobsite safety expectations. Scenario-based study makes safety rules easier to remember and easier to apply when a question describes a real jobsite condition.

7) Use spaced review to build long-term retention. Contractor exams reward candidates who can recall and apply information consistently. Instead of reading once and moving on, schedule recurring review sessions for your most important topics. The goal is familiarity and recall—not just completion.

8) Practice open-book navigation on purpose. Open-book success comes from practicing the same workflow you’ll use on exam day:

  • Read the question carefully and identify what it’s really testing.
  • Decide which reference is most likely to contain the answer.
  • Use the index or chapter structure to find the correct section quickly.
  • Confirm exceptions, conditions, and the exact wording that affects the outcome.
  • Answer confidently and move on—avoid “over-searching.”

Combined, these habits turn a book package into a true exam-prep tool: organized study sessions, faster review, stronger recall, and a clearer path from reading to real understanding.
