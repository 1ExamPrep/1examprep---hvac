HVAC License Exam: 15 Questions People Ask, Answered

How long does it take to get an HVAC license, is the test hard, is it open book and what is the difference between EPA 608 and a state license? Straight answers to the 15 questions HVAC techs search most. Each answer is short, plain and checked against the official board and exam sources.

An HVAC license comes from your state or local board and usually takes two to five years of supervised work plus a trade exam. EPA 608 is a separate federal certification for handling refrigerant. Many HVAC trade exams are open book, with a pass mark around 70 percent. Here are the 15 questions HVAC techs search most.

What are the different HVAC licenses?

The names change by state, but most fall into a few levels. An apprentice or helper works under supervision. A journeyman or technician license lets you work on your own but not run a business. A master or contractor license lets you design systems, pull permits and own an HVAC company. Some states split licenses by type of work, such as heating only, cooling only, refrigeration or full mechanical. Arizona, for example, licenses air conditioning and refrigeration contractors as C-39. Oklahoma has an HVACR Unlimited contractor class. Your state board's site lists the classes and what each one covers.

Is HVAC a certification or a license?

Both, and they are different things. A license comes from your state or city and gives you the legal right to do HVAC work or run an HVAC business there. A certification proves a skill. The big one is EPA Section 608, a federal certification that anyone who buys or handles refrigerant must hold. Others, like NATE, are voluntary and show employers you know your stuff. Most working techs need EPA 608 no matter where they live, plus whatever license their state or city requires.

How can I get an HVAC license?

The path looks like this in most states:

  1. Work under a licensed HVAC contractor, or finish a trade school or apprenticeship program.
  2. Log the hours your board asks for, with proof such as employer letters.
  3. Get your EPA 608 certification.
  4. Apply to the state board and get approved to test.
  5. Pass the trade exam, and a business and law exam if your state has one.
  6. Show insurance and a bond if you are getting a contractor license.

Each state tweaks the details, so start with your board's application and candidate bulletin.

How long does it take to get an HVAC license?

Plan on two to five years for most contractor or master licenses, because that is how long the experience rules take. A trade school program can take six months to two years and often counts toward part of the time. The exam step is short by comparison. Once your application is approved, you can often test within a few weeks, and studying takes six to eight weeks for most people. EPA 608 can be done in a day or two of study, so get it out of the way early.

How many hours do you need for an HVAC license?

Hours depend on the state and level. Many states ask for two to four years of full-time supervised work, roughly 4,000 to 8,000 hours, before a contractor or master exam. Some count classroom hours from a trade program toward part of that. The hours have to be documented and usually signed off by a licensed contractor. Keep records as you go, including dates, employers and the type of work, because missing paperwork is the most common reason applications stall.

How long is an HVAC license good for?

Most state HVAC licenses renew every one to three years. Renewal usually means a fee and, in many states, continuing education hours on code changes, safety and refrigerants. Contractor licenses also need current insurance and a bond. EPA 608 certification is different: it does not expire, so once you pass it you keep it for life. Put your state renewal date on your calendar as soon as the license arrives, and finish your hours early in the cycle.

What is a master HVAC license?

A master HVAC license is the top individual license in states that use the title. It shows you can design, lay out and supervise HVAC systems, not just install and repair them. In many states a master can pull permits and qualify an HVAC contractor business, which means the company can bid and sign contracts under that license. Getting it usually takes more years of experience than a journeyman license and a harder exam that leans on load calculations, duct design, venting and the mechanical and fuel gas codes.

Is the HVAC license test hard?

The HVAC test is a real test, but a fair one. The questions come straight from the code books and trade references, so the trick is knowing where to look. Heavy topics include load and duct sizing, combustion air, venting, refrigerant piping, electrical controls and safety. Math trips up a lot of people, especially airflow, BTU and duct problems. Most candidates who fail say they ran out of time or did not know the books well enough. Timed practice fixes both.

Is the HVAC test open book?

Many are. The Arizona C-39 trade exam is open book, and so is the Oklahoma HVACR Unlimited exam, which allows only the 2024 International Mechanical Code and International Fuel Gas Code. Several Nevada, Connecticut and Idaho exams also allow approved books. Other exams, and EPA 608, are closed book. Your candidate bulletin lists exactly which books and editions you can bring. If books are allowed, highlighted and tabbed sets save the most time.

How many questions are on an HVAC license exam?

Question counts vary by state. Oklahoma's HVACR Unlimited Contractor trade exam is a good example: 90 questions in 240 minutes, and you need 70 percent, or 63 correct, to pass. Other states run longer or shorter exams, and some add a separate business and law exam. The bulletin for your exam lists the question count, the time limit and how many questions come from each topic. Use that outline to plan your study hours.

How do you study for the HVAC license exam?

Six to eight weeks at about an hour a day works for most working techs. Start with the exact books on your bulletin and learn their layout. Then work practice questions one topic at a time and write down the code section for every question you miss. In the last two weeks, take full-length timed exams. Flash cards help for values you should know without looking, like refrigerant pressures and common duct rules. An online course or AI tutor helps when you need someone to explain why an answer is right.

How do you pass the HVAC exam?

Manage the clock and the books. Answer what you know first, flag the long lookups and come back to them. Never leave a question blank, since a guess cannot hurt you. On open-book exams, tab the chapters and tables you use most so each lookup takes seconds. Write your formula list from memory at the start of every study session. And take at least two full practice exams under real time limits, so test day feels familiar instead of rushed.

Do you need EPA 608 to get an HVAC license?

You need EPA 608 to buy or handle refrigerant, which covers almost all HVAC work, and many states also require it before they issue a license. There are four types: Type I for small appliances, Type II for high-pressure systems, Type III for low-pressure systems and Universal for all three. Most techs go for Universal. The test is closed book and multiple choice, and the certification never expires. It is a smart first step because it opens doors to jobs while you build hours.

Frequently asked questions

Quick answers to a few more questions people ask about the exam and license.

Can I get an HVAC license without going to school?

Often yes. Many states accept supervised work hours alone. School can shorten the time, but documented field experience is what most boards require.

Does an HVAC license from one state work in another?

Sometimes. Some states have reciprocity agreements, but many make you apply and test again. Your experience usually still counts.

What score do I need to pass the HVAC license exam?

Most state HVAC exams use a pass mark of about 70 percent. Your candidate bulletin gives the exact score.

Is EPA 608 open book?

No. The EPA 608 test is closed book and multiple choice, and the certification never expires.

Which EPA 608 type should I get?

Most techs get Universal, which covers Type I small appliances, Type II high-pressure and Type III low-pressure systems.

Is the Arizona C-39 exam open book?

Yes. The C-39 trade exam is open book, and Arizona contractor applicants also prepare for a business and law exam.
