Electrical administrators have an important compliance role in Washington. ### Licensing Steps

Washington electrical administrator certification is administered through the **Washington State Department of Labor & Industries**. Administrator candidates follow a different examination pathway from electricians and Master Electricians because there are no experience prerequisites and no prior L&I examination approval requirement.

1. **Select the 07E Equipment Repair Administrator classification.** This specialty administrator credential corresponds to the 07E Equipment Repair electrical contractor specialty.
2. **Register directly with PSI.** Administrator candidates do not need prior examination approval from Washington Labor & Industries before scheduling.
3. **Prepare for NEC & Theory.** Study the 2020 NEC with emphasis on wiring methods, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, electrical equipment, motors, hazardous locations, special occupancies, and electrical theory.
4. **Prepare for Washington Codes.** Study RCW Chapter 19.28 and WAC Chapter 296-46B, including administrator duties, contractor requirements, examinations, permits, inspections, and 07E scope limitations.
5. **Take all required examination sections.** The first attempt begins the one-year administrator examination period.
6. **Pass each required section with at least 70%.** NEC & Theory and Washington Codes must each be passed independently.
7. **Retake failed sections when necessary.** During the active one-year examination period, sections already passed do not need to be repeated.
8. **Complete all examination sections within one year.** If the full examination is not completed during the examination period, all sections must be taken again in a new examination period.
9. **Apply for the Administrator certificate.** After passing all required sections, submit the required passing score reports and certification materials to Washington Labor & Industries.
10. **Submit the certification materials within 90 days.** L&I must receive the required materials within 90 days of the last passing score.

### State Requirements

Washington identifies Equipment Repair as electrical specialty classification **07E**. There are **no qualification or experience requirements for taking an electrical administrator examination**. Candidates may contact the testing agency directly to register for the appropriate specialty administrator examination.

Administrator certification alone does not authorize a person to perform electrical installation, maintenance, or repair work with the tools of the trade. Anyone personally performing electrical work must separately hold the appropriate Washington electrician certificate.

A specialty electrical administrator may only be assigned to an electrical contractor holding the corresponding specialty. A 07E Equipment Repair Administrator may therefore serve as the designated administrator for an electrical contractor holding the Equipment Repair specialty rather than providing general electrical contracting authority.

The statutory scope of 07E Equipment Repair involves **servicing, maintaining, repairing, or replacing utilization equipment** or related wiring, appliances, devices, or equipment as permitted by Washington law and rule.

To qualify as utilization equipment under this specialty, the equipment must be **self-contained on a single skid or frame** and factory built to standardized sizes or types. It must also be listed or field evaluated by an appropriate laboratory or otherwise approved by Washington Labor & Industries under applicable equipment-approval requirements.

The qualifying utilization equipment must be connected as a single unit to a single source of electrical power with a maximum rating of **600 volts**. The equipment may also be connected to a separate single source of electrical control power limited to a maximum of **250 volts**.

Washington excludes devices used for occupant space heating in industrial, commercial, hospital, educational, public, private commercial, and other covered buildings from the utilization equipment included in the Equipment Repair specialty.

The scope includes **like-in-kind replacement of qualifying utilization equipment** when the same unmodified electrical circuit is used to supply the replacement equipment.

The specialty also includes like-in-kind replacement or repair of remote-control components that are integral to the operation of qualifying utilization equipment, as well as like-in-kind replacement or repair of electrical components within the equipment.

A 07E contractor may disconnect, replace, and reconnect low-voltage control wiring and line-voltage supply whips not more than **six feet in length**, provided there are no modifications to the characteristics of the branch circuit.

The Equipment Repair specialty does not authorize installation, repair, or modification of wiring that interconnects equipment and remote components, branch-circuit conductors, services, feeders, panelboards, disconnect switches, motor control centers, remote magnetic starters or contactors, or raceway and conductor systems interconnecting multiple pieces of equipment or other electrical components.

The specialty also excludes work that provides electrical feeds into a power distribution unit and the installation of conduits and raceways.

Electrical work governed by NEC Articles 500, 501, 502, 503, 504, 505, 510, 511, 513, 514, 515, and 516 is generally outside the 07E scope because those provisions address classified locations. Washington law provides an exception for qualifying electrical work in sewage pumping stations.

The Equipment Repair specialty cannot perform plumbing work regulated under Washington plumbing law.

These boundaries are particularly important for an electrical administrator. When assigned to a contractor, the administrator accepts responsibility for helping ensure that the contractor complies with applicable Washington electrical laws and rules and does not perform work beyond the authority of its license.

### Test Information and Study Materials

**Wiring Methods:** Wiring methods represent one of the larger technical categories on the 07E Administrator examination. Review raceways, cables, conductors, boxes, fittings, flexible wiring methods, protection, support requirements, and equipment connections.

**Overcurrent Protection:** Study circuit breakers, fuses, equipment protection, conductor protection, branch-circuit ratings, and overcurrent requirements that may affect utilization equipment.

**Grounding and Bonding:** Review equipment grounding conductors, bonding, continuity, grounding of equipment enclosures, and grounding requirements for equipment supplied at different voltages.

**Motors and Related Equipment:** Equipment Repair frequently involves motor-driven machinery and utilization equipment. Review motors, controllers, overload protection, disconnecting means, generators, transformers, and related electrical requirements.

**Electrical Equipment:** Study switches, receptacles, flexible cords, equipment wiring, transformers, storage batteries, capacitors, and other electrical equipment relevant to the published exam outline.

**Hazardous Locations:** The examination includes hazardous-location knowledge even though 07E generally excludes work governed by the identified classified-location NEC articles. Candidates should understand the technical concepts and the Washington specialty limitation.

**Special Occupancies and Special Conditions:** Review applicable NEC requirements for specialized locations, occupancies, and electrical conditions that may affect utilization equipment.

**General Trade Knowledge and Theory:** Practice voltage, current, resistance, power, Ohm's law, series and parallel circuits, electrical troubleshooting, motor fundamentals, and other trade calculations.

**07E Utilization Equipment Definition:** Know that qualifying equipment must be self-contained on a single skid or frame, factory built to standardized sizes or types, appropriately listed, field evaluated, or approved, and connected as a single unit to a qualifying electrical source.

**Voltage Limits:** Remember the maximum 600-volt electrical power source and the separate maximum 250-volt electrical control-power source.

**Permitted Repair Activities:** Study like-in-kind equipment replacement, integral remote-control component repair, internal electrical-component repair, and the six-foot supply-whip provision.

**Excluded Work:** Understand the limits involving branch-circuit conductors, services, feeders, panelboards, disconnect switches, motor control centers, remote starters, interconnecting wiring, power distribution unit feeds, conduits, and raceways.

**Administrator Responsibilities:** Review contractor assignments, specialty authority, examinations, certification, permits, inspections, compliance responsibilities, and the distinction between administrator certification and electrician certification.

A practical study strategy is to identify the likely source before beginning a code lookup. Technical questions involving grounding, motors, wiring methods, or overcurrent protection will generally point toward the NEC. Questions involving legal scope, assignments, administrator responsibilities, or contractor authority are more likely to require RCW Chapter 19.28 or WAC Chapter 296-46B.
