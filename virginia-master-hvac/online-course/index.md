### Open Book Test

The Virginia Master HVAC examination is open book. Candidates must bring their own permitted references and follow current PSI rules governing the preparation and use of examination materials.

Open-book testing should not be confused with an exam that requires little preparation. Technical books can contain hundreds of pages of code provisions, tables, definitions, diagrams, and instructional material. Candidates who first become familiar with the books on exam day may spend too much time searching for information.

A better preparation strategy is to learn the organization of each reference. Candidates should know which book is most likely to contain information about a specific topic and how to move through the table of contents, index, definitions, chapters, tables, and cross-references.

A Virginia licensing question may direct the candidate toward the *Virginia Board for Contractors, Individual License and Certification Regulations*. Mechanical-system requirements may require the *International Mechanical Code, 2021*. Oil-burning equipment questions may call for NFPA 31, while refrigeration, air conditioning, heating, electrical, and troubleshooting questions may involve *Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning*.

PSI permits approved examination references to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the exam. Permanent tabs may also be used under current testing rules. Candidates should make sure any reference preparation complies with current examination requirements.

Reference navigation should be practiced as part of normal study. For each practice question, identify the subject first, decide which book applies, locate the supporting information, and confirm the answer. Repeating this process helps make the books more familiar before testing.

### State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors regulates individual HVAC tradesmen. The Master HVAC credential is issued to an individual and should be distinguished from a Virginia contractor business license.

A contractor business must separately obtain the applicable Class A, Class B, or Class C contractor license and hold the appropriate classification or specialty for the work it performs. The individual Master HVAC credential does not by itself create or replace a contractor business license.

Virginia requires applicants for individual tradesman examinations to receive Board approval before scheduling the examination. Applicants should make sure their application and experience documentation meet current requirements before attempting to schedule with PSI.

Current Master applicant pathways include one year of practical experience as a licensed Journeyman in the trade, three years of practical experience as a Virginia licensed residential HVAC tradesman, or nine years of practical experience in the trade, depending on the pathway used to qualify.

Virginia also regulates continuing education for licensed tradesmen. Continuing education used for license renewal is separate from exam preparation and must meet Board requirements for approved education. An online exam prep course should not be treated as a substitute for required Board-approved continuing education unless specifically approved for that purpose.

Candidates should also remember that state licensing requirements and examination requirements can change. Preparation should be based on the current Virginia Board for Contractors requirements and the current PSI candidate information available when applying and scheduling.

### Test Information and Study Materials

Effective Virginia Master HVAC exam preparation should combine technical review with regular work in the reference materials. Candidates can organize study sessions around specific subjects instead of attempting to read every reference from beginning to end without a plan.

Mechanical-code preparation should include regular navigation of the *International Mechanical Code, 2021*. Become familiar with the chapters and terminology used for equipment installation, ventilation, exhaust, duct systems, combustion air, chimneys, vents, and other mechanical requirements.

Ventilation and exhaust systems deserve focused study because they affect indoor air quality, moisture control, contaminant removal, temperature, pressure relationships, and building operation. Candidates should understand the purpose of these systems and know how to find applicable code requirements.

Duct-system study should include materials, supports, construction, insulation, penetrations, clearances, and installation requirements. Understanding how air distribution systems are assembled and regulated can help candidates connect field experience to code-based questions.

Refrigeration preparation should include the refrigeration cycle, compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, refrigerants, pressure-temperature relationships, charging concepts, and system operation. *Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning* provides broad material for reviewing these topics.

Air-conditioning preparation should include equipment operation, system components, airflow, temperature control, humidity considerations, servicing, and troubleshooting. Candidates should understand not only individual components but also how the entire system operates together.

Heating preparation should include heating theory, equipment types, combustion concepts, controls, distribution systems, venting, and safe operation. Oil-burning equipment should be reviewed with NFPA 31 so candidates become comfortable with the specialized provisions found in that standard.

Electrical knowledge is an important part of HVAC work. Candidates should review motors, relays, contactors, thermostats, control circuits, electrical troubleshooting, and the interaction between electrical and mechanical components.

Troubleshooting should be practiced as a reasoning process rather than simply memorizing symptoms and answers. Candidates should understand normal system operation so they can interpret symptoms, identify likely causes, and determine which component or operating condition should be checked.

Virginia regulations should be incorporated throughout the study process. Candidates should practice locating definitions, qualification requirements, professional obligations, and other state-specific information instead of waiting until immediately before testing to study regulatory material.

Reference-navigation practice should also be repeated regularly. Use practice questions to identify the topic, select the appropriate reference, search with the correct terminology, and verify the answer from the applicable section or table.
