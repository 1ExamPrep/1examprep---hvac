## About this A-HVAC/R Master Electrician package

Prepare for the Washington HVAC/Refrigeration Master Electrician examination with a focused book package built around the electrical code, Washington electrical law, and administrative rules used for the state's **06A HVAC/Refrigeration specialty**. This package includes the **Revised Code of Washington (RCW) Chapter 19.28**, **Washington Administrative Code (WAC) Chapter 296-46B**, and the **National Electrical Code, NEC, 2020 Edition**.

Washington identifies HVAC/Refrigeration as electrical specialty classification **06A**. The HVAC/R Master Electrician credential is a specialty master certification for experienced electricians who already hold the corresponding Washington specialty electrician certificate and meet the state's master electrician eligibility requirements. The examination requires candidates to apply electrical code requirements to HVAC and refrigeration equipment while also understanding Washington-specific licensing rules, specialty limitations, and electrical regulations.

Washington electrical examinations are **open book**. Candidates may use approved reference materials during testing, making effective code navigation an important part of preparation. Candidates should become comfortable deciding whether an examination question is controlled by the NEC, RCW Chapter 19.28, or WAC Chapter 296-46B and locating the applicable provision efficiently.

The 06A HVAC/Refrigeration specialty covers defined electrical work associated with HVAC and refrigeration systems. Its scope includes specified telecommunications and Class 2 low-voltage control wiring, line-voltage components located within HVAC/refrigeration equipment, certain disconnecting means and controller components, limited raceway work for physical protection of low-voltage cables, qualifying flexible supply whips, and other work specifically authorized by Washington rules.

The Washington 06A specialty is not generally limited by voltage, phase, or amperage. However, the specialty remains limited to the HVAC/refrigeration scope established by Washington regulations. Master-level certification does not convert the 06A specialty into an unrestricted general electrical certification.

### Licensing Steps

Washington HVAC/Refrigeration Master Electrician certification is administered through the **Washington State Department of Labor & Industries**. Candidates follow the specialty master electrician pathway.

1. **Hold a Washington 06A HVAC/Refrigeration Electrician certificate.** The applicant must first be certified by Labor & Industries in the applicable specialty.
2. **Hold the specialty certificate for at least two years.** Washington requires specialty Master Electrician applicants to have been certified in the applicable specialty for at least two years.
3. **Apply for the Master Electrician examination.** Submit the applicable examination application through Washington Labor & Industries.
4. **Receive examination approval.** L&I reviews the application and verifies that the candidate meets the qualification requirements.
5. **Prepare for NEC & Theory and Washington Codes.** Study the NEC, RCW, and WAC with emphasis on HVAC/R electrical equipment, motors, controls, and Washington specialty rules.
6. **Schedule through PSI.** After approval, use the applicable scheduling instructions to arrange the 06A Master Electrician examination.
7. **Pass each required examination section.** Washington requires a minimum score of 70% on each section.
8. **Complete certification requirements.** After passing the examination, follow Washington Labor & Industries procedures for issuance of the specialty Master Electrician certificate.

Washington Master Electricians may work with the tools of the trade within the scope of their certification. A Master Electrician may also serve as the designated master electrician for an electrical contractor when the applicable contractor classification and assignment requirements are satisfied.

When a master electrician is assigned to an electrical contractor, the master electrician accepts responsibility for helping ensure that the contractor complies with Washington electrical laws and rules.

### State Requirements

Washington identifies HVAC/Refrigeration as electrical specialty classification **06A**. To qualify for the specialty Master Electrician examination, an applicant must have been certified by Washington Labor & Industries in the 06A specialty for at least **two years**.

The underlying 06A HVAC/Refrigeration Electrician certification requires **4,000 hours of qualifying work experience**. Trainees working toward the 06A specialty must work under an appropriately certified specialty electrician or a general journey level electrician and must be supervised at least **75 percent of the time**.

Washington also requires candidates in the 4,000-hour specialty pathway to complete **48 hours of basic classroom instruction** before they can qualify for examination under the applicable pathway.

The 06A specialty is not limited by voltage, phase, or amperage, but its work must remain within the HVAC/refrigeration scope defined by Washington rules. Washington places additional supervision restrictions on trainees working on HVAC/refrigeration systems containing circuits rated over 600 volts.

The specialty may perform specified telecommunications and Class 2 low-voltage control wiring and component work associated with HVAC/refrigeration systems. Washington rules distinguish between residential occupancies and other occupancies when defining portions of this authority.

The 06A scope also permits installation, repair, replacement, and maintenance of line-voltage components located within HVAC/refrigeration equipment. This can include qualifying equipment accessories installed within and powered from the HVAC/refrigeration system.

The specialty may repair, replace, or maintain internal components of HVAC/refrigeration equipment disconnecting means or controllers when the work meets the limitations established by Washington rules.

Washington also permits the specialty to install short sections of raceway for physical protection of low-voltage cables and to perform qualifying repair, replacement, or maintenance involving line-voltage flexible supply whips when the work falls within the stated restrictions.

The 06A specialty does not provide unrestricted authority to install general line-voltage services, feeders, branch circuits, external controllers, or disconnect switches. WAC 296-46B establishes specific exceptions and limitations that candidates should study carefully.

**Grounding and Bonding:** Review equipment grounding conductors, bonding, grounding electrode systems, service grounding, separately derived systems, equipment enclosures, raceways, and grounding requirements associated with HVAC/R equipment.

**Wiring Methods:** Study cables, raceways, boxes, fittings, flexible wiring methods, wet and damp locations, equipment connections, conductor protection, supports, and installation requirements commonly encountered around HVAC and refrigeration systems.

**Electrical Equipment:** Review disconnecting means, controllers, flexible cords and cables, transformers, utilization equipment, equipment wiring, and other provisions that can apply to heating, cooling, and refrigeration installations.

**Equipment Over 600 Volts:** The Master examination includes equipment-over-600-volts content. Candidates should understand the applicable NEC concepts and Washington supervision restrictions affecting HVAC/R systems containing higher-voltage circuits.

**Hazardous Locations:** Refrigeration and mechanical equipment can be installed in areas where hazardous-location requirements apply. Review classified locations, suitable equipment, wiring methods, and installation restrictions.

**General Trade Knowledge and Theory:** The examination includes a significant theory component. Practice voltage, current, resistance, power, conductor calculations, motor calculations, electrical relationships, and general HVAC/R electrical troubleshooting principles.

**Washington HVAC/R Specialty Scope:** Learn the exact authority and limitations of classification 06A. Master-level candidates should understand what electrical work the specialty may perform and when another electrical classification is required.
