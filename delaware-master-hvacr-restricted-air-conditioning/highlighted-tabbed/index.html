Exam Details

The Delaware Master HVACR Restricted Air Conditioning examination is part of the licensing process overseen by the Delaware Board of Plumbing, Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Examiners. Applicants pursuing licensure by examination must receive Board approval before scheduling the test with the examination provider.

The examination tests knowledge of the design, installation, maintenance, repair, fabrication, alteration, and extension of air-conditioning and refrigeration systems. Its scope also includes piping vessels, warm-air heating, low- and high-pressure boilers, ventilation systems, duct systems, insulation, and related components within a complete system.

The examination contains 95 questions, and candidates are allowed four hours to complete it. The subject outline includes General Knowledge, Plan Reading, Systems and Sizing, Piping, Equipment, and Maintenance.

General Knowledge includes 22 questions. Candidates should review refrigeration principles, heat transfer, temperature, pressure, humidity, airflow, electrical fundamentals, combustion, safety practices, tools, insulation, system operation, and common HVACR terminology.

Plan Reading includes eight questions. Candidates may need to interpret mechanical symbols, equipment schedules, duct layouts, piping routes, load information, system notes, dimensions, and equipment locations.

Systems and Sizing includes 17 questions. Preparation should include residential and commercial load calculations, equipment selection, airflow requirements, duct sizing, capacity, indoor and outdoor design conditions, building characteristics, ventilation loads, and system-performance considerations.

Piping includes 14 questions. Candidates should review refrigerant piping, fuel-gas piping, approved materials, valves, regulators, joining methods, supports, sizing procedures, pressure considerations, testing, and protection from physical damage.

Equipment includes 18 questions. Study should address compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, heat pumps, air handlers, furnaces, refrigeration systems, motors, controls, safety devices, and other air-conditioning components.

Maintenance includes 16 questions. Candidates should understand system testing, troubleshooting, refrigerant-side evaluation, electrical checks, airflow measurements, equipment cleaning, control verification, leak detection, startup procedures, and preventive maintenance.

The four-hour testing period requires candidates to manage their time across direct-answer questions, calculations, reference searches, and plan interpretation. Regular practice with the books can help candidates develop a consistent method for finding information.

Open Book Test

The Delaware Master HVACR Restricted Air Conditioning examination is an open-book test. Authorized printed references may be used during testing under the examination provider’s rules.

An open-book format still requires substantial preparation. Technical references contain large amounts of information, and candidates may not have enough time to research every subject from the beginning. Efficient testing requires trade knowledge, calculation ability, and familiarity with the organization of each book.

The highlighting and tabs included in this package are designed to support organized study. Tabs provide recognizable navigation points for major chapters, code topics, formulas, load-calculation procedures, and equipment subjects. Highlighting helps selected information stand out during repeated review.

The International Mechanical Code is useful for questions involving mechanical equipment, refrigeration systems, ventilation, exhaust, duct construction, boilers, combustion air, chimneys, vents, supports, clearances, and installation requirements.

The International Fuel Gas Code supports study involving gas-piping materials, pipe sizing, regulators, shutoff valves, appliance connections, combustion air, venting, pressure testing, and fuel-gas system safety.

Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning supports technical preparation involving refrigeration cycles, refrigerants, compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, air-conditioning systems, heat pumps, heating equipment, motors, controls, installation, troubleshooting, and maintenance.

Manual N supports commercial heating and cooling load calculations for small commercial buildings. Candidates should understand how construction, occupancy, lighting, equipment, ventilation, infiltration, windows, orientation, and design conditions affect calculated loads.

Manual J supports residential heating and cooling load calculations. Preparation should include building construction, insulation, windows, doors, orientation, infiltration, climate, occupancy, and internal heat gains.

Highlighted passages should be read in context. Definitions, exceptions, table notes, equipment conditions, and surrounding provisions may change how a rule or procedure applies. Highlighting and tabs support navigation but do not replace understanding the complete information.

Candidates should practice choosing the correct book before beginning a search. A mechanical installation question may lead to the International Mechanical Code. A fuel-gas question may require the International Fuel Gas Code. A refrigeration or equipment question may be easier to research in Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning.

Residential load questions may require Manual J, while small commercial load questions may require Manual N. Learning these distinctions can reduce unnecessary searching during timed practice and testing.

Licensing Steps

Begin by confirming that the Delaware Master HVACR Restricted Air Conditioning license matches the services you plan to provide. A restricted license permits work only within the specialty identified on the license.

Create an account through Delaware Professional Regulation Online Services and begin the appropriate Master HVACR Restricted application in DELPROS. Delaware professional licensing applications are submitted through the online system.

Gather the documentation required for the applicable licensing pathway. Supporting records may include employment verification, apprenticeship documents, professional license verification, certification records, and other information requested by the Board.

Applicants for the restricted air-conditioning classification must provide the required certification and qualifying experience documentation. The application pathway depends on the applicant’s apprenticeship history, work experience, and current licensing status.

An applicant with an acceptable journeyman certificate must submit the applicable apprenticeship completion documentation and proof of the required qualifying experience obtained under the supervision of a master licensee.

An applicant without an acceptable journeyman certificate must document the required period of qualifying supervised experience. The Board determines whether the applicant must complete the applicable section of the Apprenticeship By-Pass Examination.

Submit the completed application and all required attachments through DELPROS. The Board reviews the application and determines whether the candidate qualifies to take the Master HVACR Restricted Air Conditioning examination.

After receiving approval, follow the examination provider’s instructions to register and schedule the test. Review identification requirements, appointment procedures, calculator policies, authorized reference rules, and test-day requirements.

Prepare with the highlighted and tabbed books in this package. Study general HVACR knowledge, plan reading, systems and sizing, piping, equipment, maintenance, mechanical requirements, fuel-gas provisions, and load calculations.

Complete the examination and earn the required passing score. After the official result is received, complete any remaining licensing requirements shown in DELPROS.

Passing the examination is one part of the licensing process. Final eligibility and license issuance remain subject to Board review and completion of all applicable application requirements.

State Requirements

Delaware requires professionals providing regulated HVACR services to hold the appropriate state license unless a legal exemption applies. Holding an HVACR license in another jurisdiction does not automatically authorize work in Delaware.

A Master HVACR Restricted license permits the holder to provide services only within the specialty listed on the license. Delaware restricted specialties include forced-air heating, ventilation and gas piping; hydronic heating and gas piping; commercial hood systems; refrigeration; air conditioning; and gas piping.

The restricted air-conditioning classification applies to work within the state-defined air-conditioning specialty. A contractor who plans to provide services across multiple restricted specialties should pursue the unrestricted Master HVACR license.

Applicants should submit complete and consistent supporting documentation. Missing experience records, incomplete certification information, or conflicting application details may delay review and examination authorization.

The Delaware Board determines whether an applicant qualifies for examination and licensure. The examination provider administers the test but does not determine whether the applicant satisfies the state’s licensing requirements.

A Master HVACR Restricted Air Conditioning license does not automatically authorize plumbing work involving systems used to bring water into a building or remove liquid and water-carried waste. Separate plumbing authority may be required for that work.

After passing the examination, candidates must complete all remaining application requirements before the professional license can be issued. Applicants should monitor their DELPROS accounts and email for status updates or requests for additional information.

Licensed HVACR professionals must comply with the renewal, continuing education, professional conduct, and other requirements associated with the license.

A professional HVACR license is separate from a business license. Individuals planning to operate an air-conditioning contracting company may also need business registration, a legal entity, tax accounts, insurance, and local operational approvals.
