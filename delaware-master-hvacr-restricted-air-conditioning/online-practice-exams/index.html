Exam Details

The current Delaware Master HVACR Restricted - Air Conditioning examination contains 95 questions. Candidates receive 4 hours to complete the examination.

The current examination content outline includes:

  • General Knowledge: 22 questions
  • Plan Reading: 8 questions
  • Systems & Sizing: 17 questions
  • Piping: 14 questions
  • Equipment: 18 questions
  • Maintenance: 16 questions

General Knowledge is the largest individual category with 22 questions. Candidates should be comfortable with refrigeration principles, air conditioning fundamentals, heating concepts, airflow, controls, system operation, safety, and general HVACR terminology.

Equipment accounts for 18 questions. This category can involve compressors, condensers, evaporators, air handlers, furnaces, controls, motors, fans, coils, refrigeration components, and other equipment found in complete air conditioning systems.

Systems & Sizing represents 17 questions. Candidates should understand heating and cooling loads, equipment selection, duct and airflow relationships, system capacity, design conditions, and the factors that affect proper sizing.

Maintenance accounts for 16 questions, while Piping accounts for 14 questions and Plan Reading accounts for 8 questions. Together, these categories require candidates to understand both system design and the practical work of installing, servicing, evaluating, and maintaining HVACR equipment.

Delaware requires candidates to achieve at least 70% on the licensure examination.

Licensing Steps

  1. Confirm that Restricted Air Conditioning is the correct license. Delaware offers Master HVACR and Master HVACR Restricted licenses, with restricted licenses limited to specific HVACR specialties.
  2. Create or access a DELPROS account. Delaware uses DELPROS for professional license applications and related services.
  3. Submit the Master HVACR Restricted application. Select the Air Conditioning restriction and provide the supporting documentation required by the Board.
  4. Meet the applicable experience pathway. Applicants must document the qualifying HVACR training and work experience required for restricted licensure.
  5. Complete the applicable apprenticeship bypass process when required. Applicants without an accepted journeyman certificate may need to qualify through the applicable restricted bypass pathway.
  6. Provide the required CFC Certification documentation. Delaware requires a copy of the applicable CFC Certification card for Master HVACR Restricted - Air Conditioning applicants.
  7. Receive Board approval to test. The Delaware Board must approve the candidate before the Master HVACR Restricted Air Conditioning examination can be scheduled.
  8. Register with ProV. Approved candidates receive examination scheduling and testing information from ProV.
  9. Prepare for the open-book exam. Review General Knowledge, Plan Reading, Systems and Sizing, Piping, Equipment, and Maintenance.
  10. Take the 95-question examination. Candidates receive 4 hours to complete the test.
  11. Earn the required passing score. A minimum score of 70% is required.
  12. Complete remaining licensing requirements. After the Board receives the official passing score and verifies all other requirements, the license may be issued.

State Requirements

The Master HVACR Restricted Air Conditioning license is administered by the Delaware Board of Plumbing, Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Examiners.

Delaware issues both unrestricted Master HVACR licenses and Master HVACR Restricted licenses. Restricted licenses limit the license holder's practice to the specialty identified on the license, such as air conditioning.

Applicants pursuing Restricted Air Conditioning licensure by examination must complete the applicable DELPROS application and submit the required experience, apprenticeship, training, certification, and other supporting documentation.

Applicants using an approved HVACR journeyman pathway must provide the applicable apprenticeship completion documentation and qualifying experience required by the Board. Applicants without an accepted journeyman certificate may qualify through an experience and apprenticeship bypass pathway.

Delaware updated its HVACR regulations in 2025 to clarify that an applicant pursuing a restricted HVACR license needs to pass only the portion of the apprenticeship bypass examination relevant to the requested restriction.

Applicants for the Master HVACR Restricted - Air Conditioning license must also provide a copy of their CFC Certification card.

The Board must approve the application before the candidate may take the licensure examination. Delaware requires a minimum score of 70% to pass.

Applicants who do not receive a passing score after three examination attempts are subject to Delaware's reapplication requirements and must obtain Board approval before testing again after the applicable waiting period.

Passing the examination does not automatically issue the license. The Board must also verify that the applicant has satisfied all experience, application, certification, and documentation requirements.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Delaware Master HVACR Restricted Air Conditioning study plan should begin with General Knowledge. With 22 questions, it is the largest category on the current examination.

Refrigeration theory should receive substantial attention. Candidates should understand the refrigeration cycle, compressors, condensers, metering devices, evaporators, refrigerant flow, pressure-temperature relationships, superheat, subcooling, and heat transfer.

Air conditioning fundamentals should include sensible and latent heat, humidity, psychrometric relationships, airflow, filtration, coils, condensate management, thermostatic controls, and the way individual components interact within the complete system.

Plan Reading accounts for 8 questions. Candidates should practice reading mechanical drawings, equipment schedules, duct layouts, piping arrangements, dimensions, symbols, and other information used to communicate HVACR system design.

Plan-reading questions can require more than identifying a symbol. Candidates may need to determine how equipment, piping, ducts, and spaces relate to one another and recognize whether a proposed installation matches the design.

Systems & Sizing accounts for 17 questions and should receive significant preparation. Proper air conditioning design begins with understanding the building load rather than selecting equipment solely by floor area or nominal capacity.

Manual J, 8th Edition can support residential load calculation preparation. Study how outdoor and indoor design conditions, windows, walls, ceilings, floors, infiltration, orientation, and internal loads affect heating and cooling requirements.

Manual N supports small commercial load calculation concepts. Commercial spaces may introduce different occupancy schedules, lighting loads, equipment loads, ventilation requirements, building configurations, and operating conditions.

Candidates should understand the relationship between load calculation and equipment selection. Oversizing and undersizing can affect comfort, humidity control, energy performance, equipment cycling, and overall system operation.

Piping accounts for 14 questions. Study refrigerant piping, condensate piping, gas piping concepts, materials, joints, supports, sizing, insulation, routing, testing, and protection.

Refrigerant piping preparation should include the relationship between line sizing, pressure drop, refrigerant velocity, oil return, equipment capacity, and system configuration.

Equipment accounts for 18 questions, making it one of the largest categories. Candidates should understand compressors, condensers, evaporators, air handlers, fan-coil units, packaged systems, heat pumps, motors, controls, and other HVACR equipment.

Study each component by function. Know what it does, how it interacts with the rest of the system, what conditions affect its operation, and what symptoms might indicate improper performance.

Maintenance accounts for 16 questions. Candidates should understand preventive maintenance, troubleshooting, refrigerant-system evaluation, airflow checks, coil condition, filters, electrical controls, condensate systems, equipment inspection, and recognition of abnormal operation.

Maintenance questions often require practical reasoning. Rather than memorizing isolated symptoms, understand what happens to pressures, temperatures, airflow, and equipment operation when a component becomes restricted, dirty, improperly adjusted, or defective.

The International Mechanical Code, 2018 should be used throughout preparation for mechanical equipment, duct systems, ventilation, refrigeration, installation, access, clearances, and related requirements.

The International Fuel Gas Code, 2018 can support study when HVACR work involves gas-fired equipment, venting, combustion air, fuel gas piping, or other gas-related system relationships.

Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning, 22nd Edition can help candidates reinforce the technical principles behind code and equipment questions. When a practice question exposes weakness in refrigeration theory, airflow, controls, or troubleshooting, return to the underlying concept before attempting additional questions.

For load questions, work through the process instead of memorizing typical equipment sizes. Identify the building conditions, determine which gains or losses apply, and understand how the calculated load affects system selection.

For plan-reading questions, practice tracing systems from equipment to distribution points. Follow duct routes, refrigerant piping, condensate lines, equipment connections, and mechanical symbols so the complete design becomes easier to visualize.

When a practice question is missed, identify whether the problem involves theory, code application, plan interpretation, calculation, equipment operation, or troubleshooting. This makes it easier to direct additional study toward the actual weakness.

Use the included 3 months access to build repeated practice into your preparation. Start with individual subjects, move into mixed-topic question sets, and return regularly to categories where accuracy remains lower.
