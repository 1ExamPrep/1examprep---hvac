### Exam Details

The Delaware Master HVACR Restricted Air Conditioning examination is part of the professional licensing process overseen by the Delaware Board of Plumbing, Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Examiners. Applicants pursuing licensure by examination must receive Board approval before scheduling the test through the approved examination provider.

The examination evaluates knowledge associated with the design, installation, maintenance, repair, fabrication, alteration, and extension of air-conditioning and refrigeration systems. Its scope also includes piping vessels, warm-air heating, boilers, ventilation systems, duct systems, insulation, and related components within complete systems.

The examination contains 95 questions, and candidates are allowed four hours to complete it. A minimum score of 70 percent is required to pass.

The examination outline includes General Knowledge, Plan Reading, Systems and Sizing, Piping, Equipment, and Maintenance. Candidates should balance their preparation across all six areas rather than concentrating only on refrigeration theory or code navigation.

General Knowledge includes 22 questions. Preparation should address refrigeration principles, heat transfer, temperature, pressure, humidity, airflow, electrical fundamentals, combustion, insulation, tools, safety practices, system operation, and common HVACR terminology.

Plan Reading includes eight questions. Candidates may need to interpret mechanical symbols, equipment schedules, duct layouts, piping routes, system notes, dimensions, equipment locations, load information, and installation details.

Systems and Sizing includes 17 questions. Study should include residential and commercial load calculations, equipment selection, airflow requirements, duct sizing, system capacity, design temperatures, ventilation loads, and building-performance factors.

Piping includes 14 questions. Candidates should review refrigerant piping, fuel-gas piping, approved materials, joining procedures, valves, regulators, supports, pressure considerations, sizing methods, testing, and protection from physical damage.

Equipment includes 18 questions. Preparation should cover compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, heat pumps, air handlers, furnaces, refrigeration systems, motors, controls, safety devices, and other air-conditioning components.

Maintenance includes 16 questions. Candidates should understand troubleshooting, refrigerant-system evaluation, electrical checks, airflow measurements, leak detection, equipment cleaning, control verification, startup procedures, testing, and preventive maintenance.

The four-hour testing period requires candidates to manage direct-answer questions, calculations, plan interpretation, reference searches, and final review. Timed practice with the books can help candidates develop a more consistent test-taking approach.

The International Fuel Gas Code supports questions involving gas-piping materials, sizing, appliance connections, regulators, shutoff valves, combustion air, venting, pressure testing, and installation safety.

Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning supports technical questions involving refrigeration cycles, refrigerants, compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, heat pumps, air-conditioning equipment, motors, controls, installation, maintenance, and troubleshooting.

### State Requirements

Delaware requires professionals providing regulated HVACR services to hold the appropriate professional license unless a specific legal exemption applies. Holding a license in another jurisdiction does not automatically authorize HVACR work in Delaware.

A Master HVACR Restricted license permits services only within the specialty shown on the license. Delaware restricted specialties include forced-air heating, ventilation and gas piping; hydronic heating and gas piping; commercial hood systems; refrigeration; air conditioning; and gas piping.

The restricted air-conditioning classification applies to work within the state-defined air-conditioning specialty. Professionals planning to provide services in more than one restricted specialty should review the unrestricted Delaware Master HVACR license.

Applicants should submit complete, accurate, and consistent documentation. Missing experience records, incomplete certification information, or conflicting application details may delay Board review and examination authorization.

The Delaware Board determines whether an applicant qualifies for examination and licensure. The examination provider administers the test but does not determine whether an applicant satisfies the state’s professional licensing requirements.

A Delaware Master HVACR Restricted Air Conditioning license does not automatically authorize plumbing work involving systems used to bring water into a building or remove liquid and water-carried waste. Separate plumbing authority may be required for that work.

After passing the examination, candidates must complete all remaining application requirements before the professional license can be issued. Applicants should monitor their DELPROS accounts and email for updates or requests for additional information.

Licensed HVACR professionals must comply with applicable renewal, continuing education, professional conduct, and other requirements associated with their licensing period.

A Delaware professional HVACR license is separate from a business license. Individuals planning to operate an air-conditioning contracting company may also need a legal business entity, business registration, insurance, tax accounts, and other operational approvals.
