### Exam Details

The Delaware Master HVACR Restricted Air Conditioning examination is part of the professional licensing process overseen by the Delaware Board of Plumbing, Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Examiners. Candidates applying by examination must receive Board approval before registering with the approved testing provider.

The examination contains 95 questions, and candidates are allowed four hours to complete it. Candidates should develop a pacing strategy that leaves sufficient time for direct-answer questions, calculations, plan interpretation, reference searches, and final review.

The examination outline includes General Knowledge, Plan Reading, Systems and Sizing, Piping, Equipment, and Maintenance. Each area requires candidates to combine field experience with technical understanding and efficient use of the reference materials.

General Knowledge includes 22 questions. Candidates should review heat transfer, temperature, pressure, humidity, airflow, refrigeration principles, combustion, electrical fundamentals, tools, safety practices, insulation, system operation, and common HVACR terminology.

Plan Reading includes eight questions. Candidates may need to interpret mechanical symbols, equipment schedules, duct layouts, piping routes, load information, plan notes, dimensions, equipment locations, and system details.

Systems and Sizing includes 17 questions. Preparation should include residential and commercial load calculations, equipment selection, airflow requirements, duct sizing, capacity, building characteristics, outdoor and indoor design conditions, and system-performance considerations.

Piping includes 14 questions. Candidates should review fuel-gas piping, refrigerant piping, approved materials, joining procedures, regulators, valves, supports, pressure considerations, sizing methods, testing, and protection from physical damage.

Equipment includes 18 questions. Study should address air-conditioning systems, refrigeration equipment, heat pumps, compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, air handlers, warm-air heating equipment, controls, motors, and safety devices.

Maintenance includes 16 questions. Candidates should understand inspection procedures, troubleshooting, system testing, refrigerant-side evaluation, electrical checks, airflow measurements, equipment cleaning, control verification, leak detection, and preventive maintenance.

### State Requirements

Delaware requires professionals providing regulated HVACR services to hold the appropriate license unless a specific exemption applies. A license from another jurisdiction does not automatically authorize HVACR work in Delaware.

A Master HVACR Restricted license permits services only in the specialty identified on the license. Delaware restricted specialties include forced-air heating, ventilation and gas piping; hydronic heating and gas piping; commercial hood systems; refrigeration; air conditioning; and gas piping.

The restricted air-conditioning classification applies to work within the air-conditioning specialty. Contractors planning to provide services across multiple restricted specialties should pursue the unrestricted Master HVACR license.

The examination description addresses air-conditioning and refrigeration systems, piping vessels, warm-air heating, boilers, ventilation systems, duct systems, insulation, and related components within complete systems, without a horsepower or tonnage limitation.

A Master HVACR Restricted license does not automatically provide authority for plumbing work involving systems used to bring water into a building or remove liquid and water-carried waste. Separate plumbing authority may be required for that work.

Applicants should submit complete, accurate, and consistent documentation. Missing experience records, incomplete license verification, or conflicting information may delay Board review and examination authorization.

The Delaware Board determines whether an applicant qualifies for examination and licensure. The testing provider administers the examination but does not decide whether an applicant satisfies the state’s professional licensing requirements.

After passing the examination, candidates must complete all remaining licensing requirements before the license can be issued. Applicants should monitor DELPROS and their email for updates or requests for additional information.

Delaware HVACR licenses are subject to renewal, continuing education, and professional conduct requirements. A professional HVACR license is separate from the business registrations, tax accounts, insurance, and local approvals required to operate a company.
