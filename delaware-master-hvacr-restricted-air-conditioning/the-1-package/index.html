Exam Details

The Delaware Master HVACR Restricted Air Conditioning examination is part of the professional licensing process overseen by the Delaware Board of Plumbing, Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Examiners. Candidates applying by examination must receive Board approval before registering with the approved testing provider.

The examination contains 95 questions, and candidates are allowed four hours to complete it. Candidates should develop a pacing strategy that leaves sufficient time for direct-answer questions, calculations, plan interpretation, reference searches, and final review.

The examination outline includes General Knowledge, Plan Reading, Systems and Sizing, Piping, Equipment, and Maintenance. Each area requires candidates to combine field experience with technical understanding and efficient use of the reference materials.

General Knowledge includes 22 questions. Candidates should review heat transfer, temperature, pressure, humidity, airflow, refrigeration principles, combustion, electrical fundamentals, tools, safety practices, insulation, system operation, and common HVACR terminology.

Plan Reading includes eight questions. Candidates may need to interpret mechanical symbols, equipment schedules, duct layouts, piping routes, load information, plan notes, dimensions, equipment locations, and system details.

Systems and Sizing includes 17 questions. Preparation should include residential and commercial load calculations, equipment selection, airflow requirements, duct sizing, capacity, building characteristics, outdoor and indoor design conditions, and system-performance considerations.

Piping includes 14 questions. Candidates should review fuel-gas piping, refrigerant piping, approved materials, joining procedures, regulators, valves, supports, pressure considerations, sizing methods, testing, and protection from physical damage.

Equipment includes 18 questions. Study should address air-conditioning systems, refrigeration equipment, heat pumps, compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, air handlers, warm-air heating equipment, controls, motors, and safety devices.

Maintenance includes 16 questions. Candidates should understand inspection procedures, troubleshooting, system testing, refrigerant-side evaluation, electrical checks, airflow measurements, equipment cleaning, control verification, leak detection, and preventive maintenance.

Open Book Test

The Delaware Master HVACR Restricted Air Conditioning examination is an open-book test. Candidates may use authorized printed reference materials during testing under the examination provider’s current rules.

An open-book format does not eliminate the need for focused preparation. The references contain extensive technical information, and candidates cannot depend on researching every question from the beginning. Strong preparation combines trade knowledge, calculation ability, and efficient reference navigation.

The highlighted and tabbed rental books in this package are prepared to support that process. Tabs provide recognizable locations for major chapters, code topics, formulas, equipment subjects, and calculation procedures. Highlighting helps selected information stand out during review and practice.

The International Mechanical Code supports questions involving mechanical equipment, ventilation, exhaust systems, duct construction, refrigeration systems, warm-air heating, boilers, combustion air, chimneys, vents, clearances, supports, and installation requirements.

The International Fuel Gas Code supports questions involving gas-piping materials, sizing, appliance connections, regulators, shutoff valves, combustion air, venting, pressure testing, and fuel-gas safety.

Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning supports technical preparation involving refrigeration cycles, compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, refrigerants, heat pumps, air-conditioning systems, heating equipment, motors, electrical controls, troubleshooting, installation, and maintenance.

Manual N supports commercial heating and cooling load calculations for small commercial buildings. Candidates should understand how occupancy, lighting, equipment, ventilation, construction, windows, orientation, and design conditions influence calculated loads.

Manual J supports residential heating and cooling load calculations. Preparation should include building construction, insulation, windows, doors, orientation, infiltration, climate, occupancy, and internal heat gains.

Highlighted passages should always be read in context. Definitions, exceptions, table notes, equipment conditions, and nearby provisions may affect the correct interpretation. Highlighting and tabs support navigation but do not replace understanding the complete material.

Candidates should review the current testing rules before the examination. Requirements may address permanent tabs, highlighting, handwritten notes, inserts, loose paper, removable markers, photocopies, calculators, and other test-day materials.

Licensing Steps

Begin by confirming that the Delaware Master HVACR Restricted Air Conditioning classification matches the services you intend to provide. A restricted license permits work only within the specialty listed on the license.

Create an account through Delaware Professional Regulation Online Services and begin the applicable Master HVACR Restricted application in DELPROS. Applications and supporting records are submitted through the state’s online licensing system.

Gather the documentation required for the licensing pathway. This may include employment verification, apprenticeship records, current or previous license verification, certification records, and other supporting information requested by the Board.

Applicants must document qualifying education, apprenticeship, and work experience based on the pathway under which they apply. The Board reviews the submitted records to determine whether the applicant qualifies for examination authorization.

Applicants without an acceptable journeyman certificate may be required to document qualifying supervised experience and complete the applicable Apprenticeship By-Pass Examination section. Restricted-license applicants are evaluated according to the specialty being pursued.

Submit the completed application and all required attachments through DELPROS. The Board must approve the candidate before the official Master HVACR Restricted Air Conditioning examination can be scheduled.

After approval, follow the testing provider’s registration instructions. Review appointment procedures, identification requirements, calculator policies, authorized references, and book-preparation rules before selecting a test date.

Use the one-year online course and highlighted and tabbed rental books to prepare. Complete technical review, plan-reading exercises, load calculations, code-navigation practice, piping study, equipment review, and maintenance questions.

Complete the examination and earn the required passing score. Official results are reported through the examination and licensing process.

Finish any remaining application requirements after passing. Application Service is included to help organize licensing information and supporting documents, but final eligibility and license issuance remain under the authority of the Delaware Board.

State Requirements

Delaware requires professionals providing regulated HVACR services to hold the appropriate license unless a specific exemption applies. A license from another jurisdiction does not automatically authorize HVACR work in Delaware.

A Master HVACR Restricted license permits services only in the specialty identified on the license. Delaware restricted specialties include forced-air heating, ventilation and gas piping; hydronic heating and gas piping; commercial hood systems; refrigeration; air conditioning; and gas piping.

The restricted air-conditioning classification applies to work within the air-conditioning specialty. Contractors planning to provide services across multiple restricted specialties should pursue the unrestricted Master HVACR license.

The examination description addresses air-conditioning and refrigeration systems, piping vessels, warm-air heating, boilers, ventilation systems, duct systems, insulation, and related components within complete systems, without a horsepower or tonnage limitation.

A Master HVACR Restricted license does not automatically provide authority for plumbing work involving systems used to bring water into a building or remove liquid and water-carried waste. Separate plumbing authority may be required for that work.

Applicants should submit complete, accurate, and consistent documentation. Missing experience records, incomplete license verification, or conflicting information may delay Board review and examination authorization.

The Delaware Board determines whether an applicant qualifies for examination and licensure. The testing provider administers the examination but does not decide whether an applicant satisfies the state’s professional licensing requirements.

After passing the examination, candidates must complete all remaining licensing requirements before the license can be issued. Applicants should monitor DELPROS and their email for updates or requests for additional information.

Delaware HVACR licenses are subject to renewal, continuing education, and professional conduct requirements. A professional HVACR license is separate from the business registrations, tax accounts, insurance, and local approvals required to operate a company.
