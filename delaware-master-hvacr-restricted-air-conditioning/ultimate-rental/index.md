### Exam Details

The Delaware Master HVACR Restricted Air Conditioning examination is part of the professional licensing process overseen by the Delaware Board of Plumbing, Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Examiners. Applicants pursuing licensure by examination must receive Board approval before scheduling the test through the approved examination provider.

The examination tests knowledge associated with the design, installation, maintenance, repair, fabrication, alteration, and extension of air-conditioning and refrigeration systems. The exam also addresses piping vessels, warm-air heating, boilers, ventilation systems, duct systems, insulation, and related components within complete systems.

The examination contains 95 questions, and candidates are allowed four hours to complete it. The timed format requires candidates to balance direct-answer questions, calculations, plan interpretation, technical research, and final review.

The examination outline includes General Knowledge, Plan Reading, Systems and Sizing, Piping, Equipment, and Maintenance. Each subject area requires candidates to connect field experience with technical information and code requirements.

General Knowledge includes 22 questions. Candidates should review refrigeration principles, heat transfer, temperature, pressure, humidity, airflow, electrical fundamentals, combustion, safety practices, tools, insulation, system operation, and common HVACR terminology.

Plan Reading includes eight questions. Candidates may need to interpret mechanical symbols, equipment schedules, duct layouts, piping routes, system notes, dimensions, equipment locations, load information, and installation details.

Systems and Sizing includes 17 questions. Preparation should address residential and commercial load calculations, equipment selection, system capacity, airflow requirements, duct sizing, indoor and outdoor design conditions, ventilation loads, and building-performance factors.

Piping includes 14 questions. Candidates should review refrigerant piping, fuel-gas piping, approved materials, joining procedures, supports, valves, regulators, sizing methods, pressure considerations, testing, and protection from damage.

Equipment includes 18 questions. Study should address compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, heat pumps, air handlers, furnaces, refrigeration equipment, motors, controls, safety devices, and related air-conditioning components.

Maintenance includes 16 questions. Candidates should understand testing, troubleshooting, refrigerant-system evaluation, electrical checks, airflow measurements, leak detection, equipment cleaning, control verification, startup procedures, and preventive maintenance.

### State Requirements

Delaware requires professionals providing regulated HVACR services to hold the appropriate state license unless a specific exemption applies. Holding a license in another jurisdiction does not automatically authorize HVACR work in Delaware.

A Master HVACR Restricted license permits services only in the specialty identified on the license. Delaware restricted specialties include forced-air heating, ventilation and gas piping; hydronic heating and gas piping; commercial hood systems; refrigeration; air conditioning; and gas piping.

The restricted air-conditioning classification applies to air-conditioning work within the state-defined specialty. Professionals planning to provide services across multiple restricted specialties should pursue the unrestricted Master HVACR license.

The examination scope addresses air-conditioning and refrigeration systems, piping vessels, warm-air heating, boilers, ventilation systems, duct systems, insulation, and related components within complete systems.

Applicants must provide complete, accurate, and consistent documentation. Missing experience records, incomplete certification information, or conflicting application details may delay Board review and examination authorization.

Employment experience is generally verified by a supervisor. When standard verification cannot be obtained, alternative documentation may be submitted as allowed by the state licensing process. Acceptance remains subject to Board review.

A Master HVACR Restricted Air Conditioning license does not automatically provide authority for plumbing work involving systems used to bring water into a building or remove liquid and water-carried waste. Separate plumbing authority may be required.

The Delaware Board determines whether an applicant qualifies for examination and licensure. The testing provider administers the examination but does not decide whether an applicant satisfies Delaware’s professional licensing requirements.

After passing the examination, candidates must complete all remaining requirements before the license can be issued. Applicants should monitor DELPROS and their email for updates or requests for additional information.

Delaware HVACR licenses are subject to renewal, continuing education, and professional conduct requirements. A professional HVACR license is separate from any business registrations, insurance, tax accounts, or local approvals required to operate a company.
