### Exam Details

The Delaware Master HVACR Restricted Air Conditioning examination is part of the professional licensing process overseen by the Delaware Board of Plumbing, Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Examiners. Applicants pursuing licensure by examination must receive Board approval before scheduling the test with the approved examination provider.

The examination tests knowledge associated with the design, installation, maintenance, repair, fabrication, alteration, and extension of air-conditioning and refrigeration systems. Its scope also includes piping vessels, warm-air heating, boilers, ventilation systems, duct systems, insulation, and related components within complete systems.

The examination contains 95 questions, and candidates are allowed four hours to complete it. The passing score is 70 percent. Candidates should develop a pacing strategy that allows time for direct-answer questions, calculations, plan interpretation, reference searches, and final review.

The examination outline includes General Knowledge, Plan Reading, Systems and Sizing, Piping, Equipment, and Maintenance. Candidates must combine practical trade experience with technical knowledge, calculation ability, and efficient use of the approved references.

General Knowledge includes 22 questions. Preparation should address refrigeration principles, heat transfer, temperature, pressure, humidity, airflow, electrical fundamentals, combustion, safety practices, tools, insulation, system operation, and commonly used HVACR terminology.

Plan Reading includes eight questions. Candidates may need to interpret mechanical symbols, equipment schedules, duct layouts, piping routes, load information, dimensions, system notes, equipment locations, and installation details.

Systems and Sizing includes 17 questions. Study should include residential and commercial load calculations, equipment selection, airflow requirements, system capacity, duct sizing, indoor and outdoor design conditions, building characteristics, ventilation loads, and performance considerations.

Piping includes 14 questions. Candidates should review refrigerant piping, fuel-gas piping, approved materials, joining procedures, supports, valves, regulators, sizing methods, pressure considerations, testing, and protection from physical damage.

Equipment includes 18 questions. Preparation should cover compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, heat pumps, air handlers, furnaces, refrigeration equipment, motors, controls, safety devices, and other air-conditioning components.

Maintenance includes 16 questions. Candidates should understand system testing, troubleshooting, refrigerant-side evaluation, electrical checks, airflow measurements, leak detection, equipment cleaning, control verification, startup procedures, and preventive maintenance.

An open-book format does not remove the need for focused preparation. The International Mechanical Code should be used for questions involving mechanical equipment, ventilation, exhaust, duct construction, refrigeration systems, warm-air heating, boilers, combustion air, chimneys, vents, equipment clearances, supports, and installation requirements.

The International Fuel Gas Code supports questions involving gas-piping materials, pipe sizing, regulators, shutoff valves, appliance connections, combustion air, venting, pressure testing, and fuel-gas installation safety.

Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning supports technical questions involving refrigeration cycles, refrigerants, compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, heat pumps, air-conditioning equipment, heating systems, motors, electrical controls, troubleshooting, installation, and maintenance.

Manual N supports heating and cooling load calculations for small commercial buildings. Candidates should understand how building construction, occupancy, lighting, equipment, ventilation, infiltration, windows, orientation, and design conditions affect calculated loads.

Manual J supports residential heating and cooling load calculations. Study should include insulation, windows, doors, building orientation, infiltration, climate, occupancy, internal heat gains, and other factors that influence system capacity.

The course encourages candidates to practice choosing the correct reference before beginning a search. Residential load questions may require Manual J, while small commercial load questions may require Manual N. Learning these distinctions can reduce unnecessary searching during timed testing.

Candidates should read references in context. Definitions, exceptions, table notes, equipment conditions, and related provisions can affect the answer. Selecting a response from one isolated sentence without reviewing the surrounding material may lead to an incorrect interpretation.

### Licensing Steps

Begin by confirming that the Delaware Master HVACR Restricted Air Conditioning classification matches the work you intend to perform. A restricted license permits services only within the specialty identified on the license.

Create an account through Delaware Professional Regulation Online Services and begin the applicable Master HVACR Restricted application in DELPROS. Delaware professional licensing applications and supporting records are submitted through the state’s online system.

Gather the documentation required for the licensing pathway. Supporting records may include professional license verification, apprenticeship documents, employment records, certification information, and other materials requested by the Delaware Board.

Applicants for the restricted air-conditioning classification must provide a copy of their CFC Certification card. Applicants must also document the qualifying education, apprenticeship, and work experience required for their application pathway.

An applicant with an acceptable journeyman certificate must provide the apprenticeship program Certificate of Completion and proof of at least two years of qualifying work experience under the supervision of a master licensee after receiving the certificate.

An applicant without an acceptable journeyman certificate must provide proof of at least seven years of qualifying work experience under the supervision of a master licensee. The Board determines whether the applicant must also complete the applicable Apprenticeship By-Pass Examination.

Submit the completed application and all required attachments through DELPROS. The Board must review and approve the application before the candidate can schedule the Master HVACR Restricted Air Conditioning examination.

After receiving approval, follow the examination provider’s registration and scheduling instructions. Review appointment procedures, identification requirements, calculator policies, approved references, and book-preparation rules before selecting a test date.

Use the online course and listed references to prepare. Complete technical review, plan-reading exercises, residential and commercial load calculations, code-navigation practice, piping study, equipment review, and maintenance questions.

Complete the examination and earn a score of at least 70 percent. Official examination results are reported through the testing and licensing process.

Finish any remaining licensing requirements after passing. Passing the examination is one part of the process and does not automatically issue a license when documentation or other application requirements remain outstanding.

### State Requirements

Delaware requires professionals providing regulated HVACR services to hold the appropriate state license unless a specific legal exemption applies. Holding a license in another jurisdiction does not automatically authorize HVACR work in Delaware.

A Master HVACR Restricted license permits services only within the specialty shown on the license. Delaware restricted specialties include forced-air heating, ventilation and gas piping; hydronic heating and gas piping; commercial hood systems; refrigeration; air conditioning; and gas piping.

The restricted air-conditioning classification applies to work within Delaware’s air-conditioning specialty. Professionals planning to perform work across multiple restricted specialties should review the unrestricted Master HVACR classification.

The restricted air-conditioning examination addresses air-conditioning and refrigeration systems, piping vessels, warm-air heating, boilers, ventilation systems, duct systems, insulation, and related components within complete systems.

Applicants must provide complete, accurate, and consistent supporting documentation. Missing employment records, incomplete certification information, or conflicting application details may delay Board review and examination authorization.

Employment experience is generally verified by a supervisor. When standard supervisor verification cannot be obtained, alternative documentation may be submitted as permitted by the licensing process. Acceptance of the documentation remains subject to Board review.

A Master HVACR Restricted Air Conditioning license does not automatically authorize plumbing work involving systems used to bring water into a building or remove liquid and water-carried waste. Separate plumbing authority may be required for that work.

The Delaware Board determines whether an applicant qualifies for examination and licensure. The examination provider administers the test but does not decide whether an applicant satisfies Delaware’s professional licensing requirements.

Applicants who do not pass after three examination attempts must reapply and obtain Board approval before testing again. Applicable waiting requirements must also be satisfied.

Delaware Plumbing and HVACR licenses expire on October 31 of even-numbered years. Licensees must complete the renewal process and satisfy the continuing education requirements associated with their licensing period.

A Delaware professional HVACR license is separate from a business license. Individuals planning to operate an air-conditioning contracting company may also need a legal business entity, business registration, tax accounts, insurance, and other operational approvals.
