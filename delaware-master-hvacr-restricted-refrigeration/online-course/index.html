Exam Details

The Delaware Master HVACR Restricted – Refrigeration examination evaluates the knowledge required to install, maintain, repair, and alter refrigeration systems and related equipment within the scope of the restricted specialty. Delaware defines this specialty around qualifying refrigeration systems used to cool surfaces or areas below 55 degrees Fahrenheit, or 12.9 degrees Celsius, for food and product preservation.

Preparation should cover the technical principles and practical procedures used in commercial refrigeration work. Important study areas include:

  • Refrigeration principles and the refrigeration cycle
  • Heat transfer, pressure, temperature, and refrigerant conditions
  • Compressors, condensers, evaporators, and metering devices
  • Commercial refrigeration equipment
  • Refrigeration piping and line functions
  • Motors, relays, contactors, thermostats, and pressure controls
  • Mechanical-code requirements
  • Equipment access, ventilation, and machinery-room provisions
  • System charging and operating conditions
  • Maintenance and preventive service
  • Testing and inspection
  • Troubleshooting and system diagnosis
  • Refrigeration safety and equipment protection

The refrigeration cycle is a central part of the examination. Candidates should understand how refrigerant changes pressure, temperature, and physical state as it travels through the compressor, condenser, metering device, and evaporator. Each component has a specific function, and a problem in one part of the system can affect every other part.

The compressor moves refrigerant through the system and raises the pressure of the refrigerant vapor. The condenser rejects heat and changes the refrigerant from vapor toward a liquid condition. The metering device controls refrigerant flow and creates the pressure reduction needed before the evaporator. The evaporator absorbs heat from the refrigerated space or product area.

Equipment questions may involve compressors, air-cooled and water-cooled condensers, evaporators, receivers, accumulators, expansion valves, capillary tubes, solenoid valves, pressure controls, thermostats, fans, motors, relays, contactors, and safety devices. Candidates should understand each component’s purpose and position within the refrigeration system.

Piping questions may address suction, discharge, and liquid lines. Preparation should include line functions, sizing concepts, supports, insulation, oil return, joining practices, valves, service access, pressure conditions, and system protection. Improper piping can contribute to capacity loss, poor oil return, compressor damage, noise, vibration, or unreliable operation.

Mechanical-code questions may involve refrigeration-system installation, equipment rooms, ventilation, access, clearances, system protection, mechanical appliances, piping, and related safety provisions. Candidates should practice using the code index, definitions, chapter headings, and cross-references.

Maintenance and troubleshooting questions may involve temperature and pressure readings, refrigerant flow, airflow, dirty heat-transfer surfaces, improper charge, electrical controls, restrictions, failed components, and abnormal operating conditions. A strong troubleshooting method evaluates the complete system instead of assuming that one symptom has only one possible cause.

Open Book Test

The Delaware Master HVACR Restricted Refrigeration Contractor examination is an open-book, timed test. Candidates may use authorized references during the examination, subject to the testing provider’s current rules regarding approved titles, editions, highlighting, permanent tabs, notes, inserts, calculators, and other materials.

An open-book examination still requires detailed preparation. Candidates must identify the subject of a question, select the correct reference, locate the applicable material, and interpret it accurately. Searching unfamiliar books page by page can consume valuable time and reduce the time available for technical analysis.

The International Mechanical Code, 2018 is the primary code reference for mechanical installation and refrigeration-system requirements. Candidates should become familiar with the code’s definitions, refrigeration provisions, equipment requirements, machinery-room rules, ventilation provisions, and referenced sections.

Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning, 22nd Edition supports technical questions involving refrigeration theory, equipment, commercial systems, piping, motors, controls, maintenance, troubleshooting, and service procedures. The book can help connect code requirements with the operating principles behind refrigeration systems.

Develop a consistent search process during study. Begin by identifying the main subject and key terms in the question. Select the book most likely to contain the answer, use the index or table of contents, and read the complete applicable section.

Do not stop at the first sentence that appears relevant. Review surrounding definitions, diagrams, notes, exceptions, and related sections. Technical questions often include a condition that changes which requirement, component, or service procedure applies.

Practice reference navigation under timed conditions. Choose a topic, locate the applicable material, and record how long the search takes. Revisit subjects that consistently require long searches so their locations become more familiar.

Before the examination, review the current candidate instructions issued for your appointment. Only books and materials authorized under the current testing rules should be taken into the examination.

Licensing Steps

The Delaware Board of Plumbing, Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Examiners regulates Master HVACR and Master HVACR Restricted licenses. Applicants pursuing the restricted Refrigeration classification must complete the state’s licensing process and receive approval before taking the master examination.

  1. Select the correct classification. Apply for the Delaware Master HVACR Restricted license and choose Refrigeration as the specialty.
  2. Create or access a DELPROS account. Delaware professional licensing applications are submitted through the Delaware Professional Regulation Online Services system.
  3. Complete the application. Provide the requested personal, apprenticeship, employment, experience, certification, examination, and licensing information.
  4. Gather supporting documentation. Required records may include apprenticeship credentials, a journeyperson certificate, employment verification, supervised experience documentation, refrigerant-handling certification, and license verification from other jurisdictions.
  5. Submit documents as directed. Certain records may need to be completed or submitted by an employer, supervisor, apprenticeship program, certification organization, school, or licensing authority.
  6. Complete any applicable equivalency requirement. Applicants without a qualifying apprenticeship credential may need to complete the apprenticeship equivalency or bypass examination section relevant to the Refrigeration specialty.
  7. Receive Board approval to test. The Delaware Board reviews the application and supporting documentation before authorizing the applicant to take the master examination.
  8. Register with the testing provider. Follow the examination registration and scheduling instructions received after approval.
  9. Take the examination. Follow all current requirements involving identification, authorized references, calculators, appointment procedures, and testing conduct.
  10. Complete remaining licensing requirements. The Board must receive the required examination result and all supporting documentation before issuing the professional license.

Applicants should begin collecting official records early. Apprenticeship documentation, employment verification, refrigerant certification records, and out-of-state license verification may require action by third parties. Missing documentation can delay application review and examination authorization.

This online exam prep course does not include the Delaware licensing application, Application Service, approval to test, examination registration, government fees, or professional license issuance.

State Requirements

Delaware offers an unrestricted Master HVACR license and several Master HVACR Restricted specialties. Refrigeration is one of the restricted classifications recognized by the state.

A Master HVACR Restricted – Refrigeration license authorizes the holder to provide services within the Refrigeration specialty. The restricted license does not provide the complete scope of authority associated with an unrestricted Master HVACR license.

Professionals who plan to offer services across several restricted HVACR specialties must pursue the license classification appropriate for their full scope of work. License holders are responsible for remaining within the authority granted by the Delaware credential.

One licensing pathway applies to applicants who earned a journeyperson certificate after completing a qualifying HVACR or restricted HVACR apprenticeship program. These applicants must document the required post-apprenticeship HVACR or restricted HVACR experience under qualified supervision.

Applicants without the qualifying journeyperson certificate must document the required period of HVACR or restricted HVACR experience under the supervision of a Master HVACR licensee, Master HVACR Restricted licensee, or an individual holding a similar level of licensure in another state. They must also complete the applicable apprenticeship equivalency requirement.

Experience records should demonstrate work relevant to refrigeration systems. Applicable experience may include commercial refrigeration installation, system piping, compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, electrical controls, equipment maintenance, testing, troubleshooting, repair, and alteration.

Delaware applicants pursuing Refrigeration must provide the appropriate certification for handling regulated refrigerants. The Board reviews the applicant’s experience, training, certification, examination results, and supporting documentation when determining eligibility for licensure.

A Delaware professional license is separate from a Delaware business license. Professionals planning to operate a refrigeration contracting business may also need entity registration, tax accounts, insurance, permits, local approvals, and other operating credentials.

Test Information and Study Materials

Create a study schedule that divides refrigeration theory, equipment, piping, mechanical requirements, controls, maintenance, testing, inspection, and troubleshooting into manageable sessions. Give additional time to topics that require longer reference searches or detailed technical analysis.

Begin with the basic refrigeration cycle. Review the pressure, temperature, physical state, and purpose of the refrigerant as it travels through the compressor, condenser, metering device, and evaporator. Understanding these relationships provides a foundation for equipment, piping, and troubleshooting questions.

Study major components individually and as part of the complete system. Review compressors, condensers, evaporators, receivers, accumulators, expansion valves, capillary tubes, solenoid valves, pressure controls, thermostats, motors, relays, contactors, and safety devices.

For piping preparation, review the purpose of suction, discharge, and liquid lines. Study sizing concepts, supports, insulation, fittings, oil return, valves, pressure conditions, and common installation concerns.

For mechanical-code preparation, practice using the index and definitions. Locate provisions involving refrigeration systems, machinery rooms, equipment access, ventilation, installation, and protection. Read the complete section and review related exceptions or referenced provisions.

For maintenance and troubleshooting, connect operating symptoms with possible causes. Review pressure, temperature, airflow, refrigerant flow, electrical controls, dirty components, restrictions, improper charging, and mechanical failures.

Practice evaluating the complete system. Reduced refrigeration capacity may result from airflow problems, incorrect refrigerant charge, dirty heat-transfer surfaces, restrictions, faulty controls, equipment wear, or changing load conditions. Avoid choosing an answer based on one symptom without considering the other information provided.

Complete timed practice sessions as the examination approaches. Answer familiar questions first, mark longer searches or diagnostic problems, and return to them later. Review missed answers by finding the supporting material in the appropriate reference.
