State Requirements

The Delaware Master HVACR Restricted Refrigeration license is administered through the Delaware Board of Plumbing, Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Examiners.

Delaware defines a Master HVACR Restricted license as a license that allows an individual to provide HVACR services only within the specialty identified on the license. Refrigeration is one of the recognized restricted classifications.

Delaware law defines a refrigeration system as a system used to cool a surface or area below 55 degrees Fahrenheit or 12.9 degrees Celsius. The current examination bulletin further identifies the restricted refrigeration scope as systems used for food and product preservation, unlimited in tonnage or horsepower.

Applicants seeking licensure by examination must submit a complete application and satisfy the applicable experience requirements before receiving permission to take the Board-approved examination.

Applicants with a Journeyman's Certificate from an apprenticeship program meeting applicable federal standards must provide the certificate and document qualifying post-apprenticeship HVACR experience under the direct supervision of an appropriately licensed master-level professional.

Applicants without an accepted Journeyman's Certificate are subject to a different experience pathway. Delaware's current regulations also allow restricted-license applicants to complete only the relevant portion of the apprenticeship bypass examination for the restriction they are seeking.

The Board must approve the applicant before the licensure examination can be taken. After approval, ProV provides examination scheduling information and the current Candidate Information Bulletin.

Delaware requires a minimum examination score of 70%. Applicants who fail the applicable examination three times are subject to Delaware's reapplication rules before they may receive new approval to test.

Passing the examination alone does not automatically issue the license. The Board must receive the official score and confirm that the applicant has satisfied all remaining experience, application, and licensing requirements.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Delaware Master HVACR Restricted Refrigeration study plan should begin with General Knowledge. With 24 questions, this category makes up the largest individual portion of the current examination.

Refrigeration theory should be a major focus. Candidates should understand how heat moves through a refrigeration system and how the compressor, condenser, metering device, and evaporator work together to transfer heat.

Review pressure-temperature relationships carefully. Refrigeration work frequently requires an understanding of how refrigerant saturation temperature changes with pressure and how this relationship affects diagnosis and system performance.

Superheat and subcooling are also important technical concepts. Candidates should understand what each measurement represents within the refrigeration cycle and why those measurements can provide information about system operation.

Study the function of compressors, including their role in moving refrigerant and creating the pressure relationships needed for the refrigeration cycle. Review common operating symptoms that may indicate compressor or system problems.

Condensers reject heat from the refrigerant. Candidates should understand how airflow or water flow affects condenser performance and how improper heat rejection can change system pressures and operating conditions.

Evaporators absorb heat from the conditioned or refrigerated space. Review airflow, coil condition, refrigerant distribution, frost, defrost, temperature relationships, and the effect of restricted airflow on system performance.

Metering devices regulate refrigerant flow into the evaporator. Candidates should understand their purpose and how refrigerant flow influences evaporator operation and system balance.

Piping accounts for 19 questions. Candidates should understand suction lines, discharge lines, liquid lines, piping materials, insulation, supports, routing, refrigerant velocity, oil return, pressure drop, and appropriate sizing concepts.

Refrigerant piping should not be studied only as a dimensional issue. Pipe size and configuration can influence pressure loss, refrigerant velocity, compressor oil return, system capacity, and overall reliability.

Systems & Sizing accounts for 20 questions. Candidates should understand how refrigeration load, equipment capacity, system design, piping, evaporator conditions, condenser conditions, and component selection affect the complete system.

Commercial refrigeration preparation should include an understanding of how product load, infiltration, ambient conditions, equipment operation, defrost, and other factors can influence refrigeration requirements.

The International Mechanical Code, 2018 should be used actively while practicing. Become familiar with its refrigeration-related provisions instead of depending on random searches during testing.

Code-based preparation can include refrigerant system requirements, machinery-room provisions, ventilation, system safety, equipment location, refrigerant piping, and related mechanical requirements.

Maintenance accounts for 6 questions. Study preventive maintenance, coil cleaning, airflow, refrigerant-system evaluation, controls, electrical components, condensers, evaporators, compressors, and recognition of abnormal operating conditions.

Maintenance questions often require candidates to think through cause and effect. A dirty condenser, restricted evaporator airflow, failed fan, incorrect control operation, or piping problem can change several system conditions at once.

Troubleshooting should focus on understanding the refrigeration cycle rather than memorizing a single symptom for each possible failure. Candidates should look at pressures, temperatures, airflow, refrigerant flow, equipment operation, and control conditions together.

Testing and Inspection accounts for 6 questions. Candidates should prepare for questions involving system evaluation, leak-related procedures, inspections, safety checks, operating verification, and recognition of improper installation.

Testing should be viewed as part of confirming that the refrigeration system is safe, properly installed, and operating as intended. A system that runs is not necessarily a system that has been correctly commissioned or evaluated.

The Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning, 22nd Edition associated with this product can be used to strengthen the technical foundation behind practice questions. When a question exposes weakness in refrigeration theory, return to the relevant concept before attempting more questions on the same topic.

For code questions, first determine whether the question is asking about an equipment requirement, installation condition, refrigerant-system rule, machinery-room requirement, ventilation provision, or piping requirement. Then locate the appropriate area of the Mechanical Code.

For troubleshooting questions, write down the known symptoms and consider which component or system condition could logically produce them. Avoid jumping directly to an answer based on a single symptom when the question provides several operating conditions.

For piping questions, visualize the refrigerant path through the complete system. Follow discharge gas from the compressor to the condenser, liquid refrigerant to the metering device, refrigerant through the evaporator, and suction vapor back to the compressor.

When a practice question is missed, classify the reason. The issue may involve refrigeration theory, pressure-temperature relationships, piping, system sizing, mechanical code navigation, maintenance, or testing.

Organizing missed questions into categories makes repeated weaknesses easier to identify. If piping is consistently difficult, concentrate additional study there. If theory questions are weaker, return to the refrigeration cycle, heat transfer, and component functions.

Use the included 3 months access to repeat this process across multiple study cycles. Begin with individual categories, revisit missed questions, and then move into mixed-topic practice that requires switching among theory, piping, sizing, maintenance, testing, and code requirements.
