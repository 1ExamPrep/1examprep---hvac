Exam Details

The Delaware Master HVACR Restricted – Refrigeration examination evaluates the knowledge associated with installing, maintaining, repairing, and altering refrigeration systems and related equipment. The restricted Refrigeration classification applies to systems used to cool a surface or area below 55 degrees Fahrenheit or 12.9 degrees Celsius for food and product preservation, without a tonnage limitation.

Preparation should include refrigeration theory, mechanical code requirements, system components, equipment installation, controls, piping, maintenance, service, safety, and troubleshooting. Candidates may need to apply practical field knowledge while also locating supporting information in the authorized references.

Important preparation areas include:

  • Basic refrigeration principles and the refrigeration cycle
  • Heat transfer, temperature, pressure, and system relationships
  • Compressors, condensers, evaporators, and metering devices
  • Refrigeration piping and system components
  • Mechanical equipment installation requirements
  • Equipment access, clearances, and service considerations
  • Motors, electrical controls, and safety devices
  • System charging and operating conditions
  • Commercial refrigeration equipment
  • Refrigerated spaces and product-preservation systems
  • System inspection and preventive maintenance
  • Troubleshooting and service procedures
  • Mechanical ventilation and equipment-room considerations
  • Workplace and refrigeration safety

Candidates should understand the refrigeration cycle and the function of the principal components. A refrigeration system depends on the relationship between the compressor, condenser, metering device, evaporator, refrigerant, piping, controls, and heat-transfer conditions. A change in one component or operating condition can affect the performance of the complete system.

Equipment questions may involve compressors, condensers, evaporators, receivers, accumulators, metering devices, valves, fans, motors, controls, and safety devices. Candidates should understand the purpose of each component, its position in the system, and the symptoms that may appear when it fails or operates incorrectly.

Mechanical-code preparation may address equipment installation, machinery rooms, ventilation, access, clearances, ducts, exhaust, appliances, system protection, and other requirements relevant to refrigeration work. Candidates should practice using code definitions and chapter organization rather than relying only on field terminology.

Maintenance and troubleshooting questions may involve temperature and pressure readings, airflow, refrigerant flow, dirty components, failed controls, restricted lines, improper charging, compressor concerns, and system operating symptoms. A strong preparation plan should include both system theory and practical diagnostic reasoning.

Licensing Steps

The Delaware Board of Plumbing, Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Examiners regulates Master HVACR and Master HVACR Restricted licenses. Applicants pursuing the restricted Refrigeration classification must complete the state licensing process and receive approval before taking the master examination.

  1. Select the correct classification. Apply for the Delaware Master HVACR Restricted license and choose Refrigeration as the specialty.
  2. Create or access a DELPROS account. Delaware professional licensing applications are submitted through the Delaware Professional Regulation Online Services system.
  3. Complete the application. Provide the requested personal, apprenticeship, employment, experience, examination, and licensing information.
  4. Gather supporting documentation. Required records may include apprenticeship credentials, a journeyperson certificate, employment verification, supervised experience documentation, and verification of licenses held in other jurisdictions.
  5. Submit records as directed. Certain documents may need to be completed or submitted by an employer, supervisor, apprenticeship program, school, or licensing authority.
  6. Complete any applicable equivalency requirement. Applicants without the qualifying apprenticeship credential may need to satisfy the apprenticeship bypass or equivalency requirement associated with the Refrigeration specialty.
  7. Receive Board approval to test. The Board reviews the completed application and supporting records before authorizing the applicant to take the master examination.
  8. Register with the testing provider. Follow the examination registration and scheduling instructions supplied after approval.
  9. Take the examination. Follow the current requirements for identification, authorized references, calculators, appointment procedures, and testing conduct.
  10. Complete remaining licensing requirements. The Board must receive the examination result and all required records before issuing the professional license.
  11. Establish the contracting business. Form the LLC or corporation, obtain the EIN, and complete the registrations and operational steps that apply to the company.

State Requirements

Delaware offers an unrestricted Master HVACR license and several Master HVACR Restricted specialties. Refrigeration is one of the restricted classifications recognized by the state.

A Delaware Master HVACR Restricted – Refrigeration license authorizes services within the Refrigeration specialty. A restricted license does not provide the full scope associated with an unrestricted Master HVACR license. Professionals who intend to offer services across more than one restricted HVACR specialty must pursue the appropriate broader classification.

The qualification pathway depends on the applicant’s apprenticeship training, journeyperson credential, supervised experience, examination requirements, and supporting records. Applicants using a qualifying apprenticeship pathway must provide the applicable credential and document the required experience under qualified supervision.

Applicants without the qualifying apprenticeship credential may need to document a longer period of supervised HVACR or restricted HVACR experience and complete the applicable apprenticeship bypass or equivalency requirement.

Experience records should demonstrate work relevant to refrigeration systems. Applicable experience may include installation, maintenance, repair, alteration, refrigeration piping, commercial equipment, compressors, condensers, evaporators, controls, system testing, troubleshooting, or related services performed under appropriate supervision.

The Delaware Board determines whether an applicant’s training, experience, examinations, and supporting documentation satisfy the licensing requirements. The Application Service included in this package supports organization of the process but does not control Board decisions or replace any qualification.

A Delaware professional license is separate from a Delaware business license. A refrigeration contractor planning to operate a company may also need entity registration, tax accounts, insurance, local approvals, permits, and other operating credentials.

Test Information and Study Materials

Begin preparation with a study schedule that divides refrigeration theory, mechanical requirements, equipment, controls, piping, installation, maintenance, troubleshooting, safety, and business subjects into manageable study sessions.

Start with the basic refrigeration cycle. Review the condition and purpose of the refrigerant as it moves through the compressor, condenser, metering device, and evaporator. Understand how pressure and temperature change throughout the system and how those changes make heat transfer possible.

Study major refrigeration components individually and as parts of a complete system. Review compressors, condensers, evaporators, receivers, accumulators, expansion devices, solenoid valves, pressure controls, thermostats, motors, relays, and safety devices.

For mechanical-code preparation, practice using the index and definitions. Locate requirements involving equipment installation, refrigeration systems, machinery rooms, ventilation, access, protection, and other applicable mechanical provisions. Read the entire section and check related exceptions or referenced sections.

For maintenance and troubleshooting, connect operating symptoms with possible causes. Review temperature, pressure, airflow, refrigerant flow, electrical controls, dirty components, restrictions, improper charging, and mechanical failures. Avoid relying on one symptom without evaluating the complete system.

Use timed study sessions as the exam approaches. Answer familiar questions first, mark longer reference searches or diagnostic problems, and return to them later. Review incorrect answers by locating the supporting information in the applicable reference.
