### Exam Details

The Delaware Master HVACR Restricted – Refrigeration classification authorizes refrigeration services within the scope established by Delaware. The examination evaluates the knowledge needed to install, maintain, repair, alter, and service refrigeration systems and related equipment used for qualifying food and product-preservation applications.

Preparation should include refrigeration theory, equipment operation, refrigerant piping, mechanical-code requirements, electrical and mechanical controls, maintenance, inspection, system testing, safety, and troubleshooting. Candidates should be prepared to interpret technical questions and determine how system conditions affect equipment performance.

Important areas of study include:

- Refrigeration principles and the basic refrigeration cycle
- Heat transfer, pressure, temperature, and refrigerant conditions
- Compressors, condensers, evaporators, and metering devices
- Commercial refrigeration systems and equipment
- Refrigeration piping and line functions
- Motors, relays, contactors, thermostats, and pressure controls
- Mechanical-code requirements for refrigeration installations
- Machinery rooms, ventilation, equipment access, and protection
- System charging and operating conditions
- Preventive maintenance and equipment inspection
- Testing procedures and diagnostic measurements
- Troubleshooting and refrigeration service practices
- System safety and protective devices

The refrigeration cycle is a central subject. Candidates should understand how refrigerant changes pressure, temperature, and physical state as it moves through the compressor, condenser, metering device, and evaporator. Each component performs a specific function, and a problem in one part of the system can affect performance throughout the entire refrigeration circuit.

The compressor moves refrigerant and raises the pressure of the refrigerant vapor. The condenser rejects heat and changes the condition of the refrigerant. The metering device controls refrigerant flow and creates a pressure reduction before the evaporator. The evaporator absorbs heat from the refrigerated space or product area.

Equipment questions may involve compressors, condensers, evaporators, receivers, accumulators, expansion valves, capillary tubes, solenoid valves, pressure controls, thermostats, motors, relays, contactors, and system safety devices. Candidates should understand each component’s purpose, location, and relationship to system operation.

Piping questions may address suction, discharge, and liquid lines. Preparation should include line functions, sizing concepts, joining methods, supports, insulation, oil return, valves, service access, pressure conditions, and system protection. Improper piping can contribute to pressure loss, poor oil return, reduced capacity, vibration, compressor damage, or unreliable system operation.

Maintenance and troubleshooting questions may involve pressure and temperature readings, airflow, refrigerant flow, electrical controls, dirty heat-transfer surfaces, restrictions, improper charging, failed motors, defective controls, and abnormal operating conditions. Candidates should evaluate the complete system rather than assuming that a single symptom has only one possible cause.

### State Requirements

Delaware offers an unrestricted Master HVACR license and several Master HVACR Restricted specialties. Refrigeration is one of the restricted classifications recognized by the state.

A Delaware Master HVACR Restricted – Refrigeration license authorizes the holder to provide services within the Refrigeration specialty. It does not provide the complete scope associated with an unrestricted Master HVACR license.

Professionals who intend to offer services across multiple restricted HVACR specialties must pursue the license classification appropriate for their full scope of work. License holders are responsible for remaining within the authority granted by their Delaware credential.

The applicable qualification pathway depends on the applicant’s apprenticeship training, journeyperson credential, supervised experience, certification, examination requirements, and supporting documentation.

Applicants with a qualifying journeyperson certificate earned after completing an accepted HVACR or restricted HVACR apprenticeship must document the applicable post-apprenticeship experience under qualified supervision.

Applicants without a qualifying journeyperson certificate must document the required period of HVACR or restricted HVACR experience under the direct supervision of a properly licensed master professional. These applicants may also need to satisfy the applicable apprenticeship equivalency or bypass examination requirement.

Experience records should demonstrate work relevant to refrigeration systems. Applicable experience may include commercial refrigeration installation, refrigeration piping, compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, controls, system maintenance, testing, troubleshooting, repair, or alteration.

Applicants must hold the appropriate certification for handling regulated refrigerants when required for the services performed. The Delaware Board determines whether an applicant’s experience, training, certification, examination results, and supporting records satisfy the licensing requirements.

A Delaware professional HVACR license is separate from a Delaware business license. A contractor planning to operate a refrigeration business may also need entity registration, tax accounts, insurance, permits, local approvals, and other operating credentials.
