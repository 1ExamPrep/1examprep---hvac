Exam Details

The Delaware Master HVACR Restricted – Refrigeration classification applies to refrigeration services within the scope authorized by Delaware. The examination evaluates knowledge associated with refrigeration-system installation, maintenance, service, repair, alteration, equipment operation, piping, controls, testing, inspection, and related mechanical requirements.

Refrigeration work requires an understanding of how heat is absorbed, transferred, and rejected. Candidates should understand how the compressor, condenser, metering device, evaporator, refrigerant, piping, motors, controls, and safety devices work together as a complete system.

Important examination study areas include:

  • Refrigeration principles and the basic refrigeration cycle
  • Heat transfer, temperature, pressure, and refrigerant relationships
  • Compressors, condensers, evaporators, and metering devices
  • Commercial refrigeration equipment and system configurations
  • Refrigeration piping materials and installation practices
  • Suction, discharge, and liquid-line functions
  • Motors, relays, contactors, thermostats, and system controls
  • Mechanical-code requirements for refrigeration systems
  • Equipment access, clearances, ventilation, and protection
  • System charging and operating conditions
  • Preventive maintenance and equipment inspection
  • Testing procedures and diagnostic measurements
  • Troubleshooting and service practices
  • Refrigeration safety and system protection

General refrigeration knowledge may include temperature, pressure, heat transfer, refrigerant conditions, tools, materials, system components, controls, and accepted trade practices. Candidates should understand how changes in one component or operating condition affect the complete system.

The basic refrigeration cycle begins with the compressor, which raises refrigerant pressure and moves refrigerant through the system. The condenser rejects heat and changes the refrigerant condition. The metering device controls refrigerant flow and creates the pressure reduction needed before the evaporator. The evaporator absorbs heat from the refrigerated space or product area.

Equipment questions may involve reciprocating, scroll, rotary, or other compressor designs; air-cooled or water-cooled condensers; evaporators; receivers; accumulators; metering devices; solenoid valves; pressure controls; motors; and electrical components. Candidates should understand each component’s purpose, position, and relationship to system operation.

Piping questions may address line sizing, fittings, joining methods, supports, insulation, oil return, pressure conditions, valves, service access, and protection. Improper piping can contribute to poor oil return, pressure loss, reduced capacity, equipment damage, or unreliable system operation.

Maintenance and troubleshooting questions may involve temperature and pressure readings, airflow, refrigerant flow, electrical controls, dirty heat-transfer surfaces, failed motors, restricted lines, improper charging, compressor problems, and other operating symptoms. Candidates should evaluate the complete system instead of relying on one symptom alone.

An open-book format does not eliminate the need for detailed preparation. Candidates must understand the subject well enough to determine where an answer is likely to be found. Licensing Steps

The Delaware Board of Plumbing, Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Examiners regulates Master HVACR and Master HVACR Restricted licenses. Applicants pursuing the restricted Refrigeration classification must complete the state licensing process and receive Board approval before taking the master examination.

  1. Select the correct classification. Apply for the Delaware Master HVACR Restricted license and choose Refrigeration as the requested specialty.
  2. Create or access a DELPROS account. Delaware professional licensing applications are submitted through the Delaware Professional Regulation Online Services system.
  3. Complete the application. Provide the personal, apprenticeship, employment, experience, examination, certification, and licensing information requested by the state.
  4. Gather supporting documentation. Required records may include apprenticeship credentials, a journeyperson certificate, employment verification, supervised experience records, refrigerant-handling certification, and verification of licenses held in other jurisdictions.
  5. Submit records as directed. Certain documents may need to be completed or submitted by an employer, supervisor, apprenticeship program, school, certification organization, or licensing authority.
  6. Complete any applicable equivalency requirement. Applicants without a qualifying apprenticeship credential may need to satisfy the apprenticeship equivalency or bypass requirement associated with the Refrigeration specialty.
  7. Receive Board approval to test. The Delaware Board reviews the application and supporting records before authorizing the applicant to take the master examination.
  8. Register with the testing provider. Follow the examination registration and scheduling instructions supplied after approval.
  9. Take the examination. Follow all current requirements for identification, approved references, calculators, appointment procedures, and testing conduct.
  10. Complete remaining licensing requirements. The Board must receive the required examination result and supporting documentation before issuing the professional license.

Applicants should begin gathering official records early. Apprenticeship documentation, employment verification, refrigerant certification records, and out-of-state license verification may require action by third parties. Incomplete documentation can delay application review and authorization to test.

This highlighted and tabbed book package does not include Application Service, examination registration, government fees, testing fees, professional licensing approval, or business setup services. Each applicant remains responsible for completing the Delaware process and satisfying all applicable requirements.

State Requirements

Delaware offers an unrestricted Master HVACR license and several Master HVACR Restricted specialties. Refrigeration is one of the restricted classifications recognized by the state.

A Delaware Master HVACR Restricted – Refrigeration license authorizes the holder to provide services within the Refrigeration specialty. A restricted license does not provide the full scope associated with an unrestricted Master HVACR license.

Professionals who plan to provide work across multiple restricted HVACR specialties must pursue the license classification appropriate for their complete scope of services. License holders are responsible for remaining within the authority granted by their Delaware credential.

The qualification pathway depends on the applicant’s apprenticeship training, journeyperson credential, supervised work experience, examination history, certification, and supporting documentation.

Applicants using a qualifying apprenticeship pathway must provide evidence of the applicable journeyperson certificate and document the required experience under qualified supervision after completing the apprenticeship pathway.

Applicants without a qualifying apprenticeship credential may need to document a longer period of supervised HVACR or restricted HVACR experience and complete the applicable apprenticeship equivalency or bypass examination requirement.

Experience records should demonstrate work relevant to refrigeration systems. Applicable experience may include system installation, refrigeration piping, commercial refrigeration equipment, compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, controls, testing, maintenance, troubleshooting, repair, or alteration.

Delaware law also requires HVACR applicants to hold the appropriate certification for handling regulated refrigerants. The Delaware Board determines whether the applicant’s training, experience, examination results, certification, and supporting records satisfy the professional licensing requirements.

A Delaware professional license is separate from a Delaware business license. A refrigeration contractor planning to operate a company may also need entity registration, tax accounts, insurance, permits, local approvals, and other operating credentials.

Test Information and Study Materials

Begin preparation with a study schedule that divides refrigeration theory, piping, systems, equipment, mechanical requirements, maintenance, testing, inspection, and troubleshooting into manageable sessions.

Start with the basic refrigeration cycle. Review the refrigerant’s pressure, temperature, physical state, and purpose as it moves through the compressor, condenser, metering device, and evaporator. Understanding these relationships provides a foundation for equipment and troubleshooting questions.

Study major components individually and as parts of a complete system. Review compressors, condensers, evaporators, receivers, accumulators, expansion devices, solenoid valves, pressure controls, thermostats, motors, relays, contactors, and safety devices.

For piping preparation, review the purpose of suction, discharge, and liquid lines. Study sizing, supports, insulation, oil return, fittings, valves, service access, and common installation concerns. Understand how improper piping can affect capacity, pressure, compressor lubrication, and equipment life.

For mechanical-code preparation, use the index and definitions regularly. Locate provisions involving refrigeration systems, machinery rooms, ventilation, equipment access, installation, and protection. Read complete sections and review related exceptions or referenced requirements.

For maintenance and troubleshooting, connect operating symptoms with possible causes. Review pressure, temperature, airflow, refrigerant flow, electrical controls, dirty components, restrictions, improper charging, and mechanical failures.

Practice evaluating the entire system before selecting an answer. Low capacity, for example, may be associated with airflow, refrigerant charge, restrictions, equipment condition, controls, or load conditions. One symptom may have several possible causes.

Complete timed study sessions as the examination approaches. Answer familiar questions first, mark longer reference searches or diagnostic problems, and return to them later. Review missed answers by locating the supporting information in the applicable book.
