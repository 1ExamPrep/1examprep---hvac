Exam Details

The Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation (DBPR), through the Construction Industry Licensing Board (CILB), oversees the certification requirements for Florida air conditioning contractors.

Applicants pursuing Class A or Class B Air Conditioning Contractor certification generally must complete the appropriate General Trade Knowledge examination and the Business & Finance examination, unless they qualify for an applicable exemption.

Florida Class A Air Conditioning Contractor Exam

  • Exam Name: Air Conditioning Class A General Trade Knowledge
  • Number of Scored Questions: 130
  • Time Allowed: 7 hours and 30 minutes
  • Exam Format: Computer-based, multiple-choice
  • Exam Type: Open book
  • Testing Provider: Pearson VUE

The Class A examination evaluates knowledge associated with the installation, service, maintenance, and operation of air conditioning, refrigeration, heating, and ventilation systems. Candidates should be familiar with system components, ductwork, installation practices, maintenance procedures, energy management, and workplace safety.

Florida Class B Air Conditioning Contractor Exam

  • Exam Name: Air Conditioning Class B General Trade Knowledge
  • Number of Scored Questions: 80
  • Time Allowed: 5 hours
  • Exam Format: Computer-based, multiple-choice
  • Exam Type: Open book
  • Testing Provider: Pearson VUE

The Class B examination covers technical knowledge applicable to air conditioning contracting within the scope of Florida's Class B license. Important areas include HVAC installation, equipment components, system maintenance, troubleshooting, ductwork, safety, and energy management.

Florida Business & Finance Exam

  • Exam Name: Construction Business & Finance
  • Number of Scored Questions: 120
  • Time Allowed: 6 hours and 30 minutes
  • Exam Format: Computer-based, multiple-choice
  • Exam Type: Open book
  • Testing Provider: Pearson VUE

This examination addresses the business management and financial knowledge necessary for professional contracting. Candidates should understand accounting, financial reporting, business administration, contracts, estimating, employee management, and government regulations.

Passing the examination is one part of the licensing process. Applicants must also meet the other qualifications established for their contractor classification.

State Requirements

Florida recognizes different air conditioning contractor classifications based on the scope and capacity of the systems contractors are authorized to work on.

Florida Class A Air Conditioning Contractor

A Class A Air Conditioning Contractor is authorized to perform a broad range of central air conditioning, heating, ventilation, and refrigeration contracting activities within the statutory scope of the license.

Unlike Class B, the Class A classification is not restricted by the 25-ton cooling and 500,000-BTU heating limitations.

This classification is relevant to contractors seeking broader authority to install, maintain, repair, alter, and design qualifying HVAC systems.

Florida Class B Air Conditioning Contractor

A Class B Air Conditioning Contractor works within a more limited system-capacity classification. Florida defines the Class B scope as systems involving no more than 25 tons of cooling and 500,000 BTU of heating in any one system.

Class B contractors may perform qualifying installation, repair, maintenance, alteration, and related HVAC activities within their authorized scope.

Neither classification automatically authorizes work outside its statutory definition, including certain plumbing, fuel-line, and electrical power wiring activities.

Experience and Education

Florida certified contractor applicants generally must demonstrate qualifying experience or an accepted combination of education and experience. The applicable licensing application establishes the documentation and qualifications required for the selected classification.

Applicants must also satisfy relevant financial responsibility and other licensing conditions.

Business & Finance Examination Exemptions

Contractors who already hold a current, active license issued by Florida's Construction Industry Licensing Board may qualify for an exemption from the Business & Finance examination when pursuing another applicable license category.

Applicants pursuing their initial certification should prepare for the examination requirements associated with their selected classification.
