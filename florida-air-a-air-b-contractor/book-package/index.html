Exam Details

Florida offers separate certification classifications for Class A Air Conditioning Contractors and Class B Air Conditioning Contractors. The examinations assess knowledge associated with authorized HVACR installation, maintenance, repair, system evaluation, and related construction practices.

The examinations are administered under the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation and the Construction Industry Licensing Board.

  • Examination Categories: Florida Class A Air Conditioning Contractor and Class B Air Conditioning Contractor
  • Regulatory Authority: Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation
  • Licensing Board: Construction Industry Licensing Board
  • Examination Delivery: Computer-based testing
  • Examination Format: Open book, multiple choice
  • Trade Subjects: HVAC installation, refrigeration, ductwork, maintenance, safety, energy management, and indoor air quality
  • Additional Certification Examination: Business and Finance

Both Air A and Air B examinations address pre-installation planning, sheet metal ducts, refrigeration and HVAC systems, equipment components, maintenance, safety, energy management, and indoor air quality.

Although these classifications share many technical subjects, they are separate licensing examinations with distinct scopes of work and examination content outlines.

Open Book Test

Florida's Class A and Class B Air Conditioning Contractor trade examinations are open-book tests. Candidates may bring references authorized for the applicable examination category and testing period.

Effective open-book preparation involves knowing how to locate relevant information in mechanical codes, NFPA standards, duct construction manuals, technical textbooks, and industry reference guides.

Questions may require candidates to interpret refrigeration principles, identify installation requirements, evaluate ductwork details, calculate airflow, or determine the correct application of a technical standard.

Florida publishes separate reference lists for Air A and Air B examinations. The state also distinguishes examination reference lists effective in 2026 from those effective beginning January 1, 2027.

The officially listed reference editions determine the basis for examination questions. Different editions may be permitted under the examination rules, but the published edition remains the basis for answering questions.

Reference familiarity can reduce unnecessary searching during testing. Reviewing indexes, technical illustrations, tables, definitions, and common calculation methods helps candidates develop efficient research habits.

State Requirements

Florida distinguishes Class A and Class B air conditioning contractor certification through the authorized scope of work established in state law.

Class A Air Conditioning Contractors have a broader HVACR scope involving central air conditioning, refrigeration, heating, ventilation, and associated mechanical systems within the activities authorized by Florida law.

Class B Air Conditioning Contractors are subject to equipment-capacity limitations of 25 tons of cooling and 500,000 Btu of heating in any one system. Additional statutory limits govern specific piping, electrical, and related activities.

Contractors must also understand requirements associated with building permits, mechanical installations, energy conservation, safety, and licensing compliance.

The Florida Building Code - Mechanical and Florida Building Code - Energy Conservation are important references for understanding HVAC installation and building efficiency requirements.
