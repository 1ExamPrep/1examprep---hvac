### Exam Details

Florida administers separate Trade Knowledge examinations for **Class A Air Conditioning Contractors** and **Class B Air Conditioning Contractors**. Both are part of Florida's construction contractor examination program and are administered through computer-based testing.

The **Class A Air Conditioning Contractor Trade Knowledge Exam** contains **130 scored questions** with **7.5 hours** allotted for testing.

The **Class B Air Conditioning Contractor Trade Knowledge Exam** contains **80 scored questions** with **5 hours** allotted for testing.

Florida construction examinations consist of multiple-choice questions. Candidates should be prepared for questions involving material found in the approved references as well as questions based on field experience and knowledge of trade practices.

Air-conditioning contractor candidates also encounter Florida's Business and Finance examination as part of the state certification examination path. These flash cards are focused on Air A and Air B trade preparation rather than serving as a replacement for separate Business and Finance preparation.

Because the Air A examination is longer and reflects the broader Class A scope, candidates pursuing that classification should plan for extensive preparation. Air B candidates face a shorter Trade Knowledge examination, but they must still demonstrate familiarity with important HVAC, refrigeration, duct, energy, safety, and code-related concepts.

### Open Book Test

The Florida Air A and Air B construction examinations are **open-book tests**. Candidates may bring only approved references into the testing room and must comply with Florida's examination rules for those materials.

Open book does not mean that every book or study guide may be brought into the exam. Florida publishes an approved reference list for each construction examination category. Candidates should prepare with the correct editions because examination questions are based on the editions identified for the applicable testing year.

For the 2026 Air A and Air B Trade Knowledge examinations, reference materials must be properly bound and remain bound during testing. Original editions are required unless a particular reference is specifically identified as acceptable in bound PDF form.

Underlining and highlighting in approved references are permitted. Handwritten and typewritten notes are not permitted. Moveable tabs such as removable flags are not allowed, and candidates may not make marks in their references during the examination.

An open-book exam rewards familiarity with the approved books. Candidates who know which reference covers a particular topic and how the material is organized can approach questions more efficiently than candidates attempting to search every book from the beginning.

Flash cards can support this type of preparation. When answering a card, consider not only the correct response but also which approved reference you would consult if the same subject appeared during the examination.

### State Requirements

Florida regulates certified construction contractors through the Department of Business and Professional Regulation and the Construction Industry Licensing Board. Air-conditioning contracting is divided into classifications with defined scopes of work.

A **Class A Air Conditioning Contractor** has an unlimited air-conditioning classification within the scope established by Florida law. The classification includes central air-conditioning, refrigeration, heating and ventilating systems, applicable ductwork, boiler and unfired pressure vessel systems, related equipment, piping, insulation, pressure and process piping, and pneumatic control piping when the work is otherwise permitted by law.

A **Class B Air Conditioning Contractor** is limited to systems of **25 tons of cooling and 500,000 Btu of heating in any one system**. The classification includes applicable central air-conditioning, refrigeration, heating, ventilation, ductwork, piping, insulation, controls, and related work within the scope established by Florida law.

The distinction between Air A and Air B should be considered before applying for an examination. Candidates preparing for Class B should understand the capacity limitation, while Class A candidates should prepare for the broader scope represented by that classification.

Florida certified contractor applicants are also subject to application requirements beyond examination preparation. Candidates should complete the current requirements for the specific license and application type they are pursuing.
