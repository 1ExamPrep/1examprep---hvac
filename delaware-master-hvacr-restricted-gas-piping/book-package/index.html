Exam Details

The Delaware Master HVACR Restricted Gas Piping examination evaluates knowledge associated with the design, installation, construction, maintenance, service, repair, alteration, and modification of gas piping systems and related equipment within the scope of the restricted specialty.

Applicants should prepare for questions involving general gas piping knowledge, system installation, sizing and design, liquefied petroleum gas, venting, pipefitting mathematics, isometric drawings, plan reading, and construction safety. Some questions may be answered through trade knowledge, while others require candidates to locate and interpret information in an approved reference.

Important areas of preparation include:

  • Fuel gas terminology and system components
  • Approved gas piping materials
  • Pipe fittings and joining methods
  • Gas piping supports and protection
  • Shutoff valves and related controls
  • Gas regulators and pressure considerations
  • Appliance connections
  • Gas pipe sizing and system capacity
  • Testing and inspection principles
  • Combustion air and venting
  • Liquefied petroleum gas systems
  • Pipe measurements and calculations
  • Isometric drawings and piping layouts
  • Workplace and construction safety

General knowledge questions may address tools, materials, accepted practices, terminology, system components, and the operating principles involved in gas piping work. Candidates should understand the purpose of common fittings, valves, regulators, connectors, and protective devices rather than relying only on memorized definitions.

System installation questions can involve permitted materials, joining procedures, pipe protection, supports, shutoff locations, appliance connections, testing, regulators, and installation restrictions. A small detail in the question may determine which requirement applies, so careful reading is essential.

Sizing and design questions may require candidates to use tables from the International Fuel Gas Code, 2018. Before selecting a table, identify the system conditions given in the question. Relevant factors can include fuel type, system pressure, allowable pressure drop, connected demand, developed length, and the specified sizing method.

Plan-reading and isometric questions may involve pipe routes, branches, fittings, elevations, changes in direction, and system arrangements. Candidates should practice interpreting drawings before beginning a calculation or selecting an installation method.

Pipefitting mathematics may include fractions, decimals, measurements, conversions, geometry, angles, offsets, pipe lengths, areas, volumes, and other calculations used in piping work. Showing each step and labeling units can help prevent avoidable errors.

Safety questions may address hazard recognition, personal protective equipment, ladders, hand and power tools, material handling, housekeeping, fire prevention, excavation awareness, and safe jobsite conduct. Gas piping work requires particular attention to combustible materials, ignition sources, pressurized systems, and proper testing procedures.

Open Book Test

The Delaware Master HVACR Restricted Gas Piping examination is an open-book, timed test. Candidates may use authorized references during the examination, subject to the testing provider’s current rules concerning approved editions, tabs, highlighting, notes, inserts, loose materials, calculators, and electronic devices.

An open-book format does not eliminate the need to learn the material. Candidates must be able to identify the subject of a question, select the correct reference, locate the applicable chapter or table, and interpret the information accurately. Searching unfamiliar books page by page can consume valuable testing time.

Begin developing a reference-navigation routine early in your preparation. Use the index, table of contents, chapter headings, definitions, tables, and cross-references. When you locate a possible answer, read the complete section and review nearby exceptions, notes, and table conditions before making a selection.

The International Fuel Gas Code, 2018 will be the primary source for many technical questions involving fuel gas piping, system sizing, installation, appliance connections, combustion air, and venting. Basic Safety, 2009 supports preparation for workplace and construction safety questions. Mathematics for Plumbers and Pipefitters, 8th Edition supports calculations, measurements, geometry, and pipefitting math.

Practice deciding which book to open first. A question about pipe capacity or a venting requirement will generally direct you toward the fuel gas code. A question involving personal protective equipment may point to the safety reference. A question involving an offset, measurement, or conversion may require the pipefitting mathematics book.

Use timed practice sessions to improve your search speed. Track the subjects that require the longest searches and review those sections again. The goal is not to memorize every page. The goal is to understand the material and know how to find supporting information efficiently.

Review the current candidate bulletin and appointment instructions before the exam. Testing rules may change, and the testing provider determines which editions and book preparations are permitted. Bring only the references and materials allowed under the instructions issued for your examination.

Licensing Steps

The Delaware Board of Plumbing, Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Examiners regulates the Master HVACR and Master HVACR Restricted license classifications. Candidates must complete the state licensing process and receive approval before taking the master examination.

  1. Choose the correct classification. Select the Delaware Master HVACR Restricted license and identify Gas Piping as the requested specialty.
  2. Create or access a DELPROS account. Delaware professional licensing applications are managed through the Delaware Professional Regulation Online Services system.
  3. Complete the application. Provide the requested personal, apprenticeship, employment, experience, examination, and licensing information.
  4. Gather supporting documentation. Records may include a journeyperson certificate, apprenticeship information, employment verification, supervised experience documentation, and license verification from another jurisdiction.
  5. Submit documents as directed. Some records may be uploaded by the applicant, while others may need to be submitted by an employer, supervisor, apprenticeship program, school, or licensing authority.
  6. Complete any applicable equivalency requirement. Applicants without the qualifying journeyperson certificate may need to complete the apprenticeship equivalency examination requirement applicable to the restricted specialty.
  7. Receive Board approval to test. The Board reviews the application and determines whether the applicant satisfies the conditions required to sit for the master examination.
  8. Register for the examination. After approval, follow the instructions provided for scheduling with the examination administrator.
  9. Take the examination. Follow all current rules concerning identification, arrival procedures, references, calculators, and testing conduct.
  10. Complete remaining licensing requirements. The Board must receive the required results and documentation before a professional license can be issued.

Applicants should begin gathering official records early. Employment verification, apprenticeship certificates, and out-of-state license verifications can require action by third parties. Missing or incomplete documents may delay application review or examination authorization.

State Requirements

Delaware recognizes an unrestricted Master HVACR license and several Master HVACR Restricted specialties. Gas Piping is one of the restricted classifications. A professional holding this restricted license may perform services within the gas piping specialty but does not automatically receive authority to provide every service covered by an unrestricted Master HVACR license.

Applicants using the qualifying apprenticeship pathway must provide evidence of a journeyperson certificate issued after completing an HVACR or HVACR restricted apprenticeship program that satisfies the applicable standards. They must also document two years of qualifying post-apprenticeship experience under appropriate supervision.

Applicants without the qualifying journeyperson certificate must document seven years of HVACR or HVACR restricted experience under the supervision of a master HVACR licensee, master HVACR restricted licensee, or an individual with a similar level of licensure in another state. They must also satisfy the applicable apprenticeship equivalency examination requirement.

Delaware regulations clarify that an individual pursuing a restricted HVACR license is required to complete only the bypass or equivalency examination section relevant to the requested restriction. The Board reviews the applicant’s records to determine whether the documented experience and training meet the requirements for Gas Piping.

Experience records should demonstrate work related to the classification being requested. The Board may require verification from employers, supervisors, apprenticeship programs, or other licensing agencies. Applicants should provide complete and accurate information throughout the application process.

A Delaware professional license is separate from a Delaware business license. Contractors planning to operate a company may also have obligations involving business registration, taxes, insurance, local permits, and other operating requirements. This book package does not include application, licensing, insurance, business registration, or compliance services.

Test Information and Study Materials

Start with a study schedule that divides the material into manageable categories. Assign separate sessions to gas piping installation, sizing, venting, LP gas, safety, mathematics, and plan reading. Give more time to subjects that are unfamiliar or require longer reference searches.

For code questions, identify the main keyword and use the index before searching page by page. After reaching the cited section, read the surrounding provisions. Definitions, exceptions, notes, and references to other sections may affect the correct answer.

For sizing questions, determine the system conditions before choosing a table. Verify the pressure, developed length, connected demand, fuel type, and sizing method. Read the complete table heading and notes before tracing the applicable row and column.

For pipefitting math, work through each calculation step and label the units. Practice fractions, decimals, conversions, geometry, angles, offsets, and measurements. When an answer is incorrect, identify whether the mistake came from arithmetic, unit conversion, formula selection, or interpretation.

For isometric and plan-reading questions, review the entire drawing before calculating. Identify branches, fittings, elevations, changes in direction, and measurements. A careful visual review can prevent errors caused by overlooking part of the piping arrangement.

Complete timed practice sessions as the exam approaches. Answer familiar questions first and mark longer searches or calculations for later review. Avoid spending an excessive amount of time on one difficult item when other questions may be answered more efficiently.
