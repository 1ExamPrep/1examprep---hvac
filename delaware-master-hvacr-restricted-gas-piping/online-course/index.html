Exam Details

The Delaware Master HVACR Restricted Gas Piping examination tests a candidate’s knowledge of the design, installation, maintenance, repair, alteration, and extension of gas piping systems. The examination scope includes gas mains, lines, laterals, tanks, appliances, and related appurtenances.

The exam contains 46 questions and allows three hours for completion. The official content outline divides the examination into eight subject areas:

  • General Knowledge: 7 questions
  • Isometrics and Plan Reading: 2 questions
  • Sizing and Design: 5 questions
  • Pipefitting Math: 6 questions
  • System Installation: 9 questions
  • LP Gas: 7 questions
  • Venting: 5 questions
  • Safety: 5 questions

System installation is the largest category on the examination. Candidates should be prepared to review gas piping materials, fittings, joining methods, valves, regulators, shutoff devices, appliance connections, protection methods, supports, testing principles, and other installation-related requirements.

General knowledge questions may address gas piping terminology, tools, components, accepted trade practices, system operation, and the purpose of common equipment. This portion of the exam may require candidates to combine practical field knowledge with information located in the approved references.

Sizing and design questions can involve fuel demand, developed length, piping capacity, pressure conditions, system arrangements, and the proper use of code tables. Candidates should practice identifying the correct table before performing a calculation or selecting a pipe size. Table headings, notes, pressure ranges, and sizing methods must be read carefully.

Pipefitting math questions may require work with fractions, decimals, measurements, conversions, geometry, angles, offsets, lengths, areas, volumes, and other calculations used in piping work. A strong study plan should include repeated problem-solving rather than relying only on memorized formulas.

Isometric and plan-reading questions may test the ability to interpret piping layouts, symbols, fittings, changes in direction, branches, elevations, and system arrangements. Candidates should practice converting visual information into measurements, fitting selections, and installation decisions.

LP gas questions focus on principles and requirements associated with liquefied petroleum gas systems. Venting questions may involve appliance venting, combustion products, vent connectors, chimney-related requirements, vent sizing concepts, and installation conditions. Safety questions address hazard recognition, personal protective equipment, tools, material handling, fire prevention, and safe jobsite practices.

The examination is administered by ProV Exam for the Delaware Division of Professional Regulation. Candidates must receive approval from the Delaware Board of Plumbing, Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Examiners before registering for the master examination.

Open Book Test

The Delaware Master HVACR Restricted Gas Piping Contractor exam is an open-book, timed examination. Candidates may use the approved reference books during testing, subject to the current rules established by ProV Exam and the Delaware licensing authority.

Open book does not mean that every answer will be easy to find. With 46 questions and a three-hour time limit, candidates must identify the subject of each question, choose the correct reference, locate the applicable information, and interpret it accurately. Searching unfamiliar books without a plan can use valuable testing time.

The online course helps students develop a practical reference-navigation process. Begin by identifying whether the question involves code requirements, safety, mathematics, sizing, installation, venting, LP gas, or plan reading. Then select the reference most likely to contain the answer.

The International Fuel Gas Code, 2018 is the primary source for many questions involving gas piping materials, sizing, installation, appliance connections, combustion air, venting, testing, and fuel gas system requirements. Basic Safety, 2009 supports jobsite safety questions, while Mathematics for Plumbers and Pipefitters, 8th Edition supports trade calculations and pipefitting mathematics.

Permanent tabs, highlighting, and underlining in pen are generally permitted under the published testing rules. Repositionable tabs, Post-it notes, loose inserts, unauthorized handwritten notes, and added formula sheets are not permitted. Candidates are not allowed to mark in their books during the examination.

Practice locating information before exam day. Use the index, table of contents, chapter headings, definitions, tables, and cross-references. When you find a possible answer, read the entire section and review any applicable exceptions, notes, or table conditions before making a selection.

Timed practice can help reveal where additional work is needed. Track how long it takes to locate code sections and complete calculations. Revisit topics that consistently require long searches so you can become more familiar with their location and application.

Licensing Steps

The Delaware Board of Plumbing, Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Examiners regulates the Master HVACR and Master HVACR Restricted license classifications. Applicants pursuing the Gas Piping specialty must complete the state licensing process and obtain approval before taking the master examination.

  1. Select the correct license classification. Apply for the Master HVACR Restricted license and select Gas Piping as the specialty.
  2. Create a DELPROS account. Delaware professional license applications are submitted through the Delaware Professional Regulation Online Services system.
  3. Complete the online application. Enter the requested personal, training, apprenticeship, employment, experience, and licensing information.
  4. Submit supporting documents. Required records may include apprenticeship documentation, journeyperson credentials, employment verification, experience records, and license verification from other jurisdictions.
  5. Complete an applicable bypass examination. Applicants using a non-apprenticeship pathway may need to pass the section of the apprenticeship bypass examination relevant to the restricted specialty.
  6. Receive Board approval. The Delaware Board reviews the application and determines whether the applicant is eligible to sit for the master examination.
  7. Register for the exam. After approval, follow the candidate information and registration instructions provided for scheduling with ProV Exam.
  8. Take the examination. Follow all testing rules for identification, approved references, calculators, arrival, remote testing, or in-person testing.
  9. Complete all remaining requirements. The Board must receive the required examination result and supporting documentation before issuing the license.

Applicants should begin collecting documents early. Employment verification, apprenticeship records, and official license verifications may need to be completed by employers, supervisors, training programs, or other licensing agencies. Missing documentation can delay application review and examination authorization.

The online exam prep course supports preparation for the examination but does not include the Delaware application, approval to test, examination registration, or government fees. Each candidate remains responsible for meeting the state’s eligibility and licensing requirements.

State Requirements

A Delaware Master HVACR Restricted license allows the license holder to provide HVACR services only within the specialty identified on the license. Gas Piping is a recognized restricted specialty. Professionals who plan to offer services in more than one restricted specialty must pursue the appropriate broader license classification.

The Delaware licensing process includes experience, training, documentation, and examination requirements. The pathway that applies depends on the applicant’s apprenticeship background, journeyperson credential, supervised work history, and other qualifications reviewed by the Board.

An applicant who completed a qualifying HVACR or restricted HVACR apprenticeship and received the applicable journeyperson credential may use that background as part of the licensing pathway. Additional supervised experience may be required after the apprenticeship or journeyperson stage.

An applicant who did not complete the qualifying apprenticeship pathway may need to document the required amount of supervised experience and pass the applicable apprenticeship bypass examination section. Delaware regulations clarify that a person pursuing a restricted HVACR license is required to pass only the bypass examination section relevant to the requested restriction.

Experience documentation should demonstrate work connected to the Gas Piping specialty. The Board reviews submitted records to determine whether the applicant meets the applicable legal and regulatory requirements. Examination approval and license issuance are decisions made by the Delaware Board.

A Delaware professional license is different from a Delaware business license. A person planning to operate a contracting company may also need business registration, tax accounts, insurance, local approvals, permits, and other operational credentials. The professional license establishes authority to perform regulated services within the issued classification.

License holders must remain within the scope of the restricted specialty. Gas piping authority does not automatically grant permission to perform unrelated plumbing or unrestricted HVACR services. A separate Master Plumber license may be required for work involving water-supply piping, plumbing fixtures, drainage systems, and other plumbing services outside the restricted HVACR classification.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive study plan should combine concept review, code navigation, calculation practice, and timed questions. Begin by dividing the exam outline into weekly or daily study blocks. Assign additional time to system installation, general knowledge, LP gas, pipefitting math, sizing, venting, and safety based on your current strengths.

For code-based questions, practice identifying keywords and using the index. After finding an index entry, follow it to the correct section and read the complete requirement. Do not stop at the first phrase that appears relevant. Definitions, exceptions, table notes, and related sections may affect the answer.

For sizing questions, identify the system conditions before selecting a table. Confirm the fuel type, pressure, demand, developed length, and sizing method. Practice reading table values carefully and selecting a pipe size that satisfies the stated conditions.

For pipefitting mathematics, show every step and label the units. Review fractions, decimals, conversions, geometry, offsets, and measurement calculations. When an answer is incorrect, determine whether the error came from the formula, arithmetic, units, or interpretation of the question.

For plan-reading questions, practice identifying fittings, branches, elevations, direction changes, and piping arrangements. Visual questions often require careful observation before any calculation begins.

Complete timed study sessions as your exam date approaches. Answer familiar questions first and mark longer calculations or reference searches for review. This method can help you maintain momentum and avoid spending too much time on a single difficult question.
