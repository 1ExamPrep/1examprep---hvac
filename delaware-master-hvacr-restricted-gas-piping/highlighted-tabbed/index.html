Exam Details

The Delaware Master HVACR Restricted Gas Piping examination evaluates knowledge related to gas piping systems and the work permitted within the restricted specialty. Candidates should be prepared to apply technical knowledge, interpret code requirements, complete trade calculations, and locate supporting information in the approved references.

Preparation should address the following subject areas:

  • General gas piping knowledge and terminology
  • Isometric drawings and plan reading
  • Gas piping sizing and system design
  • Pipefitting mathematics
  • System installation requirements
  • Liquefied petroleum gas concepts
  • Venting principles and requirements
  • Construction and workplace safety

General knowledge preparation may involve common materials, fittings, valves, regulators, appliance connections, tools, system components, and accepted gas piping practices. Candidates should understand both the purpose of these components and the conditions that affect their selection or installation.

Sizing and design questions may require candidates to locate and interpret fuel gas code tables. The correct answer can depend on factors such as system pressure, allowable pressure drop, developed length, connected demand, fuel type, and the sizing method stated in the question. Careful table selection is essential because similar-looking tables may apply to different system conditions.

System installation questions may address permitted piping materials, joining methods, supports, protection from damage, shutoff valves, appliance connections, testing, regulators, sediment traps, prohibited locations, and other installation considerations. Candidates should read the complete question before searching for a rule because a single condition may change which provision applies.

Pipefitting mathematics may include fractions, decimals, measurements, conversions, geometry, angles, offsets, pipe lengths, areas, volumes, and practical calculations used in piping work. Regular calculation practice can help candidates avoid errors involving arithmetic, units, formulas, or interpretation.

Isometric and plan-reading questions may require candidates to identify fittings, branches, changes in direction, elevations, measurements, and piping arrangements. These questions often test the ability to translate a drawing into a practical installation or calculation.

Safety preparation should include hazard recognition, personal protective equipment, hand and power tools, ladders, material handling, housekeeping, fire prevention, excavation awareness, and safe jobsite behavior. Gas piping work involves conditions that require careful attention to ignition sources, combustible materials, pressurized systems, tools, and installation surroundings.

Open Book Test

The Delaware Master HVACR Restricted Gas Piping examination is an open-book and timed test. Candidates may use authorized references while following the current rules established by the examination provider and the Delaware licensing authority.

Open-book testing rewards preparation and efficient navigation. It does not remove the need to understand the material. Candidates may need to determine which book applies, locate the correct chapter or table, interpret the requirement, complete a calculation, and compare possible answers within a limited amount of time.

The highlighted and tabbed books in this package are organized to support that process. Tabs provide convenient starting points for important subjects, while highlighting identifies material that deserves attention during study. Candidates should still read the surrounding text, notes, exceptions, and table conditions before selecting an answer.

A practical reference-search method begins with identifying the topic. A question involving workplace hazards will most likely lead to Basic Safety. A question involving gas piping materials, sizing, appliance installation, combustion air, or venting will generally point to the International Fuel Gas Code. A question involving offsets, conversions, geometry, or piping calculations may require Mathematics for Plumbers and Pipefitters.

Practice locating information under timed conditions. Begin with the index or table of contents, move to the most relevant chapter or section, and read the complete provision. Avoid choosing an answer based on one highlighted sentence when a nearby exception or table note may affect the result.

Candidates should review the current examination instructions before test day. Testing rules can address identification, calculators, approved books, tabs, highlighting, handwritten material, loose papers, electronic devices, and permitted items. Only materials allowed under the current candidate instructions should be taken into the examination.

Licensing Steps

The Delaware Board of Plumbing, Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Examiners regulates Master HVACR and Master HVACR Restricted licenses. Applicants pursuing the restricted Gas Piping specialty must complete the state licensing process and receive authorization before taking the master examination.

  1. Select the proper license classification. Apply for the Delaware Master HVACR Restricted license and identify Gas Piping as the requested specialty.
  2. Create or access a DELPROS account. Delaware professional licensing applications are submitted through the Delaware Professional Regulation Online Services system.
  3. Complete the online application. Provide the requested personal, apprenticeship, employment, experience, examination, and licensing information.
  4. Collect supporting documents. Required records may include apprenticeship documentation, journeyperson credentials, employment verification, experience information, and license verification from another jurisdiction.
  5. Submit documents as directed. Some documents may be uploaded by the applicant, while others may need to be sent by an employer, supervisor, school, apprenticeship program, or licensing agency.
  6. Complete any applicable preliminary requirement. Applicants using a pathway without the qualifying apprenticeship credential may need to satisfy the relevant apprenticeship bypass examination requirement.
  7. Receive approval to sit for the master exam. The Delaware Board reviews the application and determines whether the applicant is eligible to test.
  8. Register with the examination provider. After approval, follow the scheduling and registration instructions supplied with the examination authorization.
  9. Prepare for and take the examination. Bring only authorized materials and comply with the current testing rules.
  10. Complete all remaining licensing requirements. The Board must receive the required results and documentation before a license can be issued.

Applicants should begin gathering records early. Employment verification and official documents may take time to obtain, particularly when a former employer, training program, or out-of-state licensing agency must provide the information.

State Requirements

Delaware offers an unrestricted Master HVACR license and several Master HVACR Restricted specialties. Gas Piping is one of the restricted classifications recognized by the state. A restricted license authorizes the holder to provide services only within the specialty shown on the license.

Professionals who intend to perform work in more than one restricted HVACR specialty may need to pursue the unrestricted Master HVACR classification. License holders must remain within the scope of the credential issued to them.

The applicable licensing pathway depends on the applicant’s apprenticeship training, journeyperson credential, supervised work experience, and other qualifications. Applicants using a recognized apprenticeship pathway may need to document additional experience obtained after completing that stage of training.

Applicants without the qualifying apprenticeship credential may be required to document a different period of supervised experience and complete the section of the apprenticeship bypass examination that applies to the requested restricted specialty. The Delaware Board determines whether submitted experience and training meet the regulatory requirements.

Experience documentation should show work relevant to gas piping and the requested license classification. The Board may require official records from employers, supervisors, training programs, or other licensing authorities before approving an applicant.

A Delaware professional license is different from a Delaware business license. A professional planning to operate a contracting company may also need business registration, tax accounts, insurance, permits, and other credentials. Test Information and Study Materials

Create a study schedule that divides the exam subjects into manageable sessions. Separate review periods can be assigned to code navigation, gas piping installation, sizing, venting, LP gas, safety, mathematics, and plan reading.

For code questions, identify keywords and use the index before searching page by page. Read the entire section and check related definitions, exceptions, and table notes. A question may appear simple but depend on an installation condition stated elsewhere in the code.

For sizing problems, confirm the applicable pressure, developed length, connected demand, fuel type, and sizing method before selecting a table. Practice tracing rows and columns carefully and checking the table heading before choosing an answer.

For mathematical preparation, solve problems step by step and label the units. Review errors to determine whether they resulted from arithmetic, the formula, a conversion, or misunderstanding the question.

For plan-reading and isometric preparation, practice identifying fittings, branches, offsets, elevations, and changes in direction. Visual questions should be studied carefully before beginning calculations.

Timed practice can help develop better test management. Complete familiar questions first, mark longer calculations or searches, and return to them after working through the remaining items. Do not allow one difficult question to consume a disproportionate amount of the available time.
