### Exam Details

The Delaware Master HVACR Restricted Gas Piping examination evaluates knowledge related to gas piping systems and the work permitted within the restricted specialty. Candidates should be prepared to apply technical knowledge, interpret code requirements, complete trade calculations, and locate supporting information in the approved references.

Preparation should address the following subject areas:

- General gas piping knowledge and terminology
- Isometric drawings and plan reading
- Gas piping sizing and system design
- Pipefitting mathematics
- System installation requirements
- Liquefied petroleum gas concepts
- Venting principles and requirements
- Construction and workplace safety

General knowledge preparation may involve common materials, fittings, valves, regulators, appliance connections, tools, system components, and accepted gas piping practices. Candidates should understand both the purpose of these components and the conditions that affect their selection or installation.

Sizing and design questions may require candidates to locate and interpret fuel gas code tables. The correct answer can depend on factors such as system pressure, allowable pressure drop, developed length, connected demand, fuel type, and the sizing method stated in the question. Careful table selection is essential because similar-looking tables may apply to different system conditions.

System installation questions may address permitted piping materials, joining methods, supports, protection from damage, shutoff valves, appliance connections, testing, regulators, sediment traps, prohibited locations, and other installation considerations. Candidates should read the complete question before searching for a rule because a single condition may change which provision applies.

Pipefitting mathematics may include fractions, decimals, measurements, conversions, geometry, angles, offsets, pipe lengths, areas, volumes, and practical calculations used in piping work. Regular calculation practice can help candidates avoid errors involving arithmetic, units, formulas, or interpretation.

Isometric and plan-reading questions may require candidates to identify fittings, branches, changes in direction, elevations, measurements, and piping arrangements. These questions often test the ability to translate a drawing into a practical installation or calculation.

Safety preparation should include hazard recognition, personal protective equipment, hand and power tools, ladders, material handling, housekeeping, fire prevention, excavation awareness, and safe jobsite behavior. Gas piping work involves conditions that require careful attention to ignition sources, combustible materials, pressurized systems, tools, and installation surroundings.

### Test Information and Study Materials

Create a study schedule that divides the exam subjects into manageable sessions. Separate review periods can be assigned to code navigation, gas piping installation, sizing, venting, LP gas, safety, mathematics, and plan reading.

For code questions, identify keywords and use the index before searching page by page. Read the entire section and check related definitions, exceptions, and table notes. A question may appear simple but depend on an installation condition stated elsewhere in the code.

For sizing problems, confirm the applicable pressure, developed length, connected demand, fuel type, and sizing method before selecting a table. Practice tracing rows and columns carefully and checking the table heading before choosing an answer.

For mathematical preparation, solve problems step by step and label the units. Review errors to determine whether they resulted from arithmetic, the formula, a conversion, or misunderstanding the question.

For plan-reading and isometric preparation, practice identifying fittings, branches, offsets, elevations, and changes in direction. Visual questions should be studied carefully before beginning calculations.

Timed practice can help develop better test management. Complete familiar questions first, mark longer calculations or searches, and return to them after working through the remaining items. Do not allow one difficult question to consume a disproportionate amount of the available time.
