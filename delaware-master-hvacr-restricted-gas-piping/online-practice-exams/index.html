State Requirements

The Delaware Master HVACR Restricted Gas Piping license is administered by the Delaware Board of Plumbing, Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Examiners.

A Master HVACR Restricted license allows the license holder to provide HVACR services only within the specialty identified on the license. Delaware recognizes Gas Piping as one of its restricted specialties.

Applicants applying by examination must complete the appropriate DELPROS application and provide documentation showing that they satisfy the applicable experience and training requirements.

Applicants who have a journeyman certificate from a federally approved HVACR apprenticeship program must provide a copy of the apprenticeship program Certificate of Completion and proof of at least two years of work experience under the supervision of a master licensee after receiving the certificate.

Applicants without an accepted journeyman certificate must provide proof of at least seven years of work experience under the supervision of a master licensee. The Board reviews the experience before determining whether the applicant may proceed to the required apprenticeship bypass process.

Delaware's regulations clarify that an individual pursuing a restricted HVACR license needs to pass only the portion of the apprenticeship bypass examination that applies to the requested restriction.

Once all applicable preliminary requirements are satisfied, the Board may approve the applicant to sit for the Master HVACR Restricted Gas Piping examination. ProV then provides the licensure examination information and scheduling instructions.

A minimum score of 70% is required to pass. Applicants who do not achieve a passing score after three attempts are subject to Delaware's reapplication requirements and must obtain new Board approval before testing again after the applicable waiting period.

Passing the examination is one part of the licensing process. The Board also reviews the candidate's application, experience, apprenticeship documentation when applicable, and other required materials before issuing the license.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Delaware Master HVACR Restricted Gas Piping study plan should cover all eight examination categories while giving additional attention to System Installation, the largest individual portion of the test.

General Knowledge includes 7 questions. Candidates should understand fuel gas terminology, basic gas-system operation, appliance relationships, pressure concepts, system components, and the differences between common fuel gas applications.

Isometrics and Plan Reading accounts for 2 questions. Although this is a smaller category, candidates should be able to interpret piping diagrams, recognize changes in direction, identify fittings, follow gas distribution routes, and understand how two-dimensional information represents a complete piping system.

Sizing and Design accounts for 5 questions. Proper gas piping sizing depends on system demand, piping length, available pressure, fuel type, and the sizing method being applied. Candidates should practice reading code tables and identifying the correct inputs before selecting a pipe size.

Gas piping design questions may also require candidates to understand how branch arrangements, developed length, appliance demand, and system configuration influence the capacity of a piping system.

Pipefitting Math accounts for 6 questions. Review fractions, decimals, percentages, measurements, conversions, angles, offsets, geometry, and common calculations encountered during pipe layout and installation.

When practicing mathematics, write down each step. Converting measurements or working with fractions mentally can lead to avoidable errors, especially when a problem requires several operations.

System Installation accounts for 9 questions. Study gas piping materials, fittings, joints, supports, protection, routing, appliance connections, shutoff valves, sediment traps when applicable, testing, identification, and installation requirements.

Candidates should understand why gas piping installation rules exist. Proper support protects piping from physical damage and movement, shutoff valves permit isolation, approved joints reduce leakage risks, and pressure testing helps verify system integrity before service.

LP Gas accounts for 7 questions. LP gas has different operating and physical characteristics than natural gas, so candidates should understand how fuel properties can affect pressure, sizing, storage relationships, appliance supply, and safety considerations.

Do not treat natural gas and LP gas as interchangeable when working through practice questions. Identify the fuel type before selecting a table, pressure value, or system requirement.

Venting accounts for 5 questions. Candidates should understand vent types, vent connectors, draft, appliance categories, clearances, termination requirements, sizing concepts, and how combustion products are safely carried away from appliances.

Venting questions often combine code knowledge with system operation. Candidates should understand both what the requirement says and why correct venting is essential for safe combustion and appliance performance.

Safety accounts for 5 questions. Review hazard recognition, personal protective equipment, ladders, tools, excavation awareness, fire hazards, gas leaks, confined spaces, and safe jobsite behavior.

The International Fuel Gas Code, 2018 should be used actively during practice. Instead of reading the code continuously, use practice questions to identify a subject and then locate the applicable chapter, section, table, or requirement.

For gas sizing questions, practice selecting the correct table based on fuel type, system pressure, sizing method, and developed length. Table navigation can be just as important as the arithmetic involved.

Mathematics for Plumbers and Pipefitters, 8th Edition should be used to strengthen the numerical foundation behind pipefitting questions. Candidates who are comfortable with measurements, fractions, geometry, and conversions can spend more time solving the technical problem and less time correcting arithmetic.

Basic Safety, 2009 can support preparation for the Safety category by reinforcing fundamental jobsite practices and hazard awareness. Safety questions should be treated as technical content rather than an afterthought.

When a practice question is missed, determine why. The problem may involve code navigation, incorrect fuel type, misunderstanding a table, arithmetic error, weak plan-reading skills, or unfamiliarity with an installation requirement.

A useful study method is to separate missed questions into categories such as sizing, math, installation, LP gas, venting, and safety. Patterns become easier to identify when mistakes are organized by subject.

As preparation progresses, combine subjects into mixed practice sets. A gas piping scenario may involve calculating demand, selecting pipe size, interpreting a piping arrangement, determining installation requirements, and applying a safety rule.

Use the included 3 months access to create repeated study cycles. Begin with individual subjects, revisit missed questions, strengthen reference navigation, and gradually move into mixed-topic practice as the examination approaches.
