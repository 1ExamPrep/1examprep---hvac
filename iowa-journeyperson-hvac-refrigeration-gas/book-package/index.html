Fuel gas topics should receive focused preparation. NFPA 54 can be detailed, so repeated table use and chapter review are important.

Combustion air and venting are important areas for HVAC and gas preparation. Students should review appliance air requirements, indoor and outdoor combustion air concepts, vent sizing awareness, vent connector requirements, appliance venting categories when applicable, chimneys, terminations, clearances, and safety concerns. These topics often require careful reading and reference navigation.

Gas pipe sizing should be practiced throughout preparation. Students should learn how to identify the correct table, understand length and load concepts, read table values, and apply sizing logic carefully. Mistakes in gas pipe sizing can occur when candidates use the wrong table, overlook pressure assumptions, misread length, or fail to account for total connected load.

Duct systems and ventilation should also be reviewed. Candidates should study air distribution, duct sizing awareness, duct materials, supports, sealing awareness, outside air, exhaust air, mechanical ventilation, equipment airflow, filters, return air, and practical installation concerns. HVAC candidates need to understand how airflow affects equipment performance and comfort.

Controls and electrical awareness should be included in the study plan. HVAC and refrigeration systems depend on motors, contactors, relays, thermostats, safety switches, control wiring, transformers, and equipment protection. Candidates should review basic control concepts and connect them to equipment operation and troubleshooting.

Students should also practice timed reference navigation. A helpful method is to choose a topic, locate it in the code or trade reference, read the related section, and then summarize where it is found. Repeating this process helps build memory of topic locations and improves speed. Since the exam is open book, the goal is not to memorize every section, but to know the references well enough to locate and apply information efficiently.

Students should also review common mistakes. These may include using the wrong sizing table, missing an exception, overlooking a definition, confusing mechanical code and fuel gas code requirements, applying a general rule without checking equipment-specific instructions, misreading gas load information, or selecting an answer before identifying the correct reference. Careful reading and repeated reference practice can help reduce these errors.
