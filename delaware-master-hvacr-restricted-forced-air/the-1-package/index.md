### Exam Details

The Delaware Master HVACR Restricted – Heating, Forced Air Systems, Ventilation, and Gas Piping examination evaluates the technical knowledge needed to work within this specialty. The examination covers forced-air heating systems, associated ductwork, mechanical equipment, gas piping, system sizing, installation, maintenance, and plan interpretation.

The published examination outline identifies 70 questions with a three-hour time allowance. The subject areas are divided as follows:

- General Knowledge: 15 questions
- Piping: 12 questions
- Systems and Sizing: 20 questions
- Equipment: 6 questions
- Maintenance: 12 questions
- Plan Reading: 5 questions

Systems and sizing represent the largest portion of the examination. Candidates should be prepared to review airflow, duct systems, equipment capacity, fuel gas demand, piping size, ventilation, combustion air, system design principles, and installation conditions. Questions may require both technical knowledge and the use of tables, diagrams, or code provisions.

General knowledge may include HVACR terminology, system operation, tools, materials, heat transfer, combustion, airflow, controls, and standard trade practices. Piping questions may address fuel gas materials, joining methods, sizing, valves, regulators, appliance connections, supports, protection, and testing principles.

Equipment questions may involve forced-air furnaces, burners, blowers, controls, safety devices, air distribution components, and related mechanical equipment. Maintenance questions can address troubleshooting, inspection, cleaning, adjustment, component condition, airflow problems, combustion concerns, and safe servicing practices.

Plan-reading questions may require candidates to interpret mechanical drawings, symbols, duct layouts, equipment locations, piping routes, dimensions, elevations, and other details shown on construction documents. Candidates should practice reading the entire drawing before selecting an answer or beginning a calculation.

### Licensing Steps

The Delaware Board of Plumbing, Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Examiners regulates Master HVACR and Master HVACR Restricted licenses. Applicants must complete the applicable licensing process and receive Board approval before sitting for the master examination.

2. **Create or access a DELPROS account.** Delaware professional licensing applications are submitted through the state’s online licensing system.
3. **Complete the license application.** Provide the requested personal, apprenticeship, employment, experience, examination, and licensing information.
4. **Gather supporting documentation.** Required records may include apprenticeship credentials, a journeyperson certificate, employment verification, supervised experience documentation, and license verifications.
5. **Submit documents as directed.** Some records may need to be completed or submitted by employers, supervisors, training programs, or other licensing authorities.

8. **Schedule and take the examination.** Follow the registration instructions supplied after approval and comply with all current testing rules.
9. **Complete remaining license requirements.** The Board must receive the required examination result and supporting documentation before issuing the professional license.

### State Requirements

Delaware recognizes an unrestricted Master HVACR license and multiple Master HVACR Restricted specialties. The Heating – Forced Air Systems, Ventilation, and Gas Piping classification limits the license holder to the services authorized within that specialty. Contractors who intend to offer services across multiple HVACR specialties may need to pursue the unrestricted Master HVACR classification.

Applicants with a qualifying journeyperson certificate earned after completing an accepted HVACR or restricted HVACR apprenticeship must document two years of post-apprenticeship HVACR experience under appropriate supervision. Experience earned before receipt of the journeyperson certificate does not count toward that two-year requirement.

Applicants who do not possess the qualifying journeyperson certificate must document seven years of HVACR or HVACR restricted experience under the direct supervision of a master HVACR licensee, master HVACR restricted licensee, or similarly licensed professional in another state. These applicants must also satisfy the applicable apprenticeship equivalency examination requirement.

Applicants must provide documentation that demonstrates experience relevant to the requested specialty. Records may need to be supplied by employers, supervisors, apprenticeship programs, schools, or licensing agencies. The Delaware Board determines whether the applicant’s training, experience, examination results, and other qualifications meet the state’s requirements.

A Delaware professional license is separate from a Delaware business license. Most contracting businesses need both professional authorization and proper business registration. Additional obligations may involve taxes, insurance, permits, employment accounts, local requirements, and recordkeeping.

### Test Information and Study Materials

Begin preparation by dividing the exam outline into manageable study areas. Create separate sessions for general knowledge, piping, systems and sizing, equipment, maintenance, and plan reading. Give additional time to systems and sizing because this category represents the largest portion of the published examination outline.

Use course lessons together with the included books. After reviewing a lesson, locate the related subject in the appropriate reference. This reinforces technical understanding while building the navigation skills needed during an open-book test.

Practice fuel gas sizing by identifying the correct table and confirming the system conditions before selecting a pipe size. Review developed length, connected demand, pressure, allowable pressure drop, and the notes that govern the table.

For mechanical-system questions, study duct construction, ventilation, exhaust, equipment installation, airflow, combustion air, and clearances. For maintenance questions, review system operation, troubleshooting sequences, component condition, controls, airflow restrictions, burner performance, and safe servicing procedures.

Plan-reading practice should include mechanical symbols, duct dimensions, equipment locations, piping routes, elevations, and system layouts. Read the complete plan before focusing on one measurement or component.

Complete timed practice sessions as the examination approaches. Answer familiar questions first, mark longer code searches or calculations, and return to them after completing the remaining items. Review incorrect answers and locate the supporting information in the books.
