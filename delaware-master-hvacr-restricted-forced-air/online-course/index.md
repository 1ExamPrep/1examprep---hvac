### Exam Details

The Delaware Master HVACR Restricted Forced Air Systems, Ventilation & Gas Piping Contractor examination evaluates the knowledge needed to perform services within this restricted HVACR specialty. Delaware recognizes the classification as Heating – Forced Air Systems, Ventilation, and Gas Piping.

- General knowledge
- Piping
- Systems and sizing
- Equipment
- Maintenance
- Plan reading

Systems and sizing represent a major portion of the examination. Candidates should review airflow, duct systems, equipment capacity, ventilation, combustion air, fuel gas demand, piping capacity, system arrangement, and sizing principles. Some questions may require the use of code tables, while others may test the application of HVACR theory and field knowledge.

Candidates should understand how forced-air heating and ventilation components work together as a system rather than studying each component in isolation.

Piping questions may cover fuel gas piping materials, fittings, joining methods, supports, protection from damage, valves, regulators, appliance connections, pressure conditions, testing, and sizing. Candidates should practice identifying the conditions presented in a problem before selecting a code table or installation requirement.

Equipment questions may involve furnaces, burners, blowers, motors, controls, safety devices, duct components, air-distribution equipment, and other mechanical-system components. Preparation should include both equipment operation and common installation considerations.

Maintenance questions may address inspection, cleaning, adjustment, troubleshooting, airflow restrictions, burner performance, component condition, control operation, and safe service procedures. Candidates should be prepared to identify likely causes of system problems and appropriate corrective actions.

Plan-reading questions may require interpretation of mechanical drawings, symbols, equipment locations, duct layouts, piping routes, dimensions, elevations, and system arrangements. ### State Requirements

Delaware issues an unrestricted Master HVACR license and several Master HVACR Restricted licenses. A restricted license authorizes the holder to perform services only within the specialty identified on the license. The Heating – Forced Air Systems, Ventilation, and Gas Piping classification does not provide the full scope of authority associated with an unrestricted Master HVACR license.

Applicants with a qualifying journeyperson certificate earned after completing an accepted HVACR or restricted HVACR apprenticeship must document two years of applicable post-apprenticeship experience under the supervision of a properly licensed master professional.

Applicants who do not possess the qualifying journeyperson certificate must document seven years of HVACR or restricted HVACR experience under the direct supervision of a Master HVACR licensee, Master HVACR Restricted licensee, or an individual holding a similar level of licensure in another state.

The non-apprenticeship pathway also requires successful completion of the apprenticeship equivalency examination approved by the Delaware Board. A restricted HVACR applicant completes the portion applicable to the specialty being pursued.

Applicants must pass the standardized examination designated for the requested license after satisfying the applicable training and experience requirements. Delaware also requires applicants to meet professional conduct and fitness standards established by law and regulation.

Official employment and experience records should demonstrate work related to the restricted forced-air systems, ventilation, and gas piping classification. The Board determines whether the applicant’s documented experience, training, examinations, and other qualifications satisfy Delaware requirements.

A Delaware professional license is separate from a Delaware business license. Professionals who plan to operate a contracting company may also need business registration, tax accounts, insurance, permits, local approvals, and other operating credentials.

The restricted license must be used within its authorized scope. Professionals intending to provide services in multiple restricted HVACR specialties may need to apply for the unrestricted Master HVACR license.

### Test Information and Study Materials

Create a study schedule that divides the exam into general knowledge, piping, systems and sizing, equipment, maintenance, and plan reading. Give additional time to subjects that require lengthy calculations, code searches, or technical interpretation.

For code-based questions, identify the main keyword and use the index before searching page by page. Read the entire section and check definitions, exceptions, referenced sections, and table notes. A nearby condition may change how the rule applies.

For fuel gas sizing, confirm the system pressure, developed length, connected demand, allowable pressure drop, and sizing method before choosing a table. Read the complete table heading and notes before selecting a value.

For ventilation and duct-system questions, review airflow, duct sizing principles, exhaust requirements, equipment connections, combustion air, system clearances, and installation conditions. Understand the purpose behind the requirements rather than relying only on memorization.

For equipment and maintenance preparation, study forced-air furnace operation, burners, blowers, motors, controls, limit devices, airflow restrictions, inspection procedures, and troubleshooting sequences. Practice identifying how one failed or improperly adjusted component can affect the entire system.

For plan-reading questions, review the complete drawing before beginning a calculation. Identify symbols, dimensions, elevations, duct routes, equipment locations, fuel gas lines, and system connections.

Complete timed practice sessions as the examination approaches. Answer familiar questions first, mark longer searches or calculations, and return to them later. Review missed answers by locating the supporting information in the appropriate reference.
