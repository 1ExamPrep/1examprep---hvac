Exam Details

The Delaware Master HVACR Restricted - Heating - Forced Air Systems, Ventilation, and Gas Piping examination currently contains 70 questions. Candidates receive 3 hours to complete the examination.

The current examination content outline includes:

  • General Knowledge: 15 questions
  • Piping: 12 questions
  • Systems & Sizing: 20 questions
  • Equipment: 6 questions
  • Maintenance: 12 questions
  • Plan Reading: 5 questions

Systems & Sizing is the largest individual category with 20 questions. Candidates should understand airflow, duct sizing concepts, heating requirements, equipment capacity, system configuration, distribution, and the relationship between proper sizing and system performance.

General Knowledge accounts for 15 questions. This category can include heating fundamentals, combustion, forced-air operation, ventilation, controls, mechanical principles, gas-system concepts, and terminology used in HVACR work.

Piping and Maintenance each account for 12 questions. Piping preparation should include gas piping materials, routing, sizing concepts, testing, valves, fittings, supports, and appliance connections. Maintenance preparation should include troubleshooting, inspection, cleaning, adjustments, airflow checks, controls, combustion-related issues, and recognition of abnormal system operation.

Equipment accounts for 6 questions, while Plan Reading accounts for 5 questions. These areas require candidates to understand equipment function, installation relationships, mechanical drawings, duct layouts, symbols, and system configuration.

Delaware requires a minimum passing score of 70%.

The current ProV Candidate Information Bulletin identifies approved references that may be used during testing. The Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning, 22nd Edition included with this online practice-question product is used as a study reference for preparation. Candidates should follow the current ProV Candidate Information Bulletin for the exact edition and reference materials permitted inside the examination room.

Open-book testing requires efficient reference navigation. With 70 questions in 3 hours, candidates should avoid searching randomly through multiple books. A better strategy is to identify the subject first, determine which reference is most likely to contain the answer, and then navigate directly to the relevant chapter, section, table, or technical topic.

Fuel gas piping, appliance installation, combustion air, gas venting, and related requirements generally point toward the International Fuel Gas Code. Mechanical-system questions involving ductwork, ventilation, equipment installation, exhaust, air distribution, and related mechanical requirements may lead to the International Mechanical Code.

Technical heating questions may require broader trade knowledge. Practice should therefore combine code navigation with a working understanding of furnaces, blowers, controls, combustion, airflow, duct systems, heating cycles, and maintenance.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong preparation plan should begin with Systems & Sizing because it represents 20 of the 70 examination questions. Candidates should understand how heating requirements, equipment capacity, airflow, ducts, system resistance, and distribution interact.

Forced-air systems depend on correct airflow. Candidates should review blower operation, supply and return systems, duct design principles, registers, grilles, filters, dampers, and the factors that influence air delivery throughout a building.

Ductwork should be studied as a complete distribution system rather than as isolated components. A properly functioning forced-air system depends on appropriate duct sizing, routing, sealing, support, return-air design, and adequate airflow at the equipment.

Heating equipment should include furnaces and related components. Candidates should understand burners, heat exchangers, ignition systems, controls, blowers, limit devices, thermostats, safety switches, and the sequence of operation.

Combustion is another important subject. Review fuel, air, ignition, combustion products, draft, combustion air, venting, and the conditions needed for safe and efficient operation of fuel-burning equipment.

Piping accounts for 12 questions. Candidates should understand gas piping materials, fittings, joints, supports, shutoff valves, appliance connections, system testing, routing, protection, and sizing concepts.

The International Fuel Gas Code, 2018 should be used actively during study. Practice locating provisions related to gas piping, appliances, combustion air, venting, and installation requirements rather than simply reading the code from beginning to end.

Fuel gas sizing requires careful attention to system demand, length, pressure, fuel type, and the applicable sizing method. Candidates should practice determining which table or method applies before selecting a pipe size.

Venting should be connected to combustion and equipment operation. Candidates should understand why venting is required, how combustion products move through venting systems, and how equipment type and installation conditions affect vent requirements.

The International Mechanical Code, 2018 can support preparation involving forced-air systems, ventilation, duct installation, mechanical equipment, exhaust, and related requirements.

Ventilation is a key part of this restricted specialty. Study mechanical ventilation concepts, outdoor air, exhaust, air distribution, equipment requirements, and how ventilation interacts with heating and building operation.

General Knowledge accounts for 15 questions. Candidates should strengthen their understanding of heating theory, combustion, airflow, forced-air systems, controls, mechanical equipment, fuel gas principles, and general HVACR terminology.

Technical knowledge should be applied rather than memorized. When reviewing a furnace, for example, understand what each component does, how the sequence begins, how the burners and blower are controlled, and which safety devices can interrupt operation.

Equipment accounts for 6 questions. Study furnace components, blowers, motors, controls, heat exchangers, air-handling components, dampers, filters, and related mechanical equipment.

Equipment questions may ask candidates to connect a symptom with an operating condition. Understanding the normal sequence of operation makes troubleshooting questions easier because the candidate can identify which stage of the system is not behaving as expected.

Maintenance accounts for 12 questions. Candidates should review preventive maintenance, filters, belts, blower assemblies, burners, heat exchangers, controls, venting, airflow, equipment inspection, combustion-related concerns, and recognition of unsafe or abnormal conditions.

Maintenance preparation should focus on causes and effects. Restricted airflow, for example, can influence equipment temperatures and system performance. A dirty component, failed control, blocked vent, or improperly adjusted burner can affect the entire heating system.

Plan Reading accounts for 5 questions. Candidates should practice reading mechanical drawings, duct layouts, equipment symbols, dimensions, piping arrangements, and other information used to communicate the intended installation.

Plan-reading preparation should include tracing airflow from the equipment through supply ducts and back through the return system. Candidates should also be comfortable following fuel gas piping routes and identifying how equipment is arranged within a building.

The Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning, 22nd Edition included with this practice-question product can help reinforce the underlying trade concepts used in forced-air heating and mechanical systems.

When a practice question is missed, identify the actual reason. The issue may involve weak heating theory, code navigation, gas piping, sizing, airflow, equipment operation, plan interpretation, or maintenance knowledge.

Create separate review categories for repeated problem areas. If several missed questions involve gas piping, return to the relevant fuel gas material. If errors involve system sizing or airflow, concentrate study on forced-air relationships and equipment performance.

As preparation progresses, move from single-topic practice into mixed sets. A realistic heating scenario may require understanding equipment, gas piping, venting, combustion air, ductwork, controls, maintenance, and code requirements at the same time.

Use the included 3 months access to repeat this study cycle. Begin with individual topics, reinforce weak areas, practice reference navigation, and gradually work through more mixed-topic questions as examination day approaches.
