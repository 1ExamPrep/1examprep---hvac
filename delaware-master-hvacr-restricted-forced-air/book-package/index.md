### Exam Details

The Delaware Master HVACR Restricted Heating – Forced Air Systems, Ventilation, and Gas Piping examination evaluates the knowledge associated with this restricted HVACR specialty. The license classification is intended for professionals providing services involving forced-air heating systems, related ductwork, ventilation, and gas piping within the scope authorized by Delaware.

The examination contains 70 questions and allows three hours for completion. The published examination outline divides the test into the following areas:

- General Knowledge: 15 questions
- Piping: 12 questions
- Systems and Sizing: 20 questions
- Equipment: 6 questions
- Maintenance: 12 questions
- Plan Reading: 5 questions

Systems and sizing represent the largest category. Candidates should prepare to review airflow, duct systems, forced-air distribution, ventilation, combustion air, equipment capacity, system arrangement, fuel gas demand, piping capacity, and other design considerations. Some questions may require interpretation of a table, diagram, equipment specification, or code requirement.

General knowledge questions may involve heat transfer, combustion, airflow, temperature, pressure, tools, materials, terminology, controls, safety devices, and accepted HVACR practices. Candidates should understand how system components interact rather than studying equipment and code provisions as unrelated subjects.

Piping questions may address fuel gas materials, fittings, joining methods, protection, supports, valves, regulators, appliance connections, testing principles, and sizing. Candidates should be able to identify the conditions presented in a problem before selecting a code provision or sizing table.

Equipment questions may involve furnaces, burners, heat exchangers, blowers, motors, controls, limit devices, safety switches, duct components, and air-distribution equipment. Preparation should include equipment purpose, operating principles, installation considerations, and common system relationships.

Maintenance questions may address inspection procedures, cleaning, adjustment, troubleshooting, airflow restrictions, burner performance, control operation, component condition, and safe service practices. Candidates should be prepared to evaluate symptoms and identify the condition, component, or system issue most closely associated with the problem.

Plan-reading questions can require interpretation of mechanical drawings, symbols, equipment locations, duct routes, gas piping layouts, dimensions, elevations, and system connections. Review the entire drawing before selecting an answer because information shown in one area may affect the interpretation of another.

### State Requirements

Delaware offers an unrestricted Master HVACR license and multiple Master HVACR Restricted specialties. The Heating – Forced Air Systems, Ventilation, and Gas Piping license permits services within that specialty. It does not automatically provide the full scope of authority associated with an unrestricted Master HVACR license.

Professionals intending to provide services across more than one restricted HVACR specialty must pursue the license classification appropriate for the complete scope of work they plan to offer. License holders are responsible for remaining within the authority granted by their Delaware credential.

Applicants with a qualifying journeyperson certificate from an apprenticeship program that meets the applicable standards must submit the certificate with the application. These applicants must also document two years of post-apprenticeship HVACR experience under the direct supervision of a properly licensed master professional.

Experience earned before receipt of the qualifying journeyperson certificate does not count toward the two-year post-apprenticeship experience requirement.

Applicants who do not possess the qualifying journeyperson certificate must document seven years of HVACR experience under the direct supervision of a Master HVACR licensee, Master HVACR Restricted licensee, or an individual holding a similar level of licensure in another jurisdiction. These applicants must also satisfy the applicable apprenticeship equivalency examination requirements.

Employment records should demonstrate experience related to forced-air heating systems, ventilation, gas piping, or other work relevant to the requested restricted classification. The Delaware Board determines whether an applicant’s documented training, experience, examination results, and supporting records satisfy the state requirements.

A Delaware professional HVACR license is separate from a Delaware business license. A professional planning to operate a contracting company may also need business registration, tax accounts, insurance, permits, and local approvals. ### Test Information and Study Materials

Begin with a study schedule based on the six examination categories. Assign separate review sessions to general knowledge, piping, systems and sizing, equipment, maintenance, and plan reading. Give additional attention to systems and sizing because it represents the largest part of the published examination outline.

For fuel gas questions, practice locating material through the index and chapter headings. Review definitions before applying a requirement because the meaning of a technical term can determine which code provision applies.

For sizing problems, identify the system conditions before selecting a table. Confirm the pressure, allowable pressure drop, developed length, connected demand, fuel type, and sizing method. Read the complete table title and notes before selecting a value.

For mechanical-code preparation, review ventilation, exhaust, ducts, equipment installation, combustion air, appliance requirements, and system clearances. Study how general mechanical provisions apply to forced-air heating and ventilation systems.

For equipment and maintenance questions, review furnaces, burners, heat exchangers, blowers, motors, controls, safety devices, airflow restrictions, inspection procedures, and troubleshooting sequences. Understand how a failure or adjustment in one component can affect overall system operation.

For plan-reading questions, identify symbols, dimensions, equipment locations, duct routes, piping layouts, and elevations before beginning a calculation. Read notes and legends because they can provide information that is not shown directly on the drawing.

Complete timed practice sessions as the examination approaches. Answer familiar questions first, mark longer calculations or searches, and return to them later. After each session, review incorrect answers and locate the supporting information in the applicable reference.
