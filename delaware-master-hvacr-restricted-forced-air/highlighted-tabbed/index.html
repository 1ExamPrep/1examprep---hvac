Exam Details

The Delaware Master HVACR Restricted Heating – Forced Air Systems, Ventilation, and Gas Piping examination evaluates knowledge associated with work performed within this restricted HVACR specialty. The license classification covers forced-air heating systems, related ductwork, ventilation, gas piping, and connected equipment within the scope authorized by Delaware.

The examination contains 70 questions and allows three hours for completion. The published subject areas include:

  • General Knowledge
  • Piping
  • Systems and Sizing
  • Equipment
  • Maintenance
  • Plan Reading

General knowledge preparation may include heating principles, airflow, heat transfer, combustion, tools, materials, system components, controls, safety devices, terminology, and accepted HVACR practices. Candidates should understand how the individual parts of a forced-air system work together rather than studying each component as an isolated subject.

Piping questions may address fuel gas materials, fittings, joining methods, supports, protection from physical damage, valves, regulators, appliance connections, pressure conditions, system testing, and pipe sizing. Candidates should practice identifying the system conditions described in a question before selecting a code section or sizing table.

Systems and sizing require a working understanding of airflow, ventilation, duct systems, equipment capacity, fuel demand, pipe capacity, system arrangement, and installation conditions. Some questions may require interpreting a technical table, diagram, or code provision. Careful reading is important because the correct answer may depend on a specific pressure, length, capacity, location, or equipment condition.

Equipment questions may involve furnaces, burners, heat exchangers, blowers, motors, controls, limit devices, safety switches, duct components, and air-distribution equipment. Candidates should review equipment operation, component purpose, installation considerations, and common system relationships.

Maintenance questions may involve inspection, cleaning, adjustments, burner operation, airflow restrictions, troubleshooting, control problems, component condition, and safe service procedures. Practical field experience can be valuable, but candidates should also become familiar with the technical terminology used in the reference books.

Plan-reading questions may require candidates to interpret mechanical drawings, symbols, equipment locations, duct routes, fuel gas piping layouts, dimensions, elevations, and system connections. Review the complete drawing before choosing an answer because information shown in one area may affect another part of the system.

Licensing Steps

The Delaware Board of Plumbing, Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Examiners regulates Master HVACR and Master HVACR Restricted licenses. Candidates must complete the state licensing process and receive approval before taking the required master examination.

  1. Select the correct classification. Apply for the Delaware Master HVACR Restricted license and choose Heating – Forced Air Systems, Ventilation, and Gas Piping as the specialty.
  2. Create or access a DELPROS account. Delaware professional license applications are submitted through the Delaware Professional Regulation Online Services system.
  3. Complete the application. Provide the personal, apprenticeship, employment, experience, examination, and licensing information requested by the state.
  4. Gather supporting records. Documentation may include apprenticeship credentials, a journeyperson certificate, employment verification, supervised experience records, and verification of licenses held in other jurisdictions.
  5. Submit documents as directed. Some materials may be uploaded by the applicant, while others may need to be completed or submitted by an employer, supervisor, apprenticeship program, school, or licensing authority.
  6. Complete any applicable equivalency requirement. Applicants without the qualifying apprenticeship credential may need to satisfy the apprenticeship equivalency requirement applicable to the restricted specialty.
  7. Receive Board approval to test. The Delaware Board reviews the application and supporting documents before authorizing the candidate to take the master examination.
  8. Register for the examination. Follow the testing provider’s scheduling instructions after receiving approval.
  9. Take the examination. Bring only authorized materials and follow the current identification, reference-book, calculator, and testing-conduct rules.
  10. Complete outstanding licensing requirements. The Board must receive the examination result and all required documentation before issuing a professional license.

Applicants should begin collecting official records early. Employment verification, apprenticeship documentation, and out-of-state license verification may require action from third parties. Complete documentation helps the Board evaluate the applicant’s qualifications.

State Requirements

Delaware offers an unrestricted Master HVACR license and several Master HVACR Restricted specialties. The Heating – Forced Air Systems, Ventilation, and Gas Piping classification authorizes services within that specialty. It does not provide the complete scope associated with an unrestricted Master HVACR license.

Professionals intending to provide services in more than one restricted HVACR specialty must pursue the license classification appropriate for the full scope of work they plan to offer. License holders are responsible for remaining within the authority granted by their Delaware credential.

Applicants with a qualifying journeyperson certificate issued after completion of an accepted HVACR or HVACR restricted apprenticeship must document two years of applicable post-apprenticeship experience under the direct supervision of a properly licensed master professional.

Experience completed before receiving the qualifying journeyperson certificate does not count toward the required post-apprenticeship experience period.

Applicants without the qualifying journeyperson certificate must document seven years of HVACR or HVACR restricted experience under the direct supervision of a Master HVACR licensee, Master HVACR Restricted licensee, or an individual holding a similar level of licensure in another jurisdiction.

The experience pathway also requires successful completion of the apprenticeship equivalency examination approved for the applicable classification. Restricted applicants complete the portion associated with the specialty they are pursuing.

Employment records should demonstrate experience related to forced-air heating, ventilation, gas piping, equipment, maintenance, or other work relevant to the requested license. The Delaware Board determines whether the applicant’s training, experience, examination results, and supporting documents satisfy the licensing requirements.

A Delaware professional license is separate from a Delaware business license. A contractor planning to operate a company may also need business registration, tax accounts, insurance, permits, and local approvals. These requirements are separate from the professional examination and license.

Test Information and Study Materials

Begin preparation with a study schedule based on the exam subjects. Assign separate review periods to general knowledge, piping, systems and sizing, equipment, maintenance, and plan reading. Give additional attention to topics that require longer searches, calculations, or technical interpretation.

For fuel gas questions, practice locating information through the index and chapter headings. Review definitions before applying a requirement because the meaning of a technical term can determine which provision applies.

For sizing questions, identify all system conditions before selecting a table. Confirm the pressure, developed length, connected demand, allowable pressure drop, fuel type, and sizing method. Read the complete table heading and notes before choosing a value.

For mechanical-code preparation, review ventilation, exhaust, ducts, equipment installation, combustion air, appliance requirements, and system clearances. Study how general provisions apply to forced-air heating and ventilation systems.

For equipment and maintenance questions, review furnaces, burners, heat exchangers, blowers, motors, controls, safety devices, airflow restrictions, inspection procedures, and troubleshooting sequences. Understand how one malfunctioning component can affect overall system performance.

For plan-reading preparation, identify symbols, dimensions, equipment locations, duct routes, piping layouts, and elevations before beginning a calculation. Review notes and legends because they may contain information not shown directly on the drawing.

Complete timed practice sessions as the examination approaches. Answer familiar questions first, mark longer calculations or reference searches, and return to them later. Review missed answers by finding the supporting information in the appropriate book.
