Exam Details

The Delaware Master HVACR Restricted Heating – Hydronic Systems and Gas Piping examination evaluates knowledge associated with work performed within this restricted HVACR specialty. Candidates should prepare for questions involving hydronic heating, boilers, piping, pumps, valves, controls, fuel gas systems, oil, water conditions, inspection, and maintenance.

Important examination study areas include:

  • Heating principles and heat transfer
  • Hydronic system layouts and circulation
  • Hot-water and steam heating concepts
  • Boiler construction and operation
  • Low-water and pressure-related safety devices
  • Piping materials and joining methods
  • Circulating pumps and system flow
  • Valves, controls, and zoning equipment
  • Expansion tanks and pressure control
  • Air elimination and system venting
  • Fuel gas piping and appliance connections
  • Combustion air and venting requirements
  • Burner operation and combustion principles
  • Water treatment and makeup water
  • Testing, inspection, maintenance, and troubleshooting

General knowledge questions may involve heat transfer, temperature, pressure, combustion, tools, materials, equipment, controls, terminology, and accepted HVACR practices. Candidates should understand how individual components affect the operation of the complete system rather than studying each part in isolation.

Hydronic-system questions may address circulation, zoning, expansion, air removal, system pressure, temperature control, radiation equipment, and common piping arrangements. Candidates should be able to trace the movement of water or steam through a system and identify the purpose of the major components.

Piping, pump, and valve questions may involve pipe materials, joining methods, pump operation, flow direction, isolation, balancing, check valves, control valves, mixing valves, and maintenance. A question may require the candidate to determine how a component affects pressure, temperature, circulation, or overall system performance.

Boiler-related questions may cover construction, burners, controls, fittings, safety devices, operating procedures, inspections, low-water conditions, pressure, temperature, maintenance, combustion, and troubleshooting. Candidates should understand which controls regulate normal operation and which safety devices respond to unsafe conditions.

Water-related subjects may include treatment, makeup water, circulation, expansion, air removal, scale, corrosion, and system conditions that influence boiler efficiency and service life. Testing and inspection questions may address procedures used to evaluate piping, controls, safety devices, equipment condition, and system operation.

Licensing Steps

The Delaware Board of Plumbing, Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Examiners regulates Master HVACR and Master HVACR Restricted licenses. Applicants must complete the applicable licensing process and receive approval before taking the master examination.

  1. Select the proper classification. Apply for the Delaware Master HVACR Restricted license and select Heating – Hydronic Systems and Gas Piping as the specialty.
  2. Create or access a DELPROS account. Delaware professional license applications are managed through the state’s online licensing system.
  3. Complete the application. Provide the requested personal, apprenticeship, employment, experience, examination, and licensing information.
  4. Gather supporting documentation. Required records may include apprenticeship credentials, a journeyperson certificate, employment verification, supervised experience documentation, and license verification from other jurisdictions.
  5. Submit documents as directed. Certain records may need to be completed or submitted by an employer, supervisor, apprenticeship program, school, or licensing authority.
  6. Complete any applicable equivalency requirement. Applicants without the qualifying apprenticeship credential may need to satisfy the apprenticeship equivalency or bypass requirement connected with this restricted specialty.
  7. Receive Board approval to test. The Board reviews the application and supporting documentation before authorizing the applicant to sit for the master examination.
  8. Register for the examination. Follow the testing provider’s scheduling instructions after receiving approval.
  9. Take the required examination. Follow all current rules involving identification, authorized references, calculators, appointment procedures, and testing conduct.
  10. Complete remaining licensing requirements. The Board must receive the required examination result and supporting records before issuing the professional license.

Applicants should begin gathering official records early. Apprenticeship documents, employment verification, and out-of-state license records may require action by third parties. Incomplete documentation can delay application review and examination authorization.

State Requirements

Delaware offers an unrestricted Master HVACR license and several Master HVACR Restricted specialties. Heating – Hydronic Systems and Gas Piping is one of the restricted classifications recognized by the state.

A restricted license authorizes the holder to provide services within the specialty identified on the license. It does not provide the complete scope associated with an unrestricted Master HVACR license. Professionals planning to perform work in multiple restricted HVACR specialties must pursue the license classification appropriate for their full scope of services.

The qualification pathway depends on the applicant’s apprenticeship background, journeyperson credential, supervised experience, and supporting documentation. Applicants with a qualifying journeyperson certificate earned after completing an accepted HVACR or restricted HVACR apprenticeship must document the required post-apprenticeship experience under appropriate supervision.

Applicants who do not possess the qualifying journeyperson certificate must document the required period of HVACR or restricted HVACR experience under the supervision of a properly licensed master professional. These applicants must also complete the applicable apprenticeship equivalency or bypass requirements before receiving authorization for the master examination.

Experience records should demonstrate work relevant to hydronic heating systems and gas piping. Applicable experience may involve boilers, circulation systems, piping, pumps, valves, controls, burners, fuel systems, equipment installation, maintenance, testing, inspection, and related HVACR services.

The Delaware Board determines whether the applicant’s training, experience, examination results, and supporting records satisfy the requirements for licensure. Purchasing examination references does not replace any application, experience, training, examination, or professional requirement.

A Delaware professional license is separate from a Delaware business license. A contractor planning to operate a company may also need entity registration, tax accounts, insurance, permits, local approvals, and other operating credentials. Test Information and Study Materials

Begin with a study schedule organized around the major examination subjects. Assign separate review periods to general knowledge, hydronic systems, piping, pumps, valves, fuel gas, oil, boilers, water, testing, and inspection.

For hydronic-system preparation, review circulation, zoning, system pressure, temperature control, expansion, air removal, piping arrangements, and heat-distribution equipment. Practice tracing the movement of water or steam through different system configurations.

For pumps and valves, study flow direction, pump placement, pressure relationships, isolation, balancing, check valves, mixing valves, and control valves. Understand what each device does and how improper operation can affect system performance.

For boiler preparation, connect each operating control and safety device with the condition it monitors. Review burners, combustion, low-water protection, pressure controls, temperature controls, inspections, cleaning, water treatment, and common operating problems.

For fuel gas questions, practice locating information through the code index and chapter headings. Read definitions and table conditions carefully. The correct answer may depend on the equipment type, piping material, pressure, demand, location, or installation arrangement described in the question.

Complete timed practice sessions as the exam approaches. Answer familiar questions first, mark longer searches or technical problems, and return to them later. Review incorrect responses by locating the supporting information in the applicable reference.
