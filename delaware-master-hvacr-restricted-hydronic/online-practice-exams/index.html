Exam Details

The Delaware Master HVACR Restricted - Heating - Hydronic Systems and Gas Piping examination contains 80 questions. Candidates receive 3 hours to complete the test.

The current examination content outline includes:

  • General Knowledge: 22 questions
  • Hydronic Systems: 10 questions
  • Piping, Pumps, Valves: 10 questions
  • Fuel Gas and Oil: 12 questions
  • Boilers - Low and High Pressure: 14 questions
  • Water: 8 questions
  • Testing and Inspection: 4 questions

General Knowledge is the largest category with 22 questions. Candidates should be prepared for heating principles, system components, controls, combustion, temperature and pressure relationships, mechanical fundamentals, safety, and terminology used in hydronic work.

Boilers - Low and High Pressure accounts for 14 questions. Boiler preparation should include equipment, operating principles, controls, safety devices, pressure and temperature relationships, fittings, combustion, maintenance, and the role boilers play within hydronic heating systems.

Fuel Gas and Oil accounts for 12 questions. Candidates should understand fuel characteristics, piping systems, combustion, appliance connections, venting, fuel delivery, and safety requirements associated with fuel-burning heating equipment.

Hydronic Systems and Piping, Pumps, Valves each account for 10 questions. These categories require a working understanding of how water is circulated, controlled, heated, distributed, and returned throughout a complete hydronic system.

Water accounts for 8 questions, while Testing and Inspection accounts for 4 questions. Delaware requires a minimum score of 70% to pass the examination.

Open Book Test

The Delaware Master HVACR Restricted Heating, Hydronic Systems and Gas Piping examination is an open-book test.

The current ProV Candidate Information Bulletin permits the International Fuel Gas Code, 2018, International Mechanical Code, 2018, Low Pressure Boilers, 5th Edition, and an approved refrigeration and air-conditioning technical reference during testing.

The current official bulletin identifies Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning, 21st Edition as the permitted edition, or approved substitute mechanical study guides. The 22nd Edition listed with this online practice-question product is used as a study reference for preparation and should not be assumed to be permitted in the exam room unless the current testing bulletin specifically allows it.

Open-book testing still requires efficient reference use. With 80 questions in 3 hours, candidates should not expect to research every answer from the beginning. The better strategy is to understand the system or concept first and then use the appropriate reference to confirm a code rule, table, equipment requirement, or technical detail.

Fuel gas piping, appliance installation, venting, combustion air, and related gas requirements generally point toward the International Fuel Gas Code. Mechanical equipment, hydronic-system installation, piping, ventilation, and related mechanical requirements may lead to the International Mechanical Code.

Boiler questions can be reinforced with Low Pressure Boilers, while broader heating-system operation and HVACR fundamentals can be studied through Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning.

  1. Confirm that the Hydronic Systems and Gas Piping restriction matches your intended work. Delaware offers this classification as a specific Master HVACR Restricted specialty.
  2. Create or access a DELPROS account. Delaware uses DELPROS for professional licensing applications and related services.
  3. Submit the Master HVACR Restricted application. Select Heating - Hydronic Systems and Gas Piping as the requested specialty.
  4. Document the applicable work experience. Applicants must provide evidence of the supervised HVACR experience required for their licensing pathway.
  5. Provide apprenticeship documentation when applicable. Applicants using an accepted federally approved HVACR journeyman pathway must submit the required apprenticeship completion documentation.
  6. Complete the apprenticeship bypass process when required. Applicants without an accepted journeyman certificate may be required to complete the portion of the bypass examination applicable to the hydronic restriction.
  7. Receive Board approval to test. The Delaware Board must approve the applicant before the Master HVACR Restricted examination can be scheduled.
  8. Register with ProV. Approved candidates receive current scheduling instructions and examination information from ProV.
  9. Prepare for the open-book examination. Review General Knowledge, Hydronic Systems, Piping, Pumps and Valves, Fuel Gas and Oil, Boilers, Water, Testing and Inspection.
  10. Take the 80-question examination. Candidates receive 3 hours to complete the test.
  11. Earn the required passing score. Delaware requires at least 70%.
  12. Complete remaining licensing requirements. The Board must receive the official passing result and verify all applicable licensing requirements before issuing the license.

State Requirements

The Delaware Master HVACR Restricted Heating, Hydronic Systems and Gas Piping license is administered through the Delaware Board of Plumbing, Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Examiners.

Delaware recognizes several restricted HVACR specialties. The Hydronic Systems and Gas Piping classification limits the license holder's work to hydronic systems and associated gas piping services rather than the full range of HVACR work permitted under an unrestricted Master HVACR license.

Applicants must apply through DELPROS and provide the experience, apprenticeship, and other supporting documentation required for their particular licensing pathway.

Applicants using a federally approved HVACR journeyman certificate must provide their apprenticeship completion documentation and qualifying supervised work experience after receiving the certificate.

Applicants who do not hold an accepted journeyman certificate may qualify through an experience pathway and apprenticeship bypass process. Delaware's current rules allow applicants pursuing a restricted HVACR classification to take only the portion of the bypass examination relevant to the requested restriction.

The Delaware Board must approve candidates before they may take the licensure examination. ProV facilitates the required examination and supplies approved candidates with the current testing information.

Delaware requires a score of at least 70% to pass. Applicants who do not achieve a passing score after three attempts must reapply as a new applicant and obtain Board approval again after the applicable waiting period.

Passing the examination alone does not automatically issue the license. The Board must also validate the applicant's experience, application materials, apprenticeship documentation when applicable, and other licensure requirements.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong preparation plan should begin with General Knowledge, which accounts for 22 questions. Candidates should understand heat transfer, temperature, pressure, combustion, hydronic terminology, mechanical controls, heating equipment, and the relationships among system components.

Hydronic Systems account for 10 questions. Candidates should understand how heated water is circulated from the heat source through distribution equipment and back to the boiler.

Study supply and return piping, zoning, flow, temperature differences, expansion, air elimination, system pressure, circulating pumps, heat emitters, and control methods. Hydronic systems should be viewed as complete circuits rather than as unrelated pieces of equipment.

Piping, Pumps, and Valves also accounts for 10 questions. Review pipe materials, fittings, supports, valves, circulators, flow-control devices, expansion tanks, pressure-reducing components, and other equipment used to manage movement through a hydronic system.

Pump questions can require an understanding of flow and system resistance. Candidates should know the role of a circulator and how piping configuration, valves, and system components affect movement through the heating loop.

Fuel Gas and Oil accounts for 12 questions. Study fuel piping, appliance supply, combustion, burners, venting, combustion air, system safety, and the installation requirements associated with fuel-burning equipment.

The International Fuel Gas Code, 2018 should be used regularly during preparation. Practice locating requirements for gas piping, sizing, appliances, venting, combustion air, pressure testing, and shutoff devices.

Boilers - Low and High Pressure account for 14 questions. Candidates should understand boiler construction, operating controls, safety controls, burners, heat transfer, fittings, water levels, pressure, temperature, circulation, and maintenance.

The Low Pressure Boilers, 5th Edition can help reinforce boiler-specific knowledge. Focus on what each component does, how the boiler responds during normal operation, and how controls protect the equipment and system.

Hydronic boiler systems require an understanding of both equipment and water. The boiler produces heat, while pumps, piping, valves, and heat emitters distribute that energy throughout the building.

Water accounts for 8 questions. Candidates should understand water as the heat-transfer medium in hydronic systems, including temperature, pressure, expansion, circulation, system filling, and conditions that can affect equipment performance.

Water quality and air within a hydronic system can also influence performance. Candidates should understand why proper system filling, purging, pressure control, and air elimination matter.

Testing and Inspection accounts for 4 questions. Study system testing, pressure testing, inspection procedures, verification of safe equipment operation, leak detection, and recognition of improper or unsafe installations.

Testing should be viewed as part of the installation process rather than a final formality. Hydronic and gas systems must be checked to confirm that piping, equipment, controls, and connections perform safely before normal operation.

The International Mechanical Code, 2018 should be used to reinforce mechanical-system requirements, equipment installation, hydronic components, clearances, system protection, and related mechanical provisions.

The Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning, 22nd Edition included with this study product can help strengthen broader heating-system understanding, especially when a missed practice question involves equipment operation, controls, heat transfer, or troubleshooting rather than a specific code provision.

For code questions, begin by identifying whether the subject is fuel gas or mechanical. That first decision helps determine which book to open and reduces unnecessary searching.

For boiler questions, determine whether the problem involves equipment operation, control, pressure, water, combustion, or safety. Understanding the category first makes it easier to locate the correct supporting information.

For hydronic-system questions, visualize the entire flow path. Trace water from the boiler through the pump and distribution piping, through the heat emitter, and back to the heat source.

When a practice question is missed, identify the reason. The issue may involve hydronic theory, boiler knowledge, fuel gas code navigation, piping, pump operation, water relationships, or testing requirements.

Organize missed questions into categories so patterns become easier to see. If several errors involve boilers, concentrate on boiler operation and controls. If sizing or gas-system questions are weaker, return to the International Fuel Gas Code and related practice.

Use the included 3 months access to repeat this process across multiple study cycles. Start with individual categories, strengthen weak areas, and then move into mixed-topic question sets that require switching among hydronic systems, boilers, piping, fuel gas, water, and testing.
