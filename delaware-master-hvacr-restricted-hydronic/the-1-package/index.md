### Exam Details

The Delaware Master HVACR Restricted Heating – Hydronic Systems and Gas Piping examination evaluates knowledge associated with the design, installation, construction, maintenance, service, repair, alteration, and modification of hydronic systems and gas piping services for hydronic equipment.

A hydronic system uses liquid or steam to transmit or remove heat. Candidates should understand how boilers, piping, pumps, valves, controls, heat emitters, expansion devices, air-removal equipment, and related components work together to distribute heat safely and effectively.

The examination contains 80 questions and allows three hours for completion. The published content areas are:

- General Knowledge: 22 questions
- Hydronic Systems: 10 questions
- Piping, Pumps, and Valves: 10 questions
- Fuel Gas and Oil: 12 questions
- Boilers – Low and High Pressure: 14 questions
- Water: 8 questions
- Testing and Inspection: 4 questions

General knowledge is the largest category. Preparation should include heat transfer, pressure, temperature, combustion, equipment operation, tools, materials, controls, safety devices, terminology, and accepted HVACR practices.

Hydronic-system questions may address circulation, zoning, heat distribution, expansion, air removal, system pressure, temperature control, radiation equipment, and common piping arrangements. Candidates should understand the purpose of each component and how system conditions can affect performance.

Piping, pumps, and valves may include piping materials, joining methods, pump operation, flow direction, isolation, balancing, check valves, control valves, system layouts, and maintenance. Fuel gas and oil questions may involve supply piping, regulators, valves, burners, combustion air, venting, appliance connections, and safe fuel-system operation.

Boiler preparation should include construction, burners, controls, safety devices, low-water conditions, pressure, temperature, inspections, operating procedures, maintenance, and troubleshooting. Water-related questions may address treatment, makeup water, circulation, expansion, air removal, and conditions that influence boiler efficiency or service life.

### State Requirements

Delaware offers an unrestricted Master HVACR license and several Master HVACR Restricted specialties. Heating – Hydronic Systems and Gas Piping is one of the restricted classifications recognized by the state.

A restricted license authorizes services only within the specialty identified on the license. Professionals who intend to provide services across multiple restricted HVACR specialties must pursue the license classification appropriate for their complete scope of work.

One qualification pathway applies to applicants who received a journeyperson certificate after completing an HVACR or HVACR restricted apprenticeship program that meets the applicable standards. These applicants must document two years of post-apprenticeship HVACR or restricted HVACR experience under qualified supervision.

Applicants without the qualifying journeyperson certificate must document seven years of HVACR or HVACR restricted experience under the supervision of a properly licensed master professional. They must also complete the Board-approved apprenticeship equivalency requirements before receiving authorization for the master examination.

Experience records should demonstrate work relevant to hydronic heating systems and gas piping. Relevant work may include boilers, piping, pumps, valves, circulation systems, controls, fuel systems, maintenance, testing, inspection, and related HVACR services.

A Delaware professional license is separate from a Delaware business license. A contracting company may also need entity registration, tax accounts, insurance, permits, local approvals, and other operating credentials. ### Test Information and Study Materials

Begin preparation with a schedule based on the examination categories. Assign separate sessions to general knowledge, hydronic systems, piping, pumps, valves, fuel gas, oil, boilers, water, testing, and inspection.

For hydronic-system preparation, study circulation, system pressure, expansion, air removal, zoning, temperature control, piping arrangements, and heat-distribution equipment. Trace the movement of water or steam through the system and identify the purpose of each component.

For pumps and valves, review flow direction, pump placement, pressure relationships, isolation, balancing, check valves, control valves, and maintenance. Understanding component interaction can improve performance on installation and troubleshooting questions.

For boiler questions, connect each control and safety device with the condition it monitors. Review burners, combustion, low-water protection, pressure controls, temperature controls, inspection, cleaning, water treatment, and common operating problems.

For fuel gas questions, practice locating requirements through the code index and chapter headings. Read the complete section and review definitions, table notes, and related provisions before choosing an answer.

Complete timed practice sessions as the exam approaches. Answer familiar questions first, mark questions requiring longer searches or analysis, and return to them after completing the remaining items. Review missed answers in the appropriate reference.
