### Exam Details

The Delaware Master HVACR Restricted Heating – Hydronic Systems and Gas Piping examination evaluates knowledge associated with the design, installation, construction, maintenance, service, repair, alteration, and modification of hydronic systems and gas piping serving those systems.

A hydronic heating system uses water, steam, or another liquid to transfer heat. Candidates should understand the equipment and piping used to generate, distribute, control, and deliver that heat throughout a building or system.

The published examination outline identifies 80 questions with a three-hour time allowance. The tested subject areas include:

- General Knowledge: 22 questions
- Hydronic Systems: 10 questions
- Piping, Pumps, and Valves: 10 questions
- Fuel Gas and Oil: 12 questions
- Boilers – Low and High Pressure: 14 questions
- Water: 8 questions
- Testing and Inspection: 4 questions

General knowledge is the largest category on the published outline. Candidates should review heating principles, heat transfer, pressure, temperature, combustion, system components, tools, controls, terminology, materials, and accepted HVACR practices.

Hydronic-system preparation should include system arrangements, water circulation, heat distribution, expansion, air removal, temperature control, zoning, radiation equipment, and common operating principles. Candidates should understand how boilers, pumps, valves, piping, controls, and heat emitters work together.

Piping, pump, and valve questions may address piping materials, joining methods, system layouts, circulation, pump operation, valve functions, flow control, check valves, balancing, isolation, and maintenance considerations. Candidates may need to recognize the purpose of a component or determine how its position affects system operation.

Fuel gas and oil questions may involve piping materials, valves, regulators, appliance connections, combustion air, venting, burners, fuel supply, and system safety. Candidates should practice locating technical information in the fuel gas and mechanical codes while also understanding the operating principles involved.

Boiler questions can address low-pressure and high-pressure equipment, boiler construction, burners, controls, safety devices, operating procedures, inspections, maintenance, and troubleshooting. The *Low Pressure Boilers, 5th Edition* provides focused technical material for many of these subjects.

Water questions may involve water treatment, makeup water, system pressure, circulation, expansion, air elimination, temperature, and conditions that can affect boiler or hydronic-system performance. Testing and inspection questions may address procedures used to evaluate piping, equipment, safety devices, and system operation.

### State Requirements

Delaware offers an unrestricted Master HVACR license and several Master HVACR Restricted specialties. Heating – Hydronic Systems and Gas Piping is one of the recognized restricted classifications. The license holder may provide services within the specialty identified on the license.

A restricted license does not automatically authorize every type of HVACR work covered by an unrestricted Master HVACR license. Professionals who plan to provide services in more than one restricted specialty must pursue the license classification appropriate for their complete scope of work.

The qualification pathway depends on the applicant’s apprenticeship training, journeyperson credential, supervised experience, and other documentation reviewed by the Board.

An applicant with a qualifying journeyperson certificate earned through an accepted HVACR or restricted HVACR apprenticeship may be required to document applicable post-apprenticeship experience under qualified supervision.

An applicant without the qualifying apprenticeship credential may be required to document a longer period of supervised HVACR or restricted HVACR experience and complete the applicable apprenticeship equivalency or bypass examination.

Experience records should demonstrate work relevant to hydronic heating systems and gas piping. This may include boilers, circulation systems, piping, pumps, valves, controls, fuel systems, service, maintenance, testing, or related HVACR work within the requested specialty.

The Delaware Board determines whether the applicant’s training, experience, examination results, and supporting documents meet state requirements. Purchasing this package does not replace any licensing requirement or guarantee Board approval.

A Delaware professional license is separate from a Delaware business license. Contractors planning to operate a business may also need business registration, tax accounts, insurance, permits, and local approvals. These requirements are separate from the professional examination and license.

### Test Information and Study Materials

Create a study schedule based on the examination categories. Assign separate review periods to general knowledge, hydronic systems, piping, pumps, valves, fuel gas, oil, boilers, water, testing, and inspection.

Give additional attention to general knowledge and boilers because these areas represent a significant portion of the published outline. Review both technical concepts and the practical application of those concepts to equipment and system operation.

For hydronic systems, study circulation, expansion, air removal, pressure, temperature, zoning, heat distribution, and common system arrangements. Practice tracing the path of water or steam through the system and identifying the purpose of each component.

For pumps and valves, review circulation principles, pump placement, flow direction, isolation, balancing, pressure control, and common valve functions. Understanding how the parts interact can make troubleshooting questions easier to evaluate.

For fuel gas questions, practice locating information in the code through the index and chapter headings. Confirm system conditions before selecting a piping table or installation requirement.

For boiler preparation, review operating controls, safety controls, burners, low-water conditions, pressure, temperature, inspections, maintenance, combustion, and water treatment. Connect each component with the condition it monitors or controls.

Complete timed practice sessions as the examination date approaches. Answer familiar questions first, mark longer searches or technical problems, and return to them later. Review incorrect answers by locating the supporting information in the appropriate reference.
