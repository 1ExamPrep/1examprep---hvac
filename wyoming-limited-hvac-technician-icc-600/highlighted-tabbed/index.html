Open Book Test

The 600_WY Wyoming Limited HVAC Technician exam is an open book test. That’s good news—your references are allowed—but it also means your score depends heavily on how well you can use those books under time pressure.

With 40 questions in 2 hours, you don’t have time to “research” every answer from scratch. The best strategy is to prepare so you can do two things quickly:

  • Recognize the concept the question is testing (for example: load-side wiring methods, control circuits, grounding/bonding, or HVAC motor/equipment requirements).
  • Confirm the exact rule or table in the NEC (and use Ugly’s as a quick support reference when electrical fundamentals or quick calculations come into play).

Highlighted and tabbed references help by reducing page-flipping and making it easier to land on the right section consistently. Over multiple practice sessions, this becomes a rhythm: read the question, identify the topic, go to the right NEC location, confirm the requirement, answer, move on.

Licensing Steps

Wyoming’s ICC exam bulletin explains that candidates must first submit a license application with the Wyoming Department of Fire Prevention and Electrical Safety before scheduling an ICC Contractor/Trades exam. If the application is satisfactory, ICC is notified of your eligibility to test. Once you receive approval, you can proceed to schedule your exam through the ICC testing partner.

In practical terms, many candidates follow a workflow like this:

  1. Identify the correct limited technician category for your scope of work (Limited HVAC falls under the limited technician category for HVAC systems).
  2. Document qualifying work experience using acceptable verification methods.
  3. Submit your application through the Wyoming electrical licensing process.
  4. Receive eligibility to test once your application is accepted.
  5. Schedule your exam through Pearson VUE under the ICC Contractor/Trades program.
  6. Pass the exam and complete the final licensing steps through Wyoming’s eLicense portal process.

This book package supports the part of the process you can control day-to-day: building exam readiness with the approved code reference and a field-friendly electrical companion.

State Requirements

Wyoming’s electrical licensing information defines a Limited Technician as a person licensed by the Department who has 2 years (4,000 hours) of experience in the portion of the electrical wiring industry covered by the limited license and the technical knowledge to install and supervise the installation of electrical equipment associated with the limited license held, in accordance with the NEC and applicable ordinances and regulations.

For Limited HVAC work, Wyoming lists the following limited technician category:

  • LM-H: Limited electrical work for heating, ventilating and air conditioning systems, limited to electrical work on the load side of the equipment disconnect (4,000 hours)

Wyoming also states that any technician performing low voltage work must be employed at all times by a low voltage/limited contractor who carries a current Wyoming low voltage/limited contractor’s license.

On the study side, Wyoming notes that it adopts the National Electrical Code without modification and that license examinations are based on the current edition of the NEC. That’s why NEC 2023 is central to this package—your exam prep and the way you work in the field should be aligned to the same code standard.

Test Information and Study Materials

The ICC bulletin for Wyoming electrical contractor/trades testing outlines the exam format as computer-based multiple-choice testing through Pearson VUE. For 600_WY, you’ll have 2 hours to complete 40 questions in an open-book format. Because of the topic weighting, a large portion of your time should be directed to the high-impact content area: HVAC Equipment and Motors, which represents 37% of the exam.

To make your study time count, use the published content outline as your weekly structure. Here’s a practical way to turn the outline into focused preparation using NEC 2023 and Ugly’s:

  • General Knowledge, Theory, Basic Load Calculations, and Definitions (13%)
    Build a habit of checking definitions and general rules in the NEC so you interpret questions correctly. A surprising number of missed questions come from misunderstanding what the code term means in context. Use Ugly’s to reinforce electrical basics and keep your math and fundamentals sharp.
  • Feeders, Branch Circuits, and Conductors (12%)
    Practice questions that require you to move between the scenario and the correct NEC table or rule. Focus on reading carefully: many conductor questions are less about “what’s the answer” and more about “what conditions apply” (equipment type, installation context, and what the question is truly asking). Your goal is to get faster at finding and confirming the governing NEC section without getting lost.
  • Wiring Methods (Load Side of Disconnect to Equipment or Motor) (12%)
    Train yourself to see “load side of disconnect” as an immediate clue about scope. Questions here typically test whether you understand what wiring methods and installation practices are required once power is leaving the disconnect and supplying the HVAC unit or motor. Study by reading the rule, then applying it to scenario-style prompts.
  • Control Circuits (Includes Thermostat Wires) (13%)
    Control wiring is a frequent source of confusion because it blends low-voltage logic with code and installation rules. Use the NEC to anchor what is required, and practice recognizing what is being described—thermostat wiring, control circuits, routing concerns, or protection issues—so you can land quickly in the right place in your reference.
  • Equipment Grounding and Bonding (13%)
    Grounding and bonding questions often feel tricky because they test understanding rather than memorization. Practice by connecting the question’s scenario to the purpose: safety, fault clearing, and proper equipment grounding/bonding on the load side. Your speed improves when you know which NEC locations you typically use to confirm grounding and bonding rules for equipment connections.
  • HVAC Equipment and Motors (Load Side of Disconnect to Equipment or Motor) (37%)
    This is the centerpiece of 600_WY. Build your confidence with how the NEC addresses equipment and motor connections and what requirements come into play for HVAC equipment supplied from a disconnect. For practice, do sets of questions that force you to reference the NEC repeatedly. The goal isn’t to memorize every rule—it’s to become fast and accurate at verifying the correct requirement for the scenario in front of you.

Study tip that works for open-book exams: don’t stop at “right answer.” Each time you practice, write down the NEC location you used to confirm the answer. Then, later in the week, try to find that same location again without help. This repetition builds the navigation skill you’ll rely on during the timed exam.
