This Online Exam Prep is designed to help you prepare for the way the exam is written: scenario-based questions that require you to identify the code “target,” confirm the governing NEC requirement efficiently, and keep a steady pace in a timed, open-book environment.

Licensing Steps

Wyoming’s ICC Contractor/Trades testing process begins with a state application step. Before scheduling an examination, you must first make a license application with the Wyoming Department of Fire Prevention and Electrical Safety. If your application is satisfactory, ICC is notified of your eligibility to test, and you may then schedule your examination through Pearson VUE.

Many applicants follow a workflow like this:

  1. Select the correct limited technician category for your scope (HVAC systems on the load side of the disconnect).
  2. Document your experience using acceptable verification methods.
  3. Submit your application through Wyoming’s electrical licensing process.
  4. Receive eligibility approval to test once the licensing agency accepts your application.
  5. Schedule and pass the ICC 600_WY exam through Pearson VUE.
  6. Complete final licensing steps through the state portal process after passing.

This Online Exam Prep is designed to support the exam step by helping you prepare in a structured, code-focused way.

State Requirements

Wyoming’s electrical licensing information lists a limited technician category for HVAC scope as:

  • LM-H: Limited electrical work for heating, ventilating and air conditioning systems, limited to electrical work on the load side of the equipment disconnect (4,000 hours)

Wyoming also states that any technician performing low voltage work in Wyoming must be employed at all times by a low voltage/limited contractor who carries a current Wyoming low voltage/limited contractor’s license.

Wyoming further states it adopts the National Electrical Code without modification and that licensing examinations are based on the current edition of the NEC—supporting why this Online Exam Prep is centered on NEC 2023.

Test Information and Study Materials

The most effective way to prepare for a limited HVAC electrical exam is to practice the same way you’ll test: interpret the scenario, identify what drives the answer, and verify the governing NEC requirement. This Online Exam Prep emphasizes that workflow through repeated, code-confirmed practice aligned to the 600_WY outline.

Here’s how to approach each content area in a practical, exam-ready way:

  • HVAC Equipment and Motors (Load Side of Disconnect to Equipment or Motor) — 37%
    This is the biggest scoring section. Expect questions tied to equipment connection requirements, motor-related concepts, and code decisions that apply once power leaves the disconnect and supplies the HVAC unit or motor. Your goal is to become fast at identifying the equipment scenario and confirming the governing NEC rule without over-reading.
  • General Knowledge, Theory, Basic Load Calculations, and Definitions — 13%
    This portion often influences how you interpret the rest of the test. Definitions and basic calculation questions reward careful reading and a step-by-step approach. Use the NEC to confirm definitions and any code-based requirement the question depends on, and use Ugly’s to keep fundamentals sharp.
  • Control Circuits (Includes Thermostat Wires) — 13%
    Control wiring questions can be tricky because small details change what is required. Practice recognizing control-circuit clues in the scenario and confirming the correct NEC requirement efficiently. Building a consistent lookup routine here helps prevent time loss on exam day.
  • Equipment Grounding and Bonding — 13%
    Grounding and bonding questions often test understanding rather than memorization. You’ll practice identifying what the scenario is asking (bonding continuity, equipment grounding expectations, fault-clearing intent) and confirming the correct NEC requirement without second-guessing.
  • Feeders, Branch Circuits, and Conductors — 12%
    These questions often depend on conditions in the scenario—equipment type, installation context, or protection requirements. Train yourself to highlight the conditions first, then verify the NEC rule that matches them.
  • Wiring Methods (Load Side of Disconnect to Equipment or Motor) — 12%
    Wiring method questions can become time sinks if you don’t have a navigation plan. Practice identifying the wiring method “trigger” and confirming the permitted/required installation rule without wandering into unrelated sections.

Open-book speed drill: whenever you miss a practice question, write down the NEC location that supports the correct answer. Later, find that same location again without notes. This repetition builds the navigation speed that makes open-book exams work in your favor.
