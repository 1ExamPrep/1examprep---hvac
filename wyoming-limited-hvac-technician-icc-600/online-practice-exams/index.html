Exam Details

The Wyoming ICC Limited HVAC Technician examination, identified by examination code 600-WY, is part of the International Code Council's electrical Contractor/Trades examination program.

The examination is delivered through Pearson VUE. Wyoming limited electrical technician licensing is administered by the Wyoming State Fire Marshal's Department of Fire Prevention and Electrical Safety.

  • Exam Name: Wyoming Limited HVAC Technician
  • Exam Code: 600-WY
  • Examination Organization: International Code Council (ICC)
  • Testing Provider: Pearson VUE
  • Licensing Authority: Wyoming State Fire Marshal's Department of Fire Prevention and Electrical Safety
  • Question Format: Multiple choice
  • Number of Questions: 40
  • Time Limit: 2 hours
  • Exam Type: Open book
  • Electrical Code Reference: National Electrical Code, 2023
  • Additional Reference: Ugly's Electrical References
  • Product Format: Online practice questions
  • Online Access: 3 months

The ICC examination outline identifies six primary subject areas:

  • General Knowledge, Theory, Basic Load Calculations, and Definitions: 13%
  • Feeders, Branch Circuits, and Conductors: 12%
  • Wiring Methods (Load Side of Disconnect to Equipment or Motor): 12%
  • Control Circuits, Including Thermostat Wires: 13%
  • Equipment Grounding and Bonding: 13%
  • HVAC Equipment and Motors (Load Side of Disconnect to Equipment or Motor): 37%

HVAC equipment and motors represent the largest examination category, accounting for 37% of the test. This makes understanding equipment connections, motor circuits, disconnecting means, and associated electrical requirements especially valuable.

The remaining subjects establish the electrical foundation needed to understand HVAC system wiring, including conductors, circuit protection, controls, and grounding.

With two hours to complete 40 questions, candidates have an average of three minutes per question. Familiarity with electrical terminology, code organization, and technical calculations helps support an organized examination approach.

The Wyoming ICC Limited HVAC Technician 600-WY examination is an open book test. Candidates may consult designated electrical reference publications in accordance with ICC examination procedures.

The principal references are the 2023 National Electrical Code and Ugly's Electrical References.

Open-book testing evaluates the ability to recognize electrical installation requirements and locate relevant technical information. Candidates must understand the system described in a question before identifying and applying the appropriate code provision.

State Requirements

The Wyoming LM-H Limited HVAC Technician License authorizes limited electrical work involving heating, ventilation, and air conditioning systems.

The authorized scope is restricted to electrical work on the load side of the equipment disconnect.

Important Wyoming requirements include:

  • License Classification: LM-H Limited HVAC Technician.
  • Licensing Authority: Wyoming State Fire Marshal's Department of Fire Prevention and Electrical Safety.
  • Qualifying Experience: Two years of relevant electrical work experience.
  • Required Work Hours: 4,000 hours of qualifying experience.
  • Authorized Work: Limited electrical work for HVAC systems on the load side of the equipment disconnect.
  • Examination: Wyoming ICC Limited HVAC Technician 600-WY.
  • Work Verification: Employment experience must be documented through acceptable state verification procedures.
  • Employment: Technicians performing regulated work must work under an appropriately licensed Wyoming low-voltage or limited electrical contractor.

The LM-H classification does not provide unrestricted authorization to perform all electrical installations. Its scope concerns the electrical components of HVAC systems within the limits established by Wyoming.

Understanding the equipment disconnect boundary is particularly important because the examination emphasizes wiring and equipment on the load side of that disconnect.

Licensed technicians must also follow applicable electrical codes, equipment installation requirements, and safety procedures when performing authorized work.

Test Information and Study Materials

General Electrical Knowledge

General electrical knowledge provides the foundation for understanding HVAC equipment wiring. Review common electrical terms, circuit components, electrical quantities, and the functions of protective devices.

Electrical Theory

Electrical theory explains how current flows through circuits and how electrical equipment uses power. Study voltage, resistance, current, and the relationship between electrical supply and equipment operation.

Ohm's Law

Ohm's Law describes the mathematical relationship between voltage, current, and resistance. Practice identifying known quantities and using the appropriate calculation to solve electrical problems.

Electrical Power Calculations

Electrical power calculations are useful when evaluating equipment loads. Review watts, volts, amperes, and the relationships used to describe electrical power.

Basic Load Calculations

Load calculations help determine the electrical demand associated with connected equipment. Study how equipment ratings and electrical characteristics affect circuit requirements.

Electrical Definitions

The NEC defines terms used throughout electrical installation requirements. Review definitions associated with conductors, circuits, equipment, disconnecting means, grounding, and accessibility.

Feeders and Branch Circuits

Feeders and branch circuits distribute power through electrical systems. Review general circuit arrangements, conductor requirements, and the relationship between supply circuits and connected HVAC equipment.

Conductor Selection

Conductors must be selected for their intended use. Study conductor materials, insulation ratings, ampacity, and environmental conditions affecting electrical installations.

Conductor Ampacity

Ampacity describes the current a conductor can carry under specified conditions. Practice using NEC conductor tables and recognizing the installation factors that influence conductor selection.

Overcurrent Protection

Overcurrent protective devices help protect conductors and equipment against excessive current. Review circuit breakers, fuses, protective ratings, and applicable NEC requirements.

Wiring Methods

Wiring methods provide pathways and protection for electrical conductors. Study raceways, cables, fittings, boxes, and installation requirements that apply to HVAC equipment connections.

Equipment Disconnects

Disconnecting means allow electrical equipment to be isolated from its supply. Review the purpose of HVAC equipment disconnects and the importance of understanding the authorized LM-H work boundary.

Thermostat Wiring

Thermostats are commonly used to control heating and cooling equipment. Study basic thermostat circuit functions, control wiring terminology, and the electrical relationships between thermostats and HVAC equipment.

Control Circuits

Control circuits regulate equipment operation. Review basic control functions involving switches, relays, contactors, and other devices used in HVAC systems.

Low-Voltage Controls

Many HVAC systems use low-voltage control wiring. Study the role of control transformers, low-voltage circuits, and equipment control components.

Transformers

Transformers change voltage levels to supply appropriate electrical power for specific components. Review transformer fundamentals and their role in HVAC control systems.

Equipment Grounding

Equipment grounding helps establish a fault-current path for electrical safety. Study equipment grounding conductors and the relevant requirements in NEC Article 250.

Bonding

Bonding connects conductive components to establish electrical continuity. Review bonding principles and their importance in equipment installations.

HVAC Equipment

Heating and cooling systems contain electrical components that control equipment operation. Review the relationship between power supplies, motors, controls, protective devices, and associated wiring.

Air-Conditioning Equipment

Air-conditioning systems use electrical power for compressors, fans, and other components. Study the electrical provisions in NEC Article 440 and their application to relevant equipment.

Refrigeration Equipment

Refrigeration equipment also uses motors and electrical controls. Review circuit protection, equipment ratings, conductor requirements, and applicable electrical installation principles.

HVAC Motor Fundamentals

Electric motors operate compressors, blowers, and fans. Review motor ratings, circuit requirements, controls, and the purpose of electrical protective equipment.

Motor Circuit Protection

Motor circuits require appropriate protective devices. Study the distinction between circuit protection and motor overload protection, together with their applicable NEC provisions.

Motor Controllers

Motor controllers regulate equipment operation. Review contactors, switches, and related control arrangements found in HVAC electrical systems.

Motor Disconnecting Means

Motor disconnecting means support equipment isolation. Study the general requirements for disconnects and their relationship to motor installations.

NEC Article 430

Article 430 contains requirements for motors, motor circuits, and controllers. Review its organization and the sections addressing conductor selection, protection, and motor equipment.

NEC Article 440

Article 440 addresses air-conditioning and refrigerating equipment. This article is especially useful for understanding electrical requirements affecting HVAC compressors and related equipment.

Equipment Nameplates

Electrical equipment nameplates provide important rating information. Practice recognizing voltage, current, and other electrical data used when evaluating equipment requirements.

Electrical Equipment Installation

Proper equipment installation involves suitable conductors, protective devices, disconnecting means, and wiring methods. Review how these components work together in an HVAC electrical installation.

Electrical Safety Practices

HVAC electrical work involves hazards associated with energized circuits and operating machinery. Review safe isolation, protective devices, grounding, and the importance of established electrical safety procedures.

NEC Reference Navigation

Practice using the 2023 NEC table of contents, index, definitions, article headings, and technical tables. Focus on locating requirements involving conductors, motors, controls, and air-conditioning equipment.

Using Ugly's Electrical References

Ugly's Electrical References provides practical formulas and technical tables useful for electrical calculations. Develop familiarity with its organization when reviewing circuit theory and equipment-related questions.

Recommended Three-Month Study Strategy

  • Month One – Electrical Fundamentals: Review electrical theory, definitions, calculations, conductors, feeders, branch circuits, grounding, and bonding.
  • Month Two – HVAC Electrical Systems: Concentrate on NEC Articles 430 and 440, HVAC motors, control circuits, thermostat wiring, disconnects, and equipment protection.
  • Month Three – Examination Review: Revisit HVAC equipment, motor circuits, code navigation, electrical calculations, and challenging practice questions.

Because HVAC equipment and motors account for the largest portion of the examination, dedicating additional study time to those subjects helps align preparation with the official examination outline.
