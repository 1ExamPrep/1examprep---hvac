### State Requirements

Idaho requires an HVAC Specialty Journeyman applicant to document **2,000 hours of supervised specialty HVAC installation work**.

Applicants have two qualification pathways. Under the **examination pathway**, the applicant must pass the Board-approved examination and complete the required 2,000 hours of supervised specialty HVAC experience.

Under the **education pathway**, the applicant must complete at least **60 hours of education in fuel-gas code and piping installation methods** and document the same 2,000 hours of supervised specialty HVAC installation work.

The Idaho HVAC Specialty Journeyman license has a defined scope. A Specialty Journeyman is permitted to install **hearth appliances and non-duct-connected oil furnaces**, along with the **associated fuel-gas piping and venting dedicated exclusively to those installations**.

The specialty license does **not include plumbing, electrical, or duct work**. Candidates should understand this limitation because it distinguishes the specialty credential from Idaho's broader HVAC Journeyman license.

Fuel-gas code knowledge is central to the examination because the permitted work involves fuel-gas piping, appliance installation, combustion and installation requirements, and venting systems. Candidates should understand how the IFGC regulates both the piping supplying an appliance and the venting and installation conditions affecting the appliance itself.

Idaho currently uses the **2018 International Fuel Gas Code** among its adopted HVAC codes. The 2018 IFGC is also the approved examination-room reference for the HVAC Specialty Journeyman examination.

Idaho does not currently require continuing education for HVAC license renewal. The Specialty Journeyman license is issued on a three-year term under the state's HVAC licensing structure.

### Test Information and Study Materials

**IFGC Chapter 1 — Scope and Administration:** Approximately six questions come from Chapter 1. Review the purpose and scope of the code, applicability, approvals, permits, inspections, and administrative requirements.

**IFGC Chapter 2 — Definitions:** Approximately six questions come from Chapter 2. Definitions can affect how requirements throughout the code are interpreted, so candidates should become familiar with the terminology used for appliances, vents, piping systems, combustion air, equipment, and fuel-gas components.

**IFGC Chapter 3 — General Regulations:** Approximately seven questions are drawn from Chapter 3. Review appliance and equipment location, structural considerations, combustion air, installation requirements, access, clearances, protection, and general fuel-gas system provisions.

**Combustion Air:** Combustion-air questions may require candidates to identify room conditions, calculate required openings, determine acceptable air sources, or recognize when mechanical combustion-air provisions apply. Practice reading both the general requirements and any applicable exceptions.

**IFGC Chapter 4 — Gas Piping Installations:** Approximately nine questions come from Chapter 4, making it the largest examination category. Candidates should devote substantial preparation time to this chapter.

**Gas-Pipe Sizing:** Practice determining developed length, system pressure, allowable pressure drop, connected appliance loads, and the proper sizing table. Pipe-sizing questions require careful attention to both the information presented in the problem and the conditions stated in the table.

**Piping Materials and Installation:** Review approved piping materials, joining methods, protection, supports, concealed piping, piping through structural members, outdoor installations, and corrosion protection.

**Valves and Regulators:** Study shutoff-valve requirements, regulator locations, accessibility, pressure control, venting, and equipment connections.

**Testing and Purging:** Become familiar with pressure-testing procedures, test pressures, prohibited test media, leak checks, purging requirements, and safety-related provisions for placing systems into service.

**IFGC Chapter 5 — Chimneys and Vents:** Approximately eight questions come from Chapter 5. Venting is one of the most important subjects on the specialty exam because the Idaho license specifically authorizes venting dedicated to qualifying appliances.

**Vent Connectors:** Review permitted materials, sizing, slope, length, support, clearance from combustibles, connection requirements, and common-vent conditions.

**Vent Sizing:** Practice reading applicable venting tables and identifying the correct values based on appliance input, vent height, lateral length, vent type, and appliance configuration.

**Vent Terminations:** Review termination requirements involving roofs, walls, openings, mechanical air intakes, building features, and other conditions that affect safe vent discharge.

**IFGC Chapter 6 — Specific Appliances:** Approximately seven questions are drawn from Chapter 6. Review appliance-specific installation requirements, clearances, connections, controls, combustion air relationships, and equipment restrictions.

**Hearth Appliances:** Because hearth appliance installation is a core part of the Idaho Specialty Journeyman scope, candidates should pay close attention to appliance placement, clearances, venting, fuel-gas connections, and applicable manufacturer and code requirements.

**IFGC Chapter 7 — Gaseous Hydrogen Systems:** Approximately seven questions come from Chapter 7. Do not overlook this chapter simply because it may be less familiar than ordinary fuel-gas piping. It carries the same approximate question count as Chapters 3 and 6.

**Code Navigation:** Practice determining the likely chapter before searching. Definitions lead to Chapter 2, general equipment requirements to Chapter 3, piping to Chapter 4, venting to Chapter 5, and appliance-specific provisions to Chapter 6.

**Timed Practice:** The exam allows 120 minutes for 50 questions. Practice under time limits to strengthen both code-navigation efficiency and decision-making when a question requires a more detailed lookup.
