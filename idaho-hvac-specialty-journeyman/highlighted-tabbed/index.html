Open Book Test

Idaho HVAC exams are open book. Open book does not mean you can look up everything slowly. With a 2-hour specialty exam window, the goal is to combine trade understanding with efficient code confirmation.

Idaho’s HVAC exam guidance permits candidates to:

  • Highlight the reference material
  • Tab different sections
  • Leave notes in the reference material

It also prohibits loose paper in your reference materials and states only approved materials are allowed. For HVAC code references, Idaho’s guidance specifies that commentaries are not permitted. That’s why a highlighted and tabbed base code book is so useful: it helps you build an exam-compliant system directly in the IFGC that you can practice with and bring into the testing room.

A strong open-book strategy for IFGC-based specialty exams:

  • Recognize the topic quickly (piping, combustion air, venting, appliance requirements, safety provisions).
  • Navigate to the right chapter fast using a clean tab system.
  • Confirm the requirement and exceptions before choosing your answer.
  • Move on and protect time so you don’t lose points due to pacing problems.

Test Information and Study Materials

A highlighted and tabbed IFGC is most valuable when you use it to train the exact skill the exam rewards: efficient code confirmation under a timer. Here’s how to study in a way that matches a 2-hour specialty journeyman testing environment.

1) Learn the IFGC “map”
Before you chase heavy practice, get familiar with how the IFGC is organized: chapter layout, section numbering, and where key topics typically live. This reduces time wasted on random searching.

2) Train topic recognition
Most specialty questions fit into recurring categories: piping requirements, installation limitations, combustion air, venting, appliance provisions, and safety rules. If you can identify the category quickly, you’ll know where to look first.

3) Tab like an exam taker
The goal is speed. Tabs should guide you to major areas quickly. Over-tabbing can slow you down because the book becomes cluttered and harder to scan.

4) Highlight for scanning speed
Highlight keywords, thresholds, and exception triggers. Avoid highlighting entire paragraphs—over-highlighting reduces your ability to scan quickly.

5) Practice exception awareness
Many code questions hinge on a single exception. Train your eyes to scan for “Exception,” “shall not,” “unless,” and “where” language before you lock in an answer.

6) Use timed practice sets early
With a 2-hour window, you need to learn what a “reasonable lookup” feels like. Timed practice teaches you when to confirm quickly and when to make your best choice and move forward to protect your overall pacing.

A simple weekly practice rhythm:

  • Session 1: IFGC navigation drills (find key topics quickly)
  • Session 2: Timed practice set (piping/installation focus)
  • Session 3: Timed practice set (venting/combustion air + exception awareness)
  • Session 4: Review missed questions by locating the exact supporting section in the IFGC

This approach turns your book into a tool you can use quickly—so exam day feels like a familiar practice environment instead of a race against the clock.
