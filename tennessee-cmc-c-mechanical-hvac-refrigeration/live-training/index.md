### Open Book Test

The Tennessee CMC-C Mechanical HVAC / Refrigeration Contractor exam is an **open-book test**. Prov currently delivers Tennessee contractor examinations in an open-book format.

Open-book testing does not eliminate the need to study. Mechanical codes, HVAC textbooks, refrigeration references, and federal safety regulations can contain hundreds of pages of technical information. Candidates who are unfamiliar with their references may spend too much examination time looking for answers.

Reference-navigation practice should therefore be part of your study routine. Begin by identifying what a question is testing. Determine whether the subject relates primarily to mechanical codes, refrigeration, HVAC equipment, piping, controls, safety, air distribution, or another technical area. Then choose the reference most likely to contain the supporting information.

The International Mechanical Code should become familiar for mechanical-code subjects. Refrigeration and HVAC textbooks can support questions involving systems, equipment, refrigerants, controls, service practices, and technical principles. OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 is the primary safety-focused reference among the materials listed for this training.

Practice using indexes, tables of contents, chapter headings, definitions, charts, and tables. The goal is not to memorize every page. Instead, develop enough familiarity with the books that you know where to begin when a technical question appears.

Prov permits authorized references during testing and encourages candidates to prepare reference materials before the exam according to its current testing rules. Candidates should follow the reference-material requirements applicable on their examination date.

### State Requirements

The Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors regulates contractor licensing in Tennessee. A contractor license is generally required before bidding or contracting on projects of $25,000 or more, and mechanical and HVAC subcontractors are also subject to contractor licensing requirements when their portion of a project reaches the applicable state threshold.

The CMC-C classification applies specifically to HVAC/Refrigeration contracting. Candidates pursuing this classification complete the trade examination required for CMC-C as part of the contractor licensing process.

Tennessee contractor applicants are also required to satisfy the Tennessee Business and Law examination requirement. The trade exam and Business and Law exam address different areas, so candidates should organize preparation for each accordingly.

The licensing process also includes qualifying-agent information. The qualifying agent is the person associated with satisfying applicable examination requirements for the contracting entity.

Financial information is another component of Tennessee contractor licensing. The Board considers financial information as part of the licensing process and assignment of the applicable monetary limit.

Passing the CMC-C examination does not automatically issue a contractor license. Applicants must complete the remaining licensing requirements before the Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors approves and issues the applicable license.
