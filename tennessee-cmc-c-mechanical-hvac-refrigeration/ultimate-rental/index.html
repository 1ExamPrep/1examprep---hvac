Exam Details

The Tennessee CMC-C Mechanical HVAC / Refrigeration Contractor exam is a trade examination for candidates pursuing the HVAC/Refrigeration Contractor classification. The examination is administered through Prov as part of Tennessee's contractor examination program.

Preparation for the CMC-C exam requires familiarity with several areas of mechanical contracting. Candidates should be comfortable working with mechanical codes, HVAC equipment, refrigeration principles, air conditioning systems, heating systems, residential comfort systems, construction safety, and related contractor knowledge.

The reference list reflects the technical range of the classification. The International Mechanical Code provides an important code reference. Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning and Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Technology provide substantial HVAC and refrigeration material. The SMACNA manual focuses on residential comfort-system installation, while HVAC Basics for Contractors provides additional contractor-focused HVAC information.

OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 supports preparation involving construction safety. The NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law, and Project Management, 4th Edition - TN provides the business reference included with this package.

A productive study strategy should combine technical understanding with practical reference navigation. When working through a question, first identify the subject being tested. Then determine which reference is most likely to contain the supporting information.

This process can be particularly useful when preparing for an examination that draws from multiple technical books. The better you understand the purpose and organization of each reference, the easier it becomes to narrow your search when working through practice questions.

Open Book Test

The Tennessee CMC-C Mechanical HVAC / Refrigeration Contractor exam is an open-book test. Candidates should prepare by developing both technical HVAC knowledge and strong reference-navigation skills.

Open-book testing does not mean that the exam can be completed successfully by searching randomly through books. HVAC and refrigeration references can contain hundreds of pages of technical information. Candidates who are unfamiliar with their books may spend too much time locating individual subjects.

All books included with this Ultimate rental package are highlighted and tabbed. The highlighting helps identify important material, while the tabs provide an organized way to move between major sections during preparation.

Use the same references regularly throughout your study period. When reviewing mechanical-code questions, practice locating the information in the International Mechanical Code. When studying refrigeration, work with the refrigeration textbooks. When reviewing construction safety, use OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 directly.

Become familiar with indexes, tables of contents, chapter headings, definitions, charts, tables, and technical sections. Learning how each reference is structured can reduce unnecessary searching.

A useful practice method is to read a question before opening any book. Identify the main keywords and determine which reference should contain the answer. Then use the index or table of contents to locate the applicable section.

Repeated reference practice helps build a consistent process: identify the topic, select the correct book, find the applicable section, review the supporting information, and answer the question.
