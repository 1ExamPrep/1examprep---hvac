Exam Details

The Tennessee CMC-C classification is the HVAC/Refrigeration Contractor classification. It is intended for contractors working with heating, ventilation, air conditioning, refrigeration, and related mechanical systems.

The CMC-C trade examination is currently administered through Prov. Candidates preparing for the examination should become comfortable working across several different technical references rather than relying on a single textbook.

The International Mechanical Code supports preparation involving mechanical-system requirements and code-related subjects. Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning and Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Technology provide broad HVAC/R information covering heating, cooling, refrigeration, system components, controls, installation, servicing, and troubleshooting.

The SMACNA Residential Comfort System Installation Standards Manual addresses residential comfort-system installation practices, while HVAC Basics for Contractors provides additional contractor-focused HVAC material. OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 addresses federal construction-industry safety requirements.

Because these references cover different subject areas, an important part of preparation is learning which book is most likely to contain the information needed for a particular question.

For example, a mechanical-code question should generally direct your attention toward the International Mechanical Code. A refrigeration-system question may require one of the comprehensive HVAC/R textbooks. A construction-safety question should point you toward OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926.

Developing those associations can help you avoid searching randomly through every book when working through practice questions or preparing for exam day.

State Requirements

The Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors administers contractor licensing in the state. The CMC-C classification applies to HVAC/Refrigeration contracting and is one of the mechanical contractor classifications recognized by the Board.

Tennessee contractor licensing requirements depend on factors such as the classification requested, the business entity, qualifying agent, financial information, and the type and value of contracting work involved.

A Tennessee contractor license is required before bidding or offering a price on projects meeting the applicable state contractor licensing threshold. HVAC subcontractors are also subject to Tennessee contractor licensing requirements when their portion of a project reaches the applicable threshold.

The qualifying agent is associated with satisfying applicable examination requirements for the contracting entity. Applicants should make sure the appropriate qualifying agent and contractor classification are identified during the application process.

Financial information also forms part of Tennessee contractor licensing. Applicants should prepare the required business and financial documentation associated with the contractor application and requested monetary limit.

Passing the CMC-C examination does not automatically issue a Tennessee contractor license. Examination completion is one part of the licensing process, and the applicant must satisfy the remaining requirements established by the Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors.

Begin with the International Mechanical Code. Become familiar with its organization, definitions, chapter structure, index, tables, and major mechanical-system topics. Code books often require a different navigation approach from traditional textbooks, so frequent use can help improve familiarity.

HVAC Basics for Contractors should also become a regular part of your preparation. Use it to reinforce contractor-focused HVAC concepts and practical technical knowledge.

OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 should be studied as a separate safety reference. Learn how the construction regulations are organized and practice locating safety requirements by using the index and regulatory structure.
