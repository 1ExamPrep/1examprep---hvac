Exam Details

The Oklahoma HVACR Journeyman Unlimited examination evaluates a candidate's knowledge of the installation, maintenance, repair, fabrication, alteration, and extension of air conditioning, refrigeration, heating, and ventilating systems, including ductwork within complete systems unlimited in horsepower or tons, while employed by an HVACR contractor.

  • Exam: Oklahoma HVACR Journeyman Unlimited
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Number of Questions: 70
  • Time Allowed: 180 minutes
  • Passing Score: 70%
  • Passing Raw Score: 49 correct answers
  • Exam Format: Open book

The examination covers several important areas of HVACR trade knowledge. Candidates should prepare for Electrical Knowledge and Controls, Natural Gas, Air Distribution Systems, Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Systems, Heating Systems, and Safety.

Electrical Knowledge and Controls can include general electrical theory, motors, AC and DC circuits, and controls. These concepts are important because HVACR systems depend on electrical components and controls for equipment operation, system sequencing, service, and troubleshooting.

Natural Gas preparation includes piping, valves, connectors, accessories, hangers and supports, materials, sizing, testing, and installation. The International Fuel Gas Code is an important resource for candidates reviewing these subjects.

Air Distribution Systems includes duct sizing and design, assembly and installation, supports, materials, insulation, ventilation requirements, exhaust systems, fire and smoke control, testing and balancing, and plans and symbols. Candidates should understand both practical air-distribution concepts and applicable mechanical requirements.

Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Systems covers refrigeration theory, refrigerants, equipment sizing and design, components, piping, installation, system operation, troubleshooting, and maintenance. Heating preparation includes heating theory, combustion air, vents and chimneys, equipment sizing, heating equipment, installation, hydronics, operation, maintenance, and troubleshooting.

Open Book Test

The Oklahoma HVACR Journeyman Unlimited examination is an open book test. Candidates may bring approved reference materials into the examination center according to PSI testing rules.

PSI allows qualifying reference materials to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are permitted. Temporary tabs such as Post-it notes are not permitted, and references may not contain written notes.

The highlighted and tabbed books in this package are designed to support that open-book preparation process. Using prepared references during study can help candidates become familiar with how information is organized instead of trying to learn the structure of several books on examination day.

An open-book exam still requires solid HVACR knowledge. Candidates receive 180 minutes to complete 70 questions, so reference lookups need to be purposeful. Some questions can be answered directly from trade knowledge, while others may require confirmation from a code, technical reference, calculation, or safety standard.

Mechanical installation, ventilation, ductwork, equipment, combustion air, and related requirements may lead candidates to the International Mechanical Code. Fuel gas piping, sizing, materials, appliances, combustion air, testing, and venting may require the International Fuel Gas Code.

Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning can support questions involving refrigeration principles, heating, controls, electrical concepts, system components, service, operation, and troubleshooting. Mathematics for Plumbers and Pipefitters supports calculation preparation, while 29 CFR Part 1926 provides construction-industry safety standards.

Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning, 22nd Edition becomes permitted for the applicable PSI examination beginning August 13, 2026.

Licensing Steps

Oklahoma mechanical journeyman licensing is administered by the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board. Candidates pursuing the HVACR Unlimited Journeyman classification must complete the applicable experience, application, examination, and licensing requirements.

  1. Gain qualifying mechanical trade experience. Build verifiable experience performing HVACR work appropriate to the Unlimited Journeyman category.
  2. Meet the applicable journeyman qualifications. Oklahoma recognizes qualifying experience and approved education-and-experience pathways for mechanical journeyman applicants.
  3. Document Unlimited-level experience. Experience used to qualify for HVACR Unlimited licensing must demonstrate work above the capacity thresholds associated with the HVACR Limited classification.
  4. Submit the mechanical journeyman application. Apply through the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board for the HVACR Unlimited Journeyman category.
  5. Receive approval to test. Examination eligibility is determined through the state application process.
  6. Schedule the examination. Approved candidates arrange their testing appointment through PSI.
  7. Prepare for the open-book exam. Review HVACR technical concepts and become familiar with the approved reference materials.
  8. Complete the licensing process. After satisfying the examination requirements, follow the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board process for obtaining and maintaining the journeyman license.

State Requirements

The Oklahoma HVACR Unlimited Journeyman classification allows qualifying work involving cooling products, systems, and equipment, including process piping, without the 25-ton cooling-capacity restriction associated with the HVACR Limited classification.

The Unlimited classification also covers heating equipment, including related process piping, without the 500,000 Btu/h per-appliance limitation that applies to HVACR Limited licensing.

Qualifying refrigeration, sheet metal, natural gas piping, and related process piping work are included within the HVACR Unlimited category.

Oklahoma requires applicants for mechanical journeyman licensing to meet applicable age, experience, application, and examination requirements. Applicants seeking the HVACR Unlimited classification must document qualifying work above 25 tons and 500,000 Btu/h to demonstrate experience appropriate to the Unlimited category.

A licensed mechanical journeyman works for an active licensed mechanical contractor while performing work within the scope of the journeyman classification. Understanding the license scope is therefore important both for examination preparation and for performing licensed work after the credential is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Oklahoma HVACR Journeyman Unlimited study plan combines technical review, calculations, code navigation, reference practice, and construction safety preparation.

Begin with Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Systems. Review refrigeration theory, refrigerants, equipment sizing, system components, piping, installation, operation, maintenance, and troubleshooting. Work with Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning throughout your preparation to develop familiarity with where technical subjects are located.

Air Distribution Systems should include duct sizing and design, installation, materials, supports, insulation, ventilation, exhaust, fire and smoke requirements, testing and balancing, and plans and symbols. Mechanical code navigation can be important when reviewing these areas.

Electrical Knowledge and Controls should include electrical fundamentals, motors, circuits, and controls as they apply to HVACR systems. Candidates who work primarily on mechanical portions of equipment may benefit from dedicating additional study time to electrical and control concepts.

Natural Gas preparation should include piping, valves, connectors, supports, materials, sizing, testing, combustion air, appliance requirements, and venting. Practice using the International Fuel Gas Code while studying these topics so the location of important provisions becomes more familiar.

Heating preparation should include heating theory, equipment, combustion air, vents and chimneys, equipment sizing, hydronic systems, installation, operation, troubleshooting, and maintenance.

Trade mathematics should be practiced through actual calculations. Review measurements, conversions, formulas, area, volume, and other mechanical applications. Solving problems repeatedly is more useful than simply reviewing formulas without applying them.

Safety preparation should involve direct use of 29 CFR Part 1926. Become comfortable locating OSHA provisions involving personal protective equipment, fall protection, ladders, scaffolds, workplace hazards, and employee protection.

A useful open-book study method is mixed-reference practice. Work through questions from several technical areas in a single session. Before opening a book, determine which reference should contain the information. Then use the tabs, index, chapters, and tables to locate the material.

This process helps connect the subject matter with the books. Repeated practice can make it easier to recognize when the International Mechanical Code, International Fuel Gas Code, HVACR textbook, mathematics reference, or OSHA material is the most appropriate source.

The highlighted and tabbed format supports efficient navigation, but technical understanding remains essential. Candidates should use the books as tools that support knowledge rather than treating open-book testing as an alternative to learning the material.
