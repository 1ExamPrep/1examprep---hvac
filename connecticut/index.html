How does HVAC licensing work in Connecticut?

The Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection, Occupational and Professional Licensing licenses HVAC work here. The board approves your application first; then you schedule the exam and bring only the references the candidate bulletin allows.

  • Connecticut D licenses cover warm air, air conditioning and refrigeration (D-1 to D-4); S licenses cover heating, piping and cooling (S-1 to S-10).
  • Each license code has its own exam, and several S exams allow approved books in the exam room.

Can you bring books into the Connecticut exam?

Several exams in this state let you bring approved books, which is why exam-room book packages are offered. Your candidate bulletin lists the books allowed for your exam.

Who issues HVAC licenses in Connecticut?

The Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection, Occupational and Professional Licensing. Buying a package here does not register you or make you eligible; the licensing body approves you first, then the exam is scheduled.

Is the Connecticut HVAC exam open book?

Several exams in this state let you bring approved books, which is why exam-room book packages are offered. Your candidate bulletin lists the books allowed for your exam.

The one your approval or application names. 12 licenses are listed here: Limited Warm Air, A/C and Refrigeration Contractor (D-1); Limited Warm Air, A/C and Refrigeration Journeyperson (D-2); Limited Heating-Cooling Contractor (D-3); Limited Heating-Cooling Journeyperson (D-4); Limited Heating and Cooling Journeyperson (S-10); Limited Heating and Cooling Contractor (S-9); Unlimited Heating, Piping and Cooling Contractor (S-1); Unlimited Heating, Piping and Cooling Journeyperson (S-2); and more.

Do I need EPA 608 certification as well?

If you handle refrigerant, yes. EPA Section 608 is a federal certification and the state or city license is separate; most HVAC contractors hold both.
