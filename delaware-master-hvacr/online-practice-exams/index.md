### State Requirements

The Delaware Master HVACR license is administered by the **Delaware Board of Plumbing, Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Examiners**.

For applicants using a recognized journeyman pathway, Delaware requires a copy of the HVACR apprenticeship program Certificate of Completion and proof of at least **two years of work experience under the supervision of a master licensee after receiving the certificate**.

Applicants who do not have an accepted journeyman certificate must generally provide proof of at least **seven years of work experience under the supervision of a master licensee**. The Board reviews that experience and may require the applicant to complete the applicable apprenticeship bypass examination before authorization for the Master licensure examination.

Master HVACR applicants must also provide a copy of their **CFC Certification card**. This reflects the refrigeration-related scope associated with Master HVACR work.

The Board must approve candidates before they may sit for the Master examination. After approval, ProV provides the examination information, scheduling instructions, permitted testing materials, and current Candidate Information Bulletin.

Delaware requires at least **70%** to pass the Master HVACR licensure examination. Applicants who do not receive a passing score after three attempts are subject to the state's reapplication requirements before testing again.

Passing the examination alone does not automatically issue the license. The Board must also verify the applicant's work experience, certification documentation, application materials, and all other applicable licensing requirements.

### Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Delaware Master HVACR study plan should begin with **General Knowledge**. With 28 questions, this category represents the largest portion of the examination and can involve refrigeration theory, air conditioning, heating, combustion, controls, airflow, safety, and mechanical fundamentals.

**Refrigeration principles** should receive careful review. Candidates should understand the refrigeration cycle, compressor function, condensation, expansion, evaporation, superheat, subcooling, refrigerant flow, and the relationship among system pressures and temperatures.

**Heating systems** should include furnaces, boilers, heat pumps, combustion equipment, controls, hydronic relationships, and warm-air distribution. Use Low Pressure Boilers to reinforce boiler-specific concepts and safety practices.

**Piping** accounts for 12 questions. Study refrigerant piping, gas piping, condensate, hydronic piping, materials, joining methods, supports, sizing principles, installation, testing, and related mechanical requirements.

**Equipment** accounts for 18 questions. Candidates should understand equipment selection, installation, clearances, controls, service access, efficiency considerations, safety devices, and how individual components function within the complete system.

**System Sizing and Installation** is another 18-question category. Manual J can help reinforce residential load calculation concepts, while Ductulator practice can strengthen airflow and duct-sizing skills.

Candidates should understand that proper system sizing involves more than choosing equipment by nominal capacity. Building heat gain, heat loss, airflow, duct performance, system configuration, design conditions, and equipment characteristics all affect correct sizing.

**Ventilation and Exhaust Systems** accounts for 14 questions. Review outdoor air, mechanical ventilation, exhaust requirements, duct systems, air movement, make-up air, equipment exhaust, and the code provisions governing these systems.

**Testing and Inspection** accounts for 10 questions. Candidates should understand system testing, pressure testing, inspections, equipment commissioning concepts, verification of safe operation, and recognition of improper installations.

The **International Fuel Gas Code, 2018** should be used regularly during preparation. Become familiar with gas piping, appliance installation, combustion air, venting, pressure testing, and related safety requirements.

The **International Mechanical Code, 2018** should be studied alongside practical HVACR material. Code-based questions may ask about ventilation, ducts, mechanical rooms, exhaust, refrigeration, equipment installation, or clearances.

**Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning, 22nd Edition** can help strengthen broader system understanding. When a practice question exposes a weakness in refrigeration theory, airflow, controls, troubleshooting, or equipment operation, return to the underlying concept rather than memorizing the answer.

**Low Pressure Boilers, 5th Edition** can be used to reinforce boiler terminology, controls, combustion, fittings, operation, maintenance, and safety.

**Manual J** should be used to understand the process behind residential heating and cooling loads. Candidates should recognize how windows, walls, roofs, infiltration, orientation, design temperature, occupancy, and internal loads affect equipment-sizing decisions.

The **Ductulator** can support practice involving airflow, duct size, friction rate, and velocity. Candidates should become comfortable moving among airflow requirements, duct dimensions, and practical distribution-system decisions.

Practice questions should eventually combine multiple subjects. A single scenario might involve equipment selection, gas piping, venting, combustion air, duct sizing, and testing. Master-level preparation should develop the ability to see how these systems interact.

When a practice question is missed, classify the reason. The problem may involve weak HVACR theory, incorrect code navigation, calculation error, unfamiliar terminology, or confusion about equipment operation.

For code questions, identify the subject before searching. For calculation questions, write down the known values and determine what must be found. For equipment questions, think through the complete operating sequence and the role each component plays.

Use the included **3 months access** to build repetition into your study plan. Revisit weak categories, repeat mixed-topic practice, and gradually increase the amount of timed review as examination day approaches.
