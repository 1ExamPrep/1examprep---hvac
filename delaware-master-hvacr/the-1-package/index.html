Exam Details

The Delaware Master HVACR examination is part of the professional licensing process overseen by the Delaware Board of Plumbing, Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Examiners. Applicants pursuing licensure by examination must receive Board approval before scheduling the test through the approved examination provider.

The examination contains 100 questions. Candidates are allowed approximately one hour and forty minutes to complete the test, making preparation, subject recognition, and reference-navigation speed especially important.

The examination outline includes General Knowledge, Piping, Equipment, System Sizing and Installation, Ventilation and Exhaust Systems, and Testing and Inspection.

General Knowledge includes 28 questions. Candidates should review refrigeration principles, heating processes, combustion, airflow, temperature, pressure, humidity, heat transfer, electrical fundamentals, safety practices, tools, materials, system operation, and common HVACR terminology.

Piping includes 12 questions. Preparation may involve fuel-gas piping, refrigerant piping, pressure considerations, sizing methods, joining procedures, materials, supports, protection, valves, regulators, and installation practices.

Equipment includes 18 questions. Candidates should understand furnaces, boilers, air-conditioning systems, heat pumps, refrigeration equipment, burners, compressors, condensers, evaporators, controls, safety devices, air handlers, and related components.

System Sizing and Installation includes 18 questions. Study should address heating and cooling load calculations, airflow requirements, duct sizing, equipment selection, installation clearances, system design, distribution methods, and proper placement of components.

Ventilation and Exhaust Systems includes 14 questions. Candidates should review mechanical ventilation, exhaust requirements, combustion air, duct construction, air movement, ventilation rates, appliance venting, termination requirements, and system clearances.

Testing and Inspection includes 10 questions. This area may involve pressure testing, leak testing, equipment startup, performance verification, inspection procedures, operational checks, safety controls, and evaluation of completed installations.

Delaware HVACR and plumbing examinations require a passing score of at least 70 percent. Candidates who do not pass are subject to the examination provider’s retesting rules and the limits that apply to HVACR examination attempts.

Open Book Test

The Delaware Master HVACR examination is an open-book, timed examination. Candidates may use authorized printed references during testing under the examination provider’s current rules.

An open-book format does not eliminate the need for focused preparation. Candidates must complete 100 questions within a limited testing period, so searching each reference from the beginning is not an effective strategy. Strong preparation combines HVACR knowledge with the ability to identify the correct book and locate information quickly.

The International Fuel Gas Code supports questions involving fuel-gas piping, appliance connections, combustion air, venting, pressure regulation, testing, installation, and safety requirements. Candidates should become familiar with the code’s chapters, definitions, tables, sizing provisions, exceptions, and appendices.

The International Mechanical Code supports preparation involving mechanical systems, ventilation, exhaust, ductwork, equipment installation, combustion air, refrigeration, boilers, chimneys, vents, and related mechanical requirements.

Low Pressure Boilers supports review of boiler operation, components, controls, safety devices, water systems, combustion, maintenance, inspection, and operating procedures. Candidates should understand how boiler systems function and how common safety controls protect equipment and occupants.

Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning supports technical preparation involving refrigeration cycles, refrigerants, compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, electrical controls, heating systems, air conditioning, heat pumps, troubleshooting, installation, and service procedures.

Manual J supports residential heating and cooling load calculations. Candidates should understand how building construction, insulation, windows, doors, infiltration, orientation, climate, occupancy, and internal loads affect equipment-sizing decisions.

The Ductulator supports duct-sizing practice by helping candidates connect airflow, friction rate, velocity, and duct dimensions. Candidates should practice using it before exam day rather than attempting to learn the tool during the test.

Reference materials may be prepared with highlighting, underlining in pen, and permanent tabs. Handwritten notes, removable tabs, Post-it notes, added photocopies, and photocopied reference books are not permitted under the examination rules.

Highlighted passages should always be read in context. Definitions, exceptions, table notes, installation conditions, and surrounding provisions may affect the correct answer. Highlighting and tabs support navigation but do not replace understanding the complete requirement.

Licensing Steps

Begin by confirming that the Delaware Master HVACR license matches the services you plan to provide. A Master HVACR license covers multiple HVACR service areas, while restricted licenses are limited to designated specialties.

Create a DELPROS account and begin the Master HVACR application through Delaware Professional Regulation Online Services. Applications are submitted electronically through the state licensing system.

Gather the records required for the application. Applicants may need license verifications, employment documentation, apprenticeship records, a CFC Certification card, and other supporting materials based on the selected licensing pathway.

Applicants with an acceptable journeyman certificate must submit a copy of the apprenticeship program Certificate of Completion and proof of at least two years of work experience under the supervision of a master licensee after receiving the certificate.

Applicants without an acceptable journeyman certificate must provide proof of at least seven years of work experience under the supervision of a master licensee. The Board reviews the experience and may require an Apprenticeship By-Pass Examination before the applicant proceeds to the Master examination.

Submit the completed application and supporting documentation through DELPROS. The Board reviews the application before granting authorization to sit for the examination.

After receiving approval, follow the examination provider’s registration instructions and schedule the Delaware Master HVACR exam. Review all test-day requirements, including identification, approved references, calculator rules, and reference-material preparation.

Complete the examination and earn a score of at least 70 percent. Official results are reported through the testing and licensing process.

Complete any remaining licensing requirements shown in DELPROS. Passing the examination alone does not automatically issue a license when supporting documents, fees, or other application items remain outstanding.

Application Service is included with The 1 Package to help organize application information and supporting documents. Final eligibility, examination authorization, and license issuance remain under the authority of the Delaware Board.

State Requirements

A Delaware Master HVACR license permits the license holder to design, install, construct, maintain, service, repair, alter, or modify products and equipment, including gas piping, used in heating, air conditioning, refrigeration, ventilation, process cooling, and process heating systems.

A professional must obtain the appropriate Delaware license before providing regulated HVACR services in the state unless a specific exemption applies. Holding a license in another jurisdiction does not automatically authorize HVACR work in Delaware.

Applicants for the unrestricted Master HVACR license must submit a copy of their CFC Certification card as part of the licensing documentation. Proof of qualifying work experience must also be submitted for the periods required by the applicant’s pathway.

Employment experience is generally verified by a supervisor. When standard verification cannot be obtained, Delaware identifies other forms of documentation that may be reviewed, including qualifying tax records or employer affidavits. Acceptance of experience documentation remains subject to Board review.

A Master HVACR license does not replace a Master Plumber license. Work involving water-supply piping and systems that remove liquid or water-carried waste requires the appropriate plumbing authority in addition to HVACR licensing.

A professional license is separate from a business license. An HVACR contractor operating a company may also need a legal business entity, Delaware business registration, tax accounts, insurance, local registrations, and other operational approvals.

Licensees must maintain compliance with renewal, continuing education, professional conduct, and other requirements that apply to the licensing period. Business owners must also maintain current entity, tax, insurance, and registration records.

Test Information and Study Materials

Begin preparation with refrigeration and air-conditioning fundamentals. Review heat transfer, pressure-temperature relationships, refrigerant flow, compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, superheat, subcooling, and system troubleshooting.

Study fuel-gas systems, including pipe sizing, materials, appliance connections, regulators, shutoff valves, combustion air, venting, testing, and installation safety. Practice locating tables and applying their conditions accurately.

Review mechanical ventilation and exhaust requirements. Study airflow, duct construction, exhaust discharge, ventilation rates, appliance venting, clearances, supports, and air-distribution principles.

Practice residential load calculations with Manual J. Understand how insulation, windows, doors, orientation, infiltration, occupancy, climate, and internal heat gains influence heating and cooling loads.

Use the Ductulator regularly during preparation. Practice moving between airflow, friction rate, velocity, and duct size so the tool becomes familiar before the examination.

Study boiler systems, including operating controls, limit controls, safety valves, burners, feedwater systems, combustion, maintenance, inspections, and common operating problems.

Complete timed navigation sessions using all included references. Track which subjects take the longest to locate and which index terms, tables, or tabs provide the most efficient path to the information.

Keep a record of missed practice questions. Determine whether each error resulted from a technical knowledge gap, calculation mistake, incorrect reference selection, overlooked exception, or misunderstanding of the question.
