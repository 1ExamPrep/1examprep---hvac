Exam Details

The Delaware Master HVACR examination is part of the professional licensing process overseen by the Delaware Board of Plumbing, Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Examiners. Applicants pursuing licensure by examination must receive approval from the Board before registering with the examination provider.

The examination evaluates knowledge connected with the design, installation, construction, maintenance, service, repair, alteration, and modification of HVACR systems and equipment. Candidates should be ready to apply technical principles and code requirements to practical trade situations.

General HVACR knowledge may include heat transfer, temperature, pressure, humidity, airflow, refrigeration principles, combustion, electricity, tools, safety, equipment operation, materials, and trade terminology.

Piping subjects may include fuel-gas piping, refrigerant piping, approved materials, pressure limitations, sizing methods, valves, regulators, joining procedures, supports, testing, and protection from physical damage.

Equipment subjects may address furnaces, boilers, heat pumps, air conditioners, refrigeration systems, compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, air handlers, burners, controls, and safety devices.

System-sizing and installation questions may involve residential load calculations, airflow requirements, duct sizing, equipment selection, installation clearances, distribution-system design, and the proper placement of system components.

Ventilation and exhaust preparation may involve ventilation rates, mechanical exhaust, combustion air, duct construction, exhaust discharge locations, appliance venting, system supports, and required clearances.

Testing and inspection topics may include pressure testing, leak testing, equipment startup, operational checks, safety controls, performance verification, inspection procedures, and evaluation of completed installations.

Because the exam covers multiple trade disciplines, candidates should divide study time among code navigation, HVACR theory, equipment knowledge, calculations, system design, testing, and troubleshooting. Using all six references throughout preparation can help candidates recognize which publication is most useful for each subject.

Open Book Test

The Delaware Master HVACR examination is an open-book test. Candidates may use authorized printed reference materials under the current rules established by the examination provider.

An open-book examination still requires substantial preparation. The references contain extensive technical information, and candidates may not have time to research every answer from the beginning. Successful preparation involves understanding the subject and knowing which book is most likely to contain the required information.

The International Fuel Gas Code should be used for questions involving gas-piping materials, pipe sizing, appliance connections, regulators, shutoff valves, combustion air, venting, testing, pressure, and fuel-gas installation requirements.

The International Mechanical Code supports questions involving mechanical equipment, ventilation, exhaust systems, duct construction, combustion air, refrigeration systems, boilers, chimneys, vents, clearances, supports, and installation requirements.

Low Pressure Boilers supports review of boiler operation, burners, controls, safety valves, low-water protection, feedwater systems, combustion, water treatment, maintenance, and inspection.

Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning supports subjects involving refrigeration cycles, refrigerants, compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, heat pumps, furnaces, electrical controls, troubleshooting, installation, and service procedures.

Manual J supports residential heating and cooling load calculations. Candidates should understand how climate, insulation, windows, doors, orientation, infiltration, occupancy, and internal heat gains affect the final calculated load.

The Ductulator supports duct-sizing exercises involving airflow, friction rate, velocity, and duct dimensions. Candidates should practice using it repeatedly before test day so they can move between these values efficiently.

When locating information, candidates should read the complete section, table heading, note, definition, or exception. A nearby condition may affect how a provision applies. Selecting an answer based on one isolated sentence can lead to errors.

Candidates should review the latest testing rules before bringing books into the examination. Requirements may address tabs, highlighting, handwritten notes, inserts, loose paper, photocopies, removable markers, calculators, and other testing materials.

Licensing Steps

Begin by reviewing the scope of the Delaware Master HVACR license and confirming that it matches the services you intend to provide. Delaware also offers restricted HVACR licenses for specific specialty areas.

Create a user account through Delaware Professional Regulation Online Services and begin the Master HVACR application in DELPROS. Applications and supporting documents are submitted through the state’s online licensing system.

Gather the records required for the applicable licensing pathway. These may include employment verification, apprenticeship records, license verifications, certification documents, and other information requested by the Board.

Applicants pursuing unrestricted Master HVACR licensure must submit the required certification and qualifying experience documentation. The specific requirements depend on the applicant’s apprenticeship history, experience, and current licensing status.

An applicant with an acceptable journeyman certificate may need to submit the apprenticeship program Certificate of Completion and proof of qualifying work experience obtained after receiving the certificate.

An applicant without an acceptable journeyman certificate may need to document a longer period of qualifying experience under the supervision of a master licensee. The Board determines whether an Apprenticeship By-Pass Examination is required.

Submit the completed application and all required attachments through DELPROS. The Board reviews the application before authorizing an applicant to sit for the Master HVACR examination.

After receiving approval, follow the examination provider’s instructions to register and schedule the test. Review appointment procedures, identification requirements, approved references, calculator rules, and testing policies.

Prepare with the references in this package. Study fuel-gas systems, mechanical-code requirements, refrigeration, air conditioning, heating equipment, boilers, load calculations, duct sizing, testing, and inspection.

Complete the examination and earn the required passing score. After the official result is received, complete any remaining licensing requirements shown in DELPROS.

Passing the examination is one part of the licensing process. Final eligibility and license issuance remain subject to Board review and completion of all applicable requirements.

State Requirements

A Delaware Master HVACR license permits the license holder to design, install, construct, maintain, service, repair, alter, or modify products and equipment, including gas piping, used in heating, air conditioning, refrigeration, ventilation, process cooling, and process heating systems.

Master HVACR Restricted licenses limit the holder to a designated specialty. Restricted classifications include forced-air heating, ventilation and gas piping; hydronic heating and gas piping; commercial hood systems; refrigeration; air conditioning; and gas piping.

Professionals who plan to work in more than one restricted specialty should pursue the unrestricted Master HVACR classification. Applicants should select the license that accurately reflects their intended work.

A Master HVACR license does not automatically provide authority for plumbing work involving systems used to bring water into a building or remove liquid and water-carried waste. Separate plumbing authority may be required for that work.

Applicants should provide complete, accurate, and consistent documentation. Missing employment verification, incomplete certification records, or conflicting experience information may delay the Board’s review.

The Board determines whether an applicant qualifies for examination and licensure. The examination provider administers the test but does not decide whether an applicant satisfies Delaware’s professional licensing requirements.

After passing the examination, candidates must complete all remaining requirements before a professional license can be issued. Applicants should monitor their DELPROS account and email for status updates and requests for additional information.

Licensed Delaware HVACR professionals must comply with applicable renewal, continuing education, professional conduct, and other requirements associated with their licensing period.

A professional HVACR license is separate from a business license. Individuals planning to operate an HVACR contracting company may also need a business entity, tax registration, insurance, business licensing, and other operational approvals.

Begin preparation with refrigeration and air-conditioning fundamentals. Review heat transfer, pressure-temperature relationships, refrigerant flow, compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, superheat, subcooling, airflow, humidity, and troubleshooting procedures.

Review mechanical ventilation and exhaust requirements. Study ventilation rates, duct construction, exhaust discharge, combustion air, appliance venting, equipment clearances, supports, and air-distribution principles.

Practice Manual J procedures and learn how building characteristics affect heating and cooling loads. Understand how the calculation supports equipment selection rather than relying only on square footage or the capacity of existing equipment.

Use the Ductulator during preparation. Practice connecting airflow, friction rate, velocity, and duct dimensions until the tool becomes familiar and efficient to use.

Review boiler operation, burners, controls, safety valves, low-water protection, feedwater systems, combustion, inspection, maintenance, and common operating conditions.
