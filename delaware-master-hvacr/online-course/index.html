Exam Details

The Delaware Master HVACR examination evaluates the technical knowledge associated with designing, installing, constructing, maintaining, servicing, repairing, altering, and modifying heating, air-conditioning, refrigeration, ventilation, process-cooling, process-heating, and gas-piping systems.

Candidates should be prepared for questions involving general HVACR knowledge, piping, equipment, system sizing, installation, ventilation, exhaust systems, testing, and inspection. These subjects require a combination of technical understanding, calculation ability, equipment knowledge, and code interpretation.

General HVACR preparation may include heat transfer, temperature, pressure, humidity, airflow, refrigeration principles, combustion, electrical fundamentals, tools, materials, safety practices, system operation, and common trade terminology.

Piping preparation may involve fuel-gas piping, refrigerant piping, approved materials, sizing procedures, joining methods, supports, protection, regulators, valves, pressure considerations, and testing requirements.

Equipment subjects may address furnaces, boilers, heat pumps, refrigeration systems, air-conditioning equipment, compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, air handlers, burners, controls, and safety devices.

System-sizing and installation questions may involve heating and cooling loads, airflow requirements, duct sizing, equipment selection, installation clearances, distribution-system design, and proper placement of HVACR components.

Ventilation and exhaust preparation may include ventilation rates, mechanical exhaust, combustion air, duct construction, air movement, appliance venting, termination requirements, exhaust discharge locations, and equipment clearances.

Testing and inspection preparation may involve pressure tests, leak testing, equipment startup, operational checks, safety controls, performance verification, inspection procedures, and evaluation of completed systems.

The examination requires candidates to move between multiple technical publications. Strong preparation includes knowing which reference addresses each subject and how the information is organized within that book.

Open Book Test

The Delaware Master HVACR examination is an open-book test. Candidates may use authorized printed references under the current testing provider’s rules.

An open-book test still requires careful preparation. The reference materials contain extensive technical information, and candidates may not have enough time to research every question from the beginning. Effective preparation combines HVACR knowledge with fast and accurate reference navigation.

The International Fuel Gas Code should be used for questions involving gas-piping materials, sizing, appliance connections, regulators, shutoff valves, combustion air, venting, pressure testing, and fuel-gas installation requirements.

The International Mechanical Code supports questions involving mechanical equipment, ventilation, exhaust systems, duct construction, combustion air, refrigeration systems, boilers, chimneys, vents, clearances, supports, and related mechanical provisions.

Low Pressure Boilers supports study involving boiler construction, operation, burners, fittings, controls, safety devices, water systems, combustion, maintenance, inspection, and safe operating procedures.

Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning supports preparation involving refrigeration cycles, refrigerants, compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, heat pumps, heating systems, air-conditioning equipment, electrical controls, troubleshooting, installation, and maintenance.

Residential Load Calculation – Manual J supports residential heating and cooling load calculations. Candidates should understand how climate, insulation, windows, doors, orientation, infiltration, occupancy, and internal heat gains affect the calculated load.

The Ductulator supports duct-sizing work involving airflow, friction rate, velocity, and round or rectangular duct dimensions. Candidates should practice using the device repeatedly so they can interpret its scales efficiently during timed work.

Students should complete reference-navigation exercises throughout the course. Select a subject, identify the correct publication, locate the relevant chapter or table, and read enough surrounding information to understand the complete requirement.

Definitions, exceptions, table notes, equipment conditions, and surrounding provisions may affect the correct interpretation. Candidates should avoid selecting an answer based only on an isolated line or familiar phrase.

Reference preparation must follow the current testing rules. Candidates should review the latest instructions regarding tabs, highlighting, handwritten notes, inserts, loose paper, photocopies, removable markers, calculators, and other test-day materials.

Licensing Steps

Begin by reviewing the Delaware Master HVACR license classification and confirming that the unrestricted license matches the HVACR services you plan to provide. Delaware also offers restricted HVACR licenses for designated specialties.

Create an account through Delaware Professional Regulation Online Services and begin the Master HVACR application in DELPROS. Professional licensing applications and supporting documents are submitted through the state’s online system.

Gather the records required for the applicable licensing pathway. These may include license verifications, employment records, apprenticeship documentation, certification records, and other supporting information requested by the Delaware Board.

Applicants for unrestricted Master HVACR licensure must provide the required certification and qualifying experience documentation. The specific application pathway depends on the applicant’s apprenticeship background, work history, and current licenses.

An applicant with an acceptable journeyman certificate may need to provide the apprenticeship program Certificate of Completion and documentation of qualifying work experience obtained after receiving that certificate.

An applicant without an acceptable journeyman certificate may need to document a longer period of qualifying work experience under the supervision of a master licensee. The Board determines whether an Apprenticeship By-Pass Examination is required.

Submit the completed application and all required attachments through DELPROS. The Delaware Board reviews the application before authorizing a candidate to sit for the Master HVACR examination.

After receiving approval, follow the testing provider’s registration and scheduling instructions. Review appointment procedures, identification rules, approved references, calculator requirements, and testing policies before the examination date.

Use the online course and listed references to prepare. Complete technical review, code-navigation exercises, load calculations, duct-sizing practice, equipment study, and timed exam activities.

Complete the examination and earn the required passing score. The official examination result must be accepted through the Delaware licensing process.

Finish any remaining application requirements after passing. Examination success is one part of the licensing process and does not automatically issue the professional license when documents or other requirements remain outstanding.

State Requirements

A Delaware Master HVACR license permits the license holder to perform services involving heating, air conditioning, refrigeration, ventilation, process cooling, process heating, and related gas-piping systems within the scope established by the state.

A Master HVACR Restricted license allows work only within the specialty identified on the license. Restricted areas include heating involving forced-air systems, ventilation and gas piping; hydronic heating and gas piping; commercial hood systems; refrigeration; air conditioning; and gas piping.

Professionals planning to offer services in more than one restricted specialty should pursue the unrestricted Master HVACR classification. Applicants should select the license that accurately reflects their intended work.

A Master HVACR license does not automatically provide authority for plumbing work involving systems that bring water into a building or remove liquid and water-carried waste. Separate plumbing authority may be required for that work.

Applicants should provide complete, accurate, and consistent documentation. Missing employment verification, incomplete certification records, or conflicting experience information may delay the Board’s review.

The Delaware Board determines whether an applicant qualifies for examination and licensure. The testing provider administers the examination but does not decide whether an applicant meets the state’s professional licensing requirements.

After passing the examination, candidates must complete all remaining requirements before the license can be issued. Applicants should monitor their DELPROS account and email for status updates or requests for additional information.

Licensed Delaware HVACR professionals must comply with applicable renewal, continuing education, professional conduct, and other requirements associated with their licensing period.

A professional HVACR license is separate from a business license. Individuals planning to operate an HVACR contracting business may also need a legal business entity, insurance, tax registration, business licensing, and other operational approvals.

Test Information and Study Materials

Begin preparation with refrigeration and air-conditioning fundamentals. Review heat transfer, temperature-pressure relationships, refrigerant flow, compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, superheat, subcooling, airflow, humidity, and troubleshooting procedures.

Study fuel-gas systems, including approved piping materials, sizing, appliance connections, regulators, shutoff valves, combustion air, venting, testing, and installation safety. Practice reading table headings, notes, and conditions carefully.

Review mechanical ventilation and exhaust requirements. Study ventilation rates, duct construction, exhaust discharge, combustion air, appliance venting, equipment clearances, supports, and air-distribution principles.

Practice Manual J procedures and learn how building characteristics influence heating and cooling loads. Understand how the load calculation supports equipment selection instead of relying only on square footage or the size of existing equipment.

Use the Ductulator during study activities. Practice connecting airflow, friction rate, velocity, and duct dimensions until the tool becomes familiar and efficient to use.

Review boiler operation, burners, controls, safety valves, low-water protection, feedwater systems, combustion, inspection, maintenance, and common operating conditions.

Study HVACR equipment and controls. Review furnaces, heat pumps, compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, air handlers, burners, thermostats, relays, contactors, motors, and safety components.

Complete timed reference-navigation exercises using every listed publication. Track which subjects take the longest to locate and repeat those searches during later study sessions.

Keep a record of missed practice questions. Determine whether each error resulted from a technical knowledge gap, calculation mistake, incorrect reference selection, overlooked table note, or misunderstanding of the question.
