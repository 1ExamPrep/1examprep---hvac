Exam Details

The South Carolina Residential HVAC examination is administered as part of the residential specialty licensing process through the South Carolina Residential Builders Commission and PSI. The Residential HVAC technical examination contains 80 questions. Candidates need 52 correct answers, or 65%, to pass the technical portion. The time allowed for the Residential HVAC technical exam is 240 minutes.

The Residential HVAC exam covers residential HVAC work, including air conditioning and heat pump equipment, LP and natural gas, related piping, fuel oil appliances and piping, ductwork, and associated trades. The exam content is built around minimum knowledge expected of residential HVAC professionals who perform work for the public in a competent and responsible manner.

In addition to the Residential HVAC technical exam, South Carolina residential specialty contractor applicants are also required to pass the Business Management and Law for Residential Builders examination. That business and law exam contains 50 questions and requires a passing scale score of 68. This online exam prep course focuses on the Residential HVAC technical portion listed in this product title.

The Residential HVAC technical exam content includes insulation, hangers and supports, sound, vibration and seismic control, heating and cooling principles, refrigerants, load calculations, testing, adjusting and balancing, controls, air conditioning and heat pump equipment, furnaces and heaters, chimneys, flues and vents, combustion air, ducts, ventilation and exhausts, safety, fuel gas, LP gas, fuel oil, and piping.

The largest portion of the exam outline is ducts, followed by several major trade areas such as heating and cooling principles, load calculations, air conditioning and heat pump equipment, fuel gas, piping, furnaces, controls, refrigerants, and LP gas. A successful study plan should not rely on one book or one topic alone. The exam pulls from multiple technical references and general HVAC trade knowledge, so preparation should include both reference navigation and subject review.

Open Book Test

The South Carolina Residential HVAC examination is an open book test. Candidates may bring permitted reference books listed for the examination. Reference books may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed, but they must otherwise be unmarked and may not contain additional papers, loose notes, or attached pages. Permanent tabs are allowed. Temporary tabs, such as removable sticky notes or tabs that can be removed without tearing the page, are not allowed.

Because the test is open book, speed and organization are important. You should know which reference is most useful for each subject area before exam day. For example, load calculation questions may require a different study approach than fuel gas, duct construction, OSHA safety, or oil burner questions. The more familiar you are with each book’s layout, index, chapters, tables, and terminology, the easier it becomes to locate information during the exam.

Open book exams often test your ability to apply information rather than simply remember it. You may still need to understand the concept behind the question before you can choose the correct reference or interpret the answer. Licensing Steps

Individuals seeking a South Carolina Residential HVAC Installer and Repairer license by examination must first apply through the South Carolina Residential Builders Commission. The Commission reviews the application, required documentation, fees, and experience information before approving an applicant for examination eligibility.

Work experience must be verified and approved before an applicant is made eligible for the exam. Once approved, the applicant receives examination eligibility information and may schedule the required exam with PSI. Examination eligibility is valid for one year. Candidates are allowed three attempts to pass the required examination within a 12-month period.

Applicants pursuing licensure by examination must pass the Residential HVAC technical examination and the Business Management and Law for Residential Builders examination. The trade exam confirms technical knowledge in residential HVAC work, while the business and law exam focuses on business management, licensing laws, contracts, project management, estimating, financial management, taxes, liens, insurance, bonding, labor laws, environmental rules, and related business topics.

South Carolina also provides certain exam waiver paths for qualified applicants. The Residential HVAC technical portion may be waived for applicants who meet specific criteria, such as holding a qualifying South Carolina Mechanical Contractor license in the required field, holding a qualifying license from an eligible state, or holding an eligible HARV Master Mechanical certification issued by examination under the applicable program dates. Work experience requirements and the Business Management and Law portion still apply when the technical exam is waived.

Applicants should follow the current instructions from the South Carolina Residential Builders Commission when applying, submitting documentation, paying fees, and scheduling exams. The Commission determines eligibility, and PSI administers the examination process.

State Requirements

South Carolina residential HVAC work exceeding $500 requires the appropriate residential HVAC license. The Residential HVAC Installer and Repairer license is handled by the South Carolina Residential Builders Commission. Commercial HVAC work exceeding the commercial threshold is handled separately through the South Carolina Contractor’s Licensing Board.

For residential HVAC licensure by examination, applicants must meet the Commission’s application, experience, and examination requirements. The state requires applicants to submit the proper application and supporting documents before being approved to sit for the exam. Prior to application submission, applicants must have one year of Commission-approved experience under the supervision of a licensee in the trade for which they are applying in order to be eligible to take the examination for licensure.

The state’s process is designed to confirm that residential HVAC license applicants have both practical experience and technical competence. The exam measures knowledge of residential HVAC installation and repair topics, including equipment, fuels, duct systems, combustion air, piping, ventilation, safety, and calculations. Applicants should be prepared to show that they understand how residential HVAC systems are installed, serviced, and evaluated according to applicable codes, standards, and trade practices.

Fees, forms, application instructions, and eligibility rules may be updated by the state. Students should follow the current South Carolina Residential Builders Commission application instructions when preparing to apply for licensure.

Reference Books

  • Manual J - Residential Load Calcs, reprinted 2016, Eighth Full Edition
    A residential load calculation reference used to study heating and cooling load principles, building characteristics, system sizing concepts, and calculation procedures for residential HVAC design.
  • International Mechanical Code, 2021
    A mechanical code reference covering installation requirements, equipment, ventilation, exhaust systems, duct systems, combustion air, chimneys, vents, and related mechanical provisions.
  • Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA)
    A construction safety reference covering federal OSHA requirements for construction work, including jobsite safety practices that may apply to HVAC installation and repair activities.
  • Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning, 22nd edition
    A trade reference covering refrigeration and air conditioning principles, system components, service concepts, troubleshooting, equipment operation, and applied HVAC knowledge.
  • HVAC Duct Construction Standards, Metal and Flexible, 2005, 3rd Edition
    A duct construction standards reference used to study metal and flexible duct systems, duct fabrication, reinforcement, installation practices, and air distribution construction details.
  • International Fuel Gas Code, 2018
    A fuel gas code reference covering gas piping, combustion air, venting, appliances, installation requirements, safety provisions, and fuel gas system rules.
  • Residential Oil Burners, 3rd Edition
    A residential oil heat reference covering oil burner equipment, fuel oil systems, operation, service, combustion, controls, and installation-related concepts.
  • Ductulator
    A duct sizing and air distribution tool used to support duct calculation practice, airflow review, friction rate understanding, and residential duct system preparation.
