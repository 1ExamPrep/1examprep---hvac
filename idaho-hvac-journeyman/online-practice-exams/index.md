### Open Book Test

The Idaho HVAC Journeyman examination is an **open book test**.

The approved exam references are the **2018 International Mechanical Code** and the **2018 International Fuel Gas Code**. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own approved reference materials to the examination center.

The current examination rules permit pre-exam highlighting, underlining, and permanent indexing of approved references. Candidates may not write in their books during the testing session. Additional loose papers and temporary markers such as removable Post-it-style tabs are prohibited.

Commentaries, illustrated versions, handbooks, and other unauthorized editions are not substitutes for the approved code books. Candidates should prepare using the exact approved references they intend to use during testing.

An open-book examination still requires preparation. Candidates receive an average of 2.4 minutes per question, so searching an entire book from the beginning for each answer can consume valuable time. Practice should focus on learning the structure of the IMC and IFGC and recognizing which chapter is likely to contain the answer.

Online practice questions can be especially helpful for building this skill. Candidates can practice identifying whether a question belongs in the IMC or IFGC, choosing the likely chapter, locating the relevant section, and checking tables or exceptions before selecting an answer.

### Licensing Steps

Idaho HVAC Journeyman licensing is administered through the **Idaho Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses**. Idaho provides more than one qualification pathway for Journeyman applicants.

1. **Choose the appropriate qualification pathway.** Candidates may qualify through an approved Idaho apprenticeship-school pathway or the work-experience pathway.
2. **Complete approved instruction when using the apprenticeship-school pathway.** Idaho requires completion of 576 hours of instruction through a Board-approved HVAC apprenticeship program.
3. **Document supervised HVAC installation experience.** The standard apprenticeship pathway requires 8,000 hours of supervised HVAC installation work before the Journeyman license can be issued.
4. **Maintain the required apprentice registration.** Candidates earning the 8,000 Idaho work hours must meet the applicable apprentice-registration requirements.
5. **Use the work-experience pathway when applicable.** Idaho also recognizes a pathway requiring 16,000 hours of qualifying supervised HVAC installation work.
6. **Register for the examination through PSI.** Idaho HVAC examinations are currently offered through PSI.
7. **Prepare with the approved code books.** Study the 2018 International Mechanical Code and 2018 International Fuel Gas Code.
8. **Take the 100-question Journeyman examination.** Candidates receive 240 minutes to complete the exam.
9. **Achieve the required passing score.** A minimum score of 70 is required.
10. **Apply for licensure through Idaho DOPL.** After meeting the examination and qualification requirements, complete the state licensing process.

### State Requirements

Under Idaho's apprenticeship-school pathway, an HVAC Journeyman applicant completes **576 hours of instruction at a Board-approved HVAC apprenticeship program**.

The candidate must also document **8,000 hours of supervised HVAC installation work** before the Journeyman license can be issued. Idaho permits a candidate using this pathway to take the examination after completing the required 576 hours of approved instruction, but licensure is not issued until the 8,000-hour supervised work requirement is documented.

Idaho also offers a work-experience pathway requiring **16,000 hours of supervised HVAC installation experience**. Applicants using that pathway must provide the required work verification.

Idaho requires HVAC work used toward qualification to satisfy the state's licensing and supervision rules. Hours worked without the required license or registration do not count toward required on-the-job training time.

The current HVAC codes adopted for use in Idaho include the **2018 International Mechanical Code**, **2018 International Fuel Gas Code**, and applicable portions of the **2018 International Residential Code**. The Journeyman exam reference list specifically identifies the 2018 IMC and 2018 IFGC as the approved examination books.

### Test Information and Study Materials

**IMC General Regulations:** Review equipment installation, clearances, access, protection, condensate disposal, equipment locations, and other general mechanical requirements. Chapter 3 is one of the larger IMC areas on the exam.

**Ventilation:** Practice identifying ventilation requirements, outdoor-air provisions, mechanical ventilation conditions, air-distribution requirements, and applicable tables.

**Exhaust Systems:** IMC Chapter 5 represents another major examination category. Study domestic exhaust, commercial exhaust, dryer exhaust, kitchen exhaust systems, environmental air, hazardous exhaust, clearances, and terminations.

**Duct Systems:** Review duct construction, insulation, installation, support, plenums, flexible ducts, fire and smoke dampers, and other distribution-system requirements.

**Chimneys and Vents:** Study venting systems, vent connectors, terminations, clearances, and requirements that apply to mechanical appliances.

**Specific Appliances:** Review installation requirements for furnaces, unit heaters, duct furnaces, heat pumps, cooling equipment, and other mechanical appliances.

**Boilers and Pressure Vessels:** Study installation requirements, controls, safety devices, clearances, and related piping provisions.

**Refrigeration:** Review refrigerant classifications, refrigerant quantities, refrigeration machinery rooms, ventilation, detection, piping, and system requirements. The published blueprint assigns five questions to IMC Chapter 11.

**Hydronic Piping:** Practice questions involving piping systems, expansion tanks, valves, pressure testing, embedded piping, materials, and related installation requirements.

**Fuel-Oil Piping and Storage:** Review applicable fuel-oil storage, piping, tank, valve, and installation provisions within the IMC.

**Gas Piping:** IFGC Chapter 4 is one of the major fuel-gas areas. Practice pipe sizing, gas pressure, valves, regulators, appliance connections, testing, purging, and piping materials.

**Fuel-Gas Chimneys and Vents:** IFGC Chapter 5 has approximately eight questions, making it the largest IFGC category. Review vent sizing, vent connectors, draft hoods, mechanical draft systems, terminations, and clearances.

**Fuel-Gas Appliances:** Study appliance installation, combustion air, equipment connections, clearances, controls, and appliance-specific requirements.

**Code Navigation:** Practice deciding which reference controls the question before beginning the lookup. Mechanical-system questions generally lead to the IMC, while gas-piping and fuel-gas appliance questions often lead to the IFGC.

**Timed Practice:** With 100 questions in 240 minutes, candidates should practice at an exam-like pace. Timed question sets can help develop judgment about when to complete a detailed lookup and when to mark a difficult question and move forward.
