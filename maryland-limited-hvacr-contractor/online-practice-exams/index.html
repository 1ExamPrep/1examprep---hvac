Exam Details

The Maryland Limited HVACR Contractor examination is administered by PSI Services LLC through the licensing program of the Maryland Board of Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractors.

The examination assesses technical knowledge involving equipment service, electrical controls, piping, refrigeration, heating systems, air distribution, and construction safety.

  • Exam Name: Maryland Limited HVACR Contractor
  • Testing Provider: PSI Services LLC
  • Licensing Authority: Maryland Board of Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractors
  • Examination Format: Multiple-choice questions
  • Number of Questions: 50
  • Time Limit: 120 minutes (2 hours)
  • Passing Score: 70%
  • Exam Type: Open book
  • Online Practice Access: 3 months

The PSI Limited HVACR Contractor content outline identifies six examination categories:

  • Electrical Knowledge, Motors, and Controls – 15 Questions: Electrical circuits, motors, and control equipment.
  • Piping – 5 Questions: Piping materials, fittings, connections, supports, insulation, and troubleshooting.
  • Refrigeration and Air Conditioning – 10 Questions: Refrigeration theory, refrigerants, equipment components, maintenance, and repair.
  • Heating Systems – 10 Questions: Heating equipment, hydronic systems, combustion air, venting, and troubleshooting.
  • Air Distribution Systems – 5 Questions: Ductwork, airflow, ventilation, testing, and balancing.
  • Safety – 5 Questions: Protective equipment, construction hazards, workplace procedures, and safe tool use.

Electrical Knowledge, Motors, and Controls is the largest examination category, representing 30% of the test. Refrigeration and Air Conditioning and Heating Systems each account for 20%.

Together, these three categories represent 70% of the examination. Understanding their importance helps you establish study priorities while continuing to review piping, air distribution, and safety.

With two hours available for 50 questions, candidates have an average of 2.4 minutes per question. Developing familiarity with technical subjects and reference navigation can help you use this time effectively.

Open Book Test

The Maryland Limited HVACR Contractor (PSI) examination is an open book test. Candidates may consult the reference materials authorized under the PSI examination rules.

PSI permits approved reference books to contain highlighting, underlining, and acceptable permanent indexing tabs. Books must not contain handwritten notes or additional loose or attached papers. Study guides are not allowed inside the examination room.

Approved references are not supplied at the testing center.

Successful open-book preparation involves more than locating answers. Candidates benefit from recognizing technical terminology, understanding equipment functions, and knowing which reference addresses a particular subject.

Licensing Steps

Maryland Limited HVACR Contractor licensing is administered through the state's Board of Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractors.

The Limited Contractor classification is intended for qualified professionals performing HVACR maintenance and repair within the scope of their license.

  1. Obtain Journeyman Qualifications: Hold the appropriate Maryland HVACR journeyman license and gain qualifying professional experience.
  2. Complete Required Employment: Accumulate the required experience under an appropriately licensed HVACR contractor.
  3. Document Your Experience: Organize employment records and verification of qualifying HVACR work.
  4. Apply for the Examination: Submit the required examination application and supporting documentation to PSI.
  5. Prepare for Testing: Review the six examination categories and practice applying HVACR technical knowledge.
  6. Schedule Your PSI Examination: Arrange testing after your application has been approved.
  7. Pass the Examination: Achieve the required minimum passing score of 70%.
  8. Complete Your License Application: Apply for the Maryland Limited HVACR Contractor license and satisfy the applicable business and insurance obligations.

Preparing employment documentation early and organizing a consistent study schedule can make the licensing process more manageable.

State Requirements

The Maryland Board of Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractors establishes the qualifications and professional responsibilities associated with Limited Contractor licensing.

The standard qualification pathway includes:

  • Journeyman Licensing: Holding a Maryland HVACR journeyman license for at least two years.
  • Qualifying Employment: Regularly and principally performing HVACR services under the direction and control of a properly licensed contractor.
  • Recent Work Experience: Completing at least 1,000 hours of qualifying HVACR work during the year preceding the application.
  • Examination: Passing the Limited HVACR Contractor examination with a score of at least 70%.
  • License Application: Completing the requirements established by the Maryland HVACR Board.

Maryland law also authorizes the Board to evaluate certain comparable professional education, training, and work experience.

A Maryland Limited HVACR Contractor license authorizes maintenance and repair, not the installation or retrofit of complete HVACR systems. The authorized work may involve heating, air conditioning, refrigeration, ventilation, or hydronic systems within the applicable license scope.

Limited contractors performing contracting activities may also be subject to liability insurance and license-number advertising requirements under Maryland law.

Understanding the difference between limited maintenance-and-repair authority and broader contractor classifications is an important part of professional licensing preparation.

Reference Books

The following technical references support preparation for the Maryland Limited HVACR Contractor examination.

  • International Mechanical Code, 2018
    Published by the International Code Council, the 2018 International Mechanical Code covers mechanical equipment, ventilation, exhaust systems, refrigeration, ductwork, and associated installation requirements. It supports understanding of mechanical code terminology and technical requirements encountered throughout HVACR work.
  • Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning, 22nd Edition
    This comprehensive HVACR textbook explains refrigeration cycles, heating equipment, air conditioning systems, electrical motors, controls, piping, service procedures, and troubleshooting principles. It is useful for strengthening the technical knowledge associated with HVACR maintenance and repair.
  • Residential Load Calculation - Manual J, 8th Edition
    Published by the Air Conditioning Contractors of America, Manual J provides a recognized method for calculating residential heating and cooling loads. It addresses building characteristics, heat gain, heat loss, and the relationship between building conditions and HVAC equipment sizing.
  • Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA)
    These federal construction safety regulations cover workplace hazards, protective equipment, ladders, scaffolds, fall protection, excavation safety, and safe construction practices. They support review of the examination's Safety category.
  • Ductulator
    The ACCA Ductulator is a duct-sizing tool used to evaluate relationships between airflow, duct dimensions, velocity, and friction loss. It supports technical study involving ventilation and air distribution systems.

These five references address different areas of HVACR knowledge, from mechanical codes and refrigeration principles to residential loads, duct sizing, and construction safety.

Studying reference materials alongside online practice questions helps connect technical explanations with practical troubleshooting and service applications.

Electrical Knowledge, Motors, and Controls

Electrical Knowledge, Motors, and Controls accounts for 15 questions, making it the largest subject area.

Review AC and DC circuit principles, voltage, current, resistance, electrical power, motors, and electrical control devices.

HVACR systems use electrical components to operate compressors, fans, pumps, heating equipment, and other mechanical devices. Understanding these relationships is valuable when interpreting troubleshooting questions.

Motor Operation and Protection

Motors are important components in many heating and cooling systems. Review motor fundamentals, operating characteristics, common protective devices, and control arrangements.

Understanding how motors are energized and controlled helps build familiarity with refrigeration compressors, blower assemblies, and circulation equipment.

Electrical Control Circuits

Review contactors, relays, thermostats, switches, and other control components associated with HVACR equipment.

Become familiar with how control circuits respond to changing operating conditions and how connected components influence system operation.

Practice interpreting electrical control scenarios and identifying the function of individual devices.

Refrigeration and Air Conditioning

Refrigeration and Air Conditioning accounts for 10 examination questions.

Study the refrigeration cycle, compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, refrigerants, and equipment operation.

Review system maintenance, troubleshooting, and the relationship between operating conditions and refrigeration performance.

Developing a strong understanding of refrigeration theory helps connect practical service experience with examination questions.

Refrigeration System Troubleshooting

Troubleshooting requires understanding how refrigeration system components interact.

Review common service observations, system performance concepts, and the roles of refrigerant flow, heat transfer, and electrical controls.

Concentrate on understanding the principles behind system behavior rather than memorizing isolated symptoms.

Heating Systems

Heating Systems represents 10 examination questions.

Review heating equipment, combustion air, hydronic heating, equipment sizing, vents, chimneys, and operating principles.

Develop familiarity with how heating systems transfer energy and how control devices regulate equipment operation.

Review common maintenance and troubleshooting concepts associated with different heating equipment types.

Combustion and Venting

Combustion equipment requires appropriate air supply and venting arrangements.

Study basic combustion principles, combustion air, venting terminology, and the purpose of equipment safety controls.

Understanding these concepts supports preparation involving fuel-fired heating systems and related maintenance work.

Piping Systems

Piping accounts for five examination questions.

Review piping materials, joints, fittings, valves, hangers, supports, insulation, and general testing concepts.

Piping knowledge supports HVACR service work involving refrigerant lines, heating systems, and connected equipment.

Air Distribution Systems

Air Distribution Systems accounts for five examination questions.

Study duct design terminology, airflow relationships, duct materials, supports, insulation, ventilation, and testing and balancing principles.

Understanding airflow concepts helps explain how air distribution affects comfort and mechanical system operation.

Residential Load Calculations

Manual J introduces important principles for evaluating residential heating and cooling demands.

Review building characteristics, insulation, windows, heat gain, heat loss, and equipment sizing relationships.

Understanding how building loads influence equipment selection provides valuable context for HVACR technical questions.

Duct Sizing and Airflow

Review the relationship between airflow, duct dimensions, air velocity, and friction loss.

Become familiar with the ACCA Ductulator and the basic purpose of duct-sizing calculations.

Understanding these concepts supports air distribution knowledge and practical evaluation of ventilation system performance.

Construction Safety

Safety represents five examination questions.

Review OSHA construction requirements involving protective equipment, ladders, scaffolds, fall protection, tools, hazardous materials, and general workplace safety practices.

Safe work habits are important for HVACR professionals who service equipment in mechanical rooms, attics, rooftops, and other working environments.
