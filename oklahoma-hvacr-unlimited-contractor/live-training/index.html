About this HVACR Unlimited Contractor package

This program is built around the technical subjects, calculations, HVACR concepts, code requirements, safety standards, and contractor business topics associated with Oklahoma HVAC/R Unlimited Contractor licensing.

The Oklahoma HVAC/R Unlimited Contractor license is regulated by the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board and represents the highest HVAC/R mechanical contractor category in the state. The license authorizes qualifying contractors to install, alter, modify, service, maintain, or repair cooling products, systems, and equipment, including process piping; heating equipment and associated process piping; sheet metal; natural gas piping; and refrigeration systems.

Unlike the HVAC/R Limited Contractor classification, the Unlimited license is not restricted to systems of 25 tons or less or heating equipment of 500,000 Btu/h or less per appliance. Candidates preparing for this examination therefore need broad knowledge of commercial and residential HVACR systems, refrigeration, heating, ventilation, duct systems, natural gas, electrical controls, load calculations, piping, equipment installation, troubleshooting, and safety.

Exam Details

The Oklahoma HVACR Contractor Unlimited examination is administered by PSI after the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board determines that an applicant meets the qualifications to proceed with testing.

  • Exam: Oklahoma HVACR Contractor Unlimited
  • Number of Questions: 90
  • Passing Score: 70%
  • Passing Raw Score: 63 correct answers
  • Time Allowed: 240 minutes
  • Testing Provider: PSI

The technical examination covers six major subject categories:

  • Electrical Knowledge and Controls: 15 questions
  • Natural Gas: 12 questions
  • Air Distribution Systems: 25 questions
  • Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Systems: 20 questions
  • Heating Systems: 15 questions
  • Safety: 3 questions

Air Distribution Systems is the largest portion of the examination with 25 questions. This area can include duct definitions, sizing and design, assembly and installation, hangers and supports, duct materials and shapes, insulation, fire and smoke control, hoods and exhaust systems, testing and balancing, ventilation requirements, and plans and symbols.

Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Systems account for another 20 questions. Candidates should prepare for refrigeration theory, refrigerants, equipment sizing and design, equipment and components, piping, installation, system operation, troubleshooting, and maintenance.

Heating Systems account for 15 questions and include heating theory, system types, combustion air, vents and chimneys, equipment sizing, equipment installation, operation, troubleshooting, maintenance, and hydronic systems and piping. Electrical Knowledge and Controls also contains 15 questions, including electrical theory, motors, DC circuits, AC circuits, and controls.

With 90 questions and 240 minutes available, candidates have an average of approximately two minutes and forty seconds per question. Efficient use of the permitted references can be particularly important for calculations, equipment-sizing questions, duct problems, electrical questions, and detailed code requirements.

Mechanical contractor candidates also prepare for the Oklahoma Mechanical Business and Law examination. That examination contains 50 questions, requires a 70% passing score with 35 correct answers, and allows 120 minutes.

Open Book Test

The Oklahoma HVACR Contractor Unlimited examination is an open-book test. Candidates are responsible for bringing permitted reference materials to the examination center and complying with PSI's rules concerning those references.

The current examination reference list includes the ACCA Ductulator, International Mechanical Code, 2024 edition with Oklahoma revisions, International Fuel Gas Code, 2024 edition with Oklahoma revisions, Manual N - Load Calculation for Small Commercial Buildings, Fifth Edition, Mathematics for Plumbers and Pipefitters, 8th Edition, and Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA) with applicable amendments.

Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning is also used for examination preparation. PSI's current transition schedule permits the 22nd edition beginning August 13, 2026. The 20th and 21st editions are accepted under the current transition rules, with the 20th edition scheduled to be discontinued as an allowed reference beginning November 1, 2026.

Approved reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are permitted for indexing. Temporary tabs such as Post-it notes are not allowed. Candidate-added writing is not permitted in the references before or during the examination session.

For an open-book HVACR examination, simply having the books available is not enough. Candidates should repeatedly practice choosing the appropriate reference, locating the relevant section or table, interpreting the information, and applying it to the question being asked.

Licensing Steps

The Oklahoma Construction Industries Board regulates mechanical contractor licensing and determines eligibility for the HVAC/R Unlimited Contractor category.

  1. Gain qualifying mechanical experience. Build and document verifiable experience in the mechanical trade.
  2. Meet journeyman-level qualifications. Oklahoma mechanical contractor applicants must first satisfy the applicable requirements associated with journeyman qualification.
  3. Complete the additional contractor experience requirement. Mechanical contractor applicants must meet the journeyman requirements plus an additional year of qualifying mechanical trade experience.
  4. Maintain the required relationship with the mechanical firm. A mechanical contractor must be a permanent employee, owner, partner, or officer of the mechanical firm.
  5. Submit the mechanical contractor application. Provide the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board with the required application and supporting experience documentation.
  6. Receive authorization for examination. The Board reviews eligibility before an applicant proceeds to testing.
  7. Schedule examinations through PSI. Approved applicants arrange their HVACR Contractor Unlimited and applicable Mechanical Business and Law examinations.
  8. Prepare with the exam references. Study HVACR theory, codes, calculations, duct design, refrigeration, heating, natural gas, electrical controls, and safety.
  9. Complete the required examinations. Achieve the required passing scores for the contractor licensing process.
  10. Address active contractor requirements. Complete applicable insurance, bond, and other requirements before operating under an active Oklahoma mechanical contractor license.

State Requirements

Oklahoma requires mechanical contractor applicants to meet the applicable journeyman qualifications plus an additional year of mechanical trade experience. Oklahoma defines one year of verifiable experience as 2,000 hours.

All mechanical contractors must also be a permanent employee, owner, partner, or officer in the mechanical firm associated with the contractor license.

The HVAC/R Unlimited Contractor license authorizes the contractor to install, alter, modify, service, maintain, or repair cooling products, systems, and equipment, including process piping; heating equipment, including process piping; sheet metal; natural gas piping; and refrigeration.

The Unlimited category is important for contractors performing HVAC work beyond the limitations of the HVAC/R Limited classification. Oklahoma identifies the Limited category as applying to cooling systems of no more than 25 tons and heating equipment with an input of no more than 500,000 Btu/h per appliance. Work beyond those limitations requires the applicable Unlimited HVAC/R license.

After satisfying examination requirements, contractors must also address the requirements necessary to maintain an active Oklahoma mechanical contractor license, including applicable bond and insurance obligations.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive HVACR Unlimited Contractor study plan should reflect the weighting of the examination. Air Distribution Systems and Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Systems together account for 45 of the 90 questions, making these two categories especially important.

For air distribution, candidates should practice duct sizing and design, duct assembly, installation, supports, insulation, ventilation, hoods and exhaust systems, fire and smoke control, testing, balancing, and plans and symbols. Use the Ductulator and Manual N regularly rather than waiting until the final stages of preparation.

Refrigeration and air-conditioning review should include refrigeration theory, refrigerants, equipment components, system sizing, piping, installation, troubleshooting, maintenance, and system operation. Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning can support both technical review and reference-navigation practice.

Heating preparation should include equipment sizing, combustion air, vents and chimneys, heating theory, hydronic systems, piping, installation, troubleshooting, and maintenance. Natural gas study should include piping, valves, connectors, sizing, materials, hangers, supports, and testing.

Electrical Knowledge and Controls deserves dedicated study as well. Review motors, basic electrical theory, AC and DC circuits, controls, and electrical troubleshooting associated with HVACR equipment.

For Mechanical Business and Law, organize preparation around estimating, project management, contracts, financial matters, labor, workers’ compensation, insurance, bonds, payroll, liens, licensing requirements, and Oklahoma mechanical regulations.
