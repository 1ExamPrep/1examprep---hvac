Exam Details

The Oklahoma HVACR Contractor - Unlimited Examination is administered by PSI as part of the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board mechanical licensing program.

The examination measures knowledge of mechanical systems, refrigeration, air conditioning, electrical controls, natural gas, air distribution, heating equipment, and construction safety.

  • Exam Name: Oklahoma HVACR Contractor - Unlimited
  • Licensing Authority: Oklahoma Construction Industries Board
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Examination Format: Open book
  • Number of Questions: 90
  • Time Allowed: 240 minutes
  • Passing Score: 70%, equivalent to 63 correct answers

The examination covers six primary technical subject areas:

  • Electrical Knowledge and Controls
  • Natural Gas
  • Air Distribution Systems
  • Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Systems
  • Heating Systems
  • Safety

The Unlimited examination addresses mechanical work without the equipment-capacity restrictions associated with the Limited classification. Candidates should prepare for technical questions involving a wider range of mechanical equipment and commercial HVACR applications.

State Requirements

The Oklahoma Construction Industries Board regulates mechanical contractor licensing and professional requirements for covered HVACR work in Oklahoma.

The HVACR Unlimited Contractor classification encompasses authorized installation, maintenance, repair, alteration, and extension of heating, air conditioning, refrigeration, and ventilation systems without the horsepower or tonnage limitations of the Limited classification.

Contractor applicants must meet the requirements applicable to mechanical journeymen and obtain an additional year of qualifying mechanical trade experience.

Active mechanical contractors must also satisfy applicable business, insurance, surety bond, and licensing requirements.

The Oklahoma Mechanical Licensing Act and Mechanical Industry Regulations establish important responsibilities involving qualifications, licensing, and regulated mechanical work.

Understanding state regulations is essential for contractors who manage commercial or residential mechanical projects, maintain business records, and assume contractual obligations.
