Exam Details

The Oklahoma HVACR Unlimited Contractor Exam is administered by PSI for the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board. Candidates must be approved by the State of Oklahoma before scheduling the examination. Once approved, candidates are responsible for scheduling their exam appointment with PSI.

The HVACR Unlimited Contractor examination contains 90 scored items. Candidates are allowed 240 minutes to complete the exam, and the listed passing score is 70% correct, which is 63 correct answers out of 90 scored items. The examination is computer-based and is taken through an approved PSI testing location.

The exam is designed to evaluate knowledge needed for unlimited HVACR contractor work. Preparation should include electrical knowledge and controls, natural gas, air distribution systems, refrigeration and air conditioning systems, heating systems, and safety. Because the unlimited license classification is broader than the limited category, candidates should prepare for questions involving complete HVACR systems that are not restricted by horsepower or tons.

Electrical knowledge and controls topics include general electrical theory and knowledge, motors, direct current circuits, alternating current circuits, and controls. Natural gas topics include piping valves, connectors, accessories, hangers and supports, sizing, materials, and testing. Air distribution systems include duct definitions, sizing and design, duct assembly and installation, hangers and supports, duct materials and shapes, duct insulation, fire and smoke control, hoods and exhaust systems, testing and balancing, ventilation requirements, plans, and symbols.

Refrigeration and air conditioning systems include theory, refrigerants, equipment sizing and design, equipment and components, piping, equipment installation, system operation, troubleshooting, and maintenance. Heating systems include heating theory and types, combustion air, vents and chimneys, equipment sizing, heating equipment, equipment installation, system operation, troubleshooting, maintenance, hydronics, and hydronic piping. Safety topics include personal protective equipment, safety training, adequate ventilation, first aid kits, material safety data sheets, toxic materials, fall protection, lockout/tagout, ladders, and scaffolds.

Open Book Test

The Oklahoma HVACR Unlimited Contractor Exam is an open book test. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own approved references to the examination center. For code questions, the examination is based on the specific code editions listed for the exam.

Reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination session. References may not be written in before or during the examination. Candidates may use permanent tabs only. Temporary tabs, including Post-it style notes, are not allowed and must be removed before the exam begins. Candidates may not bring additional loose papers with approved references.

For downloaded references, the material must be spiral bound, hole-punched and placed in a binder, or stapled in the left-hand corner. Candidates may use a silent, non-printing, non-programmable calculator in the examination center, and a magnifying glass may be provided upon request.

Because this is an open book examination, preparation should include reference navigation. Candidates should practice locating information in the Ductulator, International Mechanical Code, International Fuel Gas Code, Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning, Manual N, Mathematics for Plumbers and Pipefitters, and OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926. Knowing the subject is helpful, but knowing where to find the answer during a timed exam is also important.

Licensing Steps

Oklahoma HVACR contractor licensing is handled through the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board. Candidates applying for an HVACR Unlimited Contractor license must meet the state’s eligibility requirements, submit the required application materials, receive approval to test, schedule the required examination through PSI, and complete the remaining licensing requirements after passing.

The licensing process begins with confirming that the applicant is pursuing the correct mechanical contractor category. HVACR Unlimited Contractor licensing is different from HVACR Limited Contractor licensing, HVACR journeyman licensing, refrigeration licensing, process piping, sheet metal, and natural gas piping categories. Candidates should match the license category to the type of work they intend to perform before applying.

After confirming the correct category, the applicant submits the required application and supporting documentation to the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board. The Board reviews the application and determines whether the candidate is eligible for examination. Once approved, the candidate schedules the PSI examination and prepares using the listed references.

Passing the HVACR Unlimited Contractor exam is an important licensing step, but contractor-level licensing also includes ongoing compliance responsibilities. Contractors must understand the scope of their license, work within Oklahoma mechanical licensing rules, maintain required business and compliance documentation, and follow applicable mechanical, fuel gas, refrigeration, piping, ventilation, and safety requirements.

After licensure, an HVACR Unlimited Contractor should keep licensing records, insurance documents, workers’ compensation compliance documentation when applicable, and renewal information current. Contractor-level authority involves more than trade knowledge. It also includes responsibility for safe, code-compliant, professional work in residential, commercial, and industrial HVACR environments.

State Requirements

Oklahoma mechanical contractor licensing is regulated by the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board. HVACR Unlimited Contractor licensing is one of the mechanical contractor classifications recognized by the state.

An HVACR Unlimited Contractor license entitles the licensee to install, alter, modify, service, maintain, or repair any cooling product, system, or equipment, including process piping, and heating equipment, including process piping. The license also covers sheet metal, natural gas piping, and refrigeration within the scope of the Oklahoma HVACR Unlimited Contractor classification.

Candidates must be approved by the State of Oklahoma before they can schedule the PSI examination. Applicants should prepare complete application materials and supporting documentation before submitting their application to the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board. Missing or incomplete documentation can delay eligibility review.

The HVACR Unlimited Contractor license is different from an HVACR Unlimited Journeyman license. The contractor license is tied to contractor-level authority and responsibility, while a journeyman license is tied to performing HVACR work while employed by a properly licensed contractor. Candidates should review current Oklahoma Construction Industries Board application materials so they understand the documentation, examination, licensing, and work-scope requirements for the correct category.

HVACR Unlimited Contractor candidates should understand the relationship between mechanical code requirements, fuel gas requirements, refrigeration principles, air distribution, commercial load calculations, heating systems, hydronics, electrical controls, equipment installation, system troubleshooting, and OSHA safety. These areas affect both exam preparation and professional work in the field.

Test Information and Study Materials

The Oklahoma HVACR Unlimited Contractor Exam should be approached with a study plan that combines HVACR trade knowledge, mechanical code review, fuel gas code review, refrigeration theory, electrical controls, duct sizing, commercial load calculation concepts, hydronics, trade math, and OSHA safety preparation. The exam contains 90 scored questions, so candidates should be able to move efficiently between the question, the correct reference, and the answer.

Electrical knowledge and controls should be reviewed carefully. Candidates should study general electrical theory, motors, direct current circuits, alternating current circuits, and controls. HVACR systems depend on electrical components and control circuits, so candidates should understand how motors, controls, switches, relays, thermostats, safety controls, and electrical fundamentals apply to equipment operation and troubleshooting.

The International Mechanical Code should be reviewed for mechanical system requirements, ventilation, exhaust, duct systems, equipment installation, combustion air coordination, appliance clearances, refrigeration-related provisions, and mechanical safety requirements. Candidates should understand how the code is organized and where common HVACR topics are located.

The International Fuel Gas Code should be reviewed for natural gas piping, gas appliances, combustion air, venting, regulators, valves, connectors, accessories, hangers and supports, sizing, materials, and testing. HVACR work often connects directly to fuel gas appliances and venting systems, so candidates should be comfortable identifying when a question belongs in the fuel gas reference instead of the mechanical code.

Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning should be used to review refrigeration cycles, refrigerants, compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, controls, heat transfer, air conditioning systems, heating systems, troubleshooting, installation practices, and service procedures. This reference supports trade knowledge that may not be answered directly by a code book.

Manual N should be reviewed for small commercial load calculation concepts. Candidates should understand the purpose of commercial load calculations, the effect of building envelope conditions, internal loads, occupancy, lighting, ventilation, design conditions, and equipment sizing considerations. Commercial load calculation questions may require careful reading because assumptions and building conditions affect the correct approach.

The Ductulator should be used for duct sizing, airflow, velocity, and friction loss review. Candidates should practice using the tool before exam day so they are comfortable reading it quickly. Air distribution system topics also include duct definitions, sizing and design, duct assembly and installation, hangers and supports, duct materials and shapes, duct insulation, fire and smoke control, hoods and exhaust systems, testing and balancing, ventilation requirements, plans, and symbols.

Heating system topics include heating theory and types, combustion air, vents and chimneys, equipment sizing, heating equipment, equipment installation, system operation, troubleshooting, maintenance, hydronics, and hydronic piping. Candidates should understand both fuel-fired and hydronic heating concepts, along with code requirements that affect appliance installation and safe operation.

Mathematics for Plumbers and Pipefitters should be used to review applied trade math. Candidates should be comfortable with measurements, conversions, formulas, basic geometry, offsets, and calculations used in mechanical work. A solid math foundation can help with duct sizing, load calculation support, piping layout, hydronic work, measurement, and practical field questions.

OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 should be reviewed for construction safety. Candidates should study personal protective equipment, safety training, ventilation, first aid kits, material safety data sheets, toxic materials, fall protection, lockout/tagout, ladders, scaffolds, tools, equipment, signs, signals, barricades, materials, and jobsite responsibilities. HVACR work can involve rooftops, ladders, mechanical rooms, confined or limited spaces, electrical hazards, gas-fired appliances, refrigerants, hydronic piping, and construction environments, so safety knowledge is an important part of preparation.

Because the exam is open book, candidates should prepare their references according to the testing rules. Highlighting, underlining, and permanent tabs can help organize important sections, but references may not contain written notes. Candidates should practice using the references repeatedly so they can locate material quickly during the timed exam.
