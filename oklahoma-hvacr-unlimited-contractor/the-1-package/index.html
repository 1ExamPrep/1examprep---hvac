Exam Details

The Oklahoma HVACR Contractor - Unlimited Examination is part of the mechanical licensing program overseen by the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board.

The examination is administered by PSI and evaluates technical knowledge involving refrigeration, heating, electrical controls, air distribution, fuel gas, and construction safety.

  • Examination: Oklahoma HVACR Contractor - Unlimited
  • Licensing Authority: Oklahoma Construction Industries Board
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Examination Format: Open book
  • Questions: 90
  • Time Allowed: 240 minutes
  • Passing Score: 70%, equivalent to 63 correct answers

The examination addresses six major technical areas: Electrical Knowledge and Controls, Natural Gas, Air Distribution Systems, Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Systems, Heating Systems, and Safety.

Questions require knowledge of mechanical equipment, electrical control components, ventilation principles, fuel gas requirements, refrigeration cycles, and installation practices.

Oklahoma mechanical contractor licensing also involves Business and Law examination requirements. State Requirements

The Oklahoma Construction Industries Board regulates mechanical contractor licensing and establishes requirements for covered HVACR work.

The HVACR Unlimited Contractor classification authorizes qualifying work involving cooling equipment, heating systems, sheet metal, natural gas piping, and refrigeration within the scope established by Oklahoma law.

Unlike the Limited classification, the Unlimited classification does not carry the same cooling-capacity and heating-input restrictions.

Mechanical contractor applicants must meet journeyman-level qualifications and have an additional year of qualifying mechanical trade experience.

To maintain an active mechanical contractor license, Oklahoma requires a $5,000 corporate surety bond and evidence of at least $50,000 in commercial general liability insurance.

Contractors must also meet applicable licensing, business, and professional compliance obligations.

Business formation is separate from contractor licensure. Establishing an LLC or corporation creates a business entity, while licensing authorizes regulated mechanical contracting work.

The included Contractor Compliance Guidance helps customers understand and organize the requirements associated with professional mechanical contracting operations.
