Exam Details

The Oklahoma HVACR Contractor Unlimited examination evaluates a candidate's knowledge of installing, maintaining, repairing, fabricating, altering, and extending air conditioning, refrigeration, heating, and ventilation systems within the unrestricted HVACR contractor classification.

  • Exam: Oklahoma HVACR Contractor Unlimited
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Number of Questions: 90
  • Time Allowed: 240 minutes
  • Passing Score: 70%
  • Passing Raw Score: 63 correct answers
  • Exam Format: Open book

The examination addresses several major areas of HVACR trade knowledge. These include Electrical Knowledge and Controls, Natural Gas, Air Distribution Systems, Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Systems, Heating Systems, and Safety.

Air Distribution Systems is a significant part of unlimited contractor preparation. Candidates should understand duct sizing and design, duct materials, installation, hangers and supports, insulation, ventilation, exhaust systems, fire and smoke control, testing and balancing, and system plans and symbols.

Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Systems preparation should include refrigeration theory, refrigerants, system components, piping, equipment sizing and design, installation, system operation, maintenance, and troubleshooting.

Heating Systems review should include heating theory, equipment, combustion air, vents and chimneys, equipment sizing, installation, hydronic systems, operation, troubleshooting, and maintenance. Natural Gas preparation includes piping, valves, connectors, supports, sizing, materials, testing, appliances, venting, and combustion air.

Electrical Knowledge and Controls can involve electrical theory, motors, circuits, and controls as they apply to HVACR equipment. Safety preparation should include construction-industry practices and OSHA requirements relevant to HVACR work.

Licensing Steps

  1. Gain qualifying mechanical trade experience. Build verifiable HVACR experience appropriate to the unlimited contractor classification.
  2. Meet journeyman-level qualifications. Oklahoma contractor applicants must satisfy the applicable requirements for mechanical journeyman licensing before progressing to contractor-level qualification.
  3. Gain additional contractor experience. Oklahoma requires contractor applicants to have additional qualifying mechanical trade experience beyond the journeyman level.
  4. Qualify for the Unlimited classification. Experience for the unlimited HVACR category must demonstrate work appropriate to the unrestricted scope of the license.
  5. Submit the contractor license application. Apply for the HVACR Unlimited Contractor classification through the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board.
  6. Receive examination eligibility. The state reviews the application and determines whether the applicant is qualified to proceed with testing.
  7. Schedule the required examination. Approved candidates schedule testing through PSI.
  8. Complete the contractor licensing process. After meeting examination requirements, complete the applicable requirements for active contractor status.

Exam preparation is one part of the licensing process. Applicants should also understand the experience, application, and active-license requirements associated with becoming an Oklahoma HVACR Unlimited Contractor.

State Requirements

The Oklahoma HVACR Unlimited Contractor classification authorizes qualifying work involving cooling products, systems, or equipment, including process piping, without the tonnage limitation applied to the HVACR Limited Contractor license.

The classification also covers heating equipment and associated process piping without the limited category's heating-input restriction. It includes qualifying sheet metal, natural gas piping, refrigeration, and related HVACR work within the unrestricted classification.

Oklahoma requires mechanical contractor applicants to meet the same general qualification requirements applicable to a journeyman and obtain an additional year of mechanical trade experience.

A mechanical contractor must also be a permanent employee, owner, partner, or officer of the mechanical firm. Candidates should understand that passing the exam does not by itself complete the contractor licensing process.

The Oklahoma Construction Industries Board oversees mechanical licensing and determines examination eligibility. Applicants should complete the licensing process for the appropriate classification and meet the requirements associated with operating under an active mechanical contractor license.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Oklahoma HVACR Unlimited Contractor study plan should combine technical review, code navigation, commercial load calculations, air-distribution practice, mathematics, and safety preparation.

Begin with the major HVACR systems. Review refrigeration theory, refrigerants, components, piping, equipment sizing, system operation, installation, maintenance, and troubleshooting. Use Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning throughout these study sessions.

Heating preparation should include equipment, combustion air, venting, chimneys, hydronic systems, sizing, installation, operation, and troubleshooting. Natural gas preparation should include piping, connectors, valves, supports, materials, sizing, combustion air, appliances, testing, and venting.

Air-distribution preparation should include duct sizing, design, installation, materials, supports, insulation, ventilation, exhaust systems, fire and smoke requirements, testing and balancing, and plans and symbols.

Use the Ductulator during duct-sizing practice instead of waiting until the end of your preparation. Repeated use can help you become more familiar with airflow, velocity, friction, and duct dimensions.

Manual N should be incorporated into commercial load-calculation preparation. The unlimited classification includes work beyond the capacity restrictions of the limited category, so commercial HVAC knowledge is an important part of a well-rounded study plan.

Code-navigation practice should also be deliberate. Choose a subject such as ventilation, combustion air, duct installation, fuel gas sizing, or equipment requirements and practice finding the appropriate information in the correct code.

Trade mathematics should be practiced by solving problems involving measurements, formulas, conversions, area, volume, and other mechanical applications. Safety preparation should involve direct use of 29 CFR Part 1926 so candidates become familiar with how OSHA information is organized.

Mixed-reference practice can help create a realistic open-book study routine. Work through questions from several different subject areas during the same session. Before opening a reference, decide which book or tool should contain the information.

This approach helps candidates develop both subject recognition and reference-navigation skills. Instead of searching randomly, the candidate learns to connect each type of question with the most appropriate source.
