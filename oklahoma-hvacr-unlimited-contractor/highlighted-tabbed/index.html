Exam Details

The Oklahoma HVACR Contractor Unlimited examination tests knowledge associated with the installation, maintenance, repair, fabrication, alteration, and extension of air conditioning, refrigeration, heating, and ventilation systems. The scope includes ductwork within complete systems without the horsepower or tonnage limitations associated with the limited HVACR classification.

  • Exam: Oklahoma HVACR Contractor Unlimited
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Number of Questions: 90
  • Time Allowed: 240 minutes
  • Passing Score: 70%
  • Passing Raw Score: 63 correct answers
  • Exam Format: Open book

The technical examination contains 15 questions in Electrical Knowledge and Controls, 12 questions in Natural Gas, 25 questions in Air Distribution Systems, 20 questions in Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Systems, 15 questions in Heating Systems, and 3 questions in Safety.

Air Distribution Systems is the largest individual section. Preparation should include duct definitions, sizing and design, assembly and installation, hangers and supports, duct materials and shapes, insulation, fire and smoke control, hoods and exhaust systems, testing and balancing, ventilation requirements, plans, and symbols.

Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Systems covers refrigeration theory, refrigerants, equipment sizing and design, components, piping, equipment installation, system operation, troubleshooting, and maintenance. Heating Systems includes heating theory, combustion air, vents and chimneys, equipment sizing, heating equipment, installation, operation, maintenance, troubleshooting, hydronics, and hydronic piping.

Electrical Knowledge and Controls includes electrical theory, motors, DC circuits, AC circuits, and controls. Natural Gas includes piping valves, connectors, accessories, hangers and supports, sizing, materials, and testing. Safety topics include personal protective equipment, fall protection, ladders, scaffolds, lockout/tagout, first aid, ventilation, employee training, and other workplace safety subjects.

Open Book Test

The Oklahoma HVACR Contractor Unlimited examination is an open book test. Candidates may bring approved reference materials into the examination center according to PSI testing rules.

Approved reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are permitted. Temporary tabs such as Post-it notes are not allowed, and candidates may not write notes in their references before or during the examination.

The highlighted and tabbed format of this package supports the reference-navigation skills needed for an open-book exam. Highlighting can draw attention to useful provisions and technical information, while permanent tabs can help candidates move between important chapters and subject areas more efficiently.

Open book does not mean every question should be researched from the beginning. Candidates have 240 minutes for 90 questions, so effective time management matters. Some questions may be answered from trade knowledge, while others may require a code section, calculation, table, or technical reference.

A mechanical installation, ventilation, or duct question may direct you toward the International Mechanical Code. Fuel gas sizing, materials, appliances, combustion air, or venting may require the International Fuel Gas Code. Commercial load-calculation questions may involve Manual N, while air-distribution calculations may involve the Ductulator.

Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning supports broader technical preparation involving refrigeration, heating, controls, equipment, system operation, installation, troubleshooting, and maintenance. Mathematics for Plumbers and Pipefitters supports trade calculations, while 29 CFR Part 1926 supports construction safety preparation.

Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning, 22nd Edition is permitted for the applicable PSI examination beginning August 13, 2026.

Licensing Steps

  1. Gain qualifying mechanical trade experience. Build verifiable HVACR experience appropriate to the unlimited contractor category.
  2. Meet contractor qualifications. Oklahoma mechanical contractor applicants must satisfy the applicable journeyman qualifications and obtain an additional year of experience in the mechanical trade.
  3. Demonstrate unlimited-level HVACR experience. Experience used to qualify for HVACR Unlimited licensing must demonstrate work above the capacity limitations associated with the HVACR Limited classification.
  4. Submit the mechanical contractor application. Apply through the Oklahoma Construction Industries Board for the HVACR Unlimited Contractor category.
  5. Receive approval to test. The Construction Industries Board determines whether an applicant meets the qualifications necessary to proceed with contractor testing.
  6. Schedule the required examination. Approved candidates complete the applicable examination scheduling process through PSI.
  7. Complete the contractor examination requirements. Prepare for and complete the technical and applicable contractor business and law testing requirements.
  8. Complete active contractor requirements. After satisfying examination requirements, complete Oklahoma's applicable requirements for active mechanical contractor status.

The exam is an important part of the process, but preparation should also include understanding the license scope and administrative requirements associated with becoming an Oklahoma HVACR Unlimited Contractor.

State Requirements

The Oklahoma HVACR Unlimited Contractor classification authorizes qualifying work involving cooling products, systems, or equipment, including process piping, without the tonnage limitations associated with the HVACR Limited classification.

The unlimited classification also covers heating equipment and related process piping without the heating-input restriction that applies to the limited classification. It additionally includes qualifying sheet metal, natural gas piping, and refrigeration work.

Oklahoma mechanical contractor applicants must meet the same general qualification requirements applicable to a journeyman and obtain an additional year of mechanical trade experience. Experience used to qualify for the HVACR Unlimited classification must demonstrate work above the capacity thresholds associated with the limited classification.

A mechanical contractor must also be a permanent employee, owner, partner, or officer of the mechanical firm. Candidates pursuing contractor licensing should plan for qualifying experience, application review, testing, and active-license requirements as part of the complete licensing process.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Oklahoma HVACR Unlimited Contractor study plan should reflect the broad technical scope of the exam. Candidates should combine HVACR theory, commercial system knowledge, reference navigation, code study, mathematics, load calculations, air distribution, and safety preparation.

Air Distribution Systems represents 25 of the 90 examination questions, making it the largest content area. Review duct sizing and design, materials, installation, insulation, ventilation, exhaust systems, fire and smoke control, testing and balancing, and plans and symbols. Work with the Ductulator while studying these topics so air-distribution calculations become part of your regular preparation.

Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Systems accounts for another 20 questions. Review refrigeration principles, refrigerants, system components, equipment sizing, piping, installation, operation, troubleshooting, and maintenance. Use Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning while studying so you become familiar with where technical information is located.

Heating Systems and Electrical Knowledge and Controls each contain 15 questions. Heating preparation should cover combustion air, vents and chimneys, equipment sizing, hydronic systems, installation, operation, and maintenance. Electrical preparation should reinforce motors, circuits, controls, and basic electrical principles as they apply to HVACR equipment.

Natural Gas accounts for 12 questions. Practice locating requirements involving piping, materials, valves, connectors, supports, sizing, and testing in the International Fuel Gas Code.

Manual N should be incorporated into preparation for commercial load calculations. Candidates pursuing an unlimited contractor classification should be comfortable with commercial HVAC concepts and equipment-sizing principles in addition to residential knowledge.

Safety preparation should include direct use of 29 CFR Part 1926. Practice locating provisions related to fall protection, personal protective equipment, ladders, scaffolds, lockout/tagout, employee training, and workplace hazards.

Mixed-reference practice can make studying more realistic. Work through several different subject areas during one session. Start with a mechanical code question, move to air distribution, then refrigeration, fuel gas, commercial load calculations, mathematics, and OSHA safety. Before opening a reference, identify which resource is most likely to contain the information.
