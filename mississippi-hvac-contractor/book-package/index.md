### Exam Details

Mississippi recognizes Heat, Ventilation, and Air Conditioning as a tested contractor specialty classification. The classification includes the installation, maintenance, repair, fabrication, alteration, and extension of heating, ventilation, and air-conditioning systems. Its scope also includes associated HVAC equipment, ductwork, duct cleaning, industrial air systems used for dust control, ventilation systems, and related work.

Applicants must begin the licensing process by submitting the appropriate contractor application to the Mississippi State Board of Contractors. The Board reviews the application and determines whether the applicant and qualifying party have completed the requirements needed to receive examination eligibility. Candidates cannot schedule the required contractor examinations until the Board sends their eligibility information to PSI.

Mississippi contractor examinations are administered by PSI. The testing process begins after PSI receives authorization from the Mississippi State Board of Contractors. PSI then provides the candidate with registration and scheduling instructions, examination information, testing locations, content outlines, and reference-material requirements.

The Mississippi Law and Business Management examination is required for contractor applicants. A trade examination may also be required based on the classification requested. Candidates pursuing the Heat, Ventilation, and Air Conditioning classification should prepare for the applicable HVAC trade examination unless the Board approves a qualifying trade-exam waiver through an available reciprocity provision.

The HVAC trade examination may address heating and cooling principles, refrigeration systems, refrigerants, air-conditioning equipment, furnaces, heat pumps, controls, piping, insulation, testing and balancing, ductwork, ventilation, exhaust, combustion air, fuel-gas systems, liquefied petroleum gas, load calculations, system design, safety, and related trade mathematics.

Some questions may test direct technical knowledge, while others may require the candidate to locate information in a code book, manual, handbook, table, or calculation tool. Preparation should include both subject review and repeated use of the listed references.

### Open Book Test

Mississippi contractor examinations administered by PSI are open book. Candidates are responsible for bringing the references permitted for the examinations they schedule and complying with the current rules for book editions, markings, tabs, notes, and supplemental materials.

An open-book examination still requires strong preparation. Candidates may have access to several books, but the available testing time does not allow for a complete search of every reference. A candidate must recognize the topic being tested, identify the most appropriate book, locate the relevant chapter or code section, and apply the information correctly.

Reference navigation should be treated as a core exam-preparation skill. Candidates should learn the wording used in each index because the terminology in an exam question may not exactly match the language used in a book. Searching for related technical terms can make it easier to locate the correct provision.

For example, a question about appliance venting may lead to the International Fuel Gas Code or International Mechanical Code. A question about LP-gas containers or piping may require NFPA 58. A residential load question may involve Manual J, while a small-commercial load question may require Manual N. Duct sizing and airflow questions may involve Manual D or the Ductulator.

Candidates should review the current PSI Candidate Information Bulletin before testing. The testing provider determines which references and editions may enter the examination room. It also establishes the rules for highlighting, permanent tabs, writing, loose papers, removable notes, and other materials.

Practice sessions should recreate open-book test conditions whenever possible. Place the books within reach, use a timer, and work through questions without relying on online searches. This helps develop familiarity with the printed references and reveals which subjects require additional study.

### State Requirements

The Mississippi State Board of Contractors administers the state contractor licensing process. Applicants must select a classification that matches the work their business intends to perform. Heat, Ventilation, and Air Conditioning is identified as a tested specialty classification within the mechanical contracting field.

The HVAC classification includes installation, maintenance, repair, fabrication, alteration, and extension of HVAC systems. It also covers associated HVAC equipment, ductwork, duct cleaning, industrial air systems used for dust control, ventilation systems, and related work.

The qualifying party must be connected to the applicant in a manner accepted by the Board. Depending on the business structure, the qualifying party may be an owner, officer, or responsible managing employee. The application may require documentation establishing that relationship.

Applicants may also need to provide business registration information, experience records, references, insurance documentation, workers’ compensation information when applicable, and financial documents. All contractor applicants must complete the Mississippi Law and Business Management requirement. A trade examination may also be required. Reciprocity does not replace the Mississippi application process or automatically issue a Mississippi license.

Passing the required examinations is only one stage of licensing. The Mississippi State Board of Contractors must receive all required documentation and approve the completed application before the license is issued.

Additional local requirements may apply after state licensing. Contractors should determine whether the city, county, permitting authority, project owner, or other jurisdiction requires local registrations, permits, inspections, insurance, or additional credentials for the work being performed.

### Test Information and Study Materials

A productive study plan should connect each examination topic with the reference most likely to address it. Candidates can divide the books into technical, code, safety, load-calculation, duct-design, and field-reference categories.

Use *Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning* for broad review of refrigeration cycles, refrigerants, heating systems, cooling systems, equipment, controls, components, installation principles, and HVAC operation.

Use the *International Mechanical Code* for mechanical-system requirements involving equipment, ventilation, exhaust, duct systems, combustion air, chimneys, vents, and installation provisions. Use the *International Fuel Gas Code* for fuel-gas piping, appliances, testing, combustion air, and venting.

Use *NFPA 58* for liquefied petroleum gas subjects, including containers, piping, equipment, separation requirements, storage, handling, and installation. Use the OSHA reference for construction safety and workplace practices.

Use the HVAC duct construction standard for duct materials, joints, reinforcement, supports, fabrication, and installation. Use Manual J for residential load calculations and Manual N for small-commercial load calculations. Use Manual D and the Ductulator for duct sizing, airflow, velocity, friction, pressure, fittings, and residential air-distribution design.

The *Pipefitter’s Handbook* supports questions involving piping dimensions, fittings, formulas, layout, measurements, and practical field calculations. Candidates should become comfortable locating information in tables and converting units when necessary.

Timed lookup exercises can improve reference-navigation speed. Choose a technical subject, identify the correct book, find a related term in the index, and locate the applicable section. Repeat difficult searches until the process becomes familiar.

Calculation practice should include identifying the known values, choosing the correct formula or procedure, checking units, completing the calculation, and reviewing whether the result is reasonable. Candidates should practice with the same manuals and tools they expect to use during the examination.

During the test, answer familiar questions first and return to difficult questions after completing the easier material. When a reference search is required, identify the main technical keyword and select the book that most directly addresses the subject.
