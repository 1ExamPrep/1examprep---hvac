Open Book Test

Mississippi contractor examinations are open-book tests. Candidates should prepare by using their technical references throughout the study process rather than waiting until examination day to begin navigating the books.

Open-book testing still requires solid HVAC knowledge. Technical references can contain hundreds of pages, and searching without first understanding the question can consume valuable exam time. Candidates should learn to identify the subject, determine the most appropriate book, and perform a targeted lookup.

For example, a mechanical-code question may direct a candidate toward the International Mechanical Code. A fuel-gas question may involve the International Fuel Gas Code or NFPA 58, depending on the system and subject. A duct construction question may require HVAC Duct Construction Standards, while an airflow or duct-sizing problem may involve the Ductulator or Manual D.

Residential load calculations can require Manual J, while commercial load-calculation concepts can involve Manual N. Refrigeration principles, components, troubleshooting, and equipment questions can often be supported by Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning.

Practice using tables of contents, indexes, chapter headings, code sections, tables, definitions, diagrams, and calculation procedures. Candidates should develop a repeatable process: identify the subject, select the reference, locate the information, and confirm the answer.

Live virtual training can help demonstrate that process in a structured setting, while self-study preparation provides additional opportunities to repeat reference searches until they become more familiar.

Licensing Steps

The Mississippi HVAC contractor licensing process begins with the Mississippi State Board of Contractors. Applicants must complete the applicable licensing process before they can proceed with required testing.

  1. Select the appropriate classification. Confirm that Heat, Ventilation, A/C – HVAC corresponds with the work the company intends to perform.
  2. Complete the Mississippi contractor application. Provide the business, ownership, qualifying-party, experience, financial, and supporting information required for the application.
  3. Identify the qualifying party. The qualifying party is the individual responsible for completing required examinations on behalf of the company.
  4. Submit the application to the Mississippi State Board of Contractors. The Board reviews the application and identifies any remaining requirements.
  5. Receive testing eligibility. MSBOC notifies PSI when the qualifying party is eligible to test.
  6. Schedule the applicable examinations. Candidates follow PSI registration and scheduling procedures after eligibility has been established.
  7. Prepare for the HVAC trade examination. Use the live virtual classes, self-study training, technical books, and reference-navigation practice to strengthen preparation.
  8. Complete the Mississippi Law and Business Management examination. Mississippi requires applicants to satisfy the applicable Law and Business Management examination requirement in addition to required trade testing.
  9. Complete final licensing requirements. Passing the required examination does not automatically issue a contractor license. The complete application must also satisfy applicable Board requirements.

State Requirements

The Mississippi State Board of Contractors identifies Heat, Ventilation, A/C – HVAC as a test-required specialty classification.

The classification includes installation, maintenance, repair, fabrication, alteration, and extension of heating, ventilating, and air conditioning systems. The scope includes HVAC-associated equipment, ductwork, duct cleaning, industrial air systems for dust control, ventilation systems, and related work.

This classification is different from Mississippi's Residential HVAC classification, which is limited to residential HVAC work. Candidates pursuing the commercial HVAC specialty classification should prepare for the broader scope associated with the Heat, Ventilation, A/C – HVAC classification.

The contractor application must identify a qualifying party responsible for satisfying the examination requirements. Mississippi defines the qualifying party as an owner, officer, or responsible managing employee who is associated with the licensed business.

Applicants must complete the applicable Board process before scheduling required examinations. MSBOC establishes testing eligibility and sends the qualifying party's information to PSI. Candidates can then follow PSI procedures to schedule the examination.

Passing the examination is one component of licensing. The applicant must also satisfy the remaining application and qualification requirements before a Mississippi contractor license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Mississippi HVAC study plan should divide preparation among HVAC theory, equipment, refrigeration, ductwork, fuel gas, load calculations, piping, mechanical codes, and safety. Candidates should also spend time practicing how to move between the different references.

Heating and Cooling Principles should include heat transfer, refrigeration fundamentals, sensible and latent heat, system operation, equipment performance, and common HVAC terminology. Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning provides broad support for these areas.

Refrigeration preparation should include compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, refrigerants, pressures, temperatures, system operation, and troubleshooting. Candidates should understand how the refrigeration cycle operates instead of relying only on memorized definitions.

Ductwork and Air Distribution preparation should include duct sizing, airflow, friction, velocity, construction methods, fittings, supports, materials, and installation practices. Use HVAC Duct Construction Standards together with Manual D and the Ductulator to connect construction requirements with airflow calculations.

Load Calculations should include regular practice with Manual J and Manual N. Candidates should understand why building characteristics, occupancy, climate, orientation, construction materials, and internal loads affect system sizing.

Fuel Gas preparation should include gas piping, combustion air, appliance installation, venting, clearances, fuel-gas system requirements, and LP-gas concepts. The International Fuel Gas Code and NFPA 58 support these areas.

Mechanical Code preparation should include navigating the 2021 International Mechanical Code. Candidates should become familiar with the organization of requirements involving mechanical equipment, ventilation, exhaust, ductwork, refrigeration, combustion air, and related systems.

Safety should be connected with practical HVAC work. Review OSHA requirements affecting ladders, fall protection, electrical exposure, tools, equipment, materials handling, personal protective equipment, and construction activities.

Use the live classes to reinforce difficult concepts and observe how technical references are navigated. During self-study sessions, repeat the same searches, work through calculations, and practice identifying which reference should be used for different question types.

Timed practice is particularly useful. Instead of reading technical books passively, use questions to drive reference searches. Identify the keyword, select the appropriate book, locate the relevant information, and verify the answer. Repeating this process helps build the habits needed for an open-book contractor examination.
