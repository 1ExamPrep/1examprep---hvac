Exam Details

The Mississippi HVAC contractor licensing process generally includes the HVAC trade examination and the Mississippi Law and Business Management examination. The qualifying party must complete the examinations required for the requested contractor classification unless an approved waiver or reciprocity provision applies.

The Mississippi HVAC examination covers heating, cooling, refrigeration, fuel-gas systems, related equipment, ductwork, safety, piping, load calculations, and other subjects connected with HVAC contracting. The exam contains 80 questions, allows four hours, and requires a score of 70 percent to pass.

HVAC trade topics include heating and cooling principles, refrigeration systems, refrigerants, air-conditioning equipment, heat pumps, furnaces, heaters, controls, testing and balancing, insulation, piping, supports, combustion air, chimneys, vents, flues, duct systems, ventilation, exhaust, fuel gas, liquefied petroleum gas, load calculations, and workplace safety.

The Mississippi Law and Business Management examination contains 50 questions, allows two hours, and requires a score of 70 percent to pass. The exam addresses contractor licensing, estimating, bidding, contracts, project management, financial management, lien law, tax responsibilities, labor laws, risk management, business organization, environmental requirements, and safety.

Mississippi contractor examinations are administered through PSI. Applicants must first complete the required application process with the Mississippi State Board of Contractors. After the Board reviews the application and submits the applicant’s eligibility to PSI, the qualifying party receives instructions for scheduling the required exams.

Candidates should plan their study schedule around both examinations. Technical HVAC knowledge is essential for the trade test, while the business exam requires familiarity with contractor law, business operations, project responsibilities, and the organization of the Mississippi NASCLA reference.

Open Book Test

The Mississippi HVAC trade and Law and Business Management examinations are open book. Candidates are responsible for bringing the permitted references to the testing center and following the current examination rules for book condition, markings, tabs, and additional materials.

An open-book format does not eliminate the need to study. Four hours for the HVAC exam and two hours for the business exam can pass quickly when a candidate must search multiple references. Effective preparation requires knowing which book applies to each topic and where the most important sections are located.

The books in this rental package are highlighted and permanently tabbed. These features are intended to make frequently used sections easier to identify during preparation and testing. Candidates should review the tabs before exam day, learn the terminology used in each index, and complete repeated lookup exercises.

Temporary tabs, removable notes, loose sheets, and unauthorized inserts may not be allowed in the examination room. Candidates should not add materials to the rental books that could violate testing-center rules or damage the references. The current PSI Candidate Information Bulletin controls what may be taken into the examination.

A practical open-book strategy begins with identifying the subject of the question. A candidate should determine whether the question involves mechanical code, fuel gas, LP gas, OSHA safety, duct construction, refrigeration, load calculations, duct sizing, pipefitting, or business law before selecting a book. This reduces unnecessary searching and preserves valuable examination time.

Licensing Steps

  1. Select the appropriate classification. Determine which Mississippi contractor classification matches the HVAC work the company intends to perform.
  2. Identify the qualifying party. Select the person who will demonstrate the required experience and complete the applicable examinations for the business.
  3. Prepare the contractor application. Complete the Mississippi State Board of Contractors application and collect the required supporting documents.
  4. Submit the application. Send the completed filing to the Board for review.
  5. Respond to application requests. Provide corrections, additional documentation, or missing information requested during the review process.
  6. Receive examination eligibility. After the Board approves the applicant to test, eligibility information is sent to PSI.
  7. Schedule the examinations. Follow PSI’s registration instructions and select available testing dates.
  8. Study the approved references. Learn the location of key information, review technical subjects, and complete timed navigation practice.
  9. Take the examinations. Bring acceptable identification and only the materials permitted under the testing provider’s current rules.
  10. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Submit any outstanding items needed for final Board review.
  11. Receive the licensing decision. Passing the examinations does not automatically issue a license. The complete application remains subject to approval by the Mississippi State Board of Contractors.

State Requirements

The Mississippi State Board of Contractors regulates the applicable state contractor licensing process. The classification listed on the application should match the work the company plans to offer. HVAC work is associated with the mechanical contracting field and may include heating, ventilation, air-conditioning, refrigeration, ductwork, fuel-gas systems, and related equipment.

The qualifying party must satisfy the Board’s requirements and pass the examinations required for the requested classification unless an approved waiver applies. Contractor applicants are generally required to complete the Mississippi Law and Business Management examination. The trade examination requirement depends on the classification and any applicable reciprocity or waiver approval.

Commercial contractor applicants may be required to submit financial documentation prepared by a certified public accountant. The required level of financial reporting can depend on the classification and application type. Applicants should follow the current Board application instructions when preparing financial statements and supporting documents.

Additional requirements may include business registration records, insurance information, workers’ compensation documentation when applicable, references, ownership information, and details about the qualifying party’s relationship to the applicant.

Passing the examinations is only one part of the licensing process. The Board must also review the completed application and supporting documents. A candidate should avoid scheduling projects that require a contractor license until the license has been approved and issued.

Contractors may also need to comply with local permits, registrations, tax accounts, insurance obligations, and other requirements connected with the location and scope of their work. State licensing does not remove responsibilities imposed by cities, counties, project owners, or other authorities.

Test Information and Study Materials

Begin preparation by dividing the rental set into major subject groups. Use the NASCLA Mississippi guide for business and law. Use the International Mechanical Code, International Fuel Gas Code, and NFPA 58 for code-related questions. Use the OSHA reference for construction safety. Use Manual J, Manual N, Manual D, and the Ductulator for load and duct calculations.

Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning supports broad technical review involving refrigeration, heating, cooling, equipment, controls, and system principles. The HVAC Duct Construction Standards reference supports duct fabrication and installation questions, while the Pipefitter’s Handbook provides piping information, formulas, and field calculations.

Practice should include timed reference searches. Choose a subject, identify the likely book, locate the term in the index, and find the applicable section. Repeat difficult searches until the location and index terminology become familiar.

Calculation preparation should include load calculations, duct sizing, airflow, friction, piping relationships, and unit conversions. Candidates should practice showing each step, checking units, and confirming that the selected answer is reasonable.

During the examination, answer familiar questions first and mark difficult questions for review. Avoid spending too much time searching one reference. When a question is unclear, identify its main technical term and use that term to select the most appropriate book.
