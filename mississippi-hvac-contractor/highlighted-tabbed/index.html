Exam Details

The Mississippi HVAC contractor classification applies to work involving the installation, maintenance, repair, fabrication, alteration, and extension of heating, ventilation, and air-conditioning systems. The classification also includes associated HVAC equipment, ductwork, duct cleaning, industrial air systems used for dust control, ventilation systems, and related work.

Mississippi contractor applicants must submit an application to the Mississippi State Board of Contractors before scheduling the required examinations. The Board reviews the application, identifies missing requirements when applicable, and sends eligible candidate information to PSI. Candidates cannot schedule their examinations until PSI receives their eligibility and provides scheduling instructions.

The Mississippi Law and Business Management examination is required for contractor applicants. A trade examination may also be required based on the classification requested. Candidates pursuing the HVAC classification should prepare for trade subjects as well as the business and licensing responsibilities associated with operating a contracting company.

The HVAC examination may include questions involving refrigeration systems, heating systems, cooling systems, equipment, piping, ductwork, ventilation, fuel gas, liquefied petroleum gas, combustion air, mechanical codes, safety practices, residential load calculations, commercial load calculations, duct design, airflow, pressure, and trade mathematics.

Exam preparation should involve more than reading each book from beginning to end. Candidates should study the examination content outline, identify the reference associated with each subject, and practice finding information under timed conditions. Questions may require direct code lookup, technical interpretation, practical trade knowledge, or calculations using information found in the listed references.

Open Book Test

Mississippi contractor examinations administered by PSI are open book. Candidates are responsible for bringing the permitted reference materials to the testing center and following the current examination rules for book condition, tabs, markings, notes, and additional materials.

An open-book examination still requires serious preparation. Candidates may not have enough time to search every book for every question. Successful preparation involves recognizing the subject being tested, selecting the most appropriate reference, locating the relevant chapter or code provision, and applying the information correctly.

The books included in this package are highlighted and permanently tabbed to support faster navigation. Highlighting can draw attention to important provisions, tables, formulas, and technical concepts. Tabs can help candidates move directly to commonly used chapters and subject areas without repeatedly searching the table of contents.

Candidates should become familiar with the tabs before the examination. A tab is most useful when the candidate already understands what information is located behind it. Practice should include opening the correct book, identifying keywords in the question, searching the index, locating the appropriate section, and confirming that the information applies to the situation described.

Testing-center rules determine which markings and tabs are allowed. Loose papers, removable notes, temporary tabs, and unauthorized inserts may not be permitted. Candidates should review the current PSI Candidate Information Bulletin before testing and avoid adding materials that could prevent a reference from being accepted into the examination room.

Licensing Steps

  1. Select the appropriate classification. Confirm that the Mississippi HVAC contractor classification matches the heating, ventilation, air-conditioning, ductwork, and related services the company intends to perform.
  2. Identify the applicant. Determine whether the license application will be submitted by an individual, corporation, limited liability company, partnership, or another recognized business structure.
  3. Select the qualifying party. Choose the owner, officer, or responsible managing employee who will qualify the company and complete the required examinations.
  4. Complete the contractor application. Provide the business information, qualifying-party information, work experience, references, financial documentation, insurance information, and other materials required for the application type.
  5. Submit the application to the Board. The Mississippi State Board of Contractors reviews the application and notifies the applicant when information or documentation is missing.
  6. Receive examination eligibility. When the candidate is approved to test, the Board sends the eligibility information to PSI.
  7. Schedule the examinations. Follow the PSI instructions to select an available testing location, date, and time.
  8. Study the reference books. Review trade subjects, practice calculations, and learn how to locate information using the highlighted and tabbed references.
  9. Take the required examinations. Bring acceptable identification and only the books and materials permitted under the current testing rules.
  10. Complete remaining application requirements. Submit any outstanding items requested by the Board after testing.
  11. Receive final Board review. Passing the examinations does not automatically issue a license. The full application and supporting documents must be approved.
  12. Maintain the license. After issuance, contractors are responsible for renewal, accurate business records, insurance, tax obligations, and continued compliance.

State Requirements

The Mississippi State Board of Contractors administers the applicable contractor licensing process. The applicant must choose a classification that corresponds with the work the business plans to perform. The HVAC specialty classification includes heating, ventilation, air-conditioning systems, associated equipment, ductwork, duct cleaning, industrial air systems for dust control, ventilation systems, and related work.

All contractor applicants must complete the Mississippi Law and Business Management examination. The trade examination requirement depends on the requested classification and whether the Board approves an applicable examination waiver or reciprocity provision.

The qualifying party must be properly connected to the applicant and must demonstrate the experience and knowledge required for the requested classification. The application may require proof of employment or documentation showing the qualifying party’s role within the company.

Applicants may also need to provide reference letters, general liability insurance documentation, workers’ compensation coverage when applicable, business registration information, tax identification information, and financial documents. Commercial applicants may be subject to additional financial-statement requirements.

A corporation, limited liability company, or other registered entity must use business information that matches its official state registration. Differences between the business name on the contractor application and the name in the entity’s registration records can delay the licensing process.

Passing an examination does not independently authorize a company to perform licensed contracting work. The Mississippi State Board of Contractors must complete its review and issue the applicable license or certificate before the contractor operates under that credential.

Local requirements may also apply. Contractors should determine whether a city, county, permitting authority, project owner, or other agency requires additional registrations, permits, inspections, insurance, or trade credentials for a particular project.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong study plan begins by grouping the books according to subject. Use Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning for broad technical review of refrigeration, heating, cooling, equipment, components, controls, and system principles.

Use the International Mechanical Code for questions involving mechanical installations, ventilation, exhaust, duct systems, combustion air, equipment, chimneys, and vents. Use the International Fuel Gas Code for fuel-gas piping, appliances, venting, combustion air, testing, and installation requirements.

Use NFPA 58 for liquefied petroleum gas subjects. Use the OSHA reference for workplace and construction safety. Use the HVAC duct construction standard for questions involving duct materials, reinforcement, supports, joints, fabrication, and installation.

Manual J and Manual N support residential and small-commercial load calculations. Manual D and the Ductulator support duct design, airflow, friction, pressure, and sizing. The Pipefitter’s Handbook supports practical piping questions, dimensions, layout work, and trade calculations.

Practice should include timed lookup drills. Select a topic, identify the correct reference, search the index, and locate the relevant section. Track the amount of time required and repeat difficult searches until the process becomes familiar.

Calculation practice should be completed carefully. Write down the known values, select the correct formula or procedure, confirm the units, complete the calculation, and review whether the result is reasonable. Candidates should practice with the same books and tools they plan to use during the examination.

During the test, answer familiar questions first and mark difficult questions for review. Avoid spending excessive time searching for one answer. When a question is unfamiliar, identify its main keyword and determine which book is most likely to address that subject.
