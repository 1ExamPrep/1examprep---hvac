Exam Details

Mississippi identifies Heat, Ventilation, and Air Conditioning as a tested specialty contractor classification. Its scope includes the installation, maintenance, repair, fabrication, alteration, and extension of HVAC systems. The classification also includes associated HVAC equipment, ductwork, duct cleaning, industrial air systems used for dust control, ventilation systems, and related work.

Applicants begin the licensing process by submitting the applicable contractor license application to the Mississippi State Board of Contractors. The Board reviews the application, notifies the applicant when requirements are missing, and sends eligible candidate information to PSI. Candidates cannot schedule their examinations until PSI receives their eligibility and provides registration instructions.

All Mississippi contractor applicants are required to complete the Mississippi Law and Business Management examination. A trade examination may also be required according to the classification requested. Candidates pursuing the HVAC classification should prepare for the applicable HVAC trade examination unless the Mississippi State Board of Contractors approves a trade-exam waiver under an available reciprocity provision.

The HVAC trade examination may address refrigeration principles, heating and cooling systems, HVAC equipment, controls, piping, insulation, ventilation, exhaust systems, duct construction, fuel-gas installations, liquefied petroleum gas, combustion air, chimneys, vents, load calculations, duct sizing, safety, and practical trade mathematics.

Questions may require candidates to recognize a technical principle, interpret a code provision, find information in a table, identify an installation requirement, or complete a calculation. Preparation should therefore combine subject review with active use of the listed reference materials.

The Mississippi Law and Business Management examination focuses on the responsibilities associated with operating a contracting business. Candidates should also prepare for subjects such as licensing, bidding, estimating, contracts, project management, financial management, business organization, taxes, labor responsibilities, risk management, safety, and other contractor business topics.

Open Book Test

Mississippi contractor examinations administered by PSI are open book. Candidates are responsible for bringing the references permitted for the examinations they schedule and following the current testing rules concerning book editions, markings, tabs, notes, and additional materials.

An open-book exam rewards candidates who know how the references are organized. A candidate who opens an unfamiliar book for the first time during the test may spend too much time searching for one answer. Regular practice helps build familiarity with each book’s index, table of contents, chapter structure, formulas, charts, and code arrangement.

The online course is designed to support that preparation by connecting study topics with the references where those topics are commonly addressed. When reviewing fuel-gas questions, candidates should know how to use the International Fuel Gas Code. When studying mechanical-system requirements, they should practice with the International Mechanical Code. Load calculations and duct-design questions require familiarity with the applicable ACCA manuals and calculation tools.

Effective book navigation begins with identifying the main subject of the question. Candidates should determine whether the question concerns refrigeration, mechanical code, fuel gas, LP gas, OSHA safety, duct construction, piping, residential load calculations, commercial load calculations, or duct sizing. That decision helps narrow the search to the most appropriate reference.

Candidates should review the current PSI Candidate Information Bulletin before test day. PSI determines which reference materials may enter the examination room and what types of tabs, highlighting, notes, or markings are accepted. Testing rules should be followed carefully to avoid delays or rejection of a reference at the testing center.

Licensing Steps

  1. Select the appropriate classification. Confirm that the Mississippi HVAC contractor classification matches the work the business intends to perform.
  2. Determine the applicant structure. Identify whether the application will be submitted by an individual, limited liability company, corporation, partnership, or another eligible business entity.
  3. Select the qualifying party. Designate the person who will qualify the company and complete the required examinations.
  4. Complete the contractor application. Provide the requested business, ownership, qualifying-party, financial, insurance, and supporting information.
  5. Submit the application to the Mississippi State Board of Contractors. The Board must receive and review the application before the candidate can schedule an examination.
  6. Respond to application requests. Supply missing documents, corrections, or additional information requested during the review.
  7. Receive examination eligibility. After eligibility is approved, the Board sends the candidate’s information to PSI.
  8. Register with PSI. Follow the testing provider’s instructions to schedule the required examination dates.
  9. Prepare with the online course and reference books. Review the trade subjects, practice calculations, and develop efficient reference-navigation skills.
  10. Take the required examinations. Bring acceptable identification and only the books and materials permitted under the current testing rules.
  11. Complete outstanding licensing requirements. Submit any remaining documentation needed for final application review.
  12. Receive the Board’s licensing decision. Passing the examinations does not automatically issue a license. The complete application must be reviewed and approved.

State Requirements

The Mississippi State Board of Contractors administers the contractor licensing process for the applicable HVAC classification. Candidates must apply under the classification that corresponds with the services the business intends to offer.

The Heat, Ventilation, and Air Conditioning specialty classification includes work involving HVAC system installation, maintenance, repair, fabrication, alteration, and extension. It also includes associated equipment, ductwork, duct cleaning, industrial air systems for dust control, ventilation systems, and related work.

The qualifying party must be appropriately connected to the applicant and satisfy the requirements associated with the requested classification. The application may require documentation showing the qualifying party’s role, employment, ownership, or relationship to the business.

Commercial contractor applicants may be required to submit a reviewed financial statement prepared by a certified public accountant. Other application requirements may include business registration records, tax identification information, reference letters, general liability insurance documentation, workers’ compensation information when applicable, and supporting information about the qualifying party.

Every applicant must complete the Mississippi Law and Business Management examination. The HVAC trade examination may be waived only when the Board approves an applicable reciprocity request. Reciprocity applies to the trade examination rather than the entire licensing process, so applicants must still complete the Mississippi application and satisfy the remaining requirements.

Passing an examination is one part of the contractor licensing process. A candidate should not represent the business as licensed until the Mississippi State Board of Contractors has completed its review and issued the applicable credential.

Additional local requirements may apply to HVAC work. Depending on the project and location, a contractor may need permits, inspections, registrations, insurance coverage, or approvals from a city, county, project owner, or other authority.

Reference Books

The following books support study for the Mississippi HVAC contractor trade examination. They are not included with the online course unless purchased separately.

  • Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning, 22nd Edition
    A comprehensive HVAC reference covering refrigeration principles, refrigerants, heating systems, cooling systems, equipment, controls, components, installation practices, troubleshooting concepts, and system operation.
  • Code of Federal Regulations—29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA)
    A federal construction-safety reference covering workplace hazards, personal protective equipment, fall protection, electrical safety, tools, scaffolding, materials handling, excavation, and other construction activities.
  • International Fuel Gas Code, 2021 Edition
    A code reference addressing fuel-gas piping, appliances, equipment connections, combustion air, venting, testing, installation requirements, and safety provisions.
  • HVAC Duct Construction Standards—Metal and Flexible, 2005, 3rd Edition
    A technical standard covering the fabrication, reinforcement, support, assembly, installation, and construction of metal and flexible HVAC duct systems.
  • Manual N—Commercial Load Calculation for Small Commercial Buildings, 5th Edition
    An ACCA reference for calculating heating and cooling loads in small commercial buildings using design conditions, construction details, heat transfer, occupancy, ventilation, and internal loads.
  • International Mechanical Code, 2021 Edition
    A mechanical-code reference addressing HVAC equipment, ventilation, exhaust systems, duct systems, combustion air, appliance installation, mechanical rooms, chimneys, vents, and related requirements.
  • NFPA 58 Liquefied Petroleum Gas Code, 2020 Edition
    A code covering the storage, handling, transportation, installation, and use of liquefied petroleum gas, including containers, piping, equipment, separation requirements, and safety practices.
  • Ductulator
    A practical HVAC tool used to compare airflow, duct dimensions, friction rate, and velocity when sizing or evaluating air-distribution systems.
  • Manual D, 2016, 3rd Edition
    An ACCA residential duct-design reference covering airflow requirements, available static pressure, friction rate, fittings, equivalent length, duct sizing, balancing, and system performance.
  • Pipefitter’s Handbook
    A field-oriented reference containing piping formulas, dimensions, fitting data, measurement information, layout methods, conversion tables, and practical pipefitting calculations.
  • Manual J—Residential Load Calculations, Reprinted 2016, Eighth Full Edition
    An ACCA reference for calculating residential heating and cooling loads based on climate, insulation, construction materials, windows, doors, infiltration, orientation, occupancy, and internal heat gains.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive study plan should connect each examination subject with the reference most likely to address it. Candidates should avoid treating the books as one large collection. Dividing them into clear subject groups makes studying and navigation more manageable.

Use Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning for broad trade review involving refrigeration cycles, refrigerants, heating and cooling systems, equipment, controls, components, and HVAC operating principles.

Use the International Mechanical Code for mechanical-system requirements involving equipment installation, ventilation, exhaust, duct systems, combustion air, chimneys, and vents. Use the International Fuel Gas Code for fuel-gas piping, appliance connections, testing, combustion air, and venting. Use NFPA 58 for liquefied petroleum gas subjects.

Use the OSHA reference for questions involving construction safety and workplace practices. Use the HVAC duct construction standard for duct materials, reinforcement, joints, supports, fabrication, and installation.

Manual J supports residential load calculations, while Manual N addresses small-commercial load calculations. Manual D and the Ductulator help candidates work through duct sizing, airflow, velocity, friction rate, pressure, fittings, and system-design questions. The Pipefitter’s Handbook supports piping dimensions, fitting information, formulas, and field calculations.

Timed lookup practice should be part of every study schedule. Select a topic, identify the correct book, find the term in the index, and locate the relevant section. Record how long the search takes and repeat difficult lookups until the process becomes familiar.

Calculation practice should include reading the problem carefully, listing the known values, selecting the correct formula or procedure, checking units, completing the calculation, and reviewing whether the result is reasonable. Candidates should use the applicable manuals and tools while practicing instead of relying only on memorized formulas.

During the examination, candidates can begin with questions they recognize and return to more difficult questions after completing the easier material. When a question requires a reference search, identifying its central keyword can help determine the correct book and index term.
