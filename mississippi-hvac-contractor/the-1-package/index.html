Exam Details

Mississippi contractor applicants are generally required to complete the Mississippi Law and Business Management examination. A trade examination may also be required for the HVAC classification unless the applicant qualifies for an approved examination waiver or reciprocity provision.

The Mississippi HVAC examination covers work associated with heating and cooling systems, fuel-gas systems and appliances, related equipment, and ductwork. The examination contains 80 questions, allows four hours, and requires a score of 70 percent to pass.

HVAC examination subject areas include piping, insulation, heating and cooling principles, refrigeration systems, refrigerants, load calculations, testing and balancing, controls, HVAC equipment, furnaces, heaters, combustion air, chimneys, flues, vents, ducts, ventilation, exhaust systems, fuel gas, liquefied petroleum gas, safety, and related trade calculations. Candidates should be prepared to apply both practical HVAC knowledge and information located within the approved reference materials.

The Mississippi Law and Business Management examination contains 50 questions, allows two hours, and requires a score of 70 percent to pass. Its content areas include licensing, estimating and bidding, lien law, financial management, tax laws, labor laws, project management, contracts, business organization, risk management, environmental requirements, and safety.

Mississippi contractor examinations are administered through PSI. Applicants must first submit their licensing application to the Mississippi State Board of Contractors. After the Board reviews the application and sends the applicant’s eligibility information to PSI, PSI provides instructions for scheduling the required examinations.

Open Book Test

The Mississippi HVAC contractor and Law and Business Management examinations are open book. Open book does not mean that every answer will be obvious or that there will be enough time to search every reference from the beginning. Candidates must know which book applies to a question, where information is generally located, and how to use indexes, tables of contents, formulas, charts, and code sections efficiently.

The books supplied with this package are highlighted and permanently tabbed to support faster navigation. Candidates should still study the tabs, review the highlighted areas, and practice using the books before the examination. Temporary tabs, loose papers, removable notes, and unauthorized materials may not be permitted in the testing center. The testing provider’s current candidate bulletin and examination-center rules control what may be brought into the room.

A productive open-book study routine includes learning the purpose of each reference, identifying the vocabulary used in the index, completing timed lookup exercises, and practicing questions that require calculations or interpretation. The goal is not simply to own the books. The goal is to know how to use them under examination conditions.

Licensing Steps

  1. Select the appropriate classification. Determine the Mississippi contractor classification that matches the HVAC work the business intends to perform.
  2. Establish the business entity. Form the LLC or corporation that will operate the contracting business and obtain the required business identification information.
  3. Prepare the contractor application. Complete the applicable Mississippi State Board of Contractors application and assemble the requested supporting documents.
  4. Submit the application for review. The Board reviews the application, identifies missing requirements when applicable, and determines examination eligibility.
  5. Receive authorization to test. After the Board sends eligibility information to PSI, the qualifying party may follow PSI’s instructions to schedule the examinations.
  6. Prepare for the required examinations. Study the examination content outlines, learn the approved references, and complete practice-oriented review.
  7. Take the examinations. Arrive with acceptable identification and only the materials permitted under the current testing rules.
  8. Complete outstanding licensing requirements. Submit any remaining documentation requested by the Board.
  9. Board review and license issuance. Passing an examination does not by itself issue a license. The completed application remains subject to review and approval by the Mississippi State Board of Contractors.
  10. Maintain the license and business. Track renewal dates, business records, tax responsibilities, insurance obligations, local requirements, and ongoing contractor compliance.

State Requirements

The Mississippi State Board of Contractors regulates the applicable state contractor licensing process. The classification requested should correspond to the work the company plans to perform. HVAC work falls within the mechanical contracting field, and the applicant must use the classification recognized by the Board for the intended scope of work.

Commercial contractor applicants may be required to provide a reviewed financial statement prepared by a certified public accountant. Financial requirements can differ depending on whether the applicant is seeking a major classification or a specialty classification. Applicants must use the current application instructions to determine the documents and financial information required for their filing.

The individual who qualifies the company must meet the Board’s requirements and complete the required examinations unless an approved waiver applies. All contractor applicants are required to complete the Mississippi Law and Business Management examination, while the applicable trade examination depends on the classification and any recognized reciprocity or waiver provisions.

Licensing approval is separate from business formation. Creating an LLC or corporation and obtaining an EIN can establish the business entity, but those filings do not authorize the company to perform work that requires a contractor license. The contractor application, examination, documentation, and Board approval steps must still be completed.

Contractors may also be responsible for local permits, registrations, tax accounts, insurance, workers’ compensation obligations, and other requirements related to their operations. Requirements can depend on the company’s location, workforce, project type, contract value, and scope of work.

Test Information and Study Materials

Effective preparation should combine trade review, code navigation, business-law study, calculations, and timed practice. Begin by separating the HVAC references from the business and law reference. Learn which book is most likely to contain information for each examination topic.

For the HVAC examination, focus on heating and cooling principles, refrigerants, refrigeration systems, equipment, controls, piping, fuel gas, LP gas, duct construction, ventilation, combustion air, venting, safety, and load calculations. Practice using Manual J, Manual N, Manual D, and the Ductulator rather than only reading about their purpose.

For the Law and Business Management examination, review licensing, bidding, estimating, contracts, project management, financial management, labor, taxes, risk, safety, and business organization. Learn the index language used by the NASCLA guide so that you can move quickly from a question’s key phrase to the relevant chapter.

Complete practice questions with a timer and a full reference set. Mark questions that take too long, then study why the answer was difficult to locate. Repeated navigation practice builds familiarity with the books and can reduce time lost during the actual examination.
